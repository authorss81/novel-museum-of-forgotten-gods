## What this batch has to do

Elias is suspended and has no authority in the building. **The volume's rule is that losing authority gets him more help, not less** — Ivo runs a systems inventory nobody asked him to run, Hattie Bramm becomes a source who is owed rather than managed, and Doreen Sarn, a retired pay clerk of seventy-eight, produces a private notebook she will not surrender. He also performs his **second reading**, and it is the most important scene in the batch because it does two jobs at once.

**It confirms the power and it indicts a living person.** Part of what comes out of the object is independently confirmed by a **hostile** line of evidence — a document from the body that would least want to say it — and that confirmation is what earns the formal step from Nightwatch to Echo. Part of it names a living person and accuses them of something the object saw from one side. The reader should be able to hold both facts in the same hand. This is the Volume 01 proof that **an echo is perspective-bound**: it can show a person accurately while making the surrounding situation false, and it can repeat a lie because an owner repeated it.

Do not claim the stage step by having somebody announce it. It is claimed by the shape of the evidence, on the page, and it is not clean.

**The turn at the end of the batch:** the suppression and the exposure are now competing *inside the same institution*, and Elias's method of keeping people safe is the thing producing the danger. He withholds the true count from Mara a second time, knowingly, to keep the families out of reach of an insurer and a liquidator — and this time there is no gap of four seconds in which nothing has happened yet. He has four seconds and he uses them. **Both withholdings are now on the page, and the second one is a decision rather than an oversight.** Mara's two conditions from Chapter 9 are the engine of the whole batch: *the result goes in the shared log in her hand exactly as it comes*, and *he tells her everything, every time*. **Corrected after the Batch 0001 review passes — read this twice, because the first correction was itself superseded:**

- The **first** condition was **not** broken in Chapter 10. It was **kept to the letter and defeated him anyway.** Her own rule is that the shared log takes no bare figure, so on the evidence she was given she wrote *a number*, correctly, and he had four seconds in which to hand her an object and did not. He gave her an empty hand when she asked for the notebook. **Nobody was lied to about the reading.**
- The **second** condition — *he tells her everything, every time, and not the parts he has decided were relevant* — **he breaks himself, in Chapter 10, on purpose, and in two separate ways.** First by silence: he decides that a man with a mop is not part of what he is telling her about, and does not volunteer **Perry Loach's** name, which he could have had in ninety seconds at a service desk. Then **by a lie**: when she asks him point-blank whether there is anything in the notebook that is not on the page, he says no — *that's all of it, and it's all of it in that book* — and there is. **The cleaner's name goes into the coat pocket to keep company with a headcount.**

**Be exact about this, because two earlier passes got it wrong in both directions and the prose is the authority: he tells Mara no untruth whatever about the reading itself.** The result field is correct, the units are correct, the cost is correct, the stop word is hers, and every question she asks him about the reading he answers accurately. **The only lie he tells a person in Batch 0001 is about the notebook, after the reading is over, and nobody can catch him at it.** Do not write him as having lied about the reading, and do not write him as having lied to nobody.

So this batch's second withholding is an **escalation, not a first offence**, and that is what makes it cost him. Do not restage the result field as Elias correcting Mara or as Mara making an error, and **do not restage the cleaner's name as a second empty hand** — it is a different mechanism and it is already on the page. Do not soften the lie into a silence either; he says it out loud. What is left for Movement 4 and 5 is a man who knows exactly what his own signature is worth.

**Dael moves the case forward by scheduling a public tour rather than losing it.** Chapter 20 ends with the tour booked. It does not happen in this batch. Chapters 21–30 are the run-up and the event.

**Escalation discipline, one sentence: each chapter must raise the number of people who can be hurt, not the size of the object.** Nothing escalates into a bigger monster. There is no bigger monster.

## Hard locks carried from Batch 0001

These are established in finished prose. Do not re-derive, re-date, or contradict them. Full detail is in `state/continuity.md`.

**The four counts.** 94 worked the final Sillick closing shift · 92 on the paid payroll · 2 worked and never entered in any book · 61 short-paid on the final pay run (a pay figure, a different and overlapping group, which includes the two). Fixed, separate, and never substitutable. **No fifth number may appear in this batch.**

**92 and 2 are HELD for the Chapter 25 midpoint reversal.** The Batch 0001 outline's aftermath section said all four figures would be "on the page"; two of them deliberately are not, so that a reader who already has 94 can be told it was paid as 92. **Do not spend them in this batch.** If a scene genuinely needs a payroll figure, write the conflict without a numeral — "the pay run listed fewer than were on the gate" — and say so here rather than quietly using 92.

**Sixty-one does double duty** — it is the short-paid count and Perry Loach's age. Chapter 6 used it for the count. In any chapter that names or shows Perry, render him as **"a night cleaner in his early sixties"** and keep the numeral off the page.

**The notebook has seven entries and nobody adds an eighth.** Six soundings plus one written null result (*NO SOUND, THU*). The Saturday interval is a continuation of the 21:07 line, not a line of its own.

**The object stays `answering`.** It repeats a small part of its last act and asks nothing of anybody. It is not `demanding` — that is Chapters 22–24, in the public tour, and it is Batch 0003's escalation, not this batch's. It is never `hollowed` in Volume 01. **The repair staple is over a crack and the crack is still visible; the crack must not heal.**

**The god is still unnamed.** Narration says *the hall bell*, *plinth thirty-four*, *the Sillick bell*. **"The Bell of the Unpaid Shift" is a Batch 0005 payoff**, when the true ledger's title is recovered.

**The corridor service bell is plumbing, not a relic.** Pressed brass, hard-piped to the ward-bell repeater line, slightly out of tune, and it does nothing else. It cannot count, reach a person, make a request, or be the source of anything. It is also not the dead civic repeater bracket further down the same corridor, which has no bell and has had none for years. The horror is the room, not the object.

**The unlisted room is seen only through clouded wired glass** — a swivel chair with no dust on the seat, a coat hook, a shelf of Museum lanyards with every name panel blank, dust on everything else, cold air under a shut and **latched** door. **Elias does not open that door in this volume, and the volume's last chapter depends on him not doing so.** The volume ends with that latch coming off, unopened, in front of a witness who writes down the time. It is the director who opens it, in Chapter 50, a month after the memorial, as the first act of the inquiry.

**Elias does not breach the two-person lift rule in this batch.** He was refused at the gate in Chapter 7, he did not do anything stupid, and that restraint is the reason he can be trusted with the box later. Keep it.

**The year in a Museum accession reference is the year the district *series* was opened, not the year the object came in.** This is a Low Ward house convention, it is stated aloud by Mara in Chapter 3, and it is why the Sillick box cannot be dated from its own label. A reference with no year, or one letter where the form takes two (`Acq. 4471 / B`), is not a Museum number at all. **1961 is not an event year and must not be used as one.** The mill closed thirty-one years ago, and thirty-one years is the span of every memory of it in this volume - Hattie Bramm's folder, the voice on the plinth, the ages of the objects. **Do not invent a second, different thirty-one.**

**Record formats are stable and comparable by a reader.** Accession `1961/LW/44` (the Sillick Mill papers, level B bay 9, now on a loading-bay pallet). Personnel file `PF` plus three digits; **PF 118** is on the hand-written label gummed over the printed one and is not Case's, because Case has no file. Work order `4412-S`. Municipal payroll cost centre `M.Q.S. 4`. Case's locker **81**. The device book has Elias's name at 01:52 out and 02:41 back on Thursday, with no destination, and a second unbooked removal on Friday night.

**The device book is live leverage.** Dael could pull it and has not, because Elias did not offer it. Batch 0002 is the batch in which it nearly gets pulled.

**The four photographs exist on Elias's personal phone and in no official system.** They are the only copy of the erased name. Chapter 13 is where that becomes a negotiation instead of a secret.

**At most two Index lines in this batch**, short, concrete, incomplete, interior, never used to answer a question Elias asked. The Index reports what the Museum's charter considers important, not what is morally true, and it can be wrong about importance. Two lines used with no elaboration is plenty.

**Elias's memory cost does not escalate here.** The whole of Batch 0001's memory cost is **one notebook entry in Chapter 8, written in two visibly different hands, in an order he cannot recover, with sixteen minutes of the night in neither hand.** It is not a wobble and it does not stabilise. **Three facts about Chapter 10 that Batch 0002 inherits and must not get wrong.** (1) **Mara's rule: the shared log takes no bare figure.** A result with an object under it is a record; a result with a guess under it is a rumour she will not sign her name to. The building has no apparatus for asking one. **This cannot be solved by a form, and no chapter in this batch may pretend it can.** The escalation that is available to you is not a louder bell; it is what happens to a man who said a number without being asked, whether anyone finally goes and asks him, and what it costs the person who does the asking. He is one of the short-paid, he was there for the closing count, and he will be on the floors. If nothing addresses this, the batch has not escalated the thing that actually escalated in Chapter 10.

## What each chapter owes

These are the beats the Volume 01 outline and the Movement 2 plan fix. The interior detail is yours to shape, but the outcome of each chapter is not.

**11 — The suspension.** Sunday morning, 07:00. Dael does it properly and in person, which is a kindness and a violence in the same sentence. Elias is off night duty and off the collections floors, and he keeps his keys. The week is a man standing very close to a building he cannot walk into. He learns he is not on Monday's re-inspection list. Emotional beat: the letter becomes the only room he is still allowed into, and he does not open it. No supernatural event. Resist the urge to give him a sound on Sunday — the machine is out of action and the batch should let the silence be a change in the world.

**12 — The re-inspection, and the second reading.** Monday. The 09:00 inspection happens without him; he gets it secondhand from Ivo and Mara, and what comes back is an institution **certifying containment it has not asked about**: a documented re-inspection, a Registry classification, a re-anchored case, a photograph of the hand-cut card by a bureaucrat whose job is notices, not answers. The file that has never existed now exists, in a system he cannot read. Then, tonight, the second reading. Bounded, witnessed, with a written time limit and a named cost. It is confirmed in part by a **hostile** document and it accuses a living person of something the object saw from one side. Costs on the page. Stage 1 is now earned and confirmed and is not a triumph. A number that is wrong in a room appears in this chapter, and it is the volume's second figure — but it is **not** 92 and it is **not** 2, and it must be a *different kind* of figure entirely (a headcount of carts, a number on a gate, a figure in a name) so the four counts stay clean.

**13 — Ivo's inventory and the four photographs.** Tuesday. Ivo runs a systems inventory nobody asked him to run, and it is the first time the collaboration runs the other way. The photographs have to go somewhere: to Mara, to the file, to a solicitor, to nobody. The drawer in the label room that is half a millimetre out of true has not been opened by Elias but somebody in that room has noticed it, and Fenn has been working there a long time. Make this the batch's first genuine moment of Elias being told no by a person who is not his supervisor.

**14 — Doreen Sarn.** Wednesday. The retired pay clerk, seventy-eight, with a notebook she will not surrender. She is not an oracle and she is not a device; she is a woman who kept a copy because the mill kept none. Quill & Frayle enter as reasonable solicitors for Hallowgate with immovable deadlines and no personal villainy. The ledger trail opens here and does not close.

**15 — Institutions.** Thursday. The Board's insurance position, the Registry's notice language, and Anja's conditions. This is the political-pressure chapter and it must be made of documents, deadlines, and people who each answer for a different half of the same week. Escalation: containment is getting measurably more effective everywhere else, which is worse. Nobody in this chapter is a villain and one of them is trying to help.

**16 — Hattie's queue.** Friday, Low Ward, by tram, off the record. The debt from Chapter 6 comes due: she has found out who he is, or has come close, and she has nine years of letters and sixty-one names. **The second breach is made here or is armed here** — Elias learns something that would, in the wrong hands today, put the families at risk of losing a claim or being named before the ledger is in hand. He is not protecting himself this time. That is what makes it worse.

**17 — The escalation.** Saturday. The mill site, or the building, or both, in the same week as everything else. Real pressure on real people, an actual physical risk, and a real injury or a real loss is permitted. It must not be a bigger monster. Whatever is destroyed or damaged must be something a person needed.

**18 — Aftermath.** Sunday. The recovery and emotional payoff beat. Elias and Mara in a room, and the breach is **named** — by one of them, out loud — and it is not forgiven, and the relationship survives on the terms the bible sets: they can tell the truth about risk and still choose each other. No intimacy yet. The slow burn starts with a protocol and a condition, and the condition is the relationship.

**19 — The institution decides.** Monday. Dael's move begins. The Board, the insurer and the registrar each answer for a different half of the same week, and the thing they produce is not a cover-up this time but a **procedure** — which is the same thing wearing better clothes, and the chapter should make the reader feel it.

**20 — The tour is scheduled.** Tuesday. Dael moves the case forward by scheduling a public tour rather than losing it. He is still sympathizable and the reader should be able to agree with him and be afraid for him in the same scene. **The tour does not happen in this batch.** The chapter ends on a decision and a date, and on Elias understanding that the room he wanted to keep private is now going to be full of children, and that he cannot stop it, and that he is the only person who knows the number.

## Voice notes, from the ten drafted chapters

- Third person, past tense, close on Elias. Plain declaratives with a long sentence every so often and a very short one at the beat. No purple, no fantasy diction, no invented technical language.
- Elias thinks in **doors, clocks, exits, stains on floors, and what can be written down**. His interior tic is inventory, not revelation. When he is afraid he counts things and then is embarrassed by the counting.
- The horror is administrative. A document is more frightening than a shape in a corridor, and a well-meant procedure is more frightening than a malevolent one.
- Ivo jokes when he is frightened and his jokes are how he buys room. He builds things with his hands. He is never a mystic and he is never a supporting role for Elias's power.
- Dael is a man with a liability figure he cannot carry by himself. He writes the same four lines about a shutter that he would write about a door, and hates himself a bit afterwards.
- Mara is procedural, not dismissive, and she is right. Her competence must be specific, material, and useful to the reader. She is a person who writes things down.
- Hattie Bramm is a queue, a folder, a hard hand, and thirty-one years. She is not a mouthpiece and Elias did not choose her.
- Doreen Sarn keeps her own counsel and her own copy. She is a person who has outlived every institution that wanted her paperwork.
- Do not repeat the Chapter 10 reading scene. The batch's second reading must be built on a *different* room, a *different* kind of witness, and a *different* failure, and it must not be staged in a hall full of cleaners again.
- Do not use `held`, `steady`, `plain`, `counted`, `breathed`, `wrote`, or `nodded` as mechanical filler. Elias is a man who writes things down, so *wrote* is thematically load-bearing; spend it deliberately and vary it everywhere else.
