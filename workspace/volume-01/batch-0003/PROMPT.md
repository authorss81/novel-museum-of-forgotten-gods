## What this batch has to do

Three events, in this order, and they must not be allowed to fuse.

1. **The box leaves the building on 4 November.** 1961/LW/44 goes to a bay in a quayside store in Northglass, secured, and the bay holds 1961/LW/41 to 1961/LW/52. It is not opened by the Museum, and it is not opened by the transfer clerk, and the twenty-two hourlies who sign for it do not open it. **The wage sheets go into a shipping container.** That is the horror of Movement 4 and it is administrative: nobody breaks a rule, nobody steals anything, and the document that can prove the claim is put in a steel box and lifted over a wall, and everybody who signs for it is a professional.
2. **The tour happens on Saturday 7 November** — brought forward in Chapter 26 from the date it was booked for, **Friday 13 November** — and the bell counts, and **Perry Loach answers with a number he has not used in thirty-one years and three children begin completing it**, in front of press, in a room built for school groups, at eleven on a Saturday morning. **The brought-forward date must be after 4 November. If the tour lands on or before the site clearance, Dael Ordway's deferral argument is worth nothing and the docket goes.** **This must not be a rerun of Chapter 10.** In Chapter 10 the utterance was private, unprompted, in a locked gallery at ten at night, and was suppressed. **Here it is public, prompted by a room, and answered by other people, and it cannot be unsaid and it was not asked for by him.** Chapter 10 has already spent the beat of *a man in overalls says a number without being asked*. Do not spend it again. Chapter 21 is the one place that beat may be recalled, and only in the past tense and only in about four lines.
3. **The midpoint reversal, Chapter 25.** *The Bell is not asking to be worshipped and it is not a shrine waiting to be rebuilt.* Its echo is a **wage claim**: a closing-day shift count of **ninety-four** was paid as **ninety-two**, and **two people worked and were never entered in any book.** Stated plainly, in public, by Elias, after Hattie Bramm has stated the six of the hundred and forty she can account for, and after the children have supplied five and the room has supplied four. **The reversal lands in the same scene it is stated in, and it lands because a child says a number and two people who were never entered answer from the floor of the room.**

**The public room is a hazard with teeth, not a stage.** The rules the batch must run: *one question, one anchor, a named cost, a time limit, a witness, and a way out that stays the way out.* Elias asks one question. The child who is prompted by the room cannot be a cost, an anchor, or a question. Ivo has the clamp and the cover, Mara has the log, the anchor card is on the plinth, and the stop word is Hattie's word and only Hattie's. **Perry is not on the rota and comes because he asked Mara whether Saturdays were still on.** The anchor card is spilled by a child and the clamp goes by the case and both are on the floor before Elias's hand is on anything, and the reading goes wrong **because the room filled faster than the protocol could**, and the object reaches a child because a room full of people is the loudest signal a work-god has ever had.

**The six of the hundred and forty.** After the reversal, and only in Chapters 28–30, a person may do a piece of arithmetic in public. **It is an audience count, not a mill count, and the number is not a Sillick figure.** Do not state a total and do not state a shortfall, and do not let anybody put two Sillick numbers side by side and subtract. Say: *six out of about a hundred and forty people, and there are nine dead and two in a home and one in hospital, and that is what I can account for and no more.* **Any chapter that needs a payroll figure writes the conflict without a numeral** — *the pay run listed fewer than were on the gate* — and says so in `state/continuity.md` rather than quietly using 92.

**The Registry's ruling at the end of the tour is Annexe E**, and the routing is the one Anja Ward built herself and cannot escape: a Schedule 12 object reaches an unconsenting person in a public room ⇒ it is a *disturbance* and it is stayed from all handling, use, cleaning, movement and public access until a material-change inspection; **the dispute route in the schedule is the person who made the request** — which is Elias's own request form, lodged at the front desk on 19 October with a six-word reason. A stay is not a closure. A stay is a room with the lights on that nobody may touch, which is the whole Engine and the whole Museum in one administrative gesture. **Anja does not sign it and does not argue with it and does not know how to stop it, and she is not wrong, and she loses.**
