**This is Movement 5, the last movement of Volume 01, and the volume's climax.** The Volume 01 outline's Movement 5 beats, in this order, and **the order is the batch**: a night retrieval at the mill site in heavy rain with a partial success and a real injury · an emergency Board meeting where the Director permits the memorial and attaches conditions that protect the Museum at the families' expense, **because that is what she is actually doing** · the memorial itself: no summoning, no binding-name, no stagecraft · ninety-four called aloud in the Hall of Former Faiths by a Low Ward organiser with standing, the shortfall read out in full, and the original ledger handed to the families, not to the Museum · Elias witnessing it inside a bounded, named, timed, abandonable practice with Ivo on the exit and Mara as the person who can stop him.

**The batch turn and the volume's resolution, and they must not fuse:** the bell answers once and stops asking · the god is not returned, not destroyed and not worshipped; it is **acknowledged** — a public correction with a witness and a record — and the demand ends because it has been answered in the terms it was formed in · that one final note travels the building's own pipework, and in the Quiet Wing corridor a door that has been shut and latched for eighteen years comes off its latch, unopened, in front of Mara, who writes down the time because she is a person who writes things down · nobody touches it, Elias saw that latch in Chapter 8 and did not open it, and the volume ends by opening it for him while he stands outside · and then the volume's concrete resolutions, in this order and no other: the false payroll page is identified as a forgery and the original ledger is in the hands of the families · **Wendel Case is restored to the Museum's public staff record as an acknowledged wrong, without a body and without a miracle, and his name reappears on the payroll line that should always have carried it** · Dael Ordway is removed from night command and becomes a cautious witness and never becomes Elias's ally · the Casket fiction is broken · **Elias loses his post and is no longer a Museum guard, and he is a suspended man with a notebook, a cost and no authority.**

**The clock is now running and it is Batch 0004's gift to you:** the determination is on the Registry's public register from Friday 20 November and a trustee can quote its number; the Board sits Tuesday 24 November; a Registry assessor nobody has met certifies the Gallery on the same afternoon; the books pallet leaves bay 4 on Tuesday 25 November **with the mill's own bound wage book for October 1974 on it**; the Director's written review of Elias is Wednesday 25 November at 09:25; the hearing is Thursday 26 November at 18:00; the sealed envelope opens in Ferry Bank on Friday 27 November at 16:00; and the twenty-eight-day requester's window does not expire until **Thursday 18 December 2024**, which is the only instrument in the volume that is in his favour and which nobody but Elias Venn and Aurelia Roth knows exists.

## What each chapter owes

**41 — the two days, and the rain.** Open with the closed block: **Saturday 21 November and Sunday 22 November are the two days he does not tell anybody, and they are not a chapter of their own unless you can make them one without padding; the batch opens on Friday the twenty-second and carries the two days as a fact he is living inside.** His reason is on the page twice and the second one is the good one. **Then the night retrieval at the mill site in heavy rain: a partial success and a real injury, and nobody to blame.** The site cleared on Sunday 25 October and is bare. What is there is a compound, a fence, a weighbridge with a hump and a hut and a gate behind it, a hardstanding, and the two of them. **A partial success means they get one thing and not the thing they went for, and the thing they got is small and it is on purpose.** The injury is real and it is a fracture of something and it is a different injury from the radial styloid, and **nobody is to blame, and that is the sentence.** Elias's restriction is unchanged and he has no authority at all on that site, and the lawful door is the one that has been working all month: a public procedure, or a form, or a person who is not him saying yes.

**42 — the retrieval's consequences, and the ledger.** The ledger has to come out of a bonded bay in a working day, and the only instruments are a seal number, a sheet, a bay, a hold, a period and a woman with a headset who has never known what was in a bay in her life. **The clerk is competent, courteous and completely blameless and she will be the one who makes it possible and she will do it inside the rules and it will cost somebody something.** The claim from Friday 13th is now **seven days old and either made or not made**, and if it was not made, the thing it would have claimed is pulp, and the point of the retrieval is not the sheets, it is the book. **The original wage ledger has to be in the hands of the families by the memorial and it is in a bonded warehouse four miles from a museum and the Museum does not own it and the family it belongs to is a woman of seventy-eight.** Arja Ward is a laundrette in Low Ward with eleven machines and a shelf above the boiler and a letter that is on the page and is not opened until the Friday after, and **she is the only person in the volume who can receive it and she has refused to be a witness, a signatory, a reader and a recorder, and every one of those refusals stands.**

**43 — the emergency Board meeting, and it is a decision with conditions that protect the Museum at the families' expense, because that is what she is actually doing.** This is the batch's institutional turn and it is Anja Ward's chapter, and **she would rather be hated than wrong and she is right.** The Board is on **Tuesday 24 November**; the Registry assessor is in the Gallery that afternoon; the books pallet is collected on the Tuesday; the review is on the Wednesday morning. **The decision permits the memorial and attaches conditions, and the conditions are defensible and they cost the families, and she says so in the room in about nine sentences and does not dress it up.** Nobody in that room is a villain. The conditions must be *specific and real* — a closed room, a panel, a limit on numbers, a form, a press line, a liability position, a name — and **the volume's argument is that the conditions are the price and the price is being paid by the wrong people.**

Hattie Bramm has said she will say the thing about the people who never had a letter if anybody stands her up, and **nobody stands her up, and she says it anyway, from the back, in her own voice, and it is a file and not a gallery.** **The stop word is NEXT and it is Hattie's and only Hattie's and it does not appear in this chapter and it may not be used by anybody else.** Perry Loach may be in the room. **45 — the memorial's preparation, and the bounded practice.** No summoning, no binding-name, no stagecraft. **A hall, a rope, a case, a plinth card, a panel with a workers' name on it, and a man who has written down what is going to be asked, what it costs, how long it lasts, what the word is, who says it, and where the exit is.** The anchor card is on the plinth. The clamp is on the surround. The cover is on a trestle and it is not on the case. Ivo on the exit. **Mara is the person who can stop him and she has said she will and she must be able to and he has to accept that in advance in a room with two people in it.** The practice is *named, timed, bound and abandonable*, and **the abandonability is the point and the chapter must show him meaning it.** No reading has been planned. **The question is one, the nine minutes are on the card, the cost is on the card, and the word is not his.**

**46 — the memorial itself, and it must not be a spectacle.** **Hattie Bramm calls the count, because she is the person who was the gate clerk and was there, and not because Elias chose her, and he must not have chosen her and the chapter must be able to prove it.** The shortfall is read out in full. **The original ledger is handed to the families and not to the Museum, and the handover is a document and a set of hands and nobody is given a plaque.** No one is granted a religion. **The bell answers once and stops asking, and that one note travels the building's own pipework, and it is not a sound effect and it is not described as a miracle and nobody in the room is told what it means.** Elias's reading confirms the count, the record and the date **and nothing else** — the god does not bless, appear or explain itself. **And at the end of it, in the Quiet Wing corridor, the door that has been shut and latched for eighteen years comes off its latch, unopened, in front of Mara, who writes down the time because she is a person who writes things down, and Elias is outside it, and nobody touches it.** **Do not spend anything after this in this chapter.** The volume's last gesture is that door and it is the smallest thing the object ever does.

**47 — what it cost.** The Hall closes for a month. The Board opens an inquiry. **Dael Ordway is removed from night command** and becomes a cautious witness in the inquiry and never becomes Elias's ally. **The families receive a documented claim and an interim insurance payment, not a settlement — a real, partial, unsatisfying win.** The Casket fiction is broken by a document that should not exist. **Wendel Case is restored to the Museum's public staff record as an acknowledged wrong, without a body and without a miracle, and his name reappears on the payroll line that should always have carried it, and the restoration is a line in a register and a letter to a family and a line in an inquiry report, and it is the correct ending and it is not a happy one.** The schedule of contents is rebuilt, or the finding is that it will not be.

**48–49 — the resolution and the last door.** Elias's post: **he loses it, and he is barred from the building as a security matter, and he is a suspended man with a notebook, a cost and no authority**, and **the cost curve does not resolve** — there is no second notebook entry and no second memory cost and the first major displacement remains reserved for Volume 03, **and if you want to pay the twenty-eight days you pay it as a form, not as a memory.** The quiet Wing door is **not** opened by Elias; the outline is explicit that it is the director, at the volume's last chapter, who opens it formally as the first act of the inquiry, **one month after the memorial, and not before.** New question for the next volume: a row of flats on Sillick Lane dropping off every official list at once.

**50 — the final image, and it is fixed.** *Elias stands on the pavement outside the Museum he is no longer allowed to enter, in the last hour before the Hall reopens to the public with the workers' panel on the case and no Museum staff on the door. Behind the glass, in a hall he can see and not touch, the bell gives its single note. He does not go in. He goes to Sillick Lane instead, because the question has moved to where the people are, and a man who can only guard buildings has been told he is not a building.*

## Voice notes, from the forty drafted chapters

- Third person, past tense, close on Elias. Plain declaratives with a long sentence every so often and a very short one at the beat. No purple, no fantasy diction, no invented technical language.
- **Elias thinks in doors, clocks, exits, stains on floors and what can be written down.** He has stopped counting objects and started counting **rooms**, and he does not yet know he has changed. When he is afraid he counts things and is embarrassed by the counting. He has now said a number out loud in a room, he has now concealed a fact from six people, and he has now lied to a stranger about a document and it cost nothing and was not found.
- **The horror is administrative.** A document is more frightening than a shape in a corridor, and a well-meant procedure is more frightening than a malevolent one. **The night retrieval must be written that way: a site, a fence, a van, a form and a man with a bad chest, in the rain, and a document at the end of it.**
- **Ivo jokes when he is frightened and his jokes are how he buys room. He builds things with his hands. He is never a mystic.** He has a condition from 1 December that he will not like and a sister with a chest, and he has just been given Hattie Bramm's four minutes and given them away.
- **Dael writes the same four lines about a shutter that he would write about a door and hates himself a bit afterwards. He is not Elias's ally and is not going to become one, and the third way they have of being with each other is mutual declined, and it is the most functional relationship in the building.** He is removed from night command in this batch and it is correct and it is not a defeat.
- **Mara is procedural, not dismissive, and she is right.** She has refused him twice in public and neither refusal was kind and both were correct, and she has still never had a reading and has still never seen the notebook.
- **Hattie Bramm is a queue, a folder, a hard hand and thirty-one years. Her three refusals stand and none may be reversed.** She has now had four minutes and they went to a systems technician, and the volume's last movement hands her the room.
- **Doreen Sarn keeps her own counsel and her own copy. Her four refusals stand and a fifth was added on 14 November.** Arthur Neve is a document and a bad chest. H. Lound is furniture and the most useful furniture in the volume. **Nobody in this batch is a villain and everybody in this batch is competent.**
- **Per volume outline, the climax is the workers' panel and the count in the Hall, and Chapters 41–50 are Batch 0005. Nothing above is a suggestion.**
