## The handoff, as of the close of Chapter 50

Saturday the thirteenth of December 2024, about ten to twelve in the morning, at the bottom of Sillick Lane in Low Ward. Elias Venn, 32, has **no post, no access to the building he worked in, no employer, no card, no key, and a pound.** He has a pocket notebook of a hundred and fifteen pages, a hundred and fourteen of which are in a Museum file in a plastic sleeve that he does not have back. He has a cardboard folder on a shelf between a boiler and a chair with a company's letter in it. He has a registered form with eleven stamps on it, posted to Northglass on Friday the twelfth, unanswered. He has a registered letter in a coat pocket that is not his to open. He has told one person in the world about the twenty-eight days and it was Mara Iles, and he told her by telephone on Friday afternoon, after he had posted it.

**His restriction is total and it is not a suspension any more.** Line 3 of a four-line minute: barred from the building as from 1 December 2024 as a security matter, save attendance at any hearing or inquiry at which he is a party or a witness, on notice, in company, not before eight in the morning. **There is no Museum in which he can be a man in a room.** Every lawful door he used for six weeks is closed, and every one of them was a document, and that is the point of this batch.

**What he has not done and is going to have to be asked about by somebody:** he has no income, no hours, no reference that says anything, and a letting agency in Low Ward is going to ask him on Monday what he did instead. Mara Iles's answer, given to him in a corridor and not to be improved on: *I was a night officer in a public museum for three years and I was good at doors and clocks and locks and small repairs and I believed those were sufficient. And then stop.*

**The row.** A postman of about fifty, Fisher Lane and Sillick round, about two hundred and ten addresses, one man on it because the other is long-term sick, standing outside **number 88** because **there is no 86 on his job sheet and there never has been**, and because **the name field at 88 has been empty since the sixth of December** and was not empty before that, and because nine addresses came off a road in Broad Street in three days the week before and the depot says the sheet is right. *A name field doesn't go empty by accident. Somebody's typing.* And in the notebook, at the end of Volume 01, in the last entry: **It is not the mill and it is not 1974 and it is not a bell. It is a name field and it is empty and it is the same size as a person.**

**Nobody has been inside one of those flats. Nobody in Volume 01 said the words *Sillick Lane* and *household* in the same sentence. And the objects in that row keep working, because the objects in that row have always kept working, which is the entire reason this volume opens.**

## What this batch has to do, in this order, and the beats must not fuse

1. **Monday the sixteenth of December, the knock.** He goes back to number 88 and knocks on it and a person answers, and the answer is not supernatural and is not a haunting. **The address is empty of paperwork, not empty of people.** The person at 88 is a person who has lived there for years and who cannot produce, on the table in the hall, one document with their own name on it, and who is not distressed about it, and who says the thing that is the volume's first sentence: *I've never had a letter. I've had a bin bag and a bank.* **Nobody in this batch may explain it.** Elias Venn has no authority, no instrument and no form, and his only qualification is that he writes things down, and **the first thing he does in Volume 02 is the thing he spent six weeks not doing: he asks somebody a question and waits for the answer.**
2. **A threshold, and the specific shape of it.** The Volume 02 outline's investigation focus begins here and it is four items: **threshold god-patterns, a vanished housing block, a shrine hidden inside a school, and a collector who has bought three objects whose owners were removed from the census.** **In this batch, exactly one of the four is allowed to be touched** — the threshold, and it is a domestic object, and it is not on a plinth and it has never been on a plinth and there is no accession number for it and there never will be. **The object's own words are not its description; its function is what it does at a door.** The pattern is place-based and it is about being recognised on the way in.
3. **The three names.** The row does not lose one household's paperwork. **It loses about a third of a street's addressability, in a fortnight, in a pattern nobody ordered and everybody could have ordered**, and the pattern is a **rounds** system. **The instrument is a rounds sheet.** Not a ghost, not a demon, not a haunting, not a curse: **a delivery route, a field, a date, and a body pressing a key, in that order, on a Tuesday.** **Escalation discipline: each chapter raises the number of people who can be hurt and not the size of anything. In this batch the number of people who can be hurt is about two hundred and ten addresses on one round, and then a school, and then a museum.**
4. **The batch's institutional turn: a false name, and it is the Museum's, and it is on a card.** The Gallery of Unnamed Hands is on the **second floor** and the Hall of Former Faiths is on the **first**, and the **Quiet Wing is on the FOURTH, west range, and the east end of that corridor is the door that came off its latch and was opened on Saturday the thirteenth at ten to ten by the Director, and Elias Venn was not in the building.** **Item 1 of the inquiry is done. Chapters 51–60 must not re-open that door and must not describe the room inside it.** The turn for this batch is that **the Museum's own public record has begun to lose a row of addresses, for the same reason and at the same speed as the row, and the Museum's schedule of collections is INCOMPLETE and expected to remain so and has been since 9 December, and there is nobody in the building whose job it is to notice.** The Museum is the only institution in this city with a key board, a day book, a visitors' book, a systems index of about nine hundred lines and two hundred and forty-seven items with a location in it — **and it is losing the same rows at the same rate, and it has an index, and nobody has run the comparison.**
5. **The collection of the object is not permitted.** **Elias Venn is barred from the building. He cannot take custody of a household object. He cannot enter the Gallery. He cannot sign for anything. He has no employer and therefore no professional standing, no duty of care, no access to a loading bay and no way of getting a thing out of a house without somebody with a lawful door saying yes.** **The lawful door is the only door there is, and the volume's antagonist ladder says Volume 02's pressure is local, civic and administrative.** Therefore: **a household object comes into the Museum the way everything in this book has come into this book — by a form, from a person who is competent and is not him, and somebody else carries it.**

## The ten chapter cards

2. **52 — Two Hundred and Ten.** The round. 3. **53 — The Queue.** Friday the nineteenth, the Sillick Lane branch library back room, Hattie Bramm, and about nine people from a row, and **a sentence that has to be said by somebody who is not Elias Venn and that he cannot say because he is not a member of the public of anything and has no standing in a room.** Doreen Sarn is 78 and is not coming and is asked anyway. **The hundred and forty is in a drawer and is not produced, and the reason is on the page, and the reason is the same reason as in November.**

## The voice, from the fifty drafted chapters

Third person, past tense, close on Elias. Plain declaratives with a long sentence every so often and a very short one at the beat. No purple, no fantasy diction, no invented technical language. **Elias thinks in doors, clocks, exits, stains on floors and what can be written down, and he has stopped counting objects and started counting rooms and he has not admitted it to himself in fifty chapters.** When he is afraid he counts things and is embarrassed by the counting.

**He has no post and no authority in this volume and that is not a temporary condition and it must not be written as one.** He has a notebook, a cost, and no employer. He is not a night guard. He is a man of thirty-two in a rented room above a laundrette in Low Ward with a pound and a cardboard folder.

**Dael writes the same four lines about a thing he would write about a door and hates himself a bit afterwards. Mara is procedural, not dismissive, and she is right, and she has said *I do not know what you are for* and has not unsaid it and nobody is to take it back. Ivo jokes when he is frightened and builds things with his hands and is never a mystic, and he has a condition from 1 December that he does not like and a sister with a chest, and he has been given Hattie Bramm's four minutes and given them away.** Hattie Bramm is a queue, a folder, a hard hand and thirty-one years, and she has the room, and she has the hundred and forty, and **she has still never been asked one question by him, and the tenth time is a knock on a door in her street.** Doreen Sarn keeps her own counsel and her own copy and has refused five things and none has moved. **Nobody in this batch is a villain and everybody in this batch is competent, including the person at the council and the person at the letting agency and the person at the landlord's office and the woman on the rounds.**

**Escalation discipline, one sentence: each chapter must raise the number of people who can be hurt, not the size of anything. Nothing escalates into a bigger monster.**
