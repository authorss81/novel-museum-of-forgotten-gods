# Power System

## What Counts as Power

The supernatural is made of relationships that have been repeated until they become durable patterns. A god is not an unlimited spirit that grants wishes. A god is a pressure and a promise held by a community, a trade, a household, or a place. People can honor it, abandon it, misunderstand it, or ask it to change. Its power is strongest where people still act in relationship with it.

A **saint-bearing object** is an object that has held a god's last meaningful act with enough intensity to preserve an echo after the surrounding practice has disappeared. Not every old object is saint-bearing. A tool, coin, or piece of furniture may carry ordinary human memory without carrying a divine pattern. The museum's inventory marks the difference, often incorrectly.

## The Rules of an Echo

An **echo** is an imprint of attention, not a recording of objective truth. When Elias reads one, he receives:

- a place and time, usually centered on the strongest emotional act;
- one or more perceived points of view;
- a physical sensation and a relationship in motion;
- a gap where the object was not being witnessed;
- an unfinished need, promise, or threat.

The strongest act may be an act of care, betrayal, grief, labor, or routine. It is not always the most morally important event. The memory can omit people who were present but not involved. It can repeat a lie because the owner repeated it. It can show a person accurately while making the surrounding situation false.

No echo can resurrect the dead. It can preserve an impression, a skill trace, or a claim. It cannot prove that a person is alive, innocent, or still entitled to property.

## What Elias Can Do

Elias can hear an echo by making deliberate contact with a saint-bearing object or standing within a handspan of it while focusing on one question. The first reading is involuntary and incomplete. Later readings are more controlled, but control never guarantees accuracy.

The ability is an observation and negotiation power, not a generic spellcasting system. Elias can use his security training, ordinary tools, knowledge of the building, and relationships to create the conditions for a safe ritual. He cannot command a god, force a witness to speak, or solve a problem by touching the right relic.

## Observable States of an Object

The Museum recognizes four practical states. They describe what an object is doing, not whether its god is morally good or evil.

1. **Stored:** The object carries an echo but does not answer a question or alter the room. It can be handled by trained staff if the false label and physical anchor are intact.
2. **Answering:** The object repeats a small part of its last meaningful act. A bell may ring, a key may turn toward one door, or an accession card may write a name it was never given. The effect is local and can usually be stopped by removing the object from its anchor.
3. **Demanding:** The object tries to make a living person complete the unfinished act. It may create heat, weight, misdirection, a false memory, or pressure on a relationship. Demanding objects are not proof that the god is hostile; the demand may be historically necessary and still be unacceptable now.
4. **Hollowed:** The object has lost its last meaningful act and is filling the gap with other people's expectations. It may imitate a dead person, a god, or a loved one. A hollowed object cannot be corrected by another reading. It must be isolated, documented, and removed or destroyed under an informed decision.

A false name can keep an object Stored or Answering, but it cannot make a Demanding or Hollowed object safe. A living witness can sometimes change a Demanding object's response; no witness can repair a Hollowed object's missing relationship.

## Ritual Archaeology

Ritual archaeology is the practical method used by Elias, Mara, and their partners. It has six steps:

1. **Stabilize the scene.** Secure people, power, water, fire, exits, and unstable objects before asking the echo to repeat itself.
2. **Establish provenance.** Identify maker marks, use wear, repair history, ownership changes, and the gap between the official label and physical evidence.
3. **Locate the last meaningful act.** Elias reads the object, but Mara and local witnesses check his account against material and oral history.
4. **Find a living relationship.** The team asks who still has a stake in the object, not merely who owns it. A community, family, worker, neighbor, or descendant may be the necessary witness.
5. **Choose a response.** Stabilize, translate, return, offer, release, or refuse. The choice is made with the people affected and with the limits of the object in view.
6. **Record the consequence.** The team notes what changed, who accepted the risk, and what must be repaired next. A case is not finished when the object stops moving.

The steps can fail. A missing witness can make a ritual unsafe, a false label can redirect an echo, and a well-intentioned return can cause more harm than containment.

## The Six-Turn Structure of a Safe Reading

A reading becomes safer as the team learns to do six things, not because Elias gains a higher score:

1. Ask one bounded question.
2. Establish an exit and a time limit.
3. Use a material anchor that can be abandoned.
4. Name the cost before beginning.
5. Have a witness who can stop the reading.
6. Let the object answer in its own terms, even when the answer is incomplete.

Skipping the exit, cost, or witness is a major character error, not a shortcut.

## The Index

The **Index** is the museum's in-world record-and-warning system. It began as a set of Accord registers and was later woven into the ward-bells, accession cards, and the building's electrical circuits. Elias can perceive a short Index message when he is near a resonant object or a wardline.

The Index is useful but limited:

- It reports what the museum's charter considers important, not what is morally true.
- It can warn that an object is answering, that a name is being used twice, or that a room is carrying more than one memory.
- It cannot identify the correct owner, predict the future, or choose a moral answer.
- It can be damaged, delayed, or manipulated by the Registry.
- It never grants skill points, levels, or automatic success.

Typical Index messages should be short and concrete, such as:

> The object is answering a question. Touch, speak, or leave.

> Two names are using the same record.

> The room remembers an absent witness.

The Index becomes a shared civic ledger near the end only after people build the trust and procedures needed to keep it accountable.

## The Hush Engine's Three Locks

The Engine holds divine patterns apart through three linked functions. They are civic mechanisms rather than moral laws.

1. **The name lock:** false labels, sealed indexes, and public records keep a god-pattern from being addressed as one continuing relationship. A broken name lock produces repetition, misrecognition, or an object that answers to the wrong person.
2. **The witness lock:** the Engine prevents enough living people from recognizing and answering the same obligation at once. A broken witness lock produces a local return, a sudden shrine, or a crisis when a community remembers what it was taught to forget.
3. **The return lock:** twelve ward-bells and their anchors keep scattered fragments from reassembling into a god-pattern. A broken return lock does not choose what returns; it removes the Engine's ability to decide which fragment reaches which place.

The Engine works while the three locks agree. Breaking one creates a local emergency; breaking all three ends central control. No single lock can be repaired safely by restoring the old Engine, because each repair requires someone to decide which people must be prevented from remembering.

## Power Stages

The stages below are earned functional changes. They are not a visible stat screen and do not make Elias universally powerful.

### Stage 0: Nightwatch

Before the first awakening, Elias can observe, secure, repair, persuade, and use ordinary tools. He knows the museum's routines and can tell when a person is lying about a minor physical detail. He cannot safely read an object and may misattribute anxiety, grief, or imagination to the supernatural.

**Limitation:** He is one guard against a large institution. His first mistake is believing that documenting a problem is the same as solving it.

### Stage 1: Echo

Elias can hear the last meaningful act of a saint-bearing object and sense whether a room is resonant.

**Practice:** Slow readings, written question logs, salt-and-ash anchor paper, physical exit drills, and Mara's material checks. Salt-and-ash paper is an ordinary replaceable boundary: it shows when an echo is pulling and gives a reader somewhere to stop, but it is not a shield and cannot hold a demanding object by itself.

**Limit:** He cannot separate layers of time or know whether a voice is a god, a former owner, or his own memory. The echo can lie by omission and repeat its owner's perspective.

**Cost:** Tinnitus, nosebleeds, phantom touch, vivid dreams, and short-term confusion. A severe reading can make him miss real-world danger while his attention is inside the object.

### Stage 2: Thread

Elias can follow a memory from one object to another if the objects share a person, place, act, or material trail. He can distinguish a first memory from a later hand that handled the object, though not always the truth of either.

**Practice:** Building an evidence chain, interviewing witnesses, learning the museum's repair records, and accepting Mara's corrections.

**Limit:** A thread can be planted by a collector or shaped by a later owner. The farther he follows it, the more likely he will confuse a pattern with a person.

**Cost:** Personal memories become contaminated. He may remember a conversation in the wrong order, call a loved one by the wrong name, or lose the emotional meaning of a real event. No rest restores a memory that has been displaced; another person must help him reconstruct it.

### Stage 3: Namework

With enough material evidence and a willing human witness, Elias can speak a binding-name in a bounded ritual. A binding-name is not a command or a password. It states the relationship a god-pattern recognizes and the act being requested.

**Practice:** Learning local ritual boundaries, negotiating with shrine keepers, and accepting refusal as a valid outcome.

**Limit:** A name can redirect a local effect or open a conversation, but it cannot force a god to return. A false name may redirect the effect toward the speaker. A god can demand a larger obligation than the witness understood.

**Cost:** Elias's own name becomes less stable in other people's memories. He may be briefly unable to sign a form, be recognized by a door, or answer to a title. Using a name for violence creates a debt that returns through a later object.

### Stage 4: Room

Elias can establish a temporary witness-space: a bounded area where several echoes can be heard without merging into a single false memory. He can hold an object, a person, and a god-pattern in relation long enough for a decision to be made.

**Practice:** Coordinating Ivo's physical systems, Mara's records, local witnesses, and evacuation plans. The room fails if any participant lies about the cost or refuses the exit.

**Limit:** The room does not create safety by itself. It can hold danger temporarily, not solve its cause. Elias can stabilize one crisis only by leaving another object, person, or wardline exposed.

**Cost:** Physical exhaustion, damaged anchors, and a piece of memory left in the room. The room can also preserve a decision that later participants regret; a successful ritual is not automatically a fair one.

### Stage 5: Witness

Elias can carry a god-pattern's request from one location to another and ask a living community whether it accepts the relationship. He can translate a limited request but not a complete theology. The community must answer with an act, a refusal, or a negotiated condition.

**Practice:** Regional travel, public hearings, failure at returning a hostile pattern, and relationships with people who disagree with him.

**Limit:** He is a mediator, not an oracle. Communities may reject the request, and gods may reject the community. He cannot make one answer valid for everyone.

**Cost:** He carries obligations that persist after the object is gone. If he refuses a legitimate request, the failure follows him. At this stage, the cost of a public decision can be social exile or physical injury, not just a private symptom.

### Stage 6: Commons

Elias can transfer the practical parts of witness-work to trained, consenting custodians. He can help a network of people hold several local anchors, recognize when a memory has been tampered with, and open or close a ritual space without being the sole center.

**Practice:** Delegation, teaching, public documentation, and the creation of checks that work after he is absent.

**Limit:** The network is slower, less precise, and vulnerable to disagreement. Elias cannot secretly correct every node or command a custodian. The power is genuinely shared and therefore genuinely less controllable.

**Cost:** He gives up exclusive access to most echoes, accepts that others can refuse him, and loses the private certainty that his reading is correct. His final body retains scars and missing memories even after the network is stable. Trained custodians can receive clear invitations or questions inside their local agreements, but they cannot read every layer of an object's history and must follow the same exit and consent rules. An object may also offer one clear question to an untrained person when it has deliberately chosen a public witness; that invitation is not a full memory reading.

### Final form: The Open Hand

The Open Hand is not a seventh personal skill. It is the final practice produced by Stage 6: a citywide, voluntary exchange in which people acknowledge only the divine relationships they can maintain. Elias can hold the opening, but he cannot decide its membership, retain its memories, or make it permanent by himself.

The cost is the loss of the ability to hear the majority of objects. What remains is a clear, occasional invitation from an object that has chosen to be heard. The final power is the capacity to make room for other people's relationship to a god without converting that relationship into his authority.

## Failure Modes and Counterplay

- **False perspective:** A witness can make a loving act look cruel or a cruel act look accidental. Material evidence and independent testimony are required.
- **Echo bleed:** Objects near one another can cross-contaminate. Rooms need separate anchors, witnesses, and exit routes.
- **Hollowing:** A repeatedly emptied object can begin manufacturing a memory from whatever people expect. It may use a familiar voice. Silence and removal are often safer than another reading.
- **Name debt:** A god-pattern that accepts a binding-name may interpret every later act as part of the same promise. Terms must be explicit and witnessed.
- **Institutional interference:** Labels, sealed doors, missing payroll entries, and altered records can change how an object is interpreted. The solution may require a lawyer, a locksmith, a family member, or a public argument rather than a ritual.
- **Combat:** Elias has no energy blasts or generic shields. He can use doors, bells, light, water, clamps, firebreaks, and human coordination. An active object is dangerous because it follows a remembered rule, not because it obeys level-based rules.
- **Death:** A dead person cannot be restored. A witness's death may release a memory or close a route, and the team must decide whether to honor, release, or stabilize what remains.

## Power Growth Loop

For every major advance, the story must show all four of these elements:

1. **Practice:** Elias or a partner repeats, studies, or repairs something.
2. **Cost:** The use changes his body, memory, relationships, or obligations.
3. **Failure:** A reading, plan, or compromise goes wrong in a specific way.
4. **Changed decision:** Elias chooses a different boundary, ally, or sacrifice afterward.

He may become more capable, but the story must not treat him as safe to share the same procedure with everyone. The final network works because the custodians have different skills and consent, not because the ability has become ordinary.
