# World Bible

## Morrowgate

Morrowgate is a large river city built around a tidal harbor, a shifting floodplain, and the old road network that once connected inland shrines to the sea. It has ordinary electric light, telephones, trains, ferries, cameras, paperwork, and weather. The supernatural has not replaced those systems. It hides in the places where people make promises, record ownership, repeat a story, or stop doing either.

The city is large enough to contain several social worlds. The same object can be a sacred inheritance to one neighborhood, a sale lot to another, and a safety hazard to a third. Most citizens know that the old faiths existed, but they treat them as history, folklore, or museum property. A smaller number of people still perform living rituals. Very few know that the museum's labels are part of a working containment network.

## History

### Before the Hush

The city's gods were not statues or all-powerful creators. A god was a durable pattern of reciprocal acts: hospitality, mourning, road-building, wage-keeping, threshold-keeping, or the care of shared water. People made the pattern real by answering the same need over time. A community could forget a god, lose touch with one, or change its practice, but a strong final act could remain in an object.

### The War of Last Names, 75 years before the present

Rival neighborhoods and guilds began invoking gods as military and political identities. The same divine pattern was compelled to bless opposite sides. The resulting six-year war damaged the city's ritual networks as well as its people. The old gods did not disappear all at once; they became unreliable because communities kept asking them to contradict themselves.

### The Great Hush, 68 years before the present

The Quiet Accord gathered civic engineers, archivists, clergy, ferry captains, and neighborhood witnesses. They built the Hush Engine to interrupt divine attention before another war. The engine required a central witness and twelve ward-bells. Three hundred and twelve volunteers each offered one chosen memory to the first working chamber; their names and repeated acts were recorded as identifiers and anchors alongside those memories. They did not surrender their souls. They accepted that their shared attention would be used as a public boundary.

The Hush split the old gods into bounded patterns and placed those patterns in objects, places, and records. It did not kill them. It made direct worship dangerous and left the museum responsible for the fragments it could not safely return.

### The Museum Opens, 66 years before the present

The Morrowgate Museum of Quiet Saints was built above the Undercroft as a public promise: forgotten things would be kept, studied, and shown without being exploited. In practice, the museum became the engine's most visible face. Public galleries teach people to look at divine remnants as art and history. Restricted staff learn which labels and rooms keep them dormant.

### The Casket Incident, 18 years before the present

A night breach at the museum damaged the third ward-bell and erased a night shift from municipal records. Tomas Venn, a counter-witness engineer, disappeared while trying to hold the central chamber open. Elias, then fourteen, spoke the name of a woman who had been removed from the emergency list. A blank name tag from the Casket fused to his memory. Tomas's body was never recovered. The event was closed as a structural accident.

### The Present

A maintenance failure at South Sluice has cracked the third ward-bell again. A small bronze bell from a shuttered textile mill begins answering the night. The museum's public story is still intact, but its internal records are beginning to disagree with one another.

## The Museum

The museum is a five-floor civic building with a public entrance on Civic Hill and a separate service entrance on the old service lane.

- **Hall of Former Faiths:** the public collection. Objects are displayed with dates, cultural context, and sanitized labels. The hall is popular with school groups and has a strict rule against touching.
- **Gallery of Unnamed Hands:** objects whose makers or worshippers are unknown. The gallery is popular with conspiracy-minded visitors and is officially described as an unfinished research section.
- **Conservation floor:** paper, metals, textiles, and glass treatment rooms. Mara Iles works here. The rooms have strong lighting, expensive equipment, and poor ventilation.
- **Quiet Wing:** restricted collection rooms for objects that produce a resonance. The wing has no exterior windows and a separate key-card history that has been repeatedly altered.
- **Undercroft stair:** a freight elevator and maintenance stairs leading to the old tram tunnels, cisterns, and the First Witness chamber. The public maps show a boiler room here.
- **The label room:** a windowless room where conservation staff prepare accession cards and false names. The room is mundane until a card is used on the wrong object.

The museum's official mission is preservation. Its hidden function is containment. Staff who work long enough learn that preservation, documentation, and control are not the same thing.

## Institutions and Factions

### The Morrowgate Museum Board

A public trust nominally responsible for collections, funding, and public access. It includes city officials, donors, scholars, and one surviving shrine representative. The Board wants the museum to remain open and financially stable. It is vulnerable to the Quiet Accord because the museum's charter contains clauses no ordinary trustee can fully read.

### The Quiet Accord and the Registry

The surviving administrative branch of the Hush. The Registry maintains ward-bells, restricted records, and official notices of unstable objects. It is not a secret army. It is a bureaucracy that has survived by making dangerous decisions look like maintenance. Reforming members want public oversight; hardliners believe any exposure of the ward network will restart the divine war.

### The Shrine Network

A loose association of caretakers, household ritualists, ferry families, bellmakers, and neighborhood organizers. They do not share one theology. Their common practice is to ask before acting and to return what cannot be safely carried. The Network is the principal human counterweight to the Registry.

### The Returnist Movement

A broad, unstable movement of people who believe the Hush is a crime and every forgotten god should be named again. It includes grieving families, former Accord reformers, people who want public power, and communities who have been erased. Returnists disagree about whether a god can be refused, whether a dangerous object should be destroyed, and whether the Museum deserves to survive. They are a pressure and a constituency, not a single faction with one leader.

### The Lantern Society

A club of collectors, dealers, lawyers, and wealthy patrons who believe forgotten things can be owned if the owner can pay for containment. The Society treats memory as a resource and creates black markets for resonant fragments. It is not a final villain; it is the ladder that turns institutional secrecy into profitable violence.

### Civic Works

Transit crews, bridge inspectors, pump operators, archivists, electricians, and hospital maintenance staff. They keep the physical city functioning and unknowingly maintain parts of the ward network. Their practical competence is a recurring source of solutions and a reminder that magic is embedded in labor, not separated from it.

### The Unnamed

A description, not a faction. It refers to gods with no surviving congregation, objects whose final act has lost its context, and people who have been removed from civic records. The term is politically dangerous because it can describe both a lost god and an erased citizen.

## Everyday Rules of the World

1. Most people will explain a supernatural event as damp, fraud, grief, machinery, or coincidence until the evidence becomes impossible to dismiss.
2. The museum cannot display an object that remains active after the public enters. Curators move, seal, mislabel, or remove it before the official explanation is written.
3. A god's influence is local and relational. It can pressure a household, a trade, a neighborhood, or a ritual, but it does not grant unlimited wish fulfillment.
4. A city record is part of the supernatural infrastructure. Erasing a name can change who can recognize, inherit, locate, or mourn a person.
5. The river, trains, factories, hospitals, and schools are real places with real schedules. Events must interrupt work and care, not float above them.
6. A shrine is not automatically good. A living cult can be generous, coercive, mistaken, or dangerous. The story investigates the practice before judging the god.

## Regional Scope

The first volume stays inside Morrowgate. Later volumes expand to the river parishes, the northern collections, the industrial belt, and cities that adopted different versions of the Hush. Regional groups do not share a single ruler. They share the consequences of a ritual technology that was exported before its cost was understood.

## Reveal Discipline

- Volume 1 establishes object echoes, Elias's ability, the Hush, and the Casket.
- Volumes 2–4 show that the museum's labels and the city's records are active parts of containment.
- Volume 5 reveals the Hush Engine's physical network and the fact that the Hush was deliberate.
- Volume 7 reveals Tomas's counter-witness work but not the First Witness's full nature.
- Volumes 9–11 reveal that the system is failing because current forgetting feeds it.
- Volume 12 answers the First Witness, Tomas, and Elias's ability.
- Volumes 13–14 resolve the external crisis through community consent rather than a new hidden power.

## Canon Locks

- The museum is not a portal to arbitrary other worlds. Its objects draw on the city's shared history and ritual relationships.
- Divine power is a consequence of relationships and repeated acts, not raw belief or worship alone.
- No artifact can restore a dead person. It can restore a memory, a pattern, or a claim, never the original life.
- Elias's ability is not inherited magical talent. It came from a deliberate Casket experiment and a childhood act of witness.
- The Hush Engine is an old human mechanism that has become dangerously self-preserving. It is the final systemic antagonist, not a newly introduced demon.
