# Batch Summaries

## Volume 01 — Batch 0001 (Chapters 1–10), "The First Bell"

**Status:** complete. 10 chapters drafted, 37,577 words. Movement 1 of 5 of the Volume 01 outline ("The First Bell"). The formal Nightwatch → Echo step is **not** claimed; Chapter 10 is provisional and is confirmed in Chapters 11–12.

### What happened

Elias Venn is a competent, isolated night guard at the Morrowgate Museum of Quiet Saints. A small bronze tally bell on plinth 34 in the Hall of Former Faiths, catalogued as an unremarkable industrial exhibit from a shuttered Low Ward textile works, has been sounding one note since the third ward-bell at South Sluice re-cracked on the Monday before the manuscript begins. Over one week and six shifts, Elias moves from logging a noise, through a bounded reading he is not yet able to repeat, to discovering that the Museum's records have deleted a night porter named Wendel Case while the city's own payroll bureau has been deducting his pension and his union subscriptions for eighteen months because nobody told the bureau.

### Batch goal, and whether it landed

The goal was: establish Elias as a competent, isolated guard inside a working institution; deliver the genre promise inside the first two chapters; force one voluntary, costly, incomplete reading; and leave him holding a number he cannot prove, a supervisor closing the file, and an archive box that has been moved by someone who knew what he would go looking for. All four landed. The first sound is at 02:52 on Tuesday (Ch 1); the first recurrence is at 20:55 on Wednesday (Ch 2); the first involuntary contact is at 23:40 (Ch 3); the label room and the erased name are Ch 4; Case's payroll reconciliation is Ch 5; the relabelled box is the last beat of Ch 10.

### Midpoint (Ch 4–5)

Landed, and it is not a supernatural reveal: Ch 4 finds a person's name erased under a false accession number, Ch 5 finds that the person has been deleted from every Museum file and kept alive in a municipal one. The documents, not the object, are the dangerous part. The horror is administrative, exactly as planned.

### Climax (Ch 10)

Landed with all its intended damage. Elias runs the five-line protocol on the busiest night of the week, with Mara as the named witness, Ivo at the door with the stop word, eleven minutes written down in advance, and a cost named aloud. The reading returns **ninety-four**. It costs him a nosebleed, a headache, twenty minutes of attention he did not choose to spend, and a rope texture across his right palm. A night cleaner, Perry Loach, answers the number without being asked and stops as though interrupted, so the object can reach people who have not consented — which is worse than the number. **Elias says "a number, I don't know what it counts"; Mara writes *a number* in the result field of the shared log; he had four seconds in which to correct her and watched them go past.** That is the batch's real cost, larger than the blood: the first condition of the Chapter 9 agreement broken on the page, in front of both witnesses, with nothing said about it by either of them.

### The four counts

- **94** — people on the final Sillick closing shift. On the page, Chapter 10 only.
- **61** — people short-paid on the final pay run. A pay figure, not a headcount. On the page, Chapter 6 only, in Hattie Bramm's mouth.
- **92** — the paid payroll figure. **Held.** It belongs to the Batch 0003 midpoint reversal, where 94 is stated as having been paid as 92.
- **2** — worked and never entered in any book. **Held.** Same reason, same chapter.

### Costs and limits shown

No supernatural spectacle: sound, a locked door, a smell of rain that is not in the building, a man saying a number. The two Index lines are brief, incomplete, and used once each (Ch 2, interior, misattributed to his own hearing; Ch 8, spoken aloud to a shut and latched door, and never written down). The corridor service bell is plumbing — pressed brass, hard-piped to the ward-bell repeater line, out of tune, and incapable of doing anything except repeat. Elias does not open the unlisted room and does not open the box. He is refused by the two-person lift rule and does not breach it. He does not win by noticing: everything he gets right costs him a rule, a lie, a job, or a colleague.

### The flaw, on the page

Elias's central flaw is control through secrecy, and it is visible three times in escalating cost: a private second notebook (Ch 2), a false-signed device-book entry and a deliberately displaced card (Ch 4), a false maintenance cause signed in his own hand (Ch 9), and a withheld number that puts a false entry in a shared file (Ch 10). Ivo names it out loud in Ch 9 and takes the duplicate notebook out of Elias's hand and hands it back. Mara asks him, in Ch 10, whether there is anything in the notebook that is not on the page, and he says no.
