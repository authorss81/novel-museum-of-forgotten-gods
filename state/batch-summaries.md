# Batch Summaries

## Volume 01 — Batch 0001 (Chapters 1–10), "The First Bell"

**Status:** complete, and **repaired after four review passes**. 10 chapters, **42,188 words** in `chapters/volume-01/` (37,577 before the first repair pass). Movement 1 of 5 of the Volume 01 outline ("The First Bell"). The formal Nightwatch → Echo step is **not** claimed; Chapter 10 is provisional and is confirmed in Chapters 11–12. **Recorded deviation:** the batch outline estimates ~27,000 words at 2,400–3,000 a chapter. The batch runs long — Chapters 5, 9 and 10 are 5,289, 5,089 and 7,177 words. The excess is scene, not padding: the long chapters are the ones the outline itself flags as carrying the batch's spine (5), its cost floor (9) and its climax (10). Nothing was split or padded to reach a number and nothing should be cut in a later pass for length alone.

### What happened

Elias Venn is a competent, isolated night guard at the Morrowgate Museum of Quiet Saints. A small bronze tally bell on plinth 34 in the Hall of Former Faiths, catalogued as an unremarkable industrial exhibit from a shuttered Low Ward textile works, has been sounding one note since the third ward-bell at South Sluice re-cracked on the Monday before the manuscript begins. Over one week and six shifts, Elias moves from logging a noise, through a bounded reading he is not yet able to repeat, to discovering that the Museum's records have deleted a night porter named Wendel Case while the city's own payroll bureau has been deducting his pension and his union subscriptions for eighteen months because nobody told the bureau.
### Batch goal, and whether it landed

The goal was: establish Elias as a competent, isolated guard inside a working institution; deliver the genre promise inside the first two chapters; force one voluntary, costly, incomplete reading; and leave him holding a number he cannot prove, a supervisor closing the file, and an archive box that has been moved by someone who knew what he would go looking for. All four landed. The first sound is at 02:52 on Tuesday (Ch 1); the first recurrence is at 20:55 on Wednesday (Ch 2); the first involuntary contact is at 23:40 (Ch 3); the label room and the erased name are Ch 4; Case's payroll reconciliation is Ch 5; the relabelled box is the last beat of Ch 10.

### Midpoint (Ch 4–5)

Landed, and it is not a supernatural reveal: Ch 4 finds a person's name erased under a false accession number, Ch 5 finds that the person has been deleted from every Museum file and kept alive in a municipal one. The documents, not the object, are the dangerous part. The horror is administrative, exactly as planned.

### Climax (Ch 10)

Landed with all its intended damage, **after a re-cut of lines 191–231.** Elias runs the five-line protocol on the busiest night of the week, with Mara as the named witness, Ivo at the door, eleven minutes written down in advance, and a cost named aloud. The reading returns **ninety-four**. It costs him a nosebleed, a headache, and a rope texture across his right palm. A night cleaner, Perry Loach, answers the number without being asked and stops as though interrupted, so the object can reach people who have not consented — which is worse than the number.

**The withholding now happens where the outline wanted it: inside the protocol, not against it.** Mara rules that her log takes no bare figure — no object, no *a number*, because a figure with a guess under it is a rumour she will not put her name to. She therefore writes **Result: a number** *correctly*, on the evidence she was given. Elias has four seconds in which to supply an object; he has a mill, a catalogue number and nine seconds on a terminal, and he does not know that it is this mill. He lets the procedure do it, and when she puts her hand out for the notebook he puts his in to the inside pocket, stops, and hands her an **empty hand**. This is the batch's real cost, larger than the blood.

**The two promises are not in the same condition, and a later batch must not treat them as one.** The **first** — the result in the shared log in her hand exactly as it comes — is still intact to the letter: he answers every question she asks, accurately and in order, and the sheet says only what he said. The **second** — *he tells her everything, every time, and not the parts he has decided were relevant* — **is broken in Chapter 10, and that is new.** It was broken by the repair pass that added the occupancy beat (item 13 below), and it is what gives Batch 0002 its second withholding somewhere to stand. It breaks in two separate ways. **By silence:** he decided a man with a mop was not part of what he was telling her about, and did not volunteer the name he could have had in ninety seconds. **And by a lie:** when she asks him point-blank whether there is anything in the notebook that is not on the page, he says no, and there is. **The precise position, which must not be over-corrected into something blunter: he tells her no untruth whatever about the reading itself.** The result field is correct, the units are correct, the cost is correct, and the architecture of the climax depends on that being true. The lie is about the notebook, after the reading is finished, and it is the only lie he tells a person in this batch. Nobody can catch him at it, and the cleaner's name goes into his coat pocket to keep company with a headcount.

**The cleaner's account is now on the page as a blank box, and this is a structural addition, not a detail.** The shared log carries an occupancy line that says *one cleaner*, and a second box headed *account of any person present during result* that wants a name, a grade and a countersignature, and that box is left empty. Mara fills it in correctly by not filling it in: it takes a voluntary statement in the person's own words, she will not take one off a night cleaner at a plinth at ten o'clock on a Saturday without his supervisor, and she will not put Elias's second-hand version in a box that wants the man's words, *because the day she starts writing down what a person said to a guard, in a guard's handwriting, she has stopped keeping a record and started keeping a rumour.* So the box is blank, the form is right, and the fact that a man in overalls said a number on his way past a plinth and then said nothing else for the rest of his shift has nowhere on the sheet to go. **Batch 0002 inherits the most important gap in the volume: the only person the object has been shown to reach is the one person in the building who is not in any record. Filling that box now requires somebody to go and ask Perry Loach, and nobody in this building has any apparatus for doing that.**

The first draft had Elias say the number, say it was a headcount, and hear Mara read back *Result: ninety-four* — and then withhold anyway, in a paragraph that flatly denied he had said it. The block was re-cut rather than line-edited. **No plot beat moved**, and the state file that had recorded the withholding as a *breach* has been corrected.

### Costs and limits shown

No supernatural spectacle: sound, a locked door, a smell of rain that is not in the building, a man saying a number. The two Index lines are brief, incomplete, **interior, with no in-world source**, and used once each (Ch 2, misattributed to the beginning of a tinnitus; Ch 8, with that excuse now unavailable, and never written down). The corridor service bell is plumbing — pressed brass, hard-piped to the ward-bell repeater line, out of tune, and incapable of doing anything except repeat. Elias does not open the unlisted room and does not open the box. He is refused by the two-person lift rule and does not breach it. He does not win by noticing: everything he gets right costs him a rule, a lie, a job, or a colleague.

### The flaw, on the page

Elias's central flaw is control through secrecy, and it is visible in escalating cost: a private second notebook (Ch 2); a signed device-book entry, a deliberately displaced card, and a card read on a restricted door that logged him by name (Ch 4); a false maintenance cause signed in his own hand (Ch 9); and a number kept in a coat pocket while a correct-looking entry goes into a shared file, together with a cleaner's name he could have supplied and did not (Ch 10). Ivo names it out loud in Ch 9, takes the duplicate notebook out of Elias's hand and hands it back, and makes him say the corridor arrangement out loud so it can be repeated to somebody later. Mara asks him, in Ch 10, whether there is anything in the notebook that is not on the page, and he hands her an empty hand and says that is the whole of it.
