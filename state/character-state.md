# Character State

Updated after Batch 0002 (Volume 01, Chapters 11–20). Read the thread and condition entries below against `state/continuity.md`, which is the authority. Ages are canon; states are as of the close of Chapter 20 / the top of Tuesday 21 October 2024.

## Elias Venn, 32

**Where he is now.** Suspended from night duty since 07:00 on Sunday 11 October. Permitted: building keys, current card, the service lane, the loading-bay desk, the security room, the ground floor, staff facilities, and attendance at a re-inspection *as escort, ground floor only, not in the room*. Barred: the Hall of Former Faiths, the Gallery of Unnamed Hands, the conservation floor, the Quiet Wing. In the payroll system the restriction is **scheduled leave in lieu of notice** — unpaid and un-dismissible — and Standing Instruction 14 clause 7 forbids any reference to fault in any record about him, and refers any further incident of the same cause to a hearing. He is therefore in a state no institution in the book knows how to describe.

**What he wants, on the page.** Not the post. The review on the fourth Monday is a thing he has stopped planning for. What he wants on the page now is **to stop being the only place a fact lives**, and he got that in Chapter 18 and it cost him the only thing he had, and he is not sure yet that it was a fair trade.

**What he has.** A pocket notebook. An unspent name. A police form with a crime number on it. A headcount in the notebook against *a number* in a shared log. A sealed envelope in a Board Secretary's file that is now, by clause six, inside the building. A longhand copy of a 1974 carbon receipt, which is the only evidence in the world that a bundle of wage sheets came into this building in a van with a lamp. Nine objects and forty-one drawers. A blue disc with a consignor's initials on it. Two hundred and ten dead letters behind him and a booking book in front of him that he had never read in two years.

**Skill state. Stage 1 — Echo, confirmed, and it is a narrow confirmation.** He can make deliberate contact within a handspan of a saint-bearing object while focusing on one bounded question, and he gets the object's **last meaningful act**, and he cannot separate the act from the point of view it was seen from. On Monday 12 October he asked a lamp who carried it out of the last building it was in and got a kitchen. He can feel a resonant room and he cannot read a god. He is **not** able to do it on demand, he is not able to ask a question of a room, and he has no procedure at all for the thing the object does that matters most, which is reach an unconsenting person.

**The flaw, on the page, and it has stopped being a habit and started being a method.** Chapter 11: he says the sentence about the letter out loud once so that it is a thing that has been said, and does not take the envelope. Chapter 13: he hands over four photographs and four hundred words of self-indictment, and the institution puts them back in a file the next Monday. Chapter 15: he stands at a basement door with his hand on it and does not open it, and goes upstairs and tells Mara everything instead — *the same thing, the second time, and this time because it is the only other option he has left.* Chapter 16: **he says the true count's location, date, row, plinth and case in a street in front of a glass door with eleven people behind it, and he had four seconds in which to say *I can't tell you that*, and it was true, and he did not take it.** Chapter 17: he trespasses on a cleared site alone because he is frightened of what he will find out, and a man of seventy-four goes into a canal. Chapter 18: he says the word *lying* out loud, in a room with a card cutter in it, to a woman who has earned the right to say the word first. Chapter 20: he stands on a landing and does not say one word to a woman walking past, because Mara told him not to, and it cost him more than the canal.

**Costs unpaid and still owed.** A promise to tell Mara everything, broken twice in Chapter 10 and now broken a second time in Chapter 16 and *named* in Chapter 18. The knowledge that the only person who can build a form for a night cleaner has told him he will not write a word of it. A police number with his name on it. The bin of blue bales that went out of the door on Friday morning with eleven years of a dead man's post in it, and three people who knew, and a book whose head nobody reads. And the number: he knows what ninety-four counts, and he knows the pay run listed fewer than were on the gate, and he has not told Mara, and he is aware on Monday the nineteenth of November that he will have to decide.

**What he is frightened of, specifically.** Not the building and not the number any more. **A hundred people in this city who need him to do it, and nobody to say the word.** A seventy-eight-year-old pay clerk in a chair by a lit gas fire said it to him before he said it to himself, and she is the only person in the volume who has said anything he did not already know.

## Mara Iles, 28

**Where she is now.** In the conservation office on the first floor of a building she has been in for five years, with a shared protocol log whose result field says *a number*, a box under it that is blank, a covering note of Sunday 18 October to the Board Secretary and the audit provider, a written request to the Board for authority to open a leaseholder's locker, a four-hundred-word statement she did not write and has read, and a form she is building for a man in overalls that he will not be allowed near.

**Her position on the two Chapter 9 conditions.** The first is intact to the letter and defeated him anyway. The second he broke by silence and then by lying to her face. **In Chapter 18 she says what she is doing about it and it is not forgiveness:** everything goes in the file the same day with a number on it, and if the file cannot take it she will build a form that can, and **nobody in this building decides on their own that a person is safer not knowing** — and then, at the stair door, the thing that is the relationship: *we are not us. There is a protocol and there are two conditions and one of them is a relationship, and the relationship is that there is no more private channel, and I am telling you that so that you understand that you are not getting it back.*

**What she has done that nobody else has done.** Clause seven of Standing Instruction 14, in the margin, initialled and dated Sunday afternoon: *the Museum will not destroy any record of an individual employee held by the municipal payroll bureau without first obtaining the bureau's own account in writing.* A permanent institutional instrument, drafted in about forty minutes on a Sunday, by a person who does not sleep on Sundays. And the Annexe C re-certification of the Hall in one sentence, which she calls *a lie of omission of a size I have signed about four hundred times.*

**What she has refused, out loud and for stated reasons.** To ring Hattie Bramm. To let him ask Hattie Bramm. To write a name in the account box on the shared protocol form. To let him write a word of the form she is building for Perry Loach. And to accept, in the note of 18 October, that a name in a document is a document — she has put **Wendel Case** in a covering note to a member of the Board and nowhere else and she says so out loud, and she knows exactly what she has done.

**Her fault, if there is one, in this batch.** She read the day book every day from 7 October and told him so on Tuesday, which is two days of silence she chose, and she is clear-eyed about it and has not forgiven herself for it and has not forgiven him either.

**Wants from him.** The result in the shared log in her hand exactly as it comes. And everything, every time. He has now broken the second one twice — once by omission, once by a lie, once knowingly and publicly — and she has said in writing what she will do about it, and she is going to do it.

**Owes nothing and has not been thanked.** She has still never had the reading. He has still not asked her for it.

## Ivo Pell, 25

**Where he is now.** On systems days 07:00–15:00, Tuesday to Saturday, and on junior-guard nights, and he has spent Batch 0002 not being asked for anything by the man he works with, and not being protected from anything either.

**What he has done, and it is the batch's most consequential act of institutional repair.** He ran an eight-week systems inventory nobody asked him to run, and in it he restored the **schedule of collections** from the paper series against the Registry's register, and in the third section of a document about bin numbers he wrote a schedule note with a number in it: *loading bay, cage 2, current position blocks the swing of a door on a designated route of escape.* Nobody had to be brave about a fire thing. They moved a cage. And he has the Registry's weekly job list, a rota of four assessors, and a material-change slot on 19 November that was empty on Monday and is not empty now, and he booked it without being asked and told Elias about it afterwards, in a workshop doorway, and said: *if you had asked me, I would have done it in a fortnight and it would have been worse.*

**His exposure, which has grown without his noticing.** He is on the fault report of 7 October, a co-defendant by silence, and he is now also the man who is dealing with the Registrar's twenty-two letters and the Registry's rota and the Museum's schedule of collections, and he has a copy of a bulletin nobody else has. He has also, without saying so, become the person who can get Elias into rooms he is barred from. He has not noticed. He has a sister with a chest and no lungs to speak of and a Saturday systems day and a night before it.

**Wants.** To be a certified Civic Works engineer, to be useful, and to go home. He has begun, deliberately, to be the reason a man can take risks at all, and he has not yet noticed that this is the dependency the series needs him to outgrow.

## Dael Ordway, 44

**Where he is now.** Off nights since 11 October, not the supervisor this week, and came onto the floor on Tuesday 20 October for the first time in nine days to do the thing the whole volume turns on. He put the tour on the calendar. Not the press, not the Board, not the Director: **Dael read the green booking book on the security desk that he had never read in eleven years, found a school group of thirty-eight booked in September, and worked out that a public event defers the disposition under the Director's own condition, and moved the case forward three weeks by waking thirty-eight children up.**

**His speech in Chapter 20 is the chapter's best argument and it is his own reasoning.** *Annexe D is the best document this building has produced in my lifetime and it will hold that bell in that case for ever, and it will do nothing whatever for sixty-one people, because a classification is not a wage.* *I am going to be the man who wakes thirty-eight children up on a Thursday morning in November, and I have never done anything else in eleven years that frightened me as much, and I am doing it, and I am not doing it for you and I am not doing it for the mill and I am not doing it for that woman on the stair landing. I am doing it because it is the only card on the table.*

**He is not clean and he is not a villain.** He cannot sign the docket any more, because the standing instruction says a director's signature is not sufficient, and he knew that before anybody told him. He has been signing a false cause for a fortnight and has told nobody but the man he did it to. He refused Elias a telephone number because a member of the public's address in a Museum file is a thing this institution has just been told it is bad at. And he has read the Act on limitation, in the evenings, and says so, and calls it ridiculous.

**Knows.** Everything in the systems. The 1974 receipt's existence, in outline. The records clerk's post.

**Does not know.** The twenty minutes, the corridor, the device book before Sunday, the reading, the lamp, Doreen Sarn, the second envelope. He is going to find all of it out in Movement 4 and he is going to write the same four lines about it.

## Doreen Sarn, 78

**On the page as of Wednesday 14 October and fully realised by Sunday 18.** 12 Ferry Bank, second floor, first door, a cardigan over a pinafore, a net curtain, a plastic tumbler on the sill, a lamp that is the mate of the Museum's in a press for thirty-one years, a blue plastic folder, nine years of letters with a crest on them, and a hardback cash book with a rubber band round it which she has never surrendered to Elias, to Mara, to Quill & Frayle, to the Registry, or to anybody, and is not going to.

**What she is.** Sillick Mill's pay clerk. She bought a lamp off the forecourt on the last morning for two shillings. She put the pay run's figure onto the wage sheet on the last morning because a mill has to close on a Friday, and she has known she did it for thirty-one years, and she told a stranger in her front room before he could get it out of her. She is the volume's moral centre and she is not a device and she is not an oracle and she has not forgiven anybody.

**What she said that the whole volume is built on.** *A book gets lent. A book gets copied. A book gets read in a room with the people it is about in it, and then it belongs to more than one person and nobody can be got at over it. And the thing you've got is a coat pocket, and a coat pocket is a book with one reader, and one reader is a liability.* And: *you'd better not be the only one who can do it. Because if you're the only one, then by Christmas there's a hundred people in this city who need you to do it, and you will do it, because you can't not, and you will be twenty-six years old in a bad night with a headache and a hundred people waiting, and there'll be nobody to say the word.*

**What she has given.** The second envelope, dated 22 October 1974, and the reason the mill's post came back to the mill, and therefore that item 5 on the 1974 receipt is very probably her own envelope. And, on Sunday, the whole of the reading, told to him and not by him, including the phrase.

**What she will not do.** She will not surrender the cash book. She will not read it to a lawyer, a trustee, a Board or a museum, and her reason is the volume's reason: *a copy in a solicitor's drawer is a copy in a drawer. I want it read in a room with the people who are affected in it.*

## Hattie Bramm, 54

**On the page as of Friday 16 October and fully realised by Tuesday 20.** Gate clerk on the closing shift; the woman who wrote the figures down when a foreman called them; one of the sixty-one; one of the ninety-four; nine years of letters; a shortfall list; a file with a stranger's name written in the back of it; **and a plastic wallet in her coat with an unopened letter in it from the site's new owner offering an independent review of the final shortfall at the company's cost, without admission of liability, on the basis that her correspondence ceases for twelve months.** She has not answered it and told nobody, and she showed it to Elias on a wet pavement because he is the only person in the city who has been rude to her for money.

**Her function, and it is the batch's moral engine.** She is the one who understands that a priced claim becomes a negotiated claim, and that the ledger is the only thing that cannot be divided up and shared out. She is the one who makes Elias say *I have never heard of it* and look at her while he says it. She is the one who told him *don't come to my queue and ask me to be careful with a number* and then, four hours later, was the reason anything came of anything. And she is the one who watched him walk past a man in a chair and said nothing, which she will use on him eventually and has not yet.

**What she now knows.** That the Museum is holding the object, that the box has gone out to a solicitor's store in Northglass, that there is a hearing on the twenty-eighth, that an assessor has looked at the bell and written *no devotional content* on a sheet, and that the man who sat in her chair in September works in the building that has the paper.
