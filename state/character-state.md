# Character State

Updated after Batch 0001 (Volume 01, Chapters 1–10) and after **three review-fix passes and a verification pass**. Neither pass moved a plot beat, a day, or the volume's ending. The second pass closed an arithmetic and clock class of defect and added one structural beat to Chapter 10 — the blank *account of any person present during result* box, and with it the first, small, deliberate breach of Mara's second condition. **Read the thread and condition entries below against `state/continuity.md`, which is the authority.** Ages are canon; states are as of the end of Chapter 10 / the top of Sunday 11 October.

## Elias Venn, 32

**Where he is now.** Suspended from night duty as of Sunday morning over work order 4412-S and a fault report he signed. No authority in the Museum, no access to level B, not permitted at Monday 09:00's re-inspection of plinth 34. He has a pocket notebook, a set of four photographs on a personal phone, and a suspended man's Thursday.

**What he wants, on the page.** Still the post and still the letter. But the batch's real movement is that his private wound got outranked in a plastic chair in a municipal library by a fifty-four-year-old woman's nine years of unanswered post, and he has not been able to put it back in its place.

**What he has.** One bounded reading he cannot repeat on purpose. A headcount — ninety-four — that exists in one place only, in a pocket notebook, and is *not* in the shared log, which says *a number*. **A cleaner's name that also exists only in that notebook, and a form with a box for it that is blank.** A hand-written label on a pallet carrying PF 118. Eleven minutes of standing at a shut door that produced no record and nothing at all. Twenty minutes of the corridor he cannot account for, of which **sixteen are in nobody's hand at all**, and one notebook entry in **two visibly different hands** whose order he can no longer recover. A card read at 02:20 on Thursday night, in the small hours that logged him on a restricted door, and an access log Anja has already put back to him face to face.

**Skill state.** Stage 0 — Nightwatch, with a provisional first demonstration of Stage 1. He can now build the conditions for a bounded contact: cover, clamp, anchor card, written question, written time limit, named cost, witness, stop word, exit. He cannot yet produce the contact on demand, cannot separate an owner's perspective from a fact, and cannot put the two halves of one notebook entry back in the order he wrote them. **He has not been promoted. Stage 1 is confirmed in Chapters 11–12, not here.**

**Flaw, on the page, four times.** A private second notebook (Ch 2). A signed device-book entry with no destination, a deliberately displaced card, and a restricted door card-read for information (Ch 4). A false maintenance cause signed in his own hand (Ch 9). And in Ch 10, **two different mechanisms, and they must not be described the same way.** The result: Mara's own rule — her log takes no bare figure — produces *Result: a number*, correctly, on the evidence he gave her. He had four seconds in which to supply an object and did not, and when she held her hand out for the notebook he gave her an empty one. That leaves his **first** Chapter 9 promise intact to the letter. His **second** promise — *tell her everything, every time, and not the parts he has decided were relevant* — **he breaks himself, on purpose, twice over**: first by silence - he decides a man with a mop is not part of what he is telling her about, and does not volunteer Perry Loach's name, which he could have had in ninety seconds at a service desk - and then by a lie, when she asks him whether there is anything in the notebook that is not on the page and he says no. **He tells her no untruth whatever about the reading itself**, and that has to be stated too, because the result field, the units and the cost are all correct and the architecture of Chapter 10 depends on it. Nobody in the building can catch him at the lie, which is now the reason he is the most dangerous person in it.

**Costs unpaid and still owed.** A headache that will not break for two days — a **new** one, begun 21:41 Saturday, distinct from the eleven-hour-and-a-minute one that began 23:41 Wednesday and broke at 10:42 Thursday. Tinnitus. A rope texture across his right palm for a week. A promise in his own handwriting to stop at ten minutes when he wrote eleven, and **a promise to tell Mara everything that he has already broken twice - once by silence and once to her face - and knows he has broken.** A colleague who is a co-defendant twice over because Elias did not ask him for anything. And an entry in his own notebook in a hand he cannot account for.

**What he is frightened of, specifically.** Not the building. That a person can be present, paid, and unlisted for a year and a half, and that everybody who looked at the rota saw nothing, because the rota was right.

## Mara Iles, 28

**Where she is now.** A shared protocol log in her own hand whose result field says *a number*, initialled and timed, in a file that goes to a Registry observer on Monday — **and that entry is correct.** She asked for the units and the object; he gave her the shape and no object; her rule is that the log takes no bare figure, so she wrote what she actually had, and she did it in front of him. She then put her hand out for the notebook, was given an empty one, and did not ask about it. **She also completed the form's occupancy line truthfully — *one cleaner* — read the box beneath it, and left it blank for reasons she states out loud and that are entirely correct.** She did not know a member of staff had been reached, because nobody told her and the form has no line for a guard's second-hand version of it. She asked him, once, whether there was anything in the notebook that was not on the page, and he said no. She looked at him for one second longer than she needed to and then scraped the anchor card into her palm and went out.

**She has done nothing wrong and knows it, which is the problem.** There is no moment in the Chapter 10 scene where she is at fault, and Batch 0002 must not find one. What she has is a result field that says *a number* and a man who said ninety-four, and a professional rule that makes those two facts reconcilable, and a growing suspicion that the reconciling is the point.

**Believes.** A noise is not yet a fact. Documentation can correct the institution. A card is written by a person in a room, and that room is the label room, and if she guesses wrong it costs two people a year.

**Does not yet believe.** That his hearing is contamination, exactly as the character bible has it. What she actually has is a guard with a bleeding nose, a bad explanation, and a result field that is one word shorter than the room was. She has not decided what that is. **The gap is between 94 and *a number*, and only Elias knows it is a gap — and, after the re-cut, only Elias knows there was anything to hide at all.** Mara's honest position is that she wrote down correctly what she was told, and she is right about that.

**Wants from him.** The result in the shared log in her hand exactly as it comes; and everything, every time. Both agreed in writing on Saturday afternoon. **The first is intact to the letter and defeated him anyway. The second he has broken twice over - once by silence, once by lying to her face when she asked him the question directly, which she believes - and that is the version she cannot detect and the reason she is not going to be able to trust the notebook for four volumes.**

**Owes nothing and has not been thanked.** She named the cost aloud and wrote it down. Nobody in his life has ever written one of his costs down anywhere.

## Ivo Pell, 25

**Where he is now.** On his own Saturday systems day, in his own workshop, with a cover cut and clamps in a bag and a jar of salt and ash he made on Thursday night in his own flat. He had already been in on the Friday controller and gone home at 00:35. He built the cover, the clamps, the anchor card, the five-line protocol, and the stop word — and, on Thursday, made Elias say the corridor arrangement out loud in the security room before agreeing to it, so that it could be repeated back to somebody later. That was his price and it was not a favour. He took the private duplicate notebook out of Elias's hand and handed it back and said, in effect, that Elias is building a set of books he alone can revise.

**What he is frightened of.** Not the corridor. Losing his sister, who has a chronic chest and no lungs to speak of. He is behind on sleep before Elias asks him for anything, and he has said so once, in his own voice, and did not labour it.

**His exposure, which he does not know he has.** Two silences, neither requested. The unlogged Thursday drop of door 7, which left no event because the door relocks before the controller finishes a handshake. And 00:30 on Friday night, coming up from the shop **with the new module still in his hand**, finding the frame already live with the old unit and the tray as he left it, and writing down neither. Five minutes over his own roster, in a system that keeps that column of times. **He is a co-defendant by accident and has not been asked to be anything.**

**Wants.** To be a certified Civic Works engineer, to be useful, and to go home. He has begun, without noticing, to be the reason Elias can take risks at all, and that is exactly the dependency the series needs him to outgrow.

## Dael Ordway, 44

**Where he is now.** Has taken Elias's false cause into an insurer's file and will be asked about it one day by a man from the Board. Has cancelled Saturday's forty-one-child tour and written the letters that go with it. Gave Elias a cover story for plinth 34 that is not a lie, and a card-reader authorising window that put Elias in a corridor, and told him to think about whether her decision is the kind that does that — *before* the night, not after.

**Sympathy test.** The reader is invited to agree with him in Chapter 2 and to feel for him in Chapter 9. He is not lying to Elias and not lying to himself. He writes the same four lines he would write about a loading-bay shutter, and he hates himself a bit afterwards, and he would do it again, and that is the whole of his character and the reason he is not a villain.

**Knows.** Elias's Wednesday movements, from a log. The absence of a file for plinth 34. The eighteen-year-old instruction's missing signatory, which he has carried for eighteen years.

**Does not know.** The corridor, the twenty minutes, the unlogged drop, and the device book. He is one drawer away from all of it and Elias did not offer.

## Anja Ward, 53

**Where she is now.** Gave authorisation, not information: a work-order number, a floor, a form, and a log reference. Told Elias she would rather have a bad paragraph on her desk on Monday than a find in an insurance file, and referred to it never again. Elias noticed that she authorised the fault as well as handing him the reference and did not resolve it.

**Her cover-up** is not on the page yet and must not be. The missing signatory on the Quiet Wing instruction is the same eighteen years as the Casket, and nobody in this batch has noticed.

## Perry Loach, 61 (prose: *a night cleaner in his early sixties*)

Named on the page for the first time in Chapter 10, by the Saturday duty manager, who also told Elias he was on the Sillick job before this place. Eleven years on these floors, forty years of floors before that, Saturdays because Saturdays pay. He answered **ninety-four** without being asked, in a voice he has not used in thirty-one years, and stopped as though interrupted, and said nothing else for the rest of the shift.

He is one of the short-paid. He was there for the closing count. **He is the volume's most dangerous kind of witness: one who cannot testify, does not know it happened, and will be back on the floors tomorrow night.** The numeral never appears in the same chapter as his name.

**He is also the only person the object has been shown to reach, and he is in no record at all.** The shared log's occupancy line names him only as *one cleaner*; the box that would carry his account, his grade and his countersignature is blank and will stay blank unless somebody goes and asks him. He is a night cleaner, not a witness, and the building has no line on any form for turning one into the other. Elias has his name and has not spent it.

## Hattie Bramm, 54

Present, unmoved, and the moral centre of the batch so far. Sillick Mill's gate clerk on the closing shift: she held the book, a foreman called the figures aloud, she wrote them down. One of the short-paid, one of the ninety-four, and she has carried the arithmetic for thirty-one years with nowhere to put it. She has a shortfall list of sixty-one, nine years of letters to a firm that has been at four addresses, and a file with the Museum's crest on it. She is not a mystic and she is not the volume's mouthpiece. **She calls the count in the climax because she was there.**

She knows only that the man in her queue is vague about his job, works nights, will not give a name, and came down a tram to ask her about a man on her street. She has said she will find out who he is. He owes her the truth and has not paid it.

## Helen Venn, 58

She has now been asked about it three times in a week and has still not handed it over, and she is right not to.

## Relationships, end of batch

- **Elias / Mara:** adversarial professional respect. A protocol and two conditions, **the first kept to the letter and turned against her by a correct procedure, the second broken by a silence and then by a lie he gets away with.** No intimacy, no romance, and the reader should feel the professional stake as the only thing on the table.
- **Elias / Ivo:** working friendship, and now complicity. Ivo is the first collaborator and the first person to name the flaw out loud. Ivo has never been asked for anything, which is the flaw's mechanism.
- **Elias / Dael:** deferred-to becoming managed. Dael assigns him to the service-lane cameras as an instruction and then files what Elias signs. The institution can lie in its own handwriting, and Elias has now signed it.
- **Elias / Hattie:** a debt, unacknowledged, four miles from a bell.
- **Elias / Anja:** assessed and reassured in the same sentence. Unresolved and deliberately so.
