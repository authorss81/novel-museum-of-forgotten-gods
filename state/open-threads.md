# Open Threads

## Active Long-Range Threads

- **The Bell of the Unpaid Shift:** A forgotten work-god is asking for acknowledgment of unpaid labor, not worship. The first volume must connect the object to real workers and the Museum's liability. The object is never named in Batch 0001; the name is a Batch 0005 payoff.
- **The Casket and Tomas Venn:** Tomas disappeared during the Casket Incident, leaving a counter-witness fragment. His body is never recovered; the father's remaining voice is a bounded echo, not a resurrection. **Volume 01 does not stage the Casket.** Elias's origin in it is a Movement 4 beat; Batch 0001 carries it as a planted rumour and an unopened letter.
- **The four Sillick counts:** 94 worked, 92 paid, 2 never entered, 61 short-paid. Fixed, separate, and not to be re-derived. **92 and 2 are held for the Chapter 25 midpoint reversal** and must not be spent in Batch 0002.
- **First Witness reveal discipline:** Volume 11 establishes only the Witness's category. Volume 12 reveals that it was assembled from 312 chosen volunteer memories and explains how Tomas and Elias became connected to it.
- **Lock progression:** The final lock is located in Volume 11, the central lock is broken in Volume 12, and the final lock must be deliberately opened in Volume 14 after a tested custodian network can receive the First Witness's burden. No one may restore sole authority.
- **Orrin's custody bridge:** Orrin is detained in Volume 12, released during the Volume 13 transfer under a surviving Accord emergency order, and reaches the ward-lines before the final shutdown. He is a separate threat to the Engine's own automatic routine, not its cause.
- **Elias's cost curve:** Volume 13's hearing loss is temporary and uneven. The permanent loss of most object-hearing is reserved for the Volume 14 choice to release Tomas's echo.
- **Shared authority:** Elias and Mara must move from secrecy and professional opposition toward a chosen partnership; Ivo and the local custodians must become able to act when Elias is absent.
- **Final dispositions:** The new Museum charter must distinguish **tended** (active under Museum shared-trust custody), **returned** (custody and interpretive authority transferred to external living stewards), and **left absent** (no living community accepts an active relationship).

## Volume 01 threads opened and inherited from Batch 0001

- **The unclosed work order 4412-S and the signed false cause.** A forty-seven-minute outage of a restricted door inside a fifty-minute authorised window, an order that was never completed, and a systems tech's login that was not at the frame. Built entirely on true maintenance records. Elias signed *confirmed by guard on duty* at about 07:30 on Saturday morning. Traceable in every direction and provable later. **Elias is suspended over it Sunday morning — the first beat of Batch 0002, unwritten.** He does not know it is coming: Chapter 10 ends with him looking at Monday's re-inspection, and no foreknowledge of a suspension appears anywhere in the batch.
- **The device tray book.** Elias's name, 01:52 out and 02:41 back on Thursday, no destination; and a second breach on Friday night when he takes the phone out of the unlocked tray with no entry at all, so the book shows the phone going home and the book is right. A third interaction nobody has accounted for is the 09:31 Saturday call to Helen from the stair landing. Dael could pull this book in Chapter 9 and did not, because Elias did not offer it. Live leverage for the inquiry.
- **The four photographs and the displaced card.** The bell's accession card is hand-cut, recent, and has been used before: the maker's line on the back was taken off thoroughly by somebody who knew what a foundry catalogue is, and a second line on the same card was taken off badly and not at all, and it reads **Wendel Case**. The motive is on the page — an *unremarkable industrial object* has no maker, and a maker can be written to. Elias photographed it four times at four angles on his own phone, with no official system holding a copy, and put it back half a millimetre out of true on purpose. He has now written in his own notebook, on the page: *Card put back crooked. Deliberate. My decision.* Fenn in the label room will remember a stranger in the drawers; Mara in the inquiry will remember there is no file.
- **Ivo's two silences, and the fact that Elias never asked him for either.** Asking would have made it a favour, and Elias wanted a fault he could have found himself; that single decision is his whole flaw, and it is why Ivo is complicit without ever having been asked. The silences: he did not write up Thursday's unlogged drop, and at 00:30 on the Friday night he came back up from the shop **with the new module still in his hand**, found the frame already live with the old unit in it, and wrote nothing down either. He is twenty-five and he is a co-defendant who does not yet know it. Ivo left at 00:35, five minutes over his own roster.
- **The lie to Hattie Bramm.** Elias was useful to her because he was vague, and she named the Museum herself from a nine-year letter file. She knows he is vague, she has said she will find his name, and the debt is live for the rest of the volume. He also now knows that **Cliff Sowerby**, a fitter's mate at the dye house, lived at number 14 and mended the standpipe every autumn for a pint he would not take, and that the standpipe is dead and has been charged for twice. Sowerby is a Low Ward neighbour and **must not drift back into being a second Wendel Case.**
- **The three lies to Helen Venn.** One telephone call, three sentences, about hours and a letter she has been holding for eighteen years. She told him, without being asked, that there is nothing in the world he cannot tell her.
- **The unlisted room.** Seen only through clouded wired glass: a swivel chair with no dust on the seat, a coat hook, a shelf of Museum lanyards with every name panel blank, dust on everything else, and cold air under a shut and latched door. The pressed-brass corridor service bell beside it is hard-piped to the ward-bell repeater line and is still live, and nobody has come to switch it off. **Elias does not open that door in this volume, and the volume's last chapter depends on him not doing so.** Somebody is keeping that room, and the design of the wing is such that a man could have stood in front of that door for as long as he liked and the building would never once have said a word about it.
- **The missing signature.** One restricted instruction, eighteen years old, reorganising the Quiet Wing card access, signed by a name that appears in no list of people who have ever worked here. Elias has decided once already **not to ask** about it, and has noticed that the decision felt like a wait rather than an investigation. Tomas's initials are in the same document's attachments and are not found until Movement 4.
- **PF 118.** A live personnel file reference, hand-written in biro on a gummed strip over a printed label, on the Sillick Mill box after somebody moved it out of bay nine in the twenty-four hours since Elias stood in front of it and did not open it. It is not the porter's file, because the porter has no file. So somebody else in this building has been looking for Wendel Case, has found the same absence Elias found, and has carried the search further.
- **The bell's own reach.** Perry Loach, a night cleaner in his early sixties and a Sillick Mill fitter, answered ninety-four without being asked, in a voice he has not used in thirty-one years, and stopped as though interrupted. Elias now knows the object can touch people who have not consented, that he has no procedure for it, and that the man will be back on the floors tomorrow night. Nothing in the batch's apparatus addresses this and Batch 0002 must not pretend it can be bounded away.
- **The two numeric things that are missing.** The reading gave ninety-four and the Museum's log says *a number*. Until that gap is closed, every downstream account of the case is resting on a man's pocket notebook. **The mechanism matters and is now fixed on the page:** Mara's log does not take a bare figure, so she wrote *a number* correctly on the evidence she had, and Elias had four seconds in which to give her an object and gave her an empty hand instead. Both of her Chapter 9 conditions are still literally unbroken, so there is no *breach* to point at — only a procedure that did the work for him.
- **The two-hands notebook entry.** Chapter 8's corridor entry exists on the page in two blocks in two visibly different hands, in an order he cannot recover, with sixteen minutes of the night in neither block. It is the only memory cost in the batch and it is **not resolved, not stabilising, and not to be quietly tidied** in a later batch. The first *major* displacement cost is reserved for Volume 03.
- **The level-two archive card read.** Elias card-read the restricted archive door at 02:20 on Wednesday night, which logged him in his own name; there is no camera on the landing. Anja has already read the log and put it to him. **That conversation is spent** — there is nothing further for him to decide about it, and Batch 0002 should not re-stage it.
- **The four C surnames.** Callaghan, Cunliffe, Coverley, Crowther, in the Museum's annual personnel binders from 1974. Two were porters. None is Case. The finding is not a suspect list; it is that the record has no seam in it at all, which is why it is still being trusted.
