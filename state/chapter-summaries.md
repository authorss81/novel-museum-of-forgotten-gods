# Chapter Summaries

Rolling window: **Volume 01, Chapters 1–10** (Batch 0001, "The First Bell"). Nothing has been archived out of this file yet.

## Chapter 0001 — Night Round

Tue 19:40 – Wed 03:10. Elias fixes the Hall of Former Faiths door (warped strike plate, thirty years of paint) and logs the time. The River Works notice dated Monday — third ward-bell at South Sluice re-cracked at 21:40, sounding a wrong note — is on the pinboard, and the crew have already taken it as the building's ready-made explanation for any noise this week. At 02:52 plinth 34 gives one note with nobody near it. Elias does not run; he checks the case, the seal, the sensor, the door, finds everything correct, and writes it into the building log as a building-noise observation, exactly as Dael would have it. He thinks about his father's unopened letter and does nothing. Ends with the sound now being a record, and forty-one children booked into that hall on Saturday.

## Chapter 0002 — The Bell in the Hall

Wed 19:00–22:00. Dael reads the log before Elias arrives and gives an instruction, not a question: west range, ductwork, note it and move on; an undocumented danger is a danger he can be dismissed for; leave the sluice notice up because the Board has been on about the ward-bell budget since March. Elias logs properly and keeps a private pocket notebook as a second, unlogged copy — the batch's first real cost, paid in risk. At 20:55 the note recurs with Elias in the hall; the case does nothing, and the hall's background hum from every other object drops out and comes back one second later. The Index surfaces one line, which he does not understand and does not write down. He meets Mara Iles in a first-floor doorway: there is no accession file 4471 over B. Saturday's booking sheet is on the desk under a lead. He writes *not investigated, per instruction*.

## Chapter 0003 — A Number That Does Not Add

Wed 22:00 – Thu 00:30. Mara examines the bell as material evidence and beats him on two counts: the label gives a cast date and an acquisition number in the same year and describes a recovery from a closure, which cannot both be true; and the wear is a left-hand grip, not a rope, so the bell was rung hard for years by somebody who never maintained it. She identifies the repair staple as a decision somebody made about a Tuesday morning, over a crack that is still visible. At 23:40, alone at the case while she fetches a tray, Elias puts his hands on the glass and gets four seconds of a gate in rain and a tired man calling out headcount figures a thousand times in a second, ending on a total he cannot catch. He comes round on the floor bleeding from one nostril and lies badly to her about a door push and a head condition. The headache runs unbroken for eleven hours.

## Chapter 0004 — The Card With Two Names

Wed 01:00 – Thu 04:00. The plinth 34 card is hand-cut, not printed, so it was made in the label room. He signs the device book — his own name, 01:52 out — takes his phone from slot twelve in front of the duty manager on her round at about two, and writes no destination, then signs it back at 02:41. The card he takes out of the drawer is the **spare** for plinth 34 — every plinth card has one — and it carries two names: the false accession number on the front, and **Wendel Case** under an erasure on the back. In the label room (camera covers the doorway and the card cutter, not the drawers) he finds that the bell's card is recent stock with recent ink and, under an erasure, an older shaking hand reading **Wendel Case** — a person's name, not a maker's. He photographs it four times and puts it back half a millimetre out of true, deliberately, as a signal to whoever comes next. He ends the night understanding that he has crossed into the behaviour he would have arrested someone for, and that telling Mara would make the card disappear on an index nobody can produce.

## Chapter 0005 — One Frame Short

Thu 19:00 – Fri 00:12, Ivo's systems night. Elias works out that Case has no line in current or archived Museum payroll, but has appeared for eighteen months on the municipal payroll bureau's monthly remittance statement, grade 2, with pension and union deductions still taken — proof of existence and of money moving, not proof the Museum's books ever carried him. Case's locker (81) is still in the basement: a hook, a half-full tin of tea, a comb, a folded newspaper, unlatched, and the erasure propagated to a printed locker card that Elias had read over for two years. The corridor stills show a shape at 01:12 on ten of fourteen nights with a fifth-of-a-second smear on the two hardest frames. Elias buys eleven minutes of unauthorised corridor time with Ivo's complicity — 22:14 to 22:25 — and the door never opens. The leaf relocks a second and a half after the fault and the eleven minutes are spent at a shut door in an ordinary corridor with a bracket that has no bell on it, and he gets nothing at all. Worse, the panel's event history for door 7 holds nothing for that night: no start, no restore, no log, because the controller gave up on the handshake, and the whole three-year history of that door is nine *restored* lines with no *started* in front of them. Ivo's silence costs nothing, because there is nothing to suppress, and Elias understands that the wing was designed so that a man can stand at a locked door for as long as he likes and the building will never once say a word about it. Waiting for a pattern from 23:40 produces nothing; at 00:04 he writes *NO SOUND, THU*; at 00:05 the bell answers with the case in frame. He cannot tell whether it was late or whether it came because he stopped looking.

## Chapter 0006 — Sillick Lane

Fri 09:00–14:00, alone. Elias goes down to the Museum's old grade-2 porter posting sheets, which route every night porter of that decade to Sillick Lane, walks the length of the row to the mill site at the bottom of the street — eleven hundred metres of corrugated hoarding, a gate at each end, and **HALLOWGATE HOLDINGS** sprayed on the fence at head height — and then goes back up to the Friday unpaid-bills queue in the back room of the Sillick Lane branch library. The mill papers and the mill land are in the same pair of hands. Hattie Bramm, 54, Sillick Mill's gate clerk on the closing shift, holds a hard hand, a folder, and nine years of letters. She gives him the scale of the grievance — sixty-one people short-paid on the final pay run, a pay figure and not a headcount — and tells him the mill's own shortfall sheet was sold with the office papers to a museum on the hill that does not need them. Elias never gives her his job title; she supplies the Museum herself from her letter file. Case lived at number 14 and mended the standpipe every autumn for a pint he would not take. Elias's private wound is outranked, in a plastic chair, by somebody else's unpaid money.

## Chapter 0007 — The Roster

Fri 19:00–21:30. Elias finds 1961/LW/44 in the catalogue — *Sillick Mill: administrative papers, disposal, level B bay 9* — tries the sub-level alone, is refused by the gate interlock, and does not do anything stupid: he calls Ivo down as a second person, stands in front of the box with a torch, and leaves it unopened. The municipal payroll terminal logs his search and returns *no current record, no archived record*, and no leaver's record either. Anja Ward reads his Wednesday access log back to him in a corridor, gives authorisation rather than information (work order 4412-S, 23:40–00:30, signed by a director, logged under Ivo's login), tells him she would rather have a bad paragraph on her desk Monday than a find in an insurance file, and mentions the one restricted document in the building that predates her — an eighteen-year-old Quiet Wing card-access instruction signed by a name that appears in no list of people who have ever worked here. Elias decides not to ask. He also decides not to ask Ivo for anything, because asking would make it a favour.

## Chapter 0008 — Quiet Wing, After Hours

Fri 23:40 – Sat 01:05. With a director's signature holding the fail-secure leaf released and the corridor camera on the same circuit, Elias takes his phone out of the unlocked tray without signing (the book is right; it says the phone went home) and waits, doing nothing, until 23:52. He walks ninety feet into the corridor and comes to on the floor twenty minutes later with his hand flat on a shut, latched door. Through clouded wired glass: a swivel chair with no dust on the seat, a coat hook, a shelf of Museum lanyards with every name panel blank, dust on everything else, and cold air under the door. He says the Index's second line out loud, photographs the corridor, writes one notebook entry in a hand that changes twice, refits the old reader at 00:27, and is through the door before the controller answers at 00:28 — a logged forty-seven-minute outage inside a fifty-minute window. At 00:25 the hall bell and the sluice ward-bell's wrong strike arrive together through the floor, with a pressed-brass corridor service bell on the ward-bell repeater line repeating half a tone out of tune. At 00:30 Ivo comes back up with the new module still in his hand, finds the frame already live with the old unit, and writes nothing down; Elias is on a stair landing and does not call out. The chapter's cost is twenty minutes he cannot account for and two notebook lines he can no longer order. He comes down off the landing at 00:50 and, because he has worked out that a thing nobody writes down is a thing that never happened, he opens the notebook and puts the Friday 00:25 bell down for the first time — with the sluice bell, the hall bell and the brass staff-call bell all in one line, and the note that the repeater cannot do anything except repeat.

## Chapter 0009 — The Log He Filed

Sat 07:00–12:30. Dael, in since 06:30, builds a fault report out of a maintenance record that is entirely true and asks for one line: that access to the corridor during the outage was not established. The device book is a drawer away and neither man mentions it. Elias signs, having read exactly which four words are false. Dael cancels Saturday's forty-one-child tour on a maintenance notice and pays for it in donor calls and registrar letters, and gives Elias a cover story for plinth 34 that is not a lie. At 09:35 Elias rings his mother from the landing and tells her three untruths in three sentences about his hours and the letter. In Ivo's workshop he builds a sheet-steel cover, four clamps, and the first salt-and-ash anchor card; they write the five-line protocol, and the stop word is *fault*. Ivo takes the private duplicate notebook out of his hand, hands it back, and tells him exactly what he is doing. Mara reads the five lines first, agrees to be the person who can stop a reading on two conditions — the result in the shared log in her hand exactly as it comes, and he tells her everything every time — and refuses in advance to be alone in the building with a faulted west range. He agrees to both, in writing, and knows what he has promised.

## Chapter 0010 — Ninety-Four

Sat 20:50 – Sun 01:30. On the one night the building is full — cleaners, conservation tech, facilities, duty manager Priest on the freight lift, Ivo in on a favour — Elias runs the protocol on the busiest night of the week because it is the only night with a door anybody can open. Sixth note at 21:07. At 21:14 the third ward-bell at South Sluice fails outright: the west range drops to the emergency circuit, the Quiet Wing readers re-key, and the hall bell repeats on a fixed interval four times in six minutes. Mara comes down at 21:25 and writes eleven minutes on the card where she said ten, which Elias notices and does not hide. The reading runs 21:30–21:41: one question, a written time limit, a named cost, a witness, a salt-and-ash card, and *fault* said by Mara at 21:38 when the hall door — strike plate riding again since Wednesday — swings four inches on the draught from the propped service door. It returns a gate in rain, a tired man reading a headcount aloud, a rope texture hauling across his palm, and **ninety-four** — which he says out loud in a hall that is not empty. Perry Loach, a night cleaner in his early sixties and a Sillick Mill fitter, answers the number without being asked in a voice he has not used in thirty-one years, and stops as though interrupted. Elias is not the only person the object can reach, and there is no line on his card for that. When Mara asks for the result, he says *a number, I don't know what it counts*; she writes **a number** in the result field of the shared log; he has four seconds to correct her and watches them go past, breaking in front of both of them the first condition he agreed to in Chapter 9. At 00:20, lawfully on the floor in the loading bay while a crew of two stages eleven damp boxes off level B, he reads a hand-written label on a dust-sheeted box: *Sillick Mill: administrative papers / 1961 LW 44 / **PF 118*** — a live personnel file reference, not the porter's, on a box somebody moved out of bay nine in the last twenty-four hours. He ends on a situation: a guard who cannot get to Monday's re-inspection, and somebody else in the building searching for the same man.
