## Chapter 0001 — Night Round

Tue 19:40 – Wed 03:10. Elias fixes the Hall of Former Faiths door (warped strike plate, thirty years of paint) and logs the time. The River Works notice dated Monday — third ward-bell at South Sluice re-cracked at 21:40, sounding a wrong note — is on the pinboard, and the crew have already taken it as the building's ready-made explanation for any noise this week. At 02:52 plinth 34 gives one note with nobody near it. Elias does not run; he checks the case, the seal, the sensor, the door, finds everything correct, and writes it into the building log as a building-noise observation, exactly as Dael would have it. He thinks about his father's unopened letter and does nothing. Ends with the sound now being a record, and forty-one children booked into that hall on Saturday.

## Chapter 0002 — The Bell in the Hall

Wed 19:00–22:10. Dael reads the log before Elias arrives and gives an instruction, not a question: west range, ductwork, note it and move on; an undocumented danger is a danger he can be dismissed for; leave the sluice notice up because the Board has been on about the ward-bell budget since March. Elias logs properly and keeps a private pocket notebook as a second, unlogged copy — the batch's first real cost, paid in risk. At 20:55 the note recurs with Elias in the hall; the case does nothing, and the hall's background hum from every other object drops out and comes back one second later. The Index surfaces one line, which he does not understand and does not write down. He meets Mara Iles in a first-floor doorway: there is no accession file 4471 over B. Saturday's booking sheet is on the desk under a lead. He writes *not investigated, per instruction*. The chapter closes on ninety minutes of silence and, at 22:10, the conservation treatment rooms going off one floor above him. **The bell does not sound again for the rest of the shift; the third note belongs to Chapter 3.**

## Chapter 0003 — A Number That Does Not Add

Wed 22:10 – Thu 00:30. Mara examines the bell as material evidence and beats him on two counts. The first is arithmetic: a Museum accession is **year / letters / number**, `1961/LW/44`, where the year is the year the district *series* was opened — so `Acq. 4471 / B`, with no year and one letter where the form takes two, is not a Museum number at all, and it is the only one of the label's three claims that anybody typed. The second is wear: the hollow is a left-hand grip, not a rope, so the bell was rung hard for years by somebody who never maintained it. She identifies the repair staple as a decision somebody made about a Tuesday morning, over a crack that is still visible. She also refuses to be told what he heard, and makes him sit through the difference between his word and an entry in a building: the Wednesday 20:55 watch is in the log in her hand, and the Tuesday 02:52 sound is a Tuesday-night entry in a building log, and the difference between those two sentences is the whole of his week. At 23:40, alone at the case while she fetches a tray, Elias puts his hands on the glass and gets four seconds of a gate in rain and a tired man calling out headcount figures a thousand times in a second, ending on a total he cannot catch. He comes round on the floor bleeding from one nostril and lies badly to her about a door push and a head condition. **The headache starts at 23:41 and is clocked from the start, because a cost you cannot time is a cost nobody has to believe in. It breaks at 10:42 Thursday, eleven hours and one minute later, in a shop on Lantern Row (Chapter 5).**

## Chapter 0004 — The Card With Two Names

Thu 01:00 – 04:00, inside the single Wednesday-evening-through-Thursday-morning shift. The plinth 34 card is hand-cut, not printed, so it was made in the label room. At 01:26 he puts his card on the locked Hall door out of pure habit and it logs the refusal — the first thing that night that puts him in the building's record, and he did it to himself, for nothing. He signs the device book at 01:52 (his own name, no destination), takes his phone from slot twelve in front of the duty manager on her round at about two, and signs it back at 02:41. In the label room at 02:05 — the lintel camera covers the doorway and the card cutter, not the drawers — he takes out the **spare** card for plinth 34 and finds it has been used before: two lines on the back, the maker's line taken off thoroughly by somebody who knew what a foundry catalogue is, and a second line gone at badly and not gone at all. **Wendel Case**, a person's name, in the line where a card keeps the person who put the thing in the case. He photographs it four times and puts it back half a millimetre out of true, deliberately, as a signal to whoever comes next. **Then, at 02:20, he card-reads the restricted door on the level-two archive corridor** — no camera on the landing, but a door that writes his name down — and finds that the Museum personnel record is paper, not a system: annual ring binders on a steel shelf behind the fire plan, one per year from 1974, every year present, every binder square, nothing disturbed. No W. Case, and no leaver's record for Case either. The horror is that the record is *undamaged* — grade 2 porter has a leaver's column with eleven tidy names in it and there is no seam anywhere in thirty years. He puts the binders back square, which is the only thing in that corridor that will be where he found it, and ends the night understanding that he has crossed into the behaviour he would have arrested someone for, and that telling Mara would make the card disappear on an index nobody can produce.

## Chapter 0005 — One Frame Short

Thu 19:00 – Fri 00:15, Ivo's systems night. Elias works out that Case has no line in current or archived Museum payroll, but has appeared for eighteen months on the municipal payroll bureau's monthly remittance statement, grade 2, with pension and union deductions still taken — proof of existence and of money moving, not proof the Museum's books ever carried him. Case's locker (81, in a run of forty-one numbered 61–101) is still in the basement: a hook, a half-full tin of tea, a comb, a folded newspaper, unlatched, and the erasure propagated to a printed locker card that Elias had read over for two years. The corridor stills show a shape at 01:12 on ten of fourteen nights, five arrival timestamps, two on the seventh night back and three on the fourteenth night back, all inside four seconds of one another, with a fifth-of-a-second smear on the two nights the figure was least complete. Elias buys eleven minutes of unauthorised corridor time with Ivo's complicity — 22:14 to 22:25 — and the door never opens. The leaf relocks a second and a half after the fault, and the eleven minutes are spent at a shut door in an ordinary corridor with a bracket that has no bell on it, and he gets nothing at all. Worse, the panel's event history for door 7 holds nothing for that night: no start, no restore, no log, because the controller gave up on the handshake, and the whole three-year history of that door is nine *restored* lines with no *started* in front of them. **Ivo's price is not a favour: he makes Elias say the arrangement out loud in the security room before he agrees, so that it can be repeated back to somebody later.** Ivo's silence then costs nothing, because there is nothing to suppress, and Elias understands that the wing was designed so that a man can stand at a locked door for as long as he likes and the building will never once say a word about it. The corridor ends at 22:25; **the watch on plinth thirty-four starts at 23:40**. Waiting for a pattern produces nothing; at 00:04 he writes *NO SOUND, THU*; at 00:05 the bell answers with the case in frame; the notebook entry is at 00:12 and the chapter closes at 00:15. **The chapter does not decide it for him.** It could have been late; it could be that a thing which only happens when you are not looking is a thing that happens when you are not looking, and no column headed *time / location / observation / action* can hold that without inventing a reason a thing happened when the reason is that you wanted it to. It could equally be coincidence, which he writes down too and does not believe, because a notebook holding only the conclusions you like in it is a list of wishes. Both possibilities sit side by side for the rest of the night, and both have the same consequence for Monday morning: there is no schedule to log.

## Chapter 0006 — Sillick Lane

Fri 09:00–14:00, alone, and he leaves the queue at 12:20 with a shift behind him rather than stay and be thorough. He goes down to the Museum's grade-2 posting sheets, which he found in the level-two card index on Thursday night, in Chapter 5, and which route every night porter of that decade to Sillick Lane, walks the length of the row to the mill site at the bottom of the street — eleven hundred metres of corrugated hoarding, a gate at each end, and **HALLOWGATE HOLDINGS** sprayed on the fence at head height — and then goes back up to the Friday unpaid-bills queue in the back room of the Sillick Lane branch library. The mill papers and the mill land are in the same pair of hands. Hattie Bramm, 54, Sillick Mill's gate clerk on the closing shift, holds a hard hand, a folder, and nine years of letters. She gives him the scale of the grievance — sixty-one people short-paid on the final pay run, a pay figure and not a headcount — and tells him the mill's own shortfall sheet was sold with the office papers to a museum on the hill that does not need them. Elias never gives her his job title; she supplies the Museum herself from her letter file. **Cliff Sowerby**, a fitter's mate at the dye house, lived at number 14 and mended the standpipe every autumn for a pint he would not take — he is a Low Ward neighbour and deliberately *not* a second Wendel Case; the name must not drift back. Elias's private wound is outranked, in a plastic chair, by somebody else's unpaid money.

Fri 19:00–21:10. Elias finds 1961/LW/44 in the catalogue — *Sillick Mill: administrative papers, disposal, level B bay 9* — tries the sub-level alone, is refused by the gate interlock, and does not do anything stupid: he calls Ivo down as a second person, stands in front of the box with a torch, and leaves it unopened. She tells him she would rather have a bad paragraph on her desk Monday than a find in an insurance file, and mentions the one restricted document in the building that predates her — an eighteen-year-old Quiet Wing card-access instruction signed by a name that appears in no list of people who have ever worked here. Elias decides not to ask. He also decides not to ask Ivo for anything, because asking would make it a favour.

## Chapter 0008 — Quiet Wing, After Hours

Fri 23:40 – Sat 01:05. With a director's signature holding the fail-secure leaf released and the corridor camera on the same circuit, Elias takes his phone out of the unlocked tray without signing (the book is right; it says the phone went home) and waits, doing nothing, until 23:52. He walks ninety feet into the corridor and comes to on the floor with his hand flat on a shut, latched door. Through clouded wired glass: a swivel chair with no dust on the seat, a coat hook, a shelf of Museum lanyards with every name panel blank, dust on everything else, and cold air under the door. **He gets the Index's second line as an interior line, with no source and no speaker, exactly as in Chapter 2 — and this time without the tinnitus excuse available — and does not write it in either book.** He photographs the corridor at 00:12 and writes one notebook entry at 00:18: **one entry, two halves, in two visibly different hands, which he cannot afterwards put in the order he wrote them and has never been able to.** He refits the old reader at 00:27 and is through the door before the controller answers at 00:28 — a logged forty-seven-minute outage inside a fifty-minute window. At 00:25 the hall bell and the sluice ward-bell's wrong strike arrive together through the floor, with a pressed-brass corridor service bell on the ward-bell repeater line repeating half a tone out of tune. At 00:30 Ivo comes back up with the new module still in his hand, finds the frame already live with the old unit, and writes nothing down; Elias is on a stair landing and does not call out. The chapter's cost is **sixteen minutes that are on the page in nobody's hand at all**: the last four minutes before the photographs are the ones he can account for, and the sixteen before them are in neither half of the entry and in neither order. He comes down off the landing at 00:50 and, because he has worked out that a thing nobody writes down is a thing that never happened, he opens the notebook and puts the Friday 00:25 bell down for the first time — with the sluice bell, the hall bell and the brass staff-call bell all in one line, and the note that the repeater is not the source of anything and is the plumbing that carries it.

## Chapter 0009 — The Log He Filed

Sat 07:00–12:30, then to 20:50. Dael, in since 06:30, builds a fault report out of a maintenance record that is entirely true and asks for one line: that access to the corridor during the outage was not established. Dael hands him the whole unvouched window instead of a suspicious one — 23:52 to 00:28, thirty-six minutes — and Elias's recognition is not that Dael picked it on purpose but that **a duration is not a witness**: the print accounts for the night continuously, the systems saw nothing, and sixteen of those minutes are in nobody's hand and never will be. The device book is a drawer away and neither man mentions it. Elias signs, having read exactly which four words are false. Dael cancels Saturday's forty-one-child tour on a maintenance notice and pays for it in donor calls and registrar letters, and gives Elias a cover story for plinth 34 that is not a lie. At 09:31 Elias rings his mother from the landing and tells her three untruths in three sentences about his hours and the letter. In Ivo's workshop — **Ivo's own Saturday systems day, and not rostered for the floor** — he builds a sheet-steel cover, four clamps, and the first salt-and-ash anchor card; they write the five-line protocol, and the stop word is *fault*, which is Ivo's word and which Elias gives to Mara as well in Chapter 10 so that a reading does not depend on one person having the right thought. Ivo takes the private duplicate notebook out of his hand, hands it back, and tells him exactly what he is doing. Mara reads the five lines first, agrees to be the person who can stop a reading on two conditions — the result in the shared log in her hand exactly as it comes, and he tells her everything every time — and refuses in advance to be alone in the building with a faulted west range. She says ten minutes; **Elias writes TEN MINUTES on the card, rubs it out, and writes 11 MIN instead, on the argument that a number she has to argue with is safer than one he agreed to in advance and then broke quietly. She writes eleven; he promises to stop at ten.** He agrees to both conditions, in writing, and knows what he has promised. He goes back on at 19:30.

## Chapter 0010 — Ninety-Four

Sat 20:50 – Sun 00:50. On the one night the building is full — cleaners, conservation tech, facilities, duty manager Priest on the freight lift, Ivo in on a favour — Elias runs the protocol on the busiest night of the week because it is the only night with a door anybody can open. Sixth note at 21:07. At 21:14 the third ward-bell at South Sluice fails outright: the west range drops to the emergency circuit, the Quiet Wing readers re-key, and the hall bell repeats on a fixed interval four times in six minutes. Mara comes down at 21:25 and writes eleven minutes on the card where she said ten, which Elias notices and does not hide. The reading runs 21:30–21:41: one question, a written time limit, a named cost, two witnesses, a salt-and-ash card, and *fault* — a word both Ivo and Mara now hold — said by Mara at 21:41 when the hall door, its strike plate riding again since Wednesday, swings four inches on the draught from the propped service door. It returns a gate in rain, a tired man reading a headcount aloud, a rope texture hauling across his palm, and **ninety-four**, which he says out loud in a hall that is not empty. Perry Loach, a night cleaner in his early sixties and a Sillick Mill fitter, answers the number without being asked in a voice he has not used in thirty-one years, and stops as though interrupted. Elias is not the only person the object can reach, and there is no line on his card for that. **The debrief now includes the form's occupancy line, which Mara completes truthfully as *one cleaner*, and the box beneath it headed *account of any person present during result*, which she correctly leaves blank** — it takes a voluntary statement in the man's own words, and she will not take one off a night cleaner at a plinth without his supervisor, and she will not put a guard's second-hand version in a box that wants the man's words, because the day she records what a person said to a guard in a guard's handwriting she has stopped keeping a record and started keeping a rumour. Elias has the name in ninety seconds at a service desk and does not supply it, because a name on that sheet starts a record. **The volume's central escalation now reaches a document, and the document cannot represent it, and the box will stay blank unless somebody goes and asks the man.** **Thirty-one hours after he wrote a page in two hands on the corridor floor, and on his way down to the service desk to ask Priest who the cleaner is, he catches himself holding the two facts together as one fact and understands that they are not one fact, and writes nothing down about it.** Then the batch's worst thing happens, and it happens inside the protocol rather than against it: Mara rules that her log **does not take a bare figure** — if she cannot say what a figure is a figure *of*, the result field gets *a number*, because a figure with an honest answer under it is a record and a figure with a guess under it is a rumour she will not sign. She writes **Result: a number** and initials it. Elias could have said *mill* and given her an object; he has a box on level B and a catalogue number and nine seconds on a terminal, and he does not know that it is this mill. **Four seconds pass and he lets the procedure do it for him, because a fact in a file with her name on it is a fact he cannot be held to, and a fact in a coat pocket is a fact he can be held to by nobody at all.** He puts his hand in to the notebook and hands her an empty one instead. Both of her Chapter 9 conditions are still technically unbroken, and that is the difficulty. At 00:20, lawfully on the floor in the loading bay while a crew of two stages eleven damp boxes off level B, he reads a hand-written label on a dust-sheeted box: *Sillick Mill: administrative papers / 1961 LW 44 / **PF 118*** — a live personnel file reference, not the porter's, on a box somebody moved out of bay nine in the last twenty-four hours. He ends on a situation, with no foreknowledge of his own suspension: a guard who cannot get to Monday's re-inspection, and somebody else in the building searching for the same man.

## Chapter 0011 — What It Was Kept From

Sun 07:00 – 21:00. Dael does the suspension in person at the service lane, properly, and the restriction is a printed sheet with the truth split in two: **withheld**, night duty and all rota allocation; **retained**, building keys, current card, the service lane and loading-bay desk, the security room, ground floor, staff facilities, and *attendance at any re-inspection of an object to which the holder has prior recorded access as escort, ground floor only, and not in the room*. Barred: the Hall of Former Faiths, the Gallery of Unnamed Hands, the conservation floor, the Quiet Wing. Review on the fourth Monday, no appeal before it, and in the payroll system the restriction is recorded as *scheduled leave in lieu of notice* — unpaid, and un-dismissible. Dael says out loud, once, that he did not get Elias, he got the systems, the systems are on his side and always will be, and that he would write the same four lines about a loading-bay shutter. The week is a man standing very close to a building he cannot walk into. He spends the day outside it, learns from the noticeboard that he is in the **second column** of Monday's re-inspection sheet, goes to the laundrette and does not open the letter on the shelf above the boiler — and says the sentence out loud once so that it is a thing that has been said, and promises to ask properly **after** he has told Mara he lied to her, and *in that order*, because "you'll be in the right order." He reads the Museum's plate on the portico for the first time in two years. No sound all day; the silence is the change in the world. **Ivo arrives at 14:40 with a toolbag, having run a systems inventory nobody asked him to run**, and says the insurance schedule is the document that matters, and Elias does not ask him for anything.

## Chapter 0012 — The Gallery That Is Not Keeping Its Own Count

Mon 07:00 – 23:00, sixteen hours on an unpaid day. Among two hundred and ten dead letters, the Museum's own letter of **9 October 1976** to *Mrs D. Sarn*, returned, forwarded twice, pencilled *Deceased — family*, with a fourth-floor forwarding address — which is how Elias gets a door to knock on. He copies it out by hand because the printed line over the tray says no photography of collections material, labels or records, and he does not want a new way of breaking the same rule in the same week. **The 09:00 re-inspection happens without him and he gets it secondhand.** Mara reads out the Registry's *QA/INSP/4* serial **QA/4/1187**: *Schedule 12 — carried object, no activity observed in the period of inspection*, and the note that a sound reported by Museum staff before the date of the inspection **was not observed**, and the assessor makes no finding as to the prior report. The case is **re-anchored**: new isolation pads, a lead to the building earth, and two brass floor plates stamped **QA/A/2** — the only brass on the floor of thirty-one plinths, whose entire function is to make the object *located*. **The card is photographed** through the glass with a scale rule, four exposures, on a Registry body camera, described as *hand-cut, ink, recent, second line erased, partial, number is not in the form of a Museum reference, not for reproduction* — so the erasure is now documented twice and the name is in nobody's file. The object is re-entered on the Museum's schedule as **unaccessioned, carried, on deposit**, with a depositor's agreement in which clause nine forbids removal without the depositor's consent and the depositor's address is the Registrar's office, so the Museum can no longer prove it owns it — **and the schedule now carries a `duly exported` flag set on 12 October on an object four floors up**, which is the single most effective thing done to that building in a hundred and forty years. Ivo rings: the **schedule of collections has been empty since 11 September** because the Registry has it. The bell is caged in a corridor off the west lavatories on a docket reading `PLACED BY TRANSFER: 20:00, Sat 16 Oct` — **signed, dated in the past, and left unmoved**, because a person who moved it would have had to destroy the form. The Chapter 10 headache breaks at 21:50. **The second reading:** **22:10–22:25** — in at 22:00, four minutes in the room at 22:04 — in the Gallery of Unnamed Hands, a room he is barred from, on a fitter's lamp, **1961/LW/51**, whose brass record strip carries a ballpoint margin entry — *Purchased at the closure of the works, 30 Oct 1974 — D. SARN*. The bound question is *who carried this lamp out of the last building it was in*, and the answer is a kitchen: a scrubbed table, a hard hand, a sheet of ruled paper, a figure entered twice, a woman saying *you'll have to ask the clerk, love*, and *put it in the post, second post, it's got to be the second post* — and **a man at a metre and from the side with no face on him at all**, because a lamp on a table cannot see a face. The last act is a sheet folded in three under a cloth, and a sentence the lamp kept for thirty-one years: **"That'll do, then."** Then the confirmation, from the room's own open drawer 6B: a **1974 carbon receipt** listing eleven items collected on the last working day by *P. LOACH (fitter)* off Sillick Lane, item 9 the lamp, item 10 *bundle of wage sheets, tied with tape, marked pay office*, received from **Quill & Frayle, 14 Lincoln's Lane** — in fifty years, never opened. Elias does not photograph it and knows exactly why: a thing in a Museum file on a night, in a suspended man's hand, is not evidence. **The count in the room: nine objects, forty-one drawers.** *The room is not keeping its own count* — the batch's Index line, unaccounted for, written nowhere. Stage 1 is confirmed here and it is not a triumph.

## Chapter 0013 — The Fifth Card

Tue 08:20 – 21:30. The eight photographs have nowhere to go, and the batch's first real work is a negotiation: he rules out Mara (handing over would make the broken promise *look* paid), Ivo (not allowed, and Ivo has a presentation and a name of his own on a fault report), the police (there is no crime he can name in a sentence), and then a fourth option he writes down and does not cross out: *nobody*. He asks, finally, and Ivo's inventory minute is the answer: the **schedule of collections is empty since 11 September** because the Registry has it, so *the institution in this city with a complete itemised list of what this building holds is not the Museum* — and the fire compartmentation note has been improved by the assessor, so containment is better and the claim is worse. Fenn. In the label room, at 16:10, with a junior guard outside the door who signed the book. The Sillick drawer has been closed and squared since two o'clock in the afternoon: **somebody read his crooked-card signal and answered it.** She tells him the card is the **fifth** sleeve from the front, not the third, because eleven sleeves and two of them are Wednesday's blanks and four behind are the Gallery's re-card — and she does it to stop him building a tidy false story. She refuses twice, correctly: she will not name the person who cut the card, *because a name on a card is a thing that follows a person*, and she will not say she let him in, because she did not, and she quotes her own exact sentence back to him: *the Low Ward series are two drawers down on the left, and don't take mine.* **The first time anybody says no to him and means it.** He writes a 400-word statement in which he breaks himself first — the two device-book entries, the two rooms, the displaced card, the signed false cause — and seals four photographs in a Museum envelope addressed in block capitals **to the Board Secretary, not to the conservation floor, not to the Registrar**, by registered post with a receipt, and has the day book entered and initialled at 18:24 by Priest. Dael finds him at 20:45, tells him the claims manager is **Cheryl Vosper at Vail & Thwaite, who is not a lawyer and has a drawer**, and that *leave in lieu of notice* means he cannot be sacked and cannot be paid. **Then he posts a second envelope.** The claims address is printed at the foot of the fault report form he falsified, in a box in the post room marked *CLAIMS — POST ROOM COPY*, and the address of the one person in the world who cannot be reached through this building has been in eleven-point type in front of him for two years. Four photographs, a second typed copy of the four hundred words, registered at the counter on Sillick Lane at about half past nine with a receipt required, and Mara Iles comes down to the service entrance with the envelope and writes nothing down. He wanted two copies and got a fact instead. Index: *A copy has been given to a person who was not named in it.* Close on the recognition that nobody in the building is protecting the thing — everyone is protecting their own floor — and that Ivo did it anyway and was never asked.

## Chapter 0014 — The Woman Who Kept a Copy

Wed 09:00 – 22:00. `12 Ferry Bank`, second floor, first door. Doreen Sarn, 78, Sillick Mill pay clerk, a cardigan over a pinafore, a net curtain and a plastic tumbler on the sill because the china is in the press. Three documents on a cloth: the 1976 letter, his copy of the Gallery's nine-entry inventory, and the 1974 receipt line by line out loud. On the seventh entry her finger stops for slightly longer than on the others and comes on, and he does not know why for four days. The lamp on her table is **the mate** of the one in the Museum, and she bought hers off the forecourt on the last morning for **two shillings** and has had it in the press for **fifty years**. She indicts herself before he can: *"I was good at it. That was the whole of the trouble. I could make a column add up out of nothing, and when the foreman gave me a figure I asked him one question and he gave me another, and I wrote the one he asked me for twice, and the sheet balanced."* And the thing that opens the trail: **the shortfall sheet went out in the second post on 22 October 1974, in her own writing, with a proper stamp** — and the mill's post came back to the mill when the mill closed, and the office cleared it into a sack, and **the sack went in the van** — so the 1974 receipt's *envelope of loose papers, sealed, contents not examined* is very probably her own envelope, and has never been opened in fifty years. He does not tell her what the lamp saw, and she notices that he has *the lamp saw* in his mouth and lets him off. He gives her the record-address fact: the Museum's letting agent was the postal address for grade-two porters from 1998 for a decade, and a letting agent is a post office in a building. **Quill & Frayle ring the door at 16:10 and are let in at 16:30, because Doreen telephoned them herself to be introduced rather than found out.** Mr Rhys is reasonable, documented and immovable: the site clears on **4 November**; the 1976 clearance schedule exists and he cannot hand it over; the file number on her 1976 letter is the same one in her folder, and their office has been at 14 Lincoln's Lane for fifty years; and a tribunal, not a front room, is what a document schedule is for. Close: her fifteen-word reconciliation with her own guilt, and then the Monday bag on the loading-bay desk — his registered envelope going out of the door, and eleven years of a dead man's post going out of the same door in the same bag, to a paper merchant, to a pulp mill, because the line at the head of the book says *post is received, not opened, not kept* and he has been signing next to it for two years.

## Chapter 0015 — A Lift and Not a Removal

Thu 09:00 – 20:00. **Anja's Director's standing conditions**, four of them, issued at 10:00: docket and countersignature; **no removal except on a docket countersigned by a person who is not an officer or employee of this Museum**; no public access between a proposed disposition and its execution, with a ten-working-day lapse; and **the Hall of Former Faiths shall not be opened to a school group while a carried object in it is caged, moved, or out of its last certified condition.** Anja says plainly that she has spent nothing on him in four days and that this is not generosity — he is the cheapest thing in the building to leave alone — and that she has been instructed by two solicitors to do the thing she cannot tell him about. **The Registry notice QA/N/34** in four lines: works at sub-level B completed; third ward-bell out of service, **will not return this quarter**; *district lock positions: unchanged*; Museum action: none. ## Chapter 0016 — What Is in the Cupboard

Fri 07:15 – 20:00. **The second breach is made here.** First, the bin: it went at 07:15 and a man with a hand truck wrote a figure in a box and left. Then the library. Hattie has a file with him in it — a stranger came in three weeks ago with a photograph of a cat and said a name out loud twice — and Siobhan Keogh's name is not spoken in that room. In the queue and out on the pavement she shows him, for the first time to anybody, a letter from the site's new owner offering **a review of the final shortfall at the company's cost, without admission of liability, on the basis that her correspondence ceases for twelve months** — a considered number, a week of nothing, and the only reason it is worth anything is that it has never been priced. She has not answered it and told nobody. He says: **nobody in the world knows, and it stays that way, and I am not going to tell anybody, and if anybody asks me whether anyone has had an offer, the answer is that I have never heard of it.** She makes him say it again and look at her while he says it. Then she asks where it is, and he tells her. **The Hall of Former Faiths, ground floor, the case at the end of the row on the east side, under a brass surround, a bronze bell the size of a fist with a staple through a crack in the shoulder, cast 1902, in a cage by the lavatories with the Director's initials on it** — eleven seconds, in a street, in front of a glass door, with eleven people behind it. He had four seconds in which to say *I can't tell you that*, and he did not, and the reason he did not is a table in Ferry Bank with a hardback cash book on it and a woman who has had the only copy in the world for fifty years and nobody to give it to: **the private channel keeps closing because he keeps closing it.** He refuses to say a number, and refuses to say a name, and Hattie says the name for him — *there's a Case at number four* — and will not say it either, and tells him it is not caution, it is the price she pays for being the person in the room who knows. Then, at one, the room empties, and the man by the door is still there: a man in his fifties in a plain dark coat with a shopping bag and a tin of tea in it and a hearing aid he does not turn on and a piece of paper with nothing on it. **Elias does not go and ask him.** He stands three feet away for ten seconds and walks out, because the man has not asked him anything, and because a man who says a number at a plinth at ten on a Saturday night has not consented to be asked, and because asking needs a form and a supervisor and the man's own hand, and because he watched a woman with a stopwatch decline to fill in exactly that box for exactly those reasons. Hattie watches him walk past and says nothing, **which is the most damning thing anybody does to him all week.** Close on the arithmetic: four locks, every one of them turned by him.

## Chapter 0018 — The Two-Hands Thing

Sun 10:00 – 22:00. The emotional floor. He tells his mother on a bench that he lied to Mara's face on Saturday night, and she says she is going to be very good about it, that he will come out feeling better and will not have been forgiven, and those are three separate things and he has only booked one — and that when Mara asks why, he will say it was to protect somebody, and that is true, and it is the worst answer in the world. **Hattie Bramm, at number 14 on Sillick Lane**, next door to a man in hospital: *you've killed it* — not the letter, the *offer*, because a hoist in a photograph is the last thing that district will ever hear the word *review* after, and it was only hers while it was unpriced. She tells him the reason he cannot stop Sowerby going up a slip is that he does not know what is at the bottom of it. She keeps the polaroid, in a house on Sillick Lane, with a man of seventy-four in a bed; **not in your building, not with me**; and *the day the two of us want the same thing we'll find each other without a lawyer between us.* **Doreen Sarn, Ferry Bank, half past one, with the fire lit since nine.** He tells her the whole of the reading, and at the end: *did it show my writing? — Then it's a document. Because a document is a thing a person wrote, and the hand's on it, and you can't get that out of a lamp.* And: *you'd better not be the only one who can do it. Because if you're the only one, then by Christmas there's a hundred people in this city who need you to do it… and there'll be nobody to say the word.* He said the word on Monday in an empty room and there was nobody there. The *Chronicle*'s Sunday picture is a lorry on a slipway; cut off at the bottom of the column, much smaller, a **weighbridge gate** in 1998 and a caption, and *Hallowgate Foyston said the site had no significant features of architectural interest* — a gate photographed, on the day the last of the paper came off the site. **The label room, half past four, escorted by the registrar herself, who signs the book at the service entrance in the escort column and does not make a joke about it, which alarms him more than anything.** She reads the Saturday protocol back to him exactly as filed — *Result: a number. Occupancy line: one cleaner. Account of any person present during result: [blank]* — and says it is accurate, is in the file for ever, and there is nothing wrong with it anywhere. She reads the car-park record of the sealed envelope and the fact that she has read the day book every day since the tenth. Her covering note of 18 October, 15:40, to the Board Secretary and the audit provider: the returned letter is not to be opened or circulated; a written instruction is requested for locker 81; *a name should be entered in the account box, withheld in this note, because the file is a document and a name in a document is a thing that follows a person* — and the name is Wendel Case, given in a covering note, on a Saturday, in a room with a table, to a member of the Board and nowhere else. **He says the sentence: he lied to her face, twice over, and he did it because a fact in one pocket is a fact nobody can take off you.** *I am not going to forgive you.* Two conditions: nothing in a coat, and **nobody in this building decides on their own that a person is safer not knowing** — and at the stair door: *we are not us. There is a protocol and there are two conditions and one of them is a relationship, and the relationship is that there is no more private channel.* And the two things she will not do: ring Hattie Bramm, and let him write a word of the form that has to be built for Perry Loach — *I can. Not you, and not in a week, and not at ten at night on a Monday.* And a standing appointment: **Monday, half past two, notebook against file** — and a figure in his notebook that is not in her file, and he has to decide what to say.

## Chapter 0020 — Thirty-Eight Children and Two Teachers

SK/BK/0912.** Dael's move: the *if a public event is scheduled, the disposition is deferred until the event has taken place* clause, which he wrote himself, and a school group booked in September. **Annexe D is the best document this building has produced in my lifetime and it will hold that bell in that case for ever, and it will do nothing whatever for sixty-one people, because a classification is not a wage.** *I am going to be the man who wakes thirty-eight children up on a Friday morning in November, and I have never done anything else in eleven years that frightened me as much, and I am doing it, and I am not doing it for you.* Mara at 16:10 writes the registrar's re-certification of the Hall — **one sentence, Annexe C: *No undue deterioration has occurred since the last certification* — *a lie of omission of a size I have signed about four hundred times*** — and then refuses two things out loud: she will not ring Hattie Bramm, because a member of the public who telephones becomes a person in a Museum file, and because *asked* is the word a claim has been built on for thirty-one years; and she will not let him ask Hattie either, because a favour is what she has been fighting for nine years. Close: the last post, a pencil line in the day book, and then the booking book copied into his notebook and read aloud to Mara at 19:06 — **thirty-eight children and two teachers, booked on the first of September by a school, in ink, in a ledger that sits on a security desk eleven feet from where they were talking** — and the last line, with **ninety-two cut out**: in three weeks he is going to stand in a corridor outside a room and listen to a woman in overalls say a number, and after that he has to find out whether the first number was ever the only one, and whether there is a second one at all, **and he has absolutely no way of finding out.**

## Chapter 0023 — The List
