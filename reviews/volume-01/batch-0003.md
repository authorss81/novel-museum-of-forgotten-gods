## The decision — item 96, the box in two places at once

`ch-0024` had the box `1961/LW/44` going out on the lorry. Five other files had it in a cage in
Low Ward. The reviewer offered two repairs; both break something load-bearing.

**Six sources were checked before choosing:**

| Source | Says |
| --- | --- |
| pinned day map, `continuity.md` | *the wrong item goes: 1961/LW/51* |
| `continuity.md` flag one | the lamp left, the box stayed |
| `ch-0025` | box in cage 2 on 26 Oct, *not opened and not moved* |
| `ch-0028`, `ch-0029` | box in cage 2 |
| `open-threads.md`, `batch-0004/PROMPT.md` | box in bay 9 from Fri 1 Nov, opened 27 Nov |

**The prose was the outlier, and the state files were written from the prose, which is why the
error looked like canon from the outside.** Neither repair option was available: the crate cannot
come back on Monday either, because the bonded store's own line says the quay lot breaks bulk on
the first working day of next week, and Chapter 25 has the box behind the mesh on the evening of
the 26th.

**The fix inverts the chapter's mechanism into what it should always have been.** The crate goes
*up* the tailgate ramp and comes back *down*. The docket's third block — countersignature required,
a person who is not an officer or employee of this Museum — is empty and always was, and the only
block on the form that is not the clerk's is **PRESENT AT REMOVAL: name, grade, time, signature**.
Elias writes five sentences into the signature space, the last of which is *that no removal took
place*. Nunn turns the crate round, strikes his line, writes *did not travel* and initials it. The
vitrine goes up in its place on a line carrying the docket's destination, because a clerk does not
improve on a document.

This satisfies the prompt's own card — *44 on the docket, 51 on the load, and the one that leaves
the building is not the one on the paper* — and satisfies *not moved*, because it never went. The
new beat is the best thing in the chapter and it is the volume's argument in one form: **a docket
that cannot be countersigned is not a removal of anything, and the thing that turns a lorry round
is a sentence in the only box on the form that belongs to a man.**

Cascade fixed in the same pass: the memorandum's *the item on the docket is in the vehicle*; the
four lines; Nunn's reason for being in the reading room at 00:52; and both custody-disc sentences
in `ch-0028`.

## Two findings closed by deciding they were not defects

**Line 3 of the four lines** — *line 4 is a paper item in bond, small* — is not an error to correct.
Line 4 is a sealed plinth object and the clerk has classified it as paper because the docket in
front of him says *one crate of papers*. **That misreading is the mechanism that shelved a
Schedule 12 object in a bonded cage.** It is now annotated in the four-lines entry as deliberate,
so that a fourth pass does not "fix" it.

**The dead-letter count.** 210 is the end of Batch 0002, not a competing figure for 26 October.
The full rising series is now written into `continuity.md` so nobody has to infer it.

## Two findings left alone on purpose

The **"sixth of October"** anchor and the **manifest that does not match the real 2024 calendar** —
both already recorded in `continuity.md` as known, deliberate-for-now, and not to be re-derived.
7 November 2024 is a Saturday in the real world and the manuscript agrees; that is the one anchor
everything else follows from. This is a book about records that are internally perfect and
externally wrong, and the one record that is wrong is its own calendar.
