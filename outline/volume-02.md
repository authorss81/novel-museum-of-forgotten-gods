## Central Pressure

Small household and neighbourhood gods are answering a mass redevelopment that has displaced Low Ward residents. *(Series outline.)*

**As Volume 02 has actually turned out, the pressure is one layer underneath that and it is the layer the series outline's own reversal depends on: a name field in a supply chain.** A row of flats in Sillick Lane is losing the names against about four hundred and twelve addresses, because a delivery-system reason code is cleared overnight and applied downstream without local verification. Nobody orders it, nobody benefits visibly, and every system that reads the field is behaving correctly. **The redevelopment is the reason there is a paper to lose; the name field is the mechanism.** Both are true and the volume needs both, and the mechanism is the one that can be documented on a sheet of A4.

## Major Factions

No faction is a villain and none is a network. **There is a rounds sheet, a keys system, a landlord, a council, a school, a museum and a door.** The district, the delivery depot, the Registry, the Board's sub-group (inquiry), the school's own governing documents, and the residents of one street who have started something themselves and have not asked anybody's permission.

## The Object

A **coir door mat**, 700 × 400 mm, wear on the side that faces *into* the house, because the person standing on it is the person being let in. It is not accessioned, it is on a plinth with a rope in front of it, and **it is the first public card in about a hundred and forty years in this building to carry anything in the position where a name goes** — a line struck through and four words written under it in a label-room finisher's own hand: ***no name available***.

**Its rhyme is canonical and predates it:** `1961/LW/19`, a mill threshold stone, one hollow, foot-shaped, right of centre, open plinth, rope line, and no name in the maker's line because the label room will not put a name on a card it cannot support. **Nobody has yet put the two in the same sentence, and Volume 02's live question is whether the pattern is the object or the place. Movement 1 does not answer it and must not.**

## Escalation Sequence

**The rule for the whole volume, unchanged from Volume 01: each movement raises the number of people who can be hurt, not the size of anything. Nothing escalates into a bigger monster. The only instrument that has ever caught one in this manuscript is a record with a name against it.**

### Movement 1 — The Threshold (Chapters 51–10 → 51–60) — DRAFTED, REPAIRED 2026-09-26

Tuesday 15 December to Monday 11 January 2025. A knock at number 88 and a man who cannot produce one document with his own name on it. A rounds sheet with a field for a name and about nine of them empty in a fortnight, and **the depot's position that the sheet is right**, stated at least once. A library back room on a Friday, and a hundred and forty names in a drawer that is not produced. A household object in a public hall with no card, because a form is a description and a description is the only thing he has left. Three days of looking for an instrument that does not exist, and a Director who cannot sign, indemnify or undertake. **A lawful door that turns out to be a line in a visitors' book**, and a night cleaner on a services contract, because there is no column in the contract return in which a person can be written — and **he says no to Elias Venn and then asks for a different question.** The Museum's own records losing the same rows at the same rate, and a systems technician running a comparison nobody has ever run. A shrine in a school, a form that is not the Museum's, and the standing bar of 18 November read aloud in a corridor for the first time. A door mat closing a gallery for ten working days, a Registry assessor who refuses and says *I am not sorry*, and a plinth card with three false claims and a body that does not exist **at the bottom of it, under a workers' panel, where nobody's eye goes.** A threshold practice with three rules and no form under it, and the eighty-one-year-old woman who tries it first and pays for it. And, at the end, a fact on a document: **the same date in two systems, in two buildings, four miles apart, with a museum downstream of both that has never been told.**

**Turn at the end of Movement 1, and it is a question and not an event:** a name field is for the bill. The form says so, on page one, since about 2011, and nobody in this business has ever read it. The question is not the one the form answers. He is asking *what it is for*, and nobody knows, because it was built for a bill and then about forty other things got hung off it.

### Movement 2 — The Sentence, and the Room (Chapters 61–70) — CARDED ONLY IN THE BATCH PROMPT, NOT YET DRAFTED

Wednesday 13 January 2025. **Clause 7 of the district supply agreement closes the same morning the Hall of Former Faiths reopens**, and Ivo Pell has to write a sentence about a person to a records officer he has never met, and Mrs Toft telephones him and asks why a museum wants to know what a field said on a Wednesday in December. **The first lawful door in the case that Elias Venn cannot walk through, because he is not the subscriber and cannot be.** The comparison is run on purpose and it is legal and the result is a document in an inquiry file, **the first thing in this business anybody has obtained on purpose.** The Museum says out loud, in public, that it has been printing somebody else's billing contact on a card about a bell since 1902. The pattern is made **portable**, which is the dangerous thing, and somebody competent inside the Museum notices and says so. And the households with no name in any system at all, which cannot be listed because nobody kept a list, stated once, plainly, and **not by Elias Venn**.

### Movement 3 — Chapters 71–80, containing the volume midpoint at about Chapter 75 — OUTLINE-LEVEL ONLY, NOT CARDED

The series outline's reversal, which the whole volume has been walking toward: **the apparent curse is a coordinated pattern of forced evictions, and what some residents are losing is not only their homes but the ability to be recognised as legal neighbours.** This lands as a reversal of a *reading*, not of a monster — in Volume 01 the same move took ninety-four from a god and found a payroll; here it takes a curse off a street and finds a redevelopment, a landlord, and a supply chain four miles long that nobody owns end to end. **The forced-eviction reading must be earned out of documents and rounds sheets and a landlord's own returns, and it must implicate institutions this volume has been careful not to vilify.** The second half of the reversal: the pattern is real and it is not the whole story, because the street also contains a man who is owed nothing and is not on any list of people who are owed something.

### Movement 4 — Chapters 81–100, containing the volume climax and resolution — OUTLINE-LEVEL ONLY, NOT CARDED

The series outline's climax: **Elias and Mara organise residents to claim a threshold god through a shared threshold practice rather than a museum ritual; the god accepts only a temporary boundary, and the neighbourhood is not restored — its residents choose to remain together in a new location.** That last clause is load-bearing and is the moral floor of the volume: the win is a covenant and public records, not a return. Resolution: **the Museum's public label is exposed as a false name, and a member of the Lantern Society is identified as a buyer.** Power change: **Echo to Thread** — he learns that memory can be planted and that evidence must be checked by more than one person. The ending image must be a room, a boundary and a record, in the order the volume taught, and it must not be a door opening.

## Midpoint Reversal (about Chapter 75, NOT YET CARDED)

The apparent curse is a coordinated pattern of forced evictions, and what is really being taken from some households is not a home but **the ability to be recognised as a legal neighbour** — a name in a field, an address on a return, a knock that gets an answer. This changes the volume's shape because:

- The correct response is a covenant, a records correction and a public register, not a ritual with a climax.
- The Museum is the **last** institution in the chain, not the first, and any attempt to lead with it will be both wrong and self-serving.
- A god may accept a **temporary boundary** and refuse to restore anything, and the volume must let the residents choose the money, the silence or the fight.
- The people with no name in any system cannot be enumerated, and **a volume about a name field must say that out loud once, in plain words, and not argue about it.**

## Volume Climax (Chapters 81–100, NOT YET CARDED)

A shared threshold practice conducted by residents, in a public room, with a written boundary that has an end date on it. The god accepts the boundary and nothing else. The neighbourhood is not restored and the residents choose to stay together in a new location. **Nobody is granted a religion and nobody is owed a miracle.** The Museum's own false name is exposed in public, from the bottom of a card, in front of witnesses, with the requester's panel still taped over it.

## Concrete Resolution

Promises closed, not deferred: the covenant exists and is public; the records are corrected at source and the correction is versioned; the Museum's label is corrected and the correction is dated; the false name's author is not named unless it is named on paper, in a file, in a room, with a name against it; **and a name field's purpose is answered on the page — it is for the bill, and the question of what it is for is answered in the same breath and by somebody who is not Elias Venn.**
