## The batch as planned, in one paragraph

The answer arrives from a claims handler who has known it since 2016 and has said it out loud to about four people and can do one thing about it, which is nothing: a date of loss is for the excess. A salvage contractor's docket book turns out to be nine hundred and six lines and a weight column that pays money nobody can check, and about nine of forty-nine dockets are a long way above the yard's own ticket. A household on a ground floor produces four words to a question nobody has ever asked it. A parish registrar of forty-one years produces a facsimile of one page on the grounds that a page is not a list and has lost a great deal by giving eleven of them. A general claims manual is found to have no drafting history and a chasing letter is answered by a null. A river engineer is asked for his conversion table in the wrong mood and it is left genuinely ambiguous. A woman of sixty-one comes down a hill and asks the first question in this case that is asked instead of answered. And the movement closes on *who may speak for a community*, asked in a room by somebody competent, and nobody answers it.
