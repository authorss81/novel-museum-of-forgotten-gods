## The batch as planned, in one paragraph

A term begins on the first of June at four flats off Low Ward and nobody in them has been given a home. A two-page claims instruction is put on the page with a box on page one that nobody in the business has read, and a competent professional says out loud that she will be proved right about it in a month's time and cannot do one thing about it. A salvage trade arrives before the water with two crews, a docket book and a weight column nobody can check. The flood comes on the night of Friday the eighteenth to Saturday the nineteenth of June and a sealed bonded store on a wharf takes water and is not opened. An access refusal is issued in writing on a numbered form with a statutory power in it and a box for a period, and the officer who signs it is right about the water. And the batch closes on a question asked in a church hall by somebody competent — *what is a date of loss for* — and nobody in the room knows, and the reader has been told twice already what the answer is going to be.

## The ten chapter cards, lifted from the batch prompt

1. **101 — The First of June.** Tuesday 1 June 2025. **The term begins and nobody is given a home.** Four flats, seven households, a weekly rent, a landlord of freehold in the ground-floor flat, and two clauses that are in force from the first hour. **Two of the four flats are still empty on the first of June and nobody is going to be moved into them by the end of the week, and the housing officer of about fifty-eight who wrote the four pages says so in a corridor and is not contradicted.** And a river notice, on a form, with a number on it, from a body with a duty. **The chapter is the day a worse answer begins, and it is the first time in three volumes that Elias Venn has been in a building where nothing is being written down.** He asked to be there and was told no, and he is there because the door is open and the door is open because it is a street of shopkeepers and one of them lets rooms by the hour.

2. **102 — The Box on Page One.** Thursday 3 to Friday 4 June. A flood claim, a claims handler of about thirty-four, and the head of a two-page instruction nobody in this business has read. **She is competent, she is not a villain, she will be proved right about the box in Chapter 111 and she will not be able to do a single thing about it, and the chapter must let the reader see that coming and must not let her say it.** And a household that has already been through this in December and is being asked to do it again in June, and does not know it is the same system.

3. **103 — Two Crews and a Docket Book.** Saturday 5 June. A salvage contractor of about forty-four, two crews, a docket book, and **a weight column that a form asks for and nobody can check.** The money is on the weight. **Nobody is a cartel, nobody is a network, nobody is a mask, and the contractor is a man who pays people on a Friday and is owed money himself and says both.** And the first refusal of the batch, which is a household saying no to having a crew in a house and being right.

4. **104 — The First Claim, Refused, Correctly.** Monday 7 to Tuesday 8 June. A claim refused, and the reason is correct, and the person it is correct for is not the person it is about. **A date of loss the household cannot produce, and a form with a box for it, and a letter that says the claim cannot be assessed without it.** The person who wanted the money is not a villain and is not a fraud and is owed it and will not get it.

5. **105 — A Reach, a Rope, and a Number.** Wednesday 9 to Thursday 10 June. **A lock house on the river, a reach board, and a length of red-ochred manila about eleven metres with a splice at four metres and knots at intervals that are not at intervals, and a canvas tag with a parish name and a year on it.** The object is described at last, in a working place, by a person who uses it. **It is not read. Nobody reads it in this batch. It is carried out of a dry store on a Wednesday in June and put somewhere dry and it is not accessioned and it is not carded and no chapter may card it, and Fenn's sentence from 25 February 2025 governs.** And the tide, a datum, and a printed table of two units, and a professional who is right that they do not agree and cannot say which is right. **The knots are not irregular and this is the chapter's one piece of real work: they are a tide-mark series, put in by a person who knew the water, and his printed table of datums is what turns a series of knots into a series of heights. That is the whole of it and it is a fact about the world and not a sentence. Nobody in this batch says *the pattern is the rope* and nobody says *the pattern is the place*, and a river engineer may say which of his two numbers he trusts and why, and must not be made to deliver a verdict in a room.** Chapter 106 takes the same man with the same rule to a wall and cannot say whose number it is.

6. **106 — The Tide.** Monday 14 to Tuesday 15 June, three days before the water. A forecast, a number, a height, and a man with a rule who says what a number on a wall means and cannot say whose number it is. **The chapter is short and the water is not in it, and the water is in Chapter 107, and the batch must not pre-empt the flood in Chapter 106.**

7. **107 — THE FLOOD.** Friday 18 to Saturday 19 June 2025, high water in the small hours of the nineteenth. **A wharf, a bonded store, a door that has not been opened since the freeholder's predecessor died, and water in a building that is full of paper.** Nobody opens the store in this chapter. **The chapter is water damage, a night crew, and about nine households on a low bank, and it is not a disaster sequence and nobody dies on the page and nothing supernatural happens, and the most frightening sentence in it is a professional saying a number out loud.**

8. **108 — The Morning After.** Sunday 20 to Tuesday 22 June. Water damage, salvage crews arriving on the Monday because a crew works a Monday, a household on the ground floor of a building in a district the case has not been in, **and a woman who went into that flat to get somebody else out and came out with nothing, and the reader is given her in this chapter and does not learn what she is for until Chapter 129.** She is not named and she is not a device and she is not asked anything about the case.

9. **109 — Schedule F, and a Power in a Box.** Wednesday 23 to Thursday 24 June. **A flood-emergency access suspension: numbered, dated, signed, with a box for a period, and a competent officer of about forty-eight who has a duty and has not seen a piece of this case's evidence and is right about the water.** The refusal is in writing and it is lawful and it is the reason nothing can be got at for a fortnight. **And the instrument is named, which is the batch's last institutional beat before the turn.**

10. **110 — What A Date Of Loss Is For.** Monday 28 June to Sunday 4 July 2025. **The batch's turn, and it is a question and not an event, exactly as Movement 1 of Volume 02 ended on a question and not an event.** *What is a name field for* was Volume 02's question and the answer was *for the bill*. **What is a date of loss for** is this batch's question and **nobody in the room knows**, and the reader has been told twice already what the answer is going to be and does not believe it. And the consequence: a household that cannot produce a date of loss is a household that cannot be paid, **and the people who most need to be paid are the ones whose whole house moved.** Ends with a document somebody obtained on purpose and a person who is owed something and has been told the truth about it.

## Divergences from `workspace/volume-03/batch-0001/PROMPT.md`, itemised
