# Volume 01 Batch 0001 Outline

**Volume:** 01 — The Night Watch
**Chapter range:** 1–10
**Movement:** 1 of 5 — "The First Bell"
**Estimated length:** 2,200–3,200 words per chapter, roughly 26,000–30,000 words total. Do not pad or split complete scenes to hit a number.

---

## Batch Goal

Establish Elias as a competent, isolated night guard inside a working institution; deliver the genre promise (an object that answers) inside the first two chapters; force him into one voluntary, costly, and **incomplete** reading; and leave him holding a number he cannot prove, a supervisor who is closing the file, and an archive box that has been moved by someone who knew exactly what he would go looking for.

This batch does **not** solve the case, does not identify the god, and does not move Elias formally into the Echo stage. It gets him to the edge of a claim he cannot yet defend.

## Starting Condition

Elias Venn, 32, works the 19:00–07:00 shift at the Morrowgate Museum of Quiet Saints. He is one guard, one partner on rotation (Ivo Pell, 25, half systems tech and half junior guard), and one supervisor (Dael Ordway, 44) who holds the incident log. The public galleries are closed; the conservation floor is lit and ventilated and empty; the Quiet Wing is card-locked. The Hall of Former Faiths holds a small bronze bell on a plinth, catalogued as an industrial exhibit, with a school group booked for the following week.

## Starting Relationships

- **Elias / Dael:** professional deference with an unspoken disagreement. Dael holds Elias's hours and his future recommendation.
- **Elias / Ivo:** easy working trust, the only person Elias talks to without editing himself.
- **Elias / Mara Iles:** first real contact in this batch. She is skeptical, precise, and unimpressed.
- **Elias / Helen Venn:** the phone call home that keeps his private want in frame without explaining it.
- **Anja Ward:** not yet on stage. She is a name on the door of a room and a signature on a form.

## Batch Midpoint (Chapters 4–5)

The case crosses from "strange noise" to **"this building has renamed a person."** In Chapter 4 Elias finds that the bell's accession card was written over an older name, and in Chapter 5 he watches a nineteen-year night porter disappear from a rota while his camera frames continue. The batch midpoint is not a supernatural reveal. It is the moment Elias understands he is inside a document problem, and that the documents are the dangerous part.

## Batch Climax (Chapter 10)

Elias holds his first bounded reading of the bell with Mara as the named witness, Ivo on the exit, and a written time limit. The bell returns a number: **ninety-four**. It costs him physically, it does not restore his judgment, and the number cannot be placed against any record he can reach tonight. The same hour, the third ward-bell at South Sluice fails outright and the sound arrives inside the Museum's own circuits, and Perry Loach — cleaning the Hall, a Sillick Mill fitter, sixty-one years old — answers the count without knowing he is doing it.

Climax outcome: a partial success that cannot be repeated, a public failure that cannot be hidden, and Elias suspended from night duty the next morning.

## Aftermath and Handoff

- Elias loses his post temporarily (Ch 10 hook and carried into Batch 0002).
- The Museum is real: a school tour, a Board, insurance, a director, and a Registry that sends notices instead of answers.
- The case is now a **wage question in disguise**, and nobody in the building has said so yet.
- The hook into Batch 0002 is physical: the archive box holding the mill's records has been moved and freshly relabelled, and the false label carries the personnel file number of a man who is not on the staff list.

## Required Character Changes

- **Elias:** from a guard who writes down what he sees to a man who has had one experience he cannot file, and who responds by building a private second record. This is the first visible appearance of his central flaw: control through secrecy. It must be *uncomfortable*, not heroic.
- **Elias / Mara:** from indifference to adversarial professional respect. She agrees to one bounded protocol in Chapter 9 under conditions he does not like. He has not told her everything, and the reader should notice.
- **Elias / Dael:** from deferred-to to managed. Dael assigns him to the service lane cameras as punishment and rewrites an incident log. Elias learns the institution can lie in its own handwriting.
- **Ivo:** promoted from comic relief to first collaborator. He builds the physical props that make a reading possible (bell cover, clamp, anchor paper, exit drill) and he is the one who says out loud that Elias is doing the thing he always does.
- **Anja Ward:** introduced in Chapter 7, calm, helpful, and one beat too precise. She is assessed and reassuring in the same sentence.

## Required Power Changes

- Elias moves from **Stage 0 — Nightwatch** to the **threshold of Stage 1 — Echo**.
- Chapter 3: first involuntary contact. Wrong, partial, and paid for with a nosebleed and a headache. He learns nothing reliable and believes he imagined the rest.
- Chapter 10: first *bounded* contact — one question, a named witness, a written exit time, a named cost, and an abandonable material anchor. The result is a number he cannot yet corroborate.
- **Rule for the writer:** the formal Nightwatch → Echo step is confirmed in Chapters 11–12, not here. Chapter 10 is provisional. Elias must not be able to do this again on demand.
- Costs shown on page: tinnitus for two days, a nosebleed that Mara sees, phantom rope-texture under his palm that lasts a week, and a first instance of being unable to order two events in his own memory correctly (a small one — which shift he was on, the night his father left — planted, not resolved).

## Required World Reveals

1. The Museum has a no-touching rule, a school-tour schedule, a Board, insurance obligations, and a public reputation it defends by paperwork.
2. The building's Index can produce one short, concrete, incomplete warning. It cannot answer questions. It can also be wrong about importance.
3. The Museum keeps a **label room** where staff prepare accession cards and false names. The false names are for objects.
4. Object states are usable as literal language and must stay in order: the Sillick bell is **stored** before the third ward-bell re-cracks, becomes **answering** from its first sound in Chapter 1, and is still **answering** in Chapter 4 — the false name on the card is why the crew keeps calling it unremarkable, not why it is quiet. Nothing in this batch is **demanding** or **hollowed**.
5. The third ward-bell at South Sluice is failing, and a bad ward-bell makes *other* objects in the building more active — which is the wrong direction, and nobody notices but Elias.
6. Elias's ability has a physical and cognitive price the first time he uses it.

## Required Physical World Details to Establish

- The Museum: five floors, public entrance on Civic Hill, service entrance on the old service lane, conservation floor with strong light and terrible ventilation, Quiet Wing with no exterior windows, label room windowless, freight elevator, and a sub-level of unprocessed storage that smells of wet cardboard.
- Night work texture: a thermos of bitter tea, a flashlight with a failing switch, a pocket notebook, camera playback on a monitor at half brightness, the tram, the walk up the hill, Helen's laundrette closing at ten.
- Low Ward: the mill site fenced and hoarded, a Hallowgate Holdings sign, Sillick Lane housing that has been tenanted out three times over.
- The bell itself: palm-sized, bronze, worn hollow inside the lip, a casting seam, no clapper damage, a repair staple, and a sound that comes from the metal rather than the strike.

## Required Index Usage

At most two Index lines across the whole batch, and only in Chapters 2 and 8. Keep them short and concrete, in the voice of a building with an old charter:

> The object is answering a question. Touch, speak, or leave.

> Two names are using the same record.

Do not use the Index for status, numbers, or advice. Never use it to answer a question Elias asked.

## Chapter 0001 — Night Round

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** Morrowgate Museum of Quiet Saints, 19:40–03:10, a wet Tuesday in autumn

**Immediate goal:** Walk the round, close the building the way it is supposed to be closed, and get to the end of the shift without being asked to explain anything.

**Resistance:** The building is a working workplace at night, not a haunted house. Doors stick, the conservation floor's extraction fans scream, the tram runs behind Civic Hill, and Elias has a genuine professional problem to solve before the routine can even start: the Hall of Former Faiths door will not latch, and he cannot log a fault he cannot explain.

**Information revealed:** Elias's competence in concrete form — he reads the door catch, finds a warped strike plate and a paint buildup, fixes it with a screwdriver and a rag, and notes the time. The Hall's layout, its plinths, its no-touching rule, and the small bronze bell on plinth 34 under a label that calls it an unremarkable industrial exhibit from a textile works. The building's staff culture: the maintenance request queue, the log, the two-person rule for the sub-level, the fact that Elias is the only guard on.

**Action or decision:** The door is fixed. Elias then does the job he is good at — clock, camera, floor stain, the smell of damp under the stairs he correctly attributes to the Low Ward water table — and calls it a night. At 02:52, the bell on plinth 34 gives one note with nobody near it. Elias does not run. He walks to it, checks the case, the plinth seal, the door, and finds everything correct, and does the correct professional thing: he writes it in the log as a building-noise observation, exactly as Dael would have it.

**Emotional change:** A small private victory, the fixed door, and then a small private unease that he immediately overwrites with competence. He thinks about his father's last letter, which he has never seen, and does not act on the thought.

**Power or resource change:** None. This is a Nightwatch chapter and must stay one. Establish his ordinary tools and his ordinary limit.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** The Museum's Hall of Former Faiths holds a bronze bell on plinth 34, catalogued as an industrial exhibit from a shuttered textile mill. The no-touching rule is absolute and public. Elias's notebook is a pocket notebook, not a department system.

**Ending type:** Discovery — the first sound, logged, explained away, and therefore made permanent.

**Next-chapter pull:** The log entry now exists in the Museum's official record. Dael will read it. And the sound Elias cannot explain is on a bell that a school group will be standing in front of in six days.

## Chapter 0002 — The Bell in the Hall

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 19:00–22:30, the next evening, with a school-group booking on the schedule

**Immediate goal:** Establish whether the bell is a mechanical fault, a building fault, or his own ears, before anyone else forms an opinion about it.

**Resistance:** Dael Ordway gets to the log before Elias does and gives him an instruction, not a question: building settling, extraction fans, the age of the west range, and if it happens again, note it and move on. The Museum is insured. A maintenance investigation means an engineer, a report, and a paragraph in a quarterly review that someone upstairs reads.

**Information revealed:** Dael's character in full: not a thug, a man with a mortgage-sized liability fear and a genuine belief that an undocumented danger is a danger he can be fired for. The Index's first line. The fact that the sound recurs twice more and that the *other* objects in the hall go quiet when it happens — a detail Elias notices and does not yet understand. The school-tour booking for Saturday, forty-one children, two teachers, and a donor's name on the confirmation.

**Action or decision:** Elias does not escalate. He does the opposite, which is his fatal habit: he documents. He logs times, ambient temperature, the state of the case seal, and the hall's occupancy, and he keeps a second copy in his own notebook, unlogged. He also does one thing that costs him — he goes up to the conservation floor to find the accession record, and meets Mara Iles for the first time in a doorway, asking her for a file she says does not exist under that number.

**Emotional change:** Elias is pleased with himself for three hours. By the end of the chapter he is frightened in a small, specific, unsatisfiable way, and he does not yet know the word for it.

**Power or resource change:** Two uncontrolled soundings witnessed and not understood. No reading. The unlogged second notebook is the first real cost of this volume and it is paid in risk, not in magic.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** Dael's instruction is a legitimate, documented risk decision. The reader must be able to sympathize with him. The Index line "The object is answering a question. Touch, speak, or leave." is spoken once, in Elias's interior, without elaboration. Saturday's school tour is scheduled and is a hard commitment for the Museum.

**Ending type:** Decision — Elias chooses to investigate alone and off the record, which is the beginning of his central flaw.

**Next-chapter pull:** The accession number is not in the catalogue. Someone has to explain a bell that is on display, insured, and unrecorded, and the only person who writes those records is the woman who just told Elias the file does not exist.

## Chapter 0003 — A Number That Does Not Add

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 22:00–00:30, conservation floor, then the Hall

**Immediate goal:** Get a competent second opinion on the bell as a physical object, without explaining what he actually wants to know.

**Resistance:** Mara Iles. She is not dismissive; she is *procedural*. She will look at the bell as material evidence and nothing else, and she makes Elias sit through a casting-seam examination while a conservation shift finishes overhead. Her resistance is the correct one, and it is also the reason he cannot shortcut this.

**Information revealed:** The object as material evidence — the seam, the wear hollowed inside the lip, a repair staple that is a later hand, a date cast into the shoulder that reads 1902, and an accession number on the plinth card that does not correspond to any acquisition file. Mara's competence: she can tell from wear alone that this bell was rung hard, for years, by someone who did not maintain it. And the first genuine contact: Elias, alone with the case for four seconds while Mara fetches a tray, hears the *count*.

**Action or decision:** It is not a vision and it is not a god. It is a man's voice, tired, at a gate in the rain, calling numbers — and the numbers are the ordinary integers of a headcount, and the voice is doing it a thousand times in one second. Elias's hands are on the case glass and he cannot remember deciding to put them there. When Mara comes back he is bleeding from one nostril and cannot produce a single fact about the memory except that there was a bell and it was rung at a gate and he has the taste of rain that is not in the building.

**Emotional change:** He is ashamed. Not of the blood — of the fact that he was reaching for it, and that for two seconds he was not choosing. He lies badly to Mara, badly enough to be noticed by someone who reads paper for a living.

**Power or resource change:** First involuntary contact. Stage 0 rules established: the echo is perspective-bound, partial, ungovernable, and physical. Cost: nosebleed, a headache that does not break for eleven hours, and a four-second hole in his night that he fills in with a plausible lie.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** The bell is cast for 1902 and does not match its catalogued date. The plinth card's accession number has no file. Mara sees the nosebleed and does not believe his explanation, and says so. Elias has now lied to the one person whose job is not to take his word.

**Ending type:** Cost — the first thing the supernatural takes from him is his credibility, not his life.

**Next-chapter pull:** If the number on the plinth is not an acquisition number, then the card was made by hand, by a person, in a room. The label room is windowless and it is the only place in the building where cards are made.

## Chapter 0004 — The Card With Two Names

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 01:00–04:00, the label room and the archive corridor

**Immediate goal:** Find the accession file that should exist for the bell, before the shift ends and he has to hand the building back as it was.

**Resistance:** The label room is a real, functioning, understaffed workshop with a card cutter, a bone folder, a wall of alphabetical drawers, and colleagues who will absolutely remember an outsider poking through them. Elias is a security guard in a room that conservation staff own. He has no authority and no cover story that holds up under a single follow-up question.

**Information revealed:** The label room's actual function, shown rather than explained: trays of prepared cards, some for objects, some with names on them that belong to no object at all. And the find: the bell's card is not old. The paper is recent, the ink is recent, and under a raking light and a bone folder there is a **previous name** written in a different, older, hand-trembling script. A person's name, not a maker's.

**Action or decision:** Elias does the competent thing and the unforgivable thing in the same gesture: he photographs the card with his phone, four times, at four angles, in a room with a camera in the corner, and then puts it back a half-millimeter out of true so that anyone who opens that drawer will know someone has been in it. He is not a thief yet. He is also not a witness, because a witness says what he saw.

**Emotional change:** The chapter ends with Elias realizing he has already crossed into the behavior he would have arrested someone for, and doing nothing about it, because the alternative is telling Mara and watching the file disappear on an index nobody can produce.

**Power or resource change:** Four photographs and a displaced card. No supernatural cost, no supernatural gain — and that is the point. The dangerous resource in this volume turns out to be paper.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** The name under the ink is **Wendel Case**, a night porter. The photographs exist on Elias's personal phone and in no official system. The card is hand-made, recent, and was not prepared in the ordinary course of accession work.

**Ending type:** Discovery, with a moral cost attached — he has broken the first rule of his own job without anyone catching him.

**Next-chapter pull:** The previous name on a bell's catalog card belongs to a person who works here. The only way to learn whether Case is a porter, a donor, or a mistake is the payroll, and the payroll is a document, and documents are kept by people who are awake at one in the morning.

## Chapter 0005 — One Frame Short

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 19:00–23:00, security room, then the Quiet Wing corridor on a card fault

**Immediate goal:** Find out whether Wendel Case is a person, a file, or a mistake — and find out without asking, because asking is a record.

**Resistance:** Ivo. Not as an obstacle but as a fact: camera playback at half brightness does not give you what you want, and the frames Elias needs to see are at the exact resolution the retention setting destroys. Ivo also has a genuine night-shift to run and a genuine problem with the Quiet Wing door reader that keeps dropping.

**Information revealed:** The horror of a routine record. The night rota says Case's shift ended. His locker is still in the basement, with a coat hook and a tea tin. His pension and union deductions are still coming out of the Museum's payroll. And in the corridor camera stills, Case is in frame at 01:12 on eleven of the last fourteen nights — with one frame short at the moment he should be fully visible, as though the recording lost a moment rather than a man.

**Action or decision:** Elias does the thing that gets people killed in horror stories and does it anyway, because he is a professional and the corridor is a workplace: he asks Ivo to kill the door reader on the Quiet Wing for eleven minutes, using Ivo's own maintenance authority, and states plainly that if they are caught, the story is card-reader fault and that he will be the one who said it. Ivo goes along, on condition that Elias is out first and does not touch anything.

**Emotional change:** Elias's fear becomes specific and personal for the first time. He is not frightened of the building. He is frightened of a specific thought: that a person can be present and unpaid and unlisted for a year and half, and that everyone who looked at that rota saw nothing, because the rota was right.

**Power or resource change:** No supernatural use. Elias gains the only kind of authority he has ever wanted — eleven minutes of a colleague's complicity — and immediately owes it back.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** Wendel Case, 58, night porter, nineteen years in the Museum's service, absent from the current staff list while his deductions continue. The corridor camera's short frame is a retention artifact and will later be established as something else entirely. Ivo's involvement in the reader fault must be traceable later, and must be something he can be disciplined for.

**Ending type:** Escalation with a moral charge — Elias has now used a colleague's authority to enter a place he had no right to be, and the reader should feel the ground move.

**Next-chapter pull:** Case is a Low Ward man with nineteen years of service, and the Museum's staff address block for that decade of night porters listed one street: Sillick Lane. The mill housing. Elias has a shift at eight. He goes to the street in the morning, on his own time, off the record, with no authority at all.

## Chapter 0006 — Sillick Lane

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 09:00–14:00, Thursday, Low Ward: the tram, the library queue, the fenced mill site

**Immediate goal:** Learn who the night porters of the Sillick Lane address block were, and whether Case had a life outside the building.

**Resistance:** The world, not a person. Low Ward is not a clue; it is a place where people have been priced out three times and are busy. Hattie Bramm, 54, running the Thursday unpaid-bills queue at the library, has no interest in bells, no interest in ghosts, and total interest in one thing: what the mill owes, and what the person in front of her is offering in exchange for her time.

**Information revealed:** The mill — Sillick Mill, weaving and dyeing, closed thirty-one years ago, on the river bend above South Sluice, site now fenced and hoarded under Hallowgate Holdings. The scale of the grievance: sixty-one people shorted on the final pay run. Hattie's nine years of letters to a liquidator's firm that answers with different addresses. And, from a woman who has heard this story before and is tired of hearing it: a list of the shortfall existed, it was not destroyed, and the mill's office papers went "to a museum that doesn't need them."

**Action or decision:** Elias has a choice between saying he is a Museum guard (which will get him the information, and get Hattie a reason to distrust everything after) and saying he is a man who was told a porter had been erased. He takes the second option, badly, because it is more useful, and he notices himself choosing usefulness over accuracy. Ivo comes along, pays for both bus fares out of his own pocket without comment, and is funny about the mill site's real-estate sign and then stops being funny when he sees the housing behind it.

**Emotional change:** Elias's private want — his father — is outranked, for the first time in the volume, by somebody else's unpaid money. That reordering is the first real growth beat in the batch and it happens in a queue, over a form.

**Power or resource change:** Zero supernatural change. Enormous resource change: Elias now has an ally who is not paid by the institution, and a case that is a wage dispute, which is a thing he knows how to hold and cannot file.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** Hattie Bramm, 54, Low Ward unpaid-wage organizer, no mystical framing, entirely practical. Hallowgate Holdings owns the mill site. The shortfall list exists and the mill's office papers are in the Museum. Elias gives his job title away on this street, which will matter when Hattie finds out.

**Ending type:** Discovery with a moral inversion — the supernatural case has a paper trail, and the paper trail is a wage claim, and a wage claim is the first thing in this book that Elias cannot solve by doing his job well.

**Next-chapter pull:** The mill's office papers are in the building Elias guards, in unprocessed storage, under a catalog number he can look up on his next shift. He goes back to work with a question, in a workplace where a question is a document, and there is one man who will read his access log before he reaches the stairs.

## Chapter 0007 — The Roster

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 19:00–21:30, the staff office, the payroll terminal, and the director's corridor

**Immediate goal:** Find the Sillick Mill records, and find out whether the night roster and the staff list can disagree for nineteen years without anyone noticing.

**Resistance:** Paperwork, and then Anja Ward. The payroll terminal is a municipal terminal that logs its searches. The storage sub-level needs two people for the lift. And when Elias gets the file index and finds nothing, the woman who comes to explain why is the director of the building.

**Information revealed:** The erasure is administrative, not violent — a person with a login removed a line, in the ordinary way, and the system did not object. Case is not in any Museum personnel file, current or archive. There is one restricted instruction, eighteen years old, that reorganized the Quiet Wing card access, and its authorizing signature is a name Elias cannot find in any list of people who have ever worked here.

**Action or decision:** Anja Ward is calm, precise, and gives Elias exactly the amount of help that lets him keep going: a log reference, a floor, and a form. She asks him, with what seems to be genuine concern and no blink at all, whether he has been sleeping. Elias decides not to lie to her, and is unsettled that this feels like a decision rather than an act of trust. He does not ask about the signature. He decides he will, and then does not, and the chapter ends on the decision not to ask.

**Emotional change:** The case acquires a shape he does not want: someone with authority arranged for a person to be unlisted, and someone with authority arranged for the removal to be *unaskable*. Elias's father moves from a private wound to a possible co-worker of whoever did it, and he feels the ground under his own want shift.

**Power or resource change:** Elias gains access he is not entitled to and a restricted document reference. The real cost is a lie of omission to a superior, which he does not yet recognize as the same behavior he has been apologizing for.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** Anja Ward is the director, former Quiet Accord archivist, and is doing nothing illegal on stage. The eighteen-year-old instruction's signature is a name that does not exist in Museum records — this is the Volume 01 introduction of Orrin Sable as a *missing name only*. Elias knows the initials before the volume ends; he does not know the name.

**Ending type:** Discovery, deliberately incomplete — the reader gets one more piece of the puzzle and one more reason to doubt the institution, and no antagonist.

**Next-chapter pull:** The log reference points to a corridor that is not on any current floor plan, and the Quiet Wing card reader has a maintenance fault scheduled for the same hour as the storage lift allows two people. Elias has a card, a fault, and eleven minutes of Ivo's goodwill left.

## Chapter 0008 — Quiet Wing, After Hours

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 01:10–02:20, the Quiet Wing corridor and an unlisted room

**Immediate goal:** Get into the corridor, look, and be out before anyone can call it a breach.

**Resistance:** The building at its most literal. The door reader drops for eleven minutes and does not come back on the first try. The corridor is cold in a way the rest of the building is not, and the room at the end is not a store — it has a chair in it, and a coat hook, and a shelf of Museum-branded lanyards with the name panels blank, and somebody has been keeping dust off the chair.

**Information revealed:** The Index's second line: "Two names are using the same record." And the horror mechanic of the volume: the second bell. A small bronze bell on a stand in that room, not in any catalogue, and it answers the hall bell when Elias is in the corridor with it. And the sound from underneath — the wrong strike on the third ward-bell — is not history. It is happening now, at South Sluice, months later, and it comes up through the floor.

**Action or decision:** Elias loses about forty minutes. When he comes back to himself he is sitting on the floor with his hand against the unlisted room's door and the door is *unlocked*, and he did not open it. He closes it, photographs the corridor, and spends the remaining time of the fault writing down what he saw in a notebook, in order, in a hand that changes twice. Then he walks out through a door that is not the one he came in by, because Ivo has already gone home and he no longer wants to be inside when the card reader comes back up.

**Emotional change:** Fear becomes specific and bodily — the first panic attack of the volume, and it happens in a corridor he is not supposed to be in, which is exactly the kind of shame that makes people keep secrets.

**Power or resource change:** Second uncontrolled contact, this time with a *second* object. He does not read either. He is a man standing in a room where things are working. The stage 0 rule is enforced hard: proximity alone creates consequence, and he cannot turn it off.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** The unlisted room exists behind the Quiet Wing corridor and contains a chair, a coat hook, blank Museum lanyards, and a second uncatalogued bronze bell. Elias does not open it in this batch. The Index line is used once. The sound from below is the third ward-bell, and it is live, not remembered.

**Ending type:** Discovery and dread, and a changed situation: the thing he wanted to prove is now a room he is afraid of, and he cannot report it without admitting the breach.

**Next-chapter pull:** Dael is waiting at the service lane with the fault report open, and the fault report is a decision about what the Museum's records say happened tonight. Elias will have to choose between correcting the record and keeping his access.

## Chapter 0009 — The Log He Filed

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 09:00–12:30, Dael's office, the workshop bench, the top of the tram line

**Immediate goal:** Keep his access, or keep his honesty. He will have to pick one tonight, and he picks before he has decided to.

**Resistance:** Dael, who is not lying to Elias and not lying to himself. The report is defensible. Card-reader fault is a real, common, expensive problem, and the version of the log that gets filed is the version that does not cost the Museum a review. Elias is the only person who could object, and objecting means explaining the corridor, the room, and why a security guard was in a restricted collection at two in the morning.

**Action or decision:** Elias signs the report as written and says nothing, and then goes to Ivo's workshop bench and builds the thing he actually needed to build: a bell cover of painted sheet and clamps, a salt-and-ash anchor card that shows a pull and gives a reader somewhere to stop, and a written protocol with five lines on it — one bounded question, a time limit, a named cost, a witness who can say the word, and an exit. He then, without noticing that he is doing it, keeps a private duplicate of every timestamp, and Ivo takes the duplicate out of his hand and gives it back to him and tells him exactly what he is doing.

**Emotional change:** Shame, and a worse thing than shame: the discovery that being right about the door was more important to him than the porter in the unlisted room. Elias does not reform. He asks Mara to meet him on the conservation floor, and she comes, and she reads his protocol, and she agrees to be the person who can stop a reading — with two conditions, one of which is that he tells her everything next time.

**Power or resource change:** The tools that make Stage 1 survivable are now physically in the story, made by hand, by a junior guard who is not a mystic. Elias's resource is now a *protocol*, which is a commitment, and Ivo has made it a shared one by putting his own labor in it.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** Dael files a false cause and Elias signs it. This is a real, logged, later-provable falsification and it must be traceable in the text. Mara agrees to be the witness on condition of full disclosure; Elias accepts the condition and has already broken it once, in Chapter 3, and told himself the nosebleed was heat. The first salt-and-ash anchor card is made in this chapter and is not a shield.

**Ending type:** Cost and a hard-won agreement — the batch's emotional floor, and the moment his flaw is named out loud by someone who loves him.

**Next-chapter pull:** The protocol needs one attempt to be worth anything, and the hall bell has sounded eleven times since Tuesday. Dael's report has scheduled a re-inspection of the bell's case for Thursday morning, the day before the school tour. Whatever Elias is going to do, he does it on Thursday night.

## Chapter 0010 — Ninety-Four

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 20:50–01:30, Thursday: the Hall of Former Faiths, then the conservation floor, then the sub-level

**Immediate goal:** Run the protocol once, on a real object, with a real witness, and find out whether the number in his head is a fact or a symptom.

**Resistance:** Everything at once, and in the right order to hurt. The third ward-bell at South Sluice fails completely at 21:14, and the failure comes up into the Museum's own circuits — the lighting drops to the emergency circuit on the west range, the card readers on the Quiet Wing re-key, and the hall bell gives one note that does not stop. Elias has to work against a building that is now actively telling the bell where to be heard. And the object will not simply answer: it gives him a gate, rain, a tired man's voice calling out integers, and a total, and then it gives him a second thing he did not ask for, a number that is *wrong in a room*, and he cannot tell whether that is the object's opinion or his own exhaustion.

**Action or decision:** Elias asks one bounded question — *how many were on the last shift* — inside the protocol: Ivo at the door with the stop word and the clamp, Mara as the named witness with the notebook and the clock, the salt-and-ash card on the plinth, eleven minutes written down in advance, and the cost named aloud as a nosebleed and a bad night, which Mara writes down because that is the part people leave out. He comes back with **ninety-four** and a headache that puts him on his hands and knees on the floor of the Hall of Former Faiths. He says the number out loud to Mara and Ivo, and it means nothing to them yet, and he cannot prove a single word of it.

**Emotional change:** Triumph that arrives as nausea and immediately curdles. Elias has what he wanted — a fact only he has — and in the same hour he watches a fifty-eight-year-old cleaner, mopping by the plinth, look up and say "ninety-four" without being asked, in a voice he has not used in thirty-one years, and then stop as though he had been interrupted. Elias understands, in a way he cannot yet prove to anyone, that he is not the only person the object can reach, and that this makes it worse rather than better.

**Power or resource change:** First bounded, corroborated-in-the-weakest-sense reading. Provisional only. Stage 1 is *not* claimed here — Elias has learned the procedure, not the power, and the batch ends with him unable to repeat it on purpose. Physical cost on page: nosebleed, severe headache, transient loss of time, and a phantom rope-texture across his palm that will not fade for a week. Tinnitus for two days after.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** The true closing count of the final Sillick shift is **ninety-four**; the paid payroll lists **ninety-two**. This discrepancy is the crime and the book's question, and it must not be explained in this batch. The reading cannot be repeated on demand. Perry Loach, 61, Museum night cleaner, fitter at Sillick Mill, one of the short-paid, is now on the record as a person the object can touch. The Museum's circuit damage from the ward-bell failure is real, reportable, and will appear in the insurance file.

**Ending type:** Cost, earned hook, and reversal in one — the batch's climax and its cliff, with the situation materially changed: the Museum has a broken circuit and a Saturday tour, Elias has a number he cannot prove, Dael has a second report to write, and downstairs, in unprocessed storage, the box marked *Sillick Mill: administrative papers* has been moved to a different shelf and given a fresh label — and the new label carries the personnel file number of a man who is not on the staff list.

**Next-chapter pull:** Someone moved the mill's records in the hours since Elias asked about them, wrote the new label by hand, and used a dead porter's file number to do it. Elias is now suspended from night duty pending an inquiry into Thursday's re-inspection, which means the next answer has to come from outside his authority.

## Writer Guardrails for This Batch

- **No more than two Index lines in ten chapters.** Both are interior, brief, and incomplete.
- **No supernatural spectacle.** Sound, weight, a locked door, a smell of rain, and a man saying a number. The horror is administrative.
- **Elias must not win by noticing.** Every time he gets something right, it should cost him a rule, a job, or a lie.
- **Dael must remain sympathizable.** A reader should be able to agree with him in Chapter 2 and be horrified for him in Chapter 9.
- **Mara is not a skeptic for the reader's benefit.** She is right in Chapters 3, 4, and 9, and the volume does not turn on her being wrong.
- **The god is never named in this batch.** The crew calls it the hall bell, and the object is stored under a false name. "The Bell of the Unpaid Shift" is a Batch 0005 payoff, when the true ledger's title is recovered.
- **No romance beyond a working boundary.** The slow burn starts with a protocol and a condition, not a moment.
- **End on a situation, not a sentence.** The final image is a relabelled archive box and a suspended guard standing in front of a building he can no longer enter.
