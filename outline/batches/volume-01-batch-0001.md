Rota and calendar locks, stated once so that no chapter has to invent one:

- **Elias Venn:** nights **Tuesday to Saturday**, 19:00–07:00. Off Sunday and Monday. The rota is printed on the board in the security room and Elias reads it at the start of Chapter 1.
- **Saturday night is not a problem, it is the one night everybody is in the building.** Night security is a five-night rota so that Saturday is always a **long** night: the cleaners come at 21:00, the conservation tech is in until 23:00, facilities are on for the ward-bell fault, and the duty manager does the freight elevator. So Chapter 10 has a full building, which is the point of it — a bounded reading has to happen somewhere with witnesses in it, and Elias has to choose a room that is busy rather than find one that is empty. Ivo is off after his Friday controller job and is **in on Saturday as a favour**, which is the first time in the batch that anybody gives Elias an unasked-for evening.
- **Ivo Pell:** systems technician on days 07:00–15:00, Tuesday to Saturday, plus **one systems night a week** — this week, **Thursday**. He is also the junior guard on rotation, so he is on the floor on Tuesday and Wednesday nights. On Friday he is rostered 19:00–00:30 to fit the door 7 controller and actually leaves at 00:35, five minutes over his own roster, which is the first thing anyone could say about his night. The rota also stacks his days: **Tuesday and Wednesday are systems days with a junior-guard night on top, and Thursday is a systems night after a systems day.** He is behind on sleep before Elias ever asks him for anything, and the chapter says so once, in his own voice, and does not labour it.
- **The school tour:** booked for **Saturday** of week one. Forty-one children, two teachers, a donor's office on the confirmation. It is **cancelled in Chapter 9** with a maintenance note and does not happen. The volume's public tour with press and forty children is a *different* event in Batch 0003 and must never be confused with this one.
- **The hall bell — six events in the batch, on six different clock times.** Elias counts *events*, not notes, and every one has a card and a clock time: **Tue 02:52** in Ch 1, the first sound, and the only one he does not run to; **Wed 20:55** in Ch 2, which he watches; **Wed 23:40** in Ch 3, while he is standing at the case; **Thu 00:05** in Ch 5, in the moment after he stops looking; **Fri ~00:25** in Ch 8, arriving through the floor at the same moment as the ward-bell's wrong strike, so the two are briefly indistinguishable; **Sat 21:07** in Ch 10, seven minutes before the ward-bell fails. Five events by the end of Chapter 9; the Saturday note and the interval after it are lines six and seven. **The interval is one line, not four.**
- **The seven notebook lines, itemised so the count is checkable and nobody adds an eighth:** 02:52, 20:55, 23:40, *no sound, Thu* (a null result Elias writes down and keeps), 00:05, ~00:25, then Saturday's 21:07 and the interval as the last two.
- **The times are not a schedule, and Chapter 5 proves it by failing.** Give the reader all six times and let them do the arithmetic themselves: there is no pattern in them that a watchkeeper could have written down, and the one time Elias was certain of a rule, the rule did not arrive. **Never say the times are regular. They are not.**
- **The second ward-bell sound:** Ch 8's wrong strike is *live*, not remembered. The River Works night crew reported the same wrong strike on the Monday re-crack, and the notice is pinned to the security-room board.

## Batch Goal

Establish Elias as a competent, isolated night guard inside a working institution; deliver the genre promise (an object that answers) inside the first two chapters; force him into one voluntary, costly, and **incomplete** reading; and leave him holding a number he cannot prove, a supervisor who is closing the file, and an archive box that has been moved by someone who knew exactly what he would go looking for.

This batch does **not** solve the case, does not identify the god, and does not move Elias formally into the Echo stage. It gets him to the edge of a claim he cannot yet defend.

## Starting Condition

Elias Venn, 32, works the 19:00–07:00 shift at the Morrowgate Museum of Quiet Saints. He is one guard, one partner on rotation (Ivo Pell, 25, half systems tech and half junior guard), and one supervisor (Dael Ordway, 44) who holds the incident log. The public galleries are closed; the conservation floor is lit and ventilated and empty; the Quiet Wing is card-locked. The Hall of Former Faiths holds a small bronze bell on a plinth, catalogued as an industrial exhibit. Since the third ward-bell re-cracked on Monday the bell has moved from **stored** to **answering**, which is why it is on Elias's round at all and why nobody else has noticed: a bell that answers at night, in a closed building, is a noise complaint and not a report.

## Starting Relationships

- **Elias / Dael:** professional deference with an unspoken disagreement. Dael holds Elias's hours and his future recommendation.
- **Elias / Ivo:** easy working trust, the only person Elias talks to without editing himself.
- **Elias / Mara Iles:** first real contact in this batch. She is skeptical, precise, and unimpressed.
- **Elias / Helen Venn:** the phone call home lands in **Chapter 9**, after Elias has signed Dael's false cause. It keeps his private want in frame without explaining it, and it is the first time he lies to her.
- **Anja Ward:** not yet on stage. She is a name on the door of a room and a signature on a form.

## Batch Midpoint (Chapters 4–5)

The case crosses from "strange noise" to **"this building has renamed a person."** In Chapter 4 Elias finds that the bell's accession card was written over an older name, and in Chapter 5 he watches a nineteen-year night porter disappear from a rota while his camera frames continue. The batch midpoint is not a supernatural reveal. It is the moment Elias understands he is inside a document problem, and that the documents are the dangerous part.

## Batch Climax (Chapter 10)

Elias holds his first bounded reading of the bell with Mara as the named witness, Ivo at the door with the stop word, and a written time limit. The bell returns a number: **ninety-four**. It costs him physically, it does not restore his judgment, and the number cannot be placed against any record he can reach tonight.

**The number does not leave the room.** When Ivo and Mara are there, Elias says only that he got a number. Mara writes *a number* in the result field of the protocol log, because that is what he told her, and the shared copy is the one that gets filed. So the withholding that Batch 0002 builds on is not a fresh secret — it is a condition Mara set in Chapter 9, broken in public view in Chapter 10, by the man who agreed to it. This is the flaw at its most expensive and it is the batch's real cost, larger than the nosebleed.

The same hour, the third ward-bell at South Sluice fails outright — an **escalation** of Monday's crack, not the crack itself — and the sound arrives inside the Museum's own circuits. Perry Loach, cleaning the Hall, a Sillick Mill fitter and one of the short-paid, answers the count without knowing he is doing it.

Climax outcome: a partial success that cannot be repeated, a public failure that cannot be hidden, and Elias suspended from night duty the next morning.

## Aftermath and Handoff

- Elias loses his post temporarily. The suspension is Sunday morning, so it lands *after* Chapter 10 closes and is the first beat of Batch 0002; Chapter 10's own last scene is the box.
- The Museum is real: a school tour that was cancelled, a Board, insurance, a director, and a Registry that sends notices instead of answers.
- The case is now a **wage question in disguise**, and nobody in the building has said so yet.
- The four figures are now on the page and separated: **94** on the shift, **92** on the payroll, **2** never entered, **61** short-paid. Batch 0002 must not produce a fifth number.
- The shared protocol log says *a number*. The real figure is in Elias's pocket notebook. That gap is the volume's engine and it is Elias's doing.
- The hook into Batch 0002 is physical: the archive box holding the mill's records has been moved and freshly relabelled, and the false label carries the personnel file number of a man who is not on the staff list.

## Required Character Changes

- **Elias:** from a guard who writes down what he sees to a man who has had one experience he cannot file, and who responds by building a private second record. This is the first visible appearance of his central flaw: control through secrecy. It must be *uncomfortable*, not heroic.
- **Elias / Mara:** from indifference to adversarial professional respect. She agrees to one bounded protocol in Chapter 9 under conditions he does not like, and he breaks the second of those conditions in Chapter 10 while standing in front of her. The reader should notice, and Mara should write down what he actually said.
- **Elias / Dael:** from deferred-to to managed. Dael assigns him to the service lane cameras as punishment and rewrites an incident log. Elias learns the institution can lie in its own handwriting.
- **Ivo:** promoted from comic relief to first collaborator. He builds the physical props that make a reading possible (bell cover, clamp, anchor paper, exit drill) and he is the one who says out loud that Elias is doing the thing he always does.
- **Anja Ward:** introduced in Chapter 7, calm, helpful, and one beat too precise. She is assessed and reassuring in the same sentence.
- **Hattie Bramm:** working presence only. She is a Sillick Mill gate clerk, on the closing shift, one of the short-paid. She is not a mystic, a seer, or an oracle, and she is not interested in Elias.

## Required Power Changes

- Elias moves from **Stage 0 — Nightwatch** to the **threshold of Stage 1 — Echo**.
- Chapter 3: first involuntary contact. Wrong, partial, and paid for with a nosebleed and a headache. He learns nothing reliable and believes he imagined the rest.
- Chapter 10: first *bounded* contact — one question, a named witness, a written exit time, a named cost, and an abandonable material anchor. The result is a number he cannot yet corroborate, and the number is not the one in the filed log.
- **Rule for the writer:** the formal Nightwatch → Echo step is confirmed in Chapters 11–12, not here. Chapter 10 is provisional. Elias must not be able to do this again on demand.
- Costs shown on page: tinnitus for two days, a nosebleed that Mara sees, phantom rope-texture under his palm that lasts a week, and a first instance of being unable to order two events correctly: **in Chapter 8 he writes two lines of a notebook entry and cannot afterwards put them in the order he wrote them.** That is the whole memory cost of the batch. Small, planted, not resolved in this volume, and not the first *major* memory-displacement cost, which is reserved for Volume 03. Do not stage the night his father left here.

## Required World Reveals

1. The Museum has a no-touching rule, a school-tour schedule, a Board, insurance obligations, and a public reputation it defends by paperwork. It also has a **device policy**: personal phones are signed into a numbered **tray** — an unlocked cupboard of numbered slots at the service entrance — and the **book** beside it is a paper log with names and times, kept at the front desk. Collections material may not be photographed. The book is the cost of Chapter 4, not the tray: a tray is evidence of nothing on its own, and that is exactly why Elias can take the phone out of it a second time.
2. The building's Index can produce one short, concrete, incomplete warning. It cannot answer questions. It can also be wrong about importance.
3. The Museum keeps a **label room** where staff prepare accession cards and false names. The false names are for objects.
4. Object states are usable as literal language and must stay in order, and the bible definition of `answering` is not to be narrowed: **an answering object repeats a small part of its last act.** The Monday re-crack of the third ward-bell is the hinge. Before it the Sillick bell is **stored** — it carries an echo and does not answer. The re-crack is what takes it into `answering`, on Monday, off-page. **The re-crack does not reach the bell. It stops covering it.** The third ward-bell is one of twelve on the district's return lock, and a ward-bell's ordinary work is to hold a district's attention down; while it is intact, a god-pattern inside that district cannot be addressed in a room where somebody is asking. When it cracks, the suppression it was maintaining goes slack, and the Sillick bell — always `stored`, carried and quiet, exactly as the false name on its card says — becomes `answering` for the rest of the volume. **The route is the absence of a lock, not a wire.** Nothing is connected to anything; something stopped being held down. The act is always the same act, the closing-day count called at a gate. What changes is its **form**. With nothing asked, the act comes out as **noise** — one note, or the same note on an interval, with no content, which is exactly what a night porter writes down as a building noise. With a question asked, inside the Chapter 10 protocol, the same act comes out as **content**: a gate, rain, a tired man's voice, integers, a total. **Tuesday at 02:52 is the first time the act happens, unasked for, and therefore the first time it is only noise** — and that is precisely why Elias can log it. It is still `answering` in Chapter 4 and still `answering` at the end of Chapter 10. The false name on the card is why the crew keeps calling it unremarkable, not why it is quiet. **Nothing in this batch is `demanding` or `hollowed`.** `Demanding` — an object trying to make a living person complete an unfinished act — is reserved for the Chapter 22–24 tour. The bell in Chapter 10 repeats the same noise on an interval and asks nothing of anyone; the pressure is that it is *correct*, which is worse.
5. The third ward-bell at South Sluice is failing, and a bad ward-bell makes *other* objects in the building more active — which is the wrong direction, and nobody notices but Elias.
6. Elias's ability has a physical and cognitive price the first time he uses it.

## Required Physical World Details to Establish

- The Museum: five floors, public entrance on Civic Hill, service entrance on the old service lane, conservation floor with strong light and terrible ventilation, Quiet Wing with no exterior windows, label room windowless, freight elevator, the ground-floor **loading bay** off the freight elevator where damp boxes are staged, and a sub-level of unprocessed storage that smells of wet cardboard.
- Night work texture: a thermos of bitter tea, a flashlight with a failing switch, a pocket notebook, camera playback on a monitor at half brightness, the tram, the walk up the hill, Helen's laundrette closing at ten, the numbered device tray and its sign-out book at the service door.
- Low Ward: the mill site fenced and hoarded, a Hallowgate Holdings sign, the **Sillick Lane branch library** with its Friday unpaid-bills queue in the back room, Sillick Lane housing that has been tenanted out three times over.
- The bell itself: palm-sized, bronze, worn hollow inside the lip, a casting seam, no clapper damage, a repair staple, and a sound that comes from the metal rather than the strike.

## Required Index Usage

At most two Index lines across the whole batch, and only in Chapters 2 and 8. Keep them short and concrete, in the voice of a building with an old charter:

> The object is answering a question. Touch, speak, or leave.

> Two names are using the same record.

Do not use the Index for status, numbers, or advice. Never use it to answer a question Elias asked.

## Chapter 0001 — Night Round

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** Morrowgate Museum of Quiet Saints, 19:40–03:10, a wet Tuesday in autumn (night one of the five)

**Immediate goal:** Walk the round, close the building the way it is supposed to be closed, and get to the end of the shift without being asked to explain anything.

**Resistance:** The building is a working workplace at night, not a haunted house. Doors stick, the conservation floor's extraction fans scream, the tram runs behind Civic Hill, and Elias has a genuine professional problem to solve before the routine can even start: the Hall of Former Faiths door will not latch, and he cannot log a fault he cannot explain.

**Information revealed:** Elias's competence in concrete form — he reads the door catch, finds a warped strike plate and a paint buildup, fixes it with a screwdriver and a rag, and notes the time. The printed guard rota on the board (Tue–Sat, 19:00–07:00; Ivo on the floor Tuesday and Wednesday; Ivo's systems night is Thursday). The Hall's layout, its plinths, its no-touching rule, and the small bronze bell on plinth 34 under a label that calls it an unremarkable industrial exhibit from a textile works. The staff culture: the maintenance request queue, the log, the two-person rule for the sub-level, the numbered device tray at the service door, and the fact that Elias is the only one out walking the floor.

**The object-state hinge belongs in this chapter, in plain sight, and nobody may misread it as a sounding that has already happened.** On the security-room notice board there is a River Works maintenance notice, dated Monday, saying the third ward-bell at South Sluice has re-cracked and is sounding a wrong note. Under it, someone has initialled *bell 3, still ringing* — meaning the ward-bell, four hundred metres and a river away. The Sillick bell on plinth 34 has been **stored** — it carries an echo and does not answer — right up until the third ward-bell re-cracked on Monday night. Since Monday it has been `answering`, and an answering bell is not a noisy one: it repeats a small part of its last act when there is a question in the room, and a closed museum at two in the morning contains no question at all. So the notice has not explained a sound. It has handed the building a ready-made explanation for the next sound, and the crew have taken it. Elias reads both and does not connect them, because there is nothing yet to connect — a nuisance from a bell in a sluice is not a bell in a hall, and a false name on a card is not a silence. The reader should be able to connect them, and should do it a good while before Elias does.

**Action or decision:** The door is fixed. Elias then does the job he is good at — clock, camera, floor stain, the smell of damp under the stairs he correctly attributes to the Low Ward water table — and calls it a night. At 02:52, the bell on plinth 34 gives one note with nobody near it. Elias does not run. He walks to it, checks the case, the plinth seal, the door, and finds everything correct, and does the correct professional thing: he writes it in the log as a building-noise observation, exactly as Dael would have it.

**Emotional change:** A small private victory, the fixed door, and then a small private unease that he immediately overwrites with competence. He thinks about his father's last letter, which he has never seen, and does not act on the thought.

**Power or resource change:** None. This is a Nightwatch chapter and must stay one. Establish his ordinary tools and his ordinary limit.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** The Museum's Hall of Former Faiths holds a bronze bell on plinth 34, catalogued as an industrial exhibit from a shuttered textile mill. The no-touching rule is absolute and public. The third ward-bell at South Sluice re-cracked on the Monday before this chapter, off-page, and is the reason the bell is now answering. Elias's notebook is a pocket notebook, not a department system. Personal phones live in the numbered tray and collections material may not be photographed.

**Ending type:** Discovery — the first sound, logged, explained away, and therefore made permanent.

**Next-chapter pull:** The log entry now exists in the Museum's official record. Dael will read it. And the sound Elias cannot explain is on a bell that forty-one schoolchildren will be standing in front of on **Saturday** — three nights and a morning from now, and every one of those nights has to be survived first.

## Chapter 0002 — The Bell in the Hall

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 19:00–22:00, Wednesday evening, with a school-group booking on the Saturday schedule

**Immediate goal:** Establish whether the bell is a mechanical fault, a building fault, or his own ears, before anyone else forms an opinion about it.

**Resistance:** Dael Ordway gets to the log before Elias does and gives him an instruction, not a question: building settling, extraction fans, the age of the west range, and if it happens again, note it and move on. The Museum is insured. A maintenance investigation means an engineer, a report, and a paragraph in a quarterly review that someone upstairs reads.

**Information revealed:** Dael's character in full: not a thug, a man with a mortgage-sized liability fear and a genuine belief that an undocumented danger is a danger he can be fired for. He also mentions, without being asked, that the ward-bell notice is the reason the board wants quiet this quarter. The Index's first line. **The sound recurs at 20:55**, nearly two hours into the shift, and Elias is standing in the hall when it happens this time and watches the case the whole time and sees nothing. The *other* objects in the hall go quiet when it sounds, a detail he notices and does not yet understand. The school-tour booking for **Saturday**: forty-one children, two teachers, and a donor's office on the confirmation.

**Action or decision:** Elias does not escalate. He does the opposite, which is his fatal habit: he documents. He logs times, ambient temperature, the state of the case seal, and the hall's occupancy, and he keeps a second copy in his own notebook, unlogged. He also does one thing that costs him — he goes up to the conservation floor to find the accession record, and meets Mara Iles for the first time in a doorway, asking her for a file she says does not exist under that number.

**Emotional change:** Elias is pleased with himself for three hours. By the end of the chapter he is frightened in a small, specific, unsatisfiable way, and he does not yet know the word for it.

**Power or resource change:** One sounding in this chapter, witnessed and not understood. No reading. The unlogged second notebook is the first real cost of the batch and it is paid in risk, not in magic.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** Dael's instruction is a legitimate, documented risk decision. The reader must be able to sympathize with him. The Index line "The object is answering a question. Touch, speak, or leave." is spoken once, in Elias's interior, without elaboration. Saturday's school tour is scheduled and is a hard commitment for the Museum. The third ward-bell re-crack is on the notice board and is the official explanation nobody has actually checked. **The second Wednesday sounding, at 23:40, belongs to Chapter 3, not to this one** — Ch 2 closes at 22:00 and its second half belongs to Mara.

**Ending type:** Decision — Elias chooses to investigate alone and off the record, which is the beginning of his central flaw.

**Next-chapter pull:** The accession number is not in the catalogue. Someone has to explain a bell that is on display, insured, and unrecorded, and the only person who writes those records is the woman who just told Elias the file does not exist.

## Chapter 0003 — A Number That Does Not Add

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 22:00–00:30, conservation floor, then the Hall

**Immediate goal:** Get a competent second opinion on the bell as a physical object, without explaining what he actually wants to know.

**Resistance:** Mara Iles. She is not dismissive; she is *procedural*. She will look at the bell as material evidence and nothing else, and she makes Elias sit through a casting-seam examination while a conservation shift finishes overhead. Her resistance is the correct one, and it is also the reason he cannot shortcut this.

**Information revealed:** The object as material evidence — the seam, the wear hollowed inside the lip, a repair staple that is a later hand, a date cast into the shoulder that reads 1902, and an accession number on the plinth card that does not correspond to any acquisition file. Mara's competence: she can tell from wear alone that this bell was rung hard, for years, by someone who did not maintain it. **At 23:40, with Mara gone to fetch a tray and Elias alone at the case, the bell gives its note for the third time — the loudest, and the only one with him standing at the glass.** It is the setup for the contact, not a separate beat: four seconds of him with his hands on the case, and the count. The third sound and the contact are the same ten seconds.

**Action or decision:** It is not a vision and it is not a god. It is a man's voice, tired, at a gate in the rain, calling numbers — and the numbers are the ordinary integers of a headcount, and the voice is doing it a thousand times in one second. Elias's hands are on the case glass and he cannot remember deciding to put them there. When Mara comes back he is bleeding from one nostril and cannot produce a single fact about the memory except that there was a bell and it was rung at a gate and he has the taste of rain that is not in the building.

**Emotional change:** He is ashamed. Not of the blood — of the fact that he was reaching for it, and that for two seconds he was not choosing. He lies badly to Mara, badly enough to be noticed by someone who reads paper for a living.

**Power or resource change:** First involuntary contact. Stage 0 rules established: the echo is perspective-bound, partial, ungovernable, and physical. Cost: nosebleed, a headache that does not break for eleven hours, and a four-second hole in his night that he fills in with a plausible lie.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** The bell is cast for 1902 and does not match its catalogued date. The plinth card's accession number has no file. Mara sees the nosebleed and does not believe his explanation, and says so. Elias has now lied to the one person whose job is not to take his word.

**Ending type:** Cost — the first thing the supernatural takes from him is his credibility, not his life.

**Next-chapter pull:** If the number on the plinth is not an acquisition number, then the card was made by hand, by a person, in a room. The label room is windowless and it is the only place in the building where cards are made.

## Chapter 0004 — The Card With Two Names

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 01:00–04:00, Thursday morning, the label room and the archive corridor — the same night as Chapters 2 and 3

**Immediate goal:** Find the accession file that should exist for the bell, before the shift ends and he has to hand the building back as it was.

**Resistance:** The label room is a real, functioning, understaffed workshop with a card cutter, a bone folder, a wall of alphabetical drawers, and colleagues who will absolutely remember an outsider poking through them. Elias is a security guard in a room that conservation staff own. He has no authority and no cover story that holds up under a single follow-up question. There is also a camera on the door lintel, and it covers the doorway and the card cutter, not the drawers — a fact he checks before he starts, because he has checked.

**The device policy is the cost of this chapter, and it is stated here for the first time.** Personal phones are signed into a numbered tray at the service entrance and signed out again; the book beside the tray has a column for name, time out, time back, and a printed line at the head that says *no photography of collections material, labels, or records*. Elias has been signing that book for two years without reading the printed line. So the beat is not a hidden photograph. It is that he writes **his own name and 01:52** in a book that the front desk keeps, walks back down to the service door, unlocks the tray, takes his phone out of a numbered slot in front of the person on the desk, and puts it back at 02:41 with his name again. There is now a piece of paper in the building that says he wanted his phone in the middle of the night, and he chose to make it say it in ink. He also does not write *where* he was taking it, which is the first half of the lie and the reason the half-truth will be discoverable in Chapter 9.

**Information revealed:** The label room's actual function, shown rather than explained: trays of prepared cards, some for objects, some with names on them that belong to no object at all. And the find: the bell's card is not old. **Power or resource change:** The tools that make Stage 1 survivable are now physically in the story, made by hand, by a junior guard who is not a mystic. Elias's resource is now a *protocol*, which is a commitment, and Ivo has made it a shared one by putting his own labor in it.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** Dael files a false cause and Elias signs it. This is a real, logged, later-provable falsification and it must be traceable in the text. Mara's two conditions are exact: *the result goes in the shared log in her hand*, and *he tells her everything, every time*. **Chapter 10 breaks the first condition in front of her.** The first salt-and-ash anchor card is made in this chapter and is not a shield. Dael's false cause is a maintenance note, not a lie told to Elias's face.

**Ending type:** Cost and a hard-won agreement — the batch's emotional floor, and the moment his flaw is named out loud by someone who works beside him and has no reason to be kind about it.

**Next-chapter pull:** The protocol needs one attempt to be worth anything, and he has exactly one night. The hall bell has sounded five times since Tuesday and he cannot find a pattern in any of them, which is why he has a notebook and no idea. Dael's maintenance note has cancelled Saturday's tour and put the formal re-inspection of plinth 34 at nine o'clock **Monday**, with a conservator and a Registry observer who will open the case without him. Whatever Elias is going to do, he does it Saturday night.

## Chapter 0010 — Ninety-Four

**POV:** Elias Venn
**Location/time:** 20:50–01:30, **Saturday** night: the Hall of Former Faiths, then the conservation floor, then the ground-floor loading bay

**Immediate goal:** Run the protocol once, on a real object, with a real witness, and find out whether the number in his head is a fact or a symptom. He has one night, because on Monday morning strangers open the case. **The reading happens at about 21:30, on the busiest night of the week**, with a cleaner on the floor, a conservation tech two doors away, facilities working the riser and the duty manager on the freight elevator. He cannot get privacy and he does not try. The cover, the clamp and the salt-and-ash card are built to make a small controlled space *inside* a busy building, and that is the entire reason Ivo spent Saturday making them: **the protocol is what lets this happen in a room with a door that anybody can open.** Nobody comes in. Mara watches the door as well as the clock, and when the hall door swings she says the word and it is over.

**Resistance:** Everything at once, and in the right order to hurt. At 21:07 the hall bell gives its note for the sixth time, and **Elias has seven minutes to decide what to do about it before anything else happens.** At 21:14 the third ward-bell at South Sluice **fails completely** — an escalation of Monday's crack, not the crack itself — and the failure comes up into the Museum's own circuits: the lighting drops to the emergency circuit on the west range, the card readers on the Quiet Wing re-key, and the hall bell begins again on a fixed interval, four times in six minutes, the same note, the same length, like a man repeating a number. **It is `answering`, not `demanding`.** It makes no request. It does not address Elias, it does not try to make him finish anything, and it does not stop him walking out of the Hall. The pressure is that it is *correct*, and that everything else in the building is now a loudspeaker for it. And the object will not simply answer: it gives him a gate, rain, a tired man's voice calling out integers, and a total, and then it stops. **There is no second figure in this chapter and no number that is wrong in a room.** Movement 2 owns that, and a second reading is where the echo turns out to repeat an owner's perspective; if Ch 10 spends it here, Batch 0002 has nothing left. What Ch 10 gives Elias instead is a total he cannot place and a headache that will not break for two days, and that is all.

**Action or decision:** Elias asks one bounded question — *how many were on the last shift* — inside the protocol: Ivo at the door with the stop word and the clamp, Mara as the named witness with the notebook and the clock, the salt-and-ash card on the plinth, eleven minutes written down in advance, and the cost named aloud as a nosebleed and a bad night, which Mara writes down because that is the part people leave out. He comes back with **ninety-four** and a headache that puts him on his hands and knees on the floor of the Hall of Former Faiths.

**Then he does the batch's worst thing, and it happens in two sentences.** He says the number once, out loud, in a hall that is not empty and has not been private for a second of it — the cleaner is a few feet away with a mop, on the other side of the cover — and there is no version of the sentence he can un-say. When Ivo and Mara are there at his side he says: *a number. I don't know what it counts.* Mara's pen is already moving, because she is a person who writes things down, and she writes **a number** in the result field of the shared log, because that is what she was told. He could correct her. He has four seconds in which to do it and he watches them go past. **The condition he agreed to in Chapter 9 — the result goes in the shared log in her hand, exactly as it comes — is broken by the man who proposed it, in front of both of them, and neither of them says anything, which is worse.**

**Emotional change:** Triumph that arrives as nausea and immediately curdles. Elias has what he wanted — a fact only he has — and in the same hour he watches a night cleaner in his early sixties, mopping by the plinth, look up and say "ninety-four" without being asked, in a voice he has not used in thirty-one years, and then stop as though he had been interrupted. Elias understands, in a way he cannot yet prove to anyone, that he is not the only person the object can reach, and that this makes it worse rather than better. And he understands the second thing, which is worse: that he has just written a false entry into a document that two people will keep, in order to keep a number to himself.

**Power or resource change:** First bounded reading, corroborated in the weakest possible sense by a man who did not know he was corroborating it. Provisional only. Stage 1 is *not* claimed here — Elias has learned the procedure, not the power, and the batch ends with him unable to repeat it on purpose. Physical cost on page: nosebleed, severe headache, transient loss of time, and a phantom rope-texture across his palm that will not fade for a week. Tinnitus for two days after.

**Continuity fact to preserve:** The true closing count of the final Sillick shift is **ninety-four**; the paid payroll lists **ninety-two**; **two** people were never entered in any book; **sixty-one** people were short-paid on the final pay run. These four figures are fixed, overlapping, and not the same event. The discrepancy is the crime and the book's question, and it must not be explained in this batch. (In the prose, Perry is *a night cleaner in his early sixties* and the numeral sixty-one does not appear in his paragraph; this card states both because it is a reference.) The reading cannot be repeated on demand. Perry Loach, 61, Museum night cleaner, fitter at Sillick Mill, one of the short-paid, is now on the record as a person the object can touch. The Museum's circuit damage from the ward-bell failure is real, reportable, and will appear in the insurance file. **The shared protocol log says *a number*.** The real figure exists only in Elias's pocket notebook, and Batch 0002 inherits that.

**Ending type:** Cost, earned hook, and reversal in one. The situation is materially changed: the Museum has a broken circuit, a cancelled Saturday tour that Dael has to explain to a donor's office, a number in a private notebook instead of a shared log, and a second report to write. **The relabelled box is found without breaking the two-person rule.** The riser failure at 21:14 wets the sub-level, and a facilities crew of two pulls damp boxes out of level B onto pallets in the **ground-floor loading bay** off the freight elevator at twenty past midnight (deliberately *not* 01:12, which is Case's frame time on the corridor camera), because that is what a night porter does with wet cardboard. Elias is lawfully on the floor, standing at the loading-bay desk with the duty manager and both members of the crew, and he does not descend and does not need to. On a pallet, wrapped in a dust sheet that has not been dust-sheeted before, is a box marked *Sillick Mill: administrative papers* — on a **fresh hand-written label**, and the label carries the personnel file number of a man who is not on the staff list. Nobody else in the loading bay is reading labels. He is.

**Next-chapter pull:** Someone moved the mill's records in the hours since Elias asked about them, wrote the new label by hand, and used a dead porter's file number to do it — and it is on a pallet in a room with a duty manager and two facilities staff in it, so the only person who has read that label is Elias and the only person who can act on it is somebody he has just been suspended. **And the label is not addressed to him.** It is addressed to the file number of a man who has not worked here for eighteen months, which means whoever wrote it was looking for Case too.

## Writer Guardrails for This Batch

- **No more than two Index lines in ten chapters.** Both are interior, brief, and incomplete.
- **No supernatural spectacle.** Sound, weight, a locked door, a smell of rain, and a man saying a number. The horror is administrative.
- **Elias must not win by noticing.** Every time he gets something right, it should cost him a rule, a job, or a lie.
- **Dael must remain sympathizable.** A reader should be able to agree with him in Chapter 2 and be horrified for him in Chapter 9.
- **Mara is not a skeptic for the reader's benefit.** She is right in Chapters 3, 4, 9, and 10, and the volume does not turn on her being wrong. **Chapter 9 has to earn her yes.** She agrees because of what the tools are *for*: a cover, a clamp, an anchor card, a written stop, and a door she can shut. She does not agree to a reading in an empty gallery at three in the morning and she says so. If Ch 10 runs on a busy long night, then Ch 9's second condition is not *tell me everything* — it is **you do this where I can shut the door, and you do not do it again until the west range is fixed.** He agrees to both, and the west range is not fixed on Saturday, and he does it anyway, which is how Batch 0002 starts.
- **The god is never named in this batch.** The crew calls it the hall bell, and the object is stored under a false name. "The Bell of the Unpaid Shift" is a Batch 0005 payoff, when the true ledger's title is recovered.
- **No romance beyond a working boundary.** The slow burn starts with a protocol and a condition, not a moment. Nobody in this batch loves anybody out loud.
- **The object stays `answering`.** It repeats a small part of its last act. It never makes a request, never addresses a person, and never prevents anyone leaving. `Demanding` is Chapters 22–24. If a draft sentence reads like an object negotiating with a living person, cut it.
- **The corridor service bell is plumbing, not a relic.** It is hard-piped to the ward-bell repeater line, it repeats, it is out of tune, and it does nothing else. The horror is the room, not the bell.
- **The maintenance record is the villain's best friend.** Everything the building knows about Friday night is true. Elias is not caught by a camera; he is caught by a work order he did not close.
- **The eleven *minutes* recur on purpose, and only twice.** Chapter 5's eleven minutes is the first time Elias buys unauthorised access with somebody else's authority; Chapter 10's eleven minutes written down in advance is the first time he bounds himself. That is the echo, and it is the only one. Do not spend *that unit* anywhere else — the Chapter 5 camera count is ten of fourteen nights, the notebook comes to seven lines, the protocol has five lines, and Chapter 3's headache runs eleven hours.
- **Two numbers that collide.** Sixty-one is both the short-paid count and Perry Loach's age. In Chapter 6 the numeral belongs to the count. In Chapter 10 render Perry as *a night cleaner in his early sixties* and keep the numeral off the page, so the two never land in the same paragraph.
- **The four counts are fixed and separate.** 94 worked, 92 paid, 2 never entered, 61 short-paid. Do not let a fifth figure appear, and do not let the batch imply that 61 is another way of saying 94.
- **The Saturday school tour does not happen in this batch.** It is cancelled in Chapter 9. The volume's public tour with press and forty children is Batch 0003's event and must arrive as an escalation, not a repeat. Chapter 10's version of Perry's utterance is private, unprompted, and immediately suppressed; Batch 0003's is public, prompted by a room, and answered by children. Do not reuse the Chapter 10 beat.
- **End on a situation, not a sentence.** The final image is a relabelled archive box on a pallet in a loading bay, and a guard who cannot get to Monday's re-inspection.
