## The batch as planned, in one paragraph

A second flood-emergency instrument is made by a woman of forty-eight who is right about the bank, and being right about the bank is what makes the next three chapters worse. A school of about three hundred and eighty children is legible as a roll and illegible as a premises, and the two competent people who hold them cannot make them talk. A registrar of twenty-eight opens the 1902 index card and the two day-book entries for the week of the casting and the finding is a null, minuted as a null in seven numbered items. A sheet with three boxes goes out and a woman puts her sister, her sister's two and a dog into the wrong one at about twenty past eleven in the morning. And at the midpoint the machine becomes visible: the district register at Northglass is not only preserving old names, it is using flood emergency powers to take people out of the register, and the reason given is arithmetic — a frontage with four hundred open claims on it is a frontage a computer will not settle a district boundary against. **And the other half of the reversal arrives one chapter later than the outline placed it: Elias Venn reads a bounded thing, believes an echo's emotional certainty, and says a living woman's name into a list of the drowned in a room in front of about forty people, and it cannot be unsaid.**
