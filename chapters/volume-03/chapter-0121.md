# Chapter 0121 — A Second Instrument, Or Not

**On Monday the ninth and Tuesday the tenth of August 2025 a woman of forty-eight with a statutory power signed a second flood-emergency instrument on a printed form in an office above a shop in Fisher Lane, having said the cost out loud first, because that is now what she does and she said it twice on the first one in June and everybody in this case has learned to wait for it. The cost is that about sixty ground floors on a low bank are not going to be assessed for a second time in ten weeks, and about four hundred metres of frontage is closed to anybody who has not first asked a statutory officer for a licence, and a household cannot ask.**

**She was right about the bank. A movement joint under her hand on the Monday had opened about four centimetres since a licensed officer put a tape to it in June, and the coping above it had a crack in it that a structural engineer looked at for nine minutes without saying anything. Being right about the bank is what makes the next three chapters worse. The clause that does the damage is not new, is not hers, and has been in the form since 2019, and it only comes into being when the form is given more than one thing to name.**

---

## And on Monday morning at about ten past nine a man of thirty-two was standing on a quay four miles downriver with nothing to do, because the first instrument stopped at twenty-three fifty-nine on the eighth of July and the building has not been opened since and is not going to be opened in this volume, and he had come because a woman in a car park on the ring road had said the word *tape* out loud on the Friday and had not known she was saying it to a man who would come

The wharf was where it always was, four hundred feet of brick with iron doors facing the water, and the doors were shut, and the padlock on the personnel door was a new one and the shutter on the office two doors along was down.

Ama Osei was on the quay with a steel tape and a woman in a borrowed jacket and a hard hat, and the man of thirty-two was stood about forty feet away with his hands in his pockets being in the way.

"**You are the coat,**" said Ama Osei.

"**I am the coat.**"

"**Then stand over there and do not stand where a man is going to put a tape.**" She was about thirty-six and had done eleven of these and had not come back to him about the other ten. "**You are here because somebody told you there was going to be a tape.**"

"**A woman in a car park on the ring road said the word out loud and I have got nothing else to do on a Monday morning,**" said Elias Venn. "**I am not going to pretend I heard it from a notice.**"

"**Do not pretend anything. Stand over there.**"

The joint was in the coping of the revetment, four feet back from the quay edge, running the length of the frontage, and it was a movement joint and it was a good joint and it had been put in by somebody who knew what a revetment does.

"**In June,**" said the woman in the hard hat. "**I am Dinah Vassallo. I am appointed under the June instrument and I have been out here four times.**"

"**How much.**"

"**Thirteen millimetres at the widest, on a joint that is a hundred and forty years old and had not moved a millimetre in the forty years before the water.**" She put the end of the tape into the gap and took it out again and wrote it on the back of her own glove. "**And in June it was nine.**"

"**Nine on the twenty-fourth of June,**" said Ama Osei, "**and I took that one, and I took it with this tape, and it is the same tape, and I wrote it on the sheet the office has.**"

"**So it has gone from nine to thirteen.**"

"**It has gone from nine to thirteen in about seven weeks,**" said Dinah Vassallo, "**and the rate is the finding, not the number, and if you want a number that means something then you have to ask me what a number means on a joint that has not moved since 1985, and I will tell you that I do not know and that four millimetres in seven weeks is either nothing or a bank going somewhere, and I am not going to guess which in front of a wall.**"

The crack in the coping was above the joint and about two metres long and had a piece of card pushed into it that somebody had left, and the card was a surveyor's card and it was folded and it had a date on it, and the date was the fourth of August.

"**That was me,**" said Dinah Vassallo. "**On the Wednesday. I do not push card into a crack, I put a telltale on it, and there is a telltale on it, and somebody has put a card in as well, and I would like to know who and I am going to leave it there and find out later, because a card in a crack is a thing that happens at about two o'clock in the afternoon.**"

Nobody said anything for a moment. The water was a long way off and doing nothing.

"**Can I ask you a question that is not the one,**" said Elias Venn.

"**You can ask me a question.**"

"**Somebody wrote a thing on a card on the fourth of August about this wall and I do not know what it said. What I want to know is whether a person writes that on a weekday morning because something is happening or because something has already happened.**"

Dinah Vassallo looked at him for a second longer than she had looked at him.

"**A person writes that on a weekday morning because something is happening,**" she said. "**I have been doing it for nineteen years and I have never once written one because something had finished.**"

## And on the Monday afternoon he wrote down four things and one of them was that a message about a condition has been in a hut on a yard since the twenty-fourth of July and nobody knows whether it was collected, and he did not ring the man, and the reason he did not ring the man is that the man asked to be asked again in a different mood and this was not it

The notebook came out of the coat at about half past four on a low wall at the top of the slipway, out of the wind.

> **Mon 9 Aug, 18:40. Thirty-five weeks and one day since the sixth of December. The practice is thirty-one weeks. The bell has said nothing for two hundred and thirty-nine days and the panel on its case is two hundred and fifty-five days old.**

> **A message about his condition has been in a hut on that yard since the morning of Saturday the twenty-fourth of July. I do not know whether it was collected. He does not know. Nobody knows. > **The store is shut. The doors have not been opened and nobody on that quay wanted them opened and the person who would have opened them has been in a boat once already and is not going in again.**

## And on the Tuesday at about eleven in the morning a woman of forty-eight put four lines in a file and read them out loud, and the four lines are the whole of the case for a second instrument, and she read them to nobody in particular, and then she went and got the form

> **Flood Emergency Powers, City Flood Office. Referred finding, Ferry Bank wharf revetment, 4 August 2025.**
> **1. The movement joint in the revetment coping at the Ferry Bank wharf was measured at 9 mm on 24 June 2025 and at 13 mm on 4 August 2025, the same tape and the same officer.**
> **2. A new crack is present in the coping above the joint, with movement since 24 June 2025. A telltale is fixed.**
> **3. No printed table in this county states the level at which water enters the Ferry Bank district behind this wall. The wall is older than the district's records.**
> **4. A second access suspension is necessary. The grounds for the first have not expired; they have continued.**
> **Y. Carrow, Flood Emergency Powers. 4 August 2025.**

"**That is four lines and I wrote them on Monday and I am reading them out on a Tuesday morning because I promised myself on the Friday in a car park on the ring road that I would decide in an office and not on a quay, and because on the Friday a man of fifty-five made me a condition and the condition was *tell me what happens to a street if the number is wrong*, and I have been carrying it about for three weeks like a shopping.**" She put the sheet down on the table in the room above the shop, which was not her office and was a room that had been lent. "**And I cannot answer it, and I want that on the page in front of the four people who are in here, because it is the reason this instrument is going out and nobody can improve on the reason.**"

"**Go on,**" said Marianne Cope.

"**Item three is the whole of it. There is no printed table anywhere that says how high the water has to be at that wall before it is inside. I have a joint that has opened four millimetres in seven weeks on a structure that is a hundred and forty years old, and a crack, and no way of turning either of those into a number that a street can be judged by, and I have got a statutory duty to decide whether four hundred metres of frontage is safe, and there is no instrument in this city that can tell me, and the man who could tell me has told me in the car park that he is not going to print a height for a street that has not got one, and he is right, and I have written that down.**"

Nobody in the room said anything about the joint and everybody in the room had an opinion about item three.

"**Say the cost,**" said Yvette Carrow, "**because I said it in June before I signed the first one and I am going to say it now and I am going to say it twice, and the second time is going to be the one that is worse and I would rather say it than have somebody else find it in September.**"

She did not read it. She had it.

"**One. About sixty ground floors on that frontage were not assessed in June because the first instrument stopped the assessors getting in, and they are not going to be assessed in the second half of August either, and about a hundred and forty households that have been waiting since December are going to be told that again.**"

"**Two.**"

"**Two. The first instrument named one building. The second one will name that building and about four hundred metres of retaining wall and the frontages behind it, and I do not know how many households are behind four hundred metres of it, and neither does the form, because the form does not ask me and I cannot ask, and that is a thing I have written down now and it is going in the file and it is the first time I have written a sentence like that in eleven years.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**Three,**" she said, "**and this is the one I would like somebody to stop me on, because I have not been stopped on anything in eleven years and I am saying it out loud so that if anybody in this room wants to stop me they have about nine seconds.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Three. Whatever I sign today, the case will be able to say in about four months that the office closed four hundred metres of a district while nine hundred claims were open, and they will be right, and I will not be able to say one word back, because every word I could say would be about the water, and the water is not the part that is in dispute.**"

"**Then do not sign it,**" said Hazel Cray.

"**If I do not sign it then in about nine days a woman of thirty-six has to stand on a quay with a bond and a cut-off and a structure that is moving, and I have to explain to her why not, and she will write it down.**" Yvette Carrow uncapped a pen. "**I am going to sign it, and I am going to say the cost out loud first, and I am going to be right, and I would like it minuted that I said the cost twice.**"

## And the form is a single sheet printed on one side and the second instrument is the fourth she has signed since the sixth of June, and the difference between the first and the second is four hundred metres, and the difference between the first and the second is one clause

> **SCHEDULE F — FLOOD-EMERGENCY ACCESS SUSPENSION. `CFO/25/SF/0231`.**
> **PREMISES: the bonded store and all outbuildings, yards, quay and revetment adjoining, wharf, district of Ferry Bank; and the retaining wall at the wharf frontage and the frontages behind it for a length of about four hundred metres.**
> **PERIOD: from 06:00 on Sunday 15 August 2025 to 23:59 on Tuesday 31 August 2025.**
> **GROUNDS: (a) that the premises and the retaining wall adjoining them are at risk of inundation within the period; (b) that a movement in the structure of the retaining wall has been measured and recorded within the last seven days; (c) that access to the premises would create a hazard to the person seeking it and to any person in the premises.**
> **4. No person shall enter any premises comprised in this Schedule except under a licence issued by this office, and a licence shall specify the premises, the purpose, and the period, and shall be held by the person named on it and produced on demand.**
> **This Schedule may be lifted early on written application. There is no appeal.**
> **This form contains a box for a period and no other box. Applications under this Schedule are not persons and are not recorded.**
> **Y. Carrow, Flood Emergency Powers. 10 August 2025, 11:41.**

Nobody read clause four out loud for about a minute and a half.

"**That is standard,**" said Yvette Carrow. "**It has been in the Schedule since 2019 and it is in every copy in this building and there are about four hundred of them. I have signed three of these since June. I have never read clause four in my life.**"

"**Because there was nothing to read it for,**" said Mara Iles. She had the form on the desk in front of her and she had not touched it. "**In June it named one building and a licensed officer went in when she went in and nobody asked. It is not that you have signed a worse instrument. It is that you have signed the same instrument about four hundred metres instead of one building and the form noticed before either of you did.**"

"**Say that again slowly,**" said Hazel Cray.

"**A licence clause does nothing to a building. A licence clause is about a person. It says that a person may not go into a place unless another person has written down that that person may go into that place, and it says the other person has to write down which place and why and for how long, and it says nothing at all about how anybody finds out that they need one.**" Mara Iles put her finger on the line. "**There is no knock in it. There is no letter. There is no box. A household cannot apply for a licence because a household is not a person in the last line of the form, and a person who is in the building on the night is not a person who can apply for anything, because they are the person it is about and item two of a claims form is the only identity operation in this business and it is in a different building in a different lane.**"

"**That is item seven,**" said Yvette Carrow.

"**That is item seven of the sheet of ten,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and I put my name against it on the fourth of August and I have not taken my name off, and I would like it said that I put my name against it before there was a clause.**"

"**Then what closes it.**"

"**Nothing she can write,**" said Mara Iles. "**We had that conversation on the fourth of August and it took about four minutes and the answer was that the two forms would have to be the same form, and they belong to two bodies, and the boundary between the two bodies is about a hundred and forty years old and is on a wall somewhere. And I have now found out what that sentence is actually for, and it is for this: it is for the moment when a form that is about water has to carry a fact about a person, and the person has to come from the other side of the wall.**"

Yvette Carrow signed at eleven forty-one and dated it at the bottom of the page and did not initial it, and said that the office does not initial, and the reason the office does not initial is that an initial is a person acknowledging a document and a document under this Schedule is not a person, and she said that out loud too, twice, and everybody at the table heard the whole sentence.

## And what it does is that a district is closed by the width of a piece of paper and a household is closed by the depth of one, and neither of those is a decision anybody took on Friday or Saturday, and on the Tuesday afternoon a registrar of twenty-eight read a list of ten items out loud to four professionals and one of the ten of them is now a clause in an instrument

> **2. Nobody in that room had ever asked what a thing is for. Four of them had spent five weeks being asked the other question — what is a box for — by a man with no post who writes everything down, and a fifth of them had answered it out loud in public in a church hall in June and did not know until the seventh of July what she had said.

The woman of forty-eight with the statutory power had said the cost twice and had been right about the bank and had signed at eleven forty-one, and about four hundred metres of a district had closed at six o'clock on Sunday the fifteenth, and the clause that did it was four lines long and had been in the form since 2019 and had never once been about a person, because for six years it had only ever been about a building.
