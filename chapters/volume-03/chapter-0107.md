# Chapter 0107 — The Flood

**High water came into the low bank in the small hours of Saturday the nineteenth of June 2025 and it came into a bonded store on a wharf four miles downriver, and the store was not opened, and nobody in this chapter died, and nothing supernatural happened, and the most frightening sentence in it is a professional reading a volume off a lease schedule on a mobile telephone on a wet quay at about half past two in the morning.**

**There were about nine households on Ferry Bank with water in a ground floor. Two doors on that bank were not opened and neither was forced. A man of fifty-eight who had gone into the store on his own initiative at about eleven o'clock and told nobody was brought out through a landward loading door at about twenty past three in the morning by two men on a ladder in a boat, and he was asked what was in there, and he said he did not know because he did not look, and nobody asked him again.**

---

## And at about ten past nine on Friday the eighteenth the wharf was doing what a wharf does, and at about twenty past ten at night the water came over the coping at the north end, and between those two facts there was a whole shift of people doing a job correctly

There is a difference between a flood and a night shift and this chapter is mostly about the night shift, because the flood in this city on the night of the eighteenth of June happened to a street that had been sent a printed sheet with a number on it, and a district that had been named in capitals in a warning, and a river with a gauge on it four miles away, and about eleven hundred feet of masonry between the low bank and the tide.

Corry Slatter had been on the flood night rota for six years and had a crew of four, a generator, a pump on a trailer, two lamps, a boat, and a board. He had a crew of three because the fourth was off.

"**We are not a rescue service,**" he said, at about ten past nine, to about nine households. "**We are a council night crew with a pump and a boat and four names on a sheet, and we are doing doors on the low bank, and the water is going to be about eleven inches in a ground floor and that is the whole of the forecast and it is going to be wrong by something and I would rather it was wrong low.**"

He wrote on the board: **21:00. RIVER ALERT IN. 22:40 OVERTOPPING N END. 01:20 PEAK.**

---

## And the district office had a floor of water in it in December and the council had put the paper somewhere with a door on it, and somewhere with a door on it was a bonded store on a wharf that a freeholder of seventy-four had never been inside

The store was about four hundred feet long, brick, with a slate roof and a parapet, and two sets of iron doors facing the quay, and a personnel door on the landward side about two hundred feet from the water. It had been built in 1897 for bonded goods and it had been let empty for nineteen years and it had been let since February 2025 to the City Flood Office as a records store, and the freeholder was a woman of seventy-four called Prudence Halloway, and she had been in the building once, in 1987, when her predecessor had shown it to a prospective tenant and had died in the August of that year.

"**I have never been in it,**" she said, on a mobile telephone, at about eleven o'clock on the Friday night, from a house eleven miles away. "**I have not been in it since 1987. I have never been in it alone and I have never been in it since. That is not a thing I am saying to make anybody feel better. It is the answer to the question and you have asked me four times in about ten minutes.**"

"**Ma'am, we need to know what is in it.**"

"**The lease says records and archives of the City of Morrowgate. I have never asked what records. I have had the rent four times a year for nineteen years and I have never asked what was on the other side of my own wall and I have thought about that a great deal in the last twenty minutes and I would like it recorded that I thought about it and got it wrong about six hours too late.**"

## And at about twenty past twelve a man who was not supposed to be in there said so on a telephone, and the crew got him out through the landward door at about twenty past three, and he could not tell them what was in the room with him for four hours

He had a contract to move a quantity of salvage paperwork out of an office two doors along, because that office is on the quay and the quay is going under. He had a torch, a mobile telephone, a key for the office, and no key for the store, and the store's side door was off its latch, and he had gone in at about eleven o'clock to start moving the store's own boxes off the floor, because they were on the bottom tier of racking and the racking is on the floor and he could see the water coming and he thought he was doing a day's work in a hurry.

"**I did not ring anybody,**" he said. "**I am going to be honest with you, I did not ring anybody, and I would do it again and I would like somebody to tell me why I should not have.**"

"**Why did you not ring.**"

"**Because I was going to have it out by one.**"

His name was Jim Tarrant and he was fifty-eight and he had been a removals man for twenty-two years and he had moved about nine boxes and stacked them on the top tier of the racking about eleven feet up, and he had a torch, and his telephone worked, and he had about four hours in a cold room with water coming up a brick wall.

"**You have been on the phone to us for four hours.**"

"**I have. I did not think anybody would come.**"

Corry Slatter got him out at about twenty past three with a boat, a ladder, a man in a wetsuit off the fire service and an electrician's torch, through a loading door on the landward side, over a yard, and up onto a ramp, and he was not hurt, and he sat on a pallet in a blanket for about ten minutes and then told them what he knew.

"**Racking,**" he said. "**Three rows the length of the place, steel, about eleven foot. The bottom tier was boxes and the boxes were wet. I moved about nine of them up. There was a lot of paper. TONS of it. Files, bundles, boxed. I didn't look at any of them, because I was moving them.**"

"**Files of what.**"

"**I don't know.**"

"**You were in there four hours with a torch.**"

"**I was in there four hours with a torch moving nine boxes and I did not open one, and if you'd asked me that at eleven o'clock I'd have said the same, and I'm not going to say something now that I didn't see.**" He put his hands out. "**That's the honest version and it's worse than a guess. A guess you'd have written down.**"

## And the crew was told the water was in a building they could not get into, and there were four reasons, and every one of them was correct, and that is the whole of the chapter

They could not get into it. Here is why, in the order that it happened, and nobody in this chapter did anything wrong.

**One.** The doors are bonded. A bonded store's doors carry a seal and the licence holder's agent has to be present and a licensed officer has to be there, and the agent's office closed at noon on the Friday and is forty minutes away and the telephone is answered by a man who says the same four sentences.

**Two.** Nobody had a key. The store's keys are at the freeholder's, and the freeholder is eleven miles away and seventy-four, and the duplicate is in a safe on the quay, and the safe is in an office that is under four feet of water.

**Three.** Opening them would have made it worse. The doors are in an iron frame that took the weight of the wall behind it and there is a head of about two feet on the quay side, and the quay is where the pump is and where the boat is and where two vans are, and the officer from the fire service said on the quay at about half past one that if those doors came in against a two-foot head the whole quay would go and they would lose the pump and the boat and the light, and she was right, and nobody argued with her, and nobody has written down the sentence in which he agreed to it.

**Four.** And the fourth is the one that is going to sit in this case for a very long time, and it is not anybody's fault, and it is not about a door. **Nobody could say whether there was anybody in there.** It was a store. It was let as a store. Every record said store, and the council's own inventory said store, and a woman of seventy-four who had never been in since 1987 said records and archives and said she had never asked which. There was no way to know and nobody to ask and no form anywhere in this city that asks it, and the crew spent about two hours not doing the one thing a person wants them to do, which was go in and have a look, because going in and having a look is how you find out that there is somebody in there.

"**Are you going to try the doors.**"

"**No.**

"**There might be a person in there.**"

"**There might be,**" said the officer of the fire service, who was about forty-one and who had been twenty-two years in and who said the rest of it standing in the rain with her hood down. "**And there might not be, and we have got a man in there already who we knew about at half past twelve and who we did not know about until then, and if I break a bonded door on a wharf at one in the morning with one boat and two men I put that man under four feet in a bay and I put the pump under four feet, and I will not do that, and if I write it up I will write why I did not do it and I will sign it, and you can take the report and complain about it and you will be wrong to.**"

"**Nobody is going to complain.**"

"**I know. That is not the reason and I would like it noted that that is not the reason.**"

## And at about half past two a professional said a number out loud on a mobile telephone, and nobody in that room has been the same since

They had the freeholder on the phone and they had the lease schedule on the phone, because she had it in a drawer because it was the only thing about the building anybody had ever sent her, and it gave the floor area and the height of the racking, and the officer from the fire service did the sum out loud, twice, and got the same answer both times, and then she said it.

"**Four hundred feet by sixty is twenty-four thousand square feet. The racking is eleven foot and it is in the way, and the floor is a foot below a sill that stands nine inches above the quay, and the water came in through the quay wall and went out through the ventilation, and by the peak there was about eleven inches of it standing on that floor. That is about six hundred and twenty cubic metres, and a tonne is a cubic metre of it, so I will call it six hundred and twenty, and I am going to say six hundred and forty because there is paper in that room and about twenty tonnes of that paper is already in the water.**" She let that sit for a second. "**And I want the next thing said properly, because it is the only reason the doors are still shut. The level inside stopped about an inch short of that sill, so it did not come in through the doors at all. It came in through a wall. And the doors are the only strong thing on that quay.**"

"**And the racking.**"

"**Steel, on a concrete floor, bolted, and a bolted steel rack in a foot of water is a rack in a foot of water. The paper is at the bottom of it and there is a great deal of it and it is not going to be a floor of paper. It is going to be a soup of about six hundred and forty tonnes of water and paper and I have never seen one and I am telling you the shape of it and not pretending to know the answer.**"

Nobody spoke on the quay for about nine seconds after that, and the freeholder said, from eleven miles away, in a voice that had gone very quiet:

"**Oh.**"

"**Ma'am.**"

"**Six hundred and forty tonnes of my building.**"

"**About that, yes.**"

"**Nobody has been in it since 1987.**" She said it and then she stopped for about four seconds and then she said the sentence that is the reason this case is going to take eight months to get anywhere. "**And I am not going to stand here and pretend that I knew. I did not know. I have a lease and a rent and a slate roof and I have never opened those doors and if anybody in this city wants a villain for what is happening on my wharf tonight then they are going to have to invent one, because the villain is a box on a lease and it has been there since February.**"

## And the peak was at about twenty to four and the number came off the gauge at the Northglass pier at about ten past one, and it was wrong by three centimetres, and nobody said anything about that either

Gwilym Petherick rang the flood office at about ten past one and the duty officer read a number off an instrument and said it into a telephone, and then, about four seconds later, said it again in a slightly different way.

"**Three point nine one.**"

"**Say that again.**"

"**Three point nine one above Newlyn at the Northglass pier. It is still rising. It passed three point eight six about eight minutes ago.**"

"**Three point eight six was the forecast.**"

"**It was.**"

"**That is three centimetres.**"

"**That is three centimetres and the alert was issued at twenty to nine yesterday morning and it was issued at medium confidence and it said a level at one gauge is not a level at any street, and I have read that sentence about four hundred times in thirty-one years and I have never once read it as carefully as I read it at twenty to nine on Monday morning.**" Petherick did not soften it. "**Three centimetres is the whole of the difference between this and a bad night. I want that written down in a place where somebody finds it in about four years and I do not care what is written around it.**"

At about twenty to four the gauge stopped rising and began to fall, and by about half past four there was about eleven inches of water in a ground-floor front room on Ander's Row and about nine inches in a hall, and the crew carried a chest of drawers, a pram, a dog, a box of papers, a television, a folding wheelchair and about four hundred pounds of shopping out of nine houses, and two doors were not opened and neither was forced, and nobody wrote a note about either, and there was nobody hurt anywhere on the low bank that night, and there was a man of fifty-eight in a blanket on a pallet on a quay who had been in a cold room for four hours and would be, in about four years, the only living person who could have said what was on the bottom tier of that racking.
