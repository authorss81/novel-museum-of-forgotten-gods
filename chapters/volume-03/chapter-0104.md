# Chapter 0104 — The First Claim, Refused, Correctly

**On Monday the seventh of June 2025 and Tuesday the eighth, a woman of thirty-four called Roisin Mellor sat in a chair on the second floor of a building on Wharf Street with her grandmother and was told by a woman of about thirty-four with nine years in the trade that her claim for the water in December could not be assessed, and that the reason was correct, and that the reason was correct about a man in the fourth file along from hers who had claimed for the same water at a house he had never lived in.**

**She is owed seven hundred and fifty pounds. She is not a fraud. She is going to be told the truth about it in about four minutes, by somebody who is competent, and she is not going to get it, and the whole of this chapter is the distance between those two sentences.**

---

## And the letter had been on her table since Friday and she had not opened it, because she could not work out who to ring about it

She rang the helpline on Monday morning at about twenty past nine and she was put through to the handling team and then to a person who read her reference back to her and then told her, in about nine seconds and without any coldness at all, that a decision had been issued on the Friday, that it had been in the post since about eleven o'clock that morning, and that it stayed open until the fifth of July.

"**I have not even got the letter,**" she said. "**It is on the table. I have had it since Friday and I have been putting off opening it because the last time one of those was about something else entirely.**"

"**Do you want me to read it to you.**"

"**No. I want to open it.**"

Elias Venn was allowed into that room and that was the correct instrument and it took about four seconds to establish. He was not the policyholder and he was not the claimant and he had no post and no card and no employer, and there is no return in that office for a man in a coat, **and Roisin Mellor asked him to come because she did not want to be told the news alone and because she had known him for about two days and had decided, on the strength of two days, that he was the honest one.** Nobody wrote his name down anywhere, because there is no box for him, and he was in the room for four hours and there is no record that he was ever there.

---

## And the claim is for a ground floor in Ferry Bank that has two feet of silt in it, and the ground floor is where the evidence was

The claim was for the contents of a ground-floor flat at 2 Cutler's Rise, a building of about nine flats off a road called Ander's Row on the low bank, and the household was Roisin Mellor and her grandmother, and the grandmother was eighty-two and had been got out of that flat at about two in the morning on the tenth of December by two men from the floor above with a torch and a length of rope.

"**I was in that flat on the ninth and I was in it on the tenth and I was not in it on the eleventh,**" said Blanche Mellor, **who had come because she was asked whether she wanted to come, and who had not expected to be asked anything, and who sat with her coat on.** "**And I have been in the first floor of my sister's at number nineteen since the fifteenth of December and I am not going back into that ground floor whatever anybody pays me.**"

"**What was in the front room.**"

"**Everything I have owned since 1981.**"

"**And what is in there now.**"

"**Silt, and a smell, and a man from a company came with a camera in March and put about forty photographs in a file.**"

---

## And the box is item four on page one, and the letter is four paragraphs, and the second paragraph is nine words long

> **Your claim reference: DL/24/10455**
> **We have received your claim form dated 13 December 2024 and your supporting correspondence and photographs.**
> **Item 4 of the form (DATE OF LOSS) is blank.**
> **Item 4 is mandatory. It is cross-referenced at Part 5, section D of the instruction to adjusters, a copy of which we enclose. Unless the item is evidenced, this claim cannot be assessed and no payment can be made on it.**
> **Your claim remains open for thirty days from the date of this letter and will then be closed. It may be reopened on production of the item. If you believe the item has been completed, contact the handling team quoting the reference above.**
> **Issued 5 June 2025. [initials]. Flood Claims Handling, City Flood Office, Wharf Street.**

"**Read it out,**" said Roisin Mellor.

Marianne Cope read it out, all of it, in the voice she used for hearing statements, and she stopped at the end of the second paragraph and looked at her grandmother.

"**Nine words,**" said the old woman. "**Forty years of washing and a cooker and my mother's chair and it is nine words and one of them is *unless*.**"

"**It is nine words,**" said Marianne Cope. "**And I wrote the paragraph and I am not going to pretend I did not, and I am going to read you the rest of it and then I am going to tell you why it is right, because you have not come here to be managed and I do not manage people.**"

---

## And she could produce a date, and the date was the right date, and the date was not the thing in the way

"**I can tell you the day,**" said Roisin Mellor. "**It was the ninth. It came up over the wall at about eleven at night and we had the sandbag thing going and at about two in the morning the men came up from the floor above and got Gran, and the water was in the front room by about half past one.**"

"**Then you can evidence it.**"

"**How.**"

"**A document.**"

"**What document.**"

"**A document that shows the date on it.**"

"**I have been through that room with a torch and a bucket,**" said Roisin Mellor, "**in December, in January, in February, and in March when your man came with the camera. There is nothing in there. The thing I would have used is the letter I got when I took the flat, and it was in the tin on top of the wardrobe with my mum's bills in it, and the tin is in the cupboard under the stairs, and the cupboard is in the front room.**"

Nobody said anything for about six seconds.

"**The meter reading,**" said Roisin Mellor. "**I have got a photograph of the meter on my phone from the second of November. Is that a document.**"

"**It is a photograph, and the instruction will not take a photograph, and that is not me. That is section D and a photograph is not a document and about four hundred claims were refused on exactly that point in December and I did not lose one night of sleep over any of them.**"

"**I could write it in now.**"

"**You could write the ninth of December in the box and it would be true and we would take it and the claim would be assessed and then it would be paid, and I am telling you that because somebody is going to tell you the opposite in about eleven days and I would rather it came from me.**" She put her hands flat on the table. "**And I am also going to tell you that if you write it in, you are doing the thing that four hundred people have done and about nine of those four hundred were telling the truth and about one of them was not, and neither I nor you nor anybody in this building can find out which is which, and the whole of the rule is that we do not have to find out. That is what a claim is. A claim is a thing somebody says where somebody in a chair has to decide whether to believe them.**"

"**And you do not believe me.**"

"**I have believed you for four minutes and I have had to write REFUSED on your file at the end of it, and those are not contradictions, they are the two halves of my job, and I have had them both since I was twenty-six.**"

---

## And the rule is correct, and the rule is correct about a man in the fourth file along, and she said so out loud, and it is the whole of the chapter

"**Say why,**" said Elias Venn. **He had not asked a question in an office for six months without having it refused, and he had not expected anybody to hand him the reason in the fourth minute.**

"**You are going to think I am hiding behind a form,**" said Marianne Cope, "**and I am not, and it is going to take me about ninety seconds and then I am going to go back to work.**"

"**I have not said anything.**"

"**You have a face that says it and I have been reading faces over a telephone since 2016. All right.**" She turned the file round so that the four refusal letters on top of it were visible, and there were four, and they were the same four lines. "**Section D exists because in November, in a flood in nineteen ninety-eight that was not this one, a man claimed on one policy for the same water at two addresses eleven miles apart, and both claims had a date of loss in them, both dates were the same date, and both were true, and the payment went out twice, and the loss was about nine thousand pounds, and it came out of a fund that about four hundred households were then waiting on, and the four hundred households waited eleven weeks longer.**"

"**So the rule stops a second claim.**"

"**The rule stops a claim that cannot be tied to a household at an address on a night. That is a different rule and it is the same rule, and it is the only one in the two pages that is about people.**" She put a finger on the top letter. "**I have refused nine claims since the eighth of December for want of a date of loss. Eight of the nine are like yours. One of the nine is a man called Petrie who claimed on a policy in his wife's name for a ground floor in Ferry Bank that he has never lived in and never been inside, and the box was blank on his too, and he was refused on the box and then he was refused again on the ground floor, and I have his second letter as well.**"

"**Then the rule works.**"

"**The rule works. That is the entire argument and I have never in nine years had anybody put it better than I have just put it to you.**" She squared the letters. "**And the eight are yours. That is the whole of it and there is no answer to it and I am not going to look for one while I am sitting in a chair with you, because the moment I start trying to work round my own rule for a woman I have liked for four minutes, then the rule is not a rule, it is a favour, and I have watched what happens to a file that has a favour in it.**"

Roisin Mellor was looking at the four letters. Her grandmother had not moved.

"**Mrs Mellor. I am going to ask you the hardest question anybody in this office is going to ask you today and I want the true answer and I am not going to hold it against you either way.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Were you in that flat on the night of the ninth.**"

"**I was in it on the ninth and on the tenth and I was got out of it at about two in the morning,**" said Blanche Mellor, **and the old woman was not asking for anything and did not know that she was the only person in the room the form had no box for.**

Marianne Cope did not write it down. That is on the record in one line of a handling file and it is the only line in nine hundred files that says a thing an office did not do.

"**Right,**" she said. "**And I am not going to be able to do one single thing with that, and I have wanted to say that out loud in a room since December.**"

---

## And seven hundred and fifty pounds is what the whole of it is worth, and it is a boiler and about five weeks of arrears, and it is the only money in this chapter

"**How much is it,**" said Roisin Mellor. "**Of the excess. If it were paid.**"

"**Seven hundred and fifty pounds.**"

"**For the lot of it.**"

"**For the lot of it. Once, per household, per event.**" Marianne Cope did not look at her when she said the next part. "**And I am going to say a thing that I have said in about nine rooms since December and have never once said in one where the claimant could hear it, because I have run out of ways of not saying it. Seven hundred and fifty pounds is about the cost of a boiler. I have got a book here of nine hundred and four claims and I am going to put in front of you the number of households on the low bank that are owed that money. It is a good number. It is a very good number, and it is the number this whole apparatus exists to produce, and I have never once in nine years seen it go to a kitchen. It goes into a household and it keeps the lights on and it is not spent on the water and it is not spent on a boiler and nobody rings anybody up to say that.**"

"**Then why are you telling me.**"

"**Because you have been told nine words and I would like you to have had one true sentence as well, and because in about four years there is going to be a person in a room with a file and your name on it who needs to know that somebody competent agreed with you on the eighth of June 2025.**"

---

## And the claim was closed on the form at about two o'clock on the Tuesday, and the box was left empty, and nobody made her fill it in, and that is the first decision anybody in this batch has got wrong in nobody's favour

"**If you leave the box empty, it closes on the fifth of July and it cannot be reopened without a document, and a document does not exist,**" said Marianne Cope.

"**If I put the ninth of December in it, it is true.**"

"**It is true and it is unevidenced and I have told you what happens with those.**"

Roisin Mellor took the pen and looked at item four for about eleven seconds, and then she put the pen down, and then she said the sentence that two professionals had already said in this case about a different instrument in a different month, and she said it without knowing she had said it before.

"**Then it can close,**" she said. "**I am not writing something in there to get seven hundred and fifty pounds. I am not going to do that. I have never done anything like that in my life and I am not starting on a Tuesday.**"

"**That is your decision and I am obliged to offer you the alternative and I have offered it and I will record that it was offered.**" Marianne Cope closed the file. "**And I am not going to record a reason, because you have not given me one and I did not ask you for one, and the form has no box for one either.**"

---

## And in a corridor at about ten past three on the Tuesday a woman of thirty-four told him the thing that puts the wharf on the map of this case, and she told it as a fact about her own week and not as a warning

"**You have about nine minutes and then I have a call,**" she said, walking. "**And you are going to ask me how many claims were refused for want of that box and I am going to tell you about forty have come to a point where it is the thing in the way, and nine have been refused on it, and I do not know which of the other thirty-one will be, and I am not going to give you either of those numbers in a way that you can put on a wall, and I want you to hear the reason and it is the only thing I am going to say that is not procedure.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Because the second there is a number of people like her in the world, every one of the eight stops being a woman I sat in a chair with on the eighth of June and starts being a proportion. And this is a catastrophe and not an injustice for about eleven reasons and one of them is that a catastrophe has a rate and an injustice has a person in it, and I have got a person in it and she is going out now, and I have got a rate in a book and it is about the same size.**"

"**All right.**"

"**And the other thing, which is not a warning and is only a fact about my week.**" She stopped at the stairs. "**The low bank claims were re-routed in the middle of January. The district office had about a foot of water in its basement and the paper went somewhere dry, and somewhere dry is a rented store on a wharf four miles from here, because the council needed a building with a door on it that week and that is what there was.**"

"**Wharf Street.**"

"**No. Four miles downriver, off the Cattle Market end, and it is a bonded store and it is not our building and it is not our room, and the council holds a lease on it and I do not know what is in it, and I have not been able to get at my own December file since the fifteenth of January.**" She went down. "**I am not complaining about that. Nobody is going to lose a night's sleep over my file. I am telling you because you are a person and not an insurer, and because you have been asking the right question for two days, and because in about four years somebody is going to want a list of the paper that was in that flood and I am the person who knows where the paper is, and I have told nobody.**"
