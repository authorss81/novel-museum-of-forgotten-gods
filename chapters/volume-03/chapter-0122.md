# Chapter 0122 — A Roll Is A Name And A House

**On Wednesday the eleventh and Thursday the twelfth of August 2025 a school of about three hundred and eighty children on a low bank, a roll that carries a name and a house in every line, and a premises inventory that carries a building and a level, sat down in two rooms of that school and could not be made to talk to each other in any instrument this city has. Neither of the two people who had them was a fool and neither of them was doing anything wrong and the registrar of twenty-eight who asked the question had the answer in her own hand and had to give it up.**

**Nobody asked a child anything. The school was not a place that anything happened in; it was a place where a household was already written down in a legible form, four hundred times over, and was not being asked about, and by Thursday afternoon four adults had established that this was the most useful record in the district and the one that could not be used, and had done it without anybody in the building ever being asked a single question.**

---

## And the reason the two of them were in a school on the Wednesday morning is a clause signed the day before, and the clause is about a person, and a school is the largest concentration of people in a district who are not on a tenancy file

Marlbank Primary had been built in 1931 in two storeys on a strip between two streets, and the whole of the ground floor had been a coat room, a hall, a kitchen and two classrooms, and the caretaker had a key to every door in the building and a paint brush and no instrument at all.

"**You are the council,**" said Douglas Nairn to the woman at the table, "**and you are not from the council, and the other one is not from the council either, and there is a man with no post at the end of my table and I have been told to put up with that.**"

"**I am the council,**" said Ina Mordaunt, "**and I have been the council in this building for nineteen years, and this is my office, and the other one is not from the council and is welcome.**"

Mara Iles put the second instrument on the table. It was a printed sheet and it had been folded twice.

"**This is signed and it runs from six o'clock on Sunday morning,**" she said. "**And clause four says that nobody enters any premises in it except under a licence, and I have been sent to say why I am in a school at ten o'clock on a Wednesday morning and it is not the school. It is that your frontage is inside four hundred metres of a wall.**"

"**Our frontage is not on a flood wall,**" said Ina Mordaunt.

"**Your frontage is four hundred metres back from a wall and the wall is between you and the river, and the wall has moved four millimetres since June, and nobody knows what four millimetres means, and until Friday there was a form with a box for a period in it and no other box, and the box is now four lines long and it is about you.**"

Douglas Nairn turned his own file round. "**I will save everybody eleven minutes. I asked for a premises re-survey of nine buildings on that strip on Saturday the seventh of August, three days after the tape went in, and the first date offered to me was Monday the twenty-fourth, and the confirmation came back on Wednesday morning with a note on it that said the surveyor could not be booked into the four hundred metres without a licence, and the surveyor is a woman of about fifty called Dhoni Perera and she is good and she is not slow and she is not the problem. So the survey of nine buildings is off until the first of September at the earliest, and there are six thousand children in those nine buildings, and I have not told any of their head teachers yet because I did not want to ring six head teachers on a Friday morning.**"

"**How many buildings in the district.**"

"**About forty on that strip and about nineteen hundred tenancies behind all of them and about two hundred and thirty in the four hundred metres the instrument actually names, and I can give you those two numbers because they are in a file, and I cannot give you the households, because the households are not in the file.**"

## And the roll is a name and a house in every line, and it is the only piece of paper in that district where a household is legible, and it has been legible for eleven years, and the person who keeps it has never been asked to give it to anybody

Ina Mordaunt had a printed roll on the table before anybody asked for it, and she had put it face down, and everybody at the table noticed and nobody remarked on it.

"**Before anybody says the word,**" she said. "**I have taught in this building for nineteen years and I have been the person who types the roll for sixteen of them, and I have typed every line of it myself out of what a parent wrote on an enrolment form when the child was between three and four years old, and I am going to say now, out loud, that I am not going to give it to anybody, and I am going to say why, and the why is not modesty.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**A school roll does not go anywhere.**" She did not raise her voice and she did not put her hands flat on the table, and it was the first thing said in either room by anybody who was not in a flood office. "**That is the whole of it. It has not gone anywhere in sixteen years. Nobody has asked me for it in sixteen years, not the council, not an inspector, not a police officer, not once, and I have never had to say no, and if I hand it to a room like this one then in about four years there will be a document somewhere that says three hundred and eighty children were on a low bank in June, and it will be a good document, and it will be a document that got made because somebody needed it for something, and nobody will remember what we used it for, and neither will I.**"

"**You do not know what we would use it for,**" said Hazel Cray.

"**That is exactly why,**" said Ina Mordaunt.

## And the premises inventory is a building, and it has a level on it, and there is no way on earth of asking a building how many people were in it, and the woman who has been the registrar of this city for five years said the sentence that stops the chapter

Douglas Nairn had one line per building and it was a spreadsheet and it had been the same spreadsheet since 2019.

> **MARLBANK PRIMARY. Ref 6.11.0193. Built 1931. Two storeys. Site level +0.42 m AOD. Ground floor internal +0.38 m. Flood risk: site within modelled extent, 2019 review. No basement. Last condition survey 2019.**

"**That is a building,**" said Douglas Nairn. "**And I have four hundred and six of them, and every one is a building, and I have never once been able to get a person out of one and I have asked three times, twice of a surveyor and once of a woman in a flood office who told me to ask the question in writing.**"

"**Can the two be matched.**"

"**By address. In about nine minutes. There is a matching tool, we use it for school catchment queries, and if I ran this roll against the address file this morning it would return about two hundred and ninety matches and about ninety that either did not match or matched to more than one.**" He closed the file. "**And I am not allowed to run it, and I want to be exact about why, because it is not my rule and I cannot see the exceptions to it. The rule says a record that links a child to an address may not be created outside the education system. The exceptions are in a schedule I have never been shown and there is a person whose office holds the schedule.**"

"**Say the reason in your own words.**"

"**The reason in my own words is that if I make it then there is a file, and the file has a child in it, and the child is not the person, and the parent is the person, and the parent is the policyholder or is not, and I have just built a record of who a household is and I am the caretaker of another building.**" He squared the file. "**I have a spreadsheet with a building in it and a roll with a household in it and I am not allowed to be the man who joins them, and I have been thinking about that for about six minutes and I have got nowhere.**"

"**The reason is the second floor,**" said Mara Iles, "**and I am not going to say it in a school, and I have not said it in a room since the fourth of June, and I have said out loud four times since the fourth of June that I have a rule about it and I have never told anybody the rule.**"

Nobody asked her what the rule was.

"**Say what the join would give you,**" said Hazel Cray, "**because I want to know whether I am asking for a document or for a person.**"

"**It would give you about two hundred and ninety children whose ground floor is on or below the modelled extent,**" said Mara Iles. "**And then it would give you nothing, because those two hundred and ninety children are not policyholders. Not one of them. The household is the policyholder at an address on the date and a child at an address is not a household, it is a dependant, and the whole of the money in this flood goes to a household and not to a dependant, and so the most legible household record in this district is a record of about three hundred and eighty people who cannot claim anything.**"

She put both hands on the table.

"**That is the whole of the answer and I have given it to a school instead of to you and I would like somebody to say whether that was a kindness or an evasion, because I do not know.**"

"**It was both,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and it was you doing in a school what I did in a room on the fourth of August, which is put a number in the gap and let the number sit there.**"

## And on the Wednesday afternoon the caretaker's own line on the gym wall is the only date in that school, and it is twenty-three inches, and it is a height and not a level, and a man of fifty-five with a rule in a van on a yard is the only person in this county who could turn it into one and he has said he will not

Nobody had asked about the wall and the caretaker told them about it anyway, in about nine sentences, in a doorway.

His name was **Colin Prowse** and he was sixty-three and he had been in the building twenty-nine years and he had a key to every door in it and he had been a plumber before he was a caretaker and he was the sort of man who knows what a measurement is for.

He had painted a line in white emulsion on the gym wall at the height of the top of the water, and he had written a date under it, in his own hand, and the date was the nineteenth of June.

"**Twenty-three inches,**" he said. "**I put the brush on the Friday, the ninth of July, three weeks after, and I measured it off the skirting with a tape and I have written the date on it, because I am not daft.**"

"**Twenty-three inches off what.**"

"**Off the skirting.**"

"**Off the floor,**" said Colin Prowse. "**And that is the whole of it and you have all come down here for two days to be told it by me, and I know what it is, because I have been a man with a rule in my hand for forty-one years and a rule is only a number if somebody knows where the ground is. You cannot take twenty-three inches off a floor and put it on a river. You would have to know where that floor is and I have never been told and I have asked twice, and both times I was told it is not my building, and it is not my floor, and I am the caretaker.**"

Elias Venn had been at the end of the table and had not said anything for about a quarter of an hour.

"**Twenty-three inches,**" he said. "**And a height with nothing under it is not a wrong answer. It is a different thing from an answer.**"

"**Is that yours or is it off a bit of paper in a van.**"

"**That is mine,**" said Elias Venn, "**and it is off a man of fifty-five who said it to me in a lock yard in June and who has said it four times since, and he is the only person in this county who can turn your twenty-three inches into something a river will recognise, and he has told me in a car park on the ring road that he is not going to print a height for a street that has not got one, and I have believed him every time and I am not going to ask him again this week, and I wrote that down on Monday and I have not rung him.**"

"**Good,**" said Colin Prowse. "**Because if you had rung him I would have heard about it.**"

## And the school asked one question at one door, and got eleven answers, and put the eleven answers in a drawer, and there is no rule in nineteen years for a drawer

On the Thursday afternoon Ina Mordaunt did the thing that four professionals had all told her not to do and nobody could stop, because it was her building and her roll and her staff.

She asked one question, at the door, of the households of the eleven children whose ground-floor classrooms had been under the caretaker's line on the night of the nineteenth of June. The question was not about water. It was: *is there anybody at your address who is not on our roll and who we should know about.*

Eleven households answered. Four said no. Three said yes and gave a name. One said yes and gave no name and would not be written down. Two were not at home and were written down as not at home. One asked, on the doorstep, whether she had to say, and the business manager said no, and the woman said that was the first anybody had said to her since the flood.

The eleven answers went into a drawer in an office on the first floor with a pad on top of them, and there was no form in nineteen years for a drawer.

"**I have four hundred and six buildings in my file and a box on each one,**" said Douglas Nairn. "**And the council has a system, and a retention rule, and a subject access request somebody could make, and if those eleven go in a school drawer then they are in a school drawer for ever, and the school cannot answer a subject access request because it is not a data controller of anything, and there is a woman of about forty-one in a school in another ward who would know what to tell you and I do not have her name.**"

"**I know it is not right,**" said Ina Mordaunt. "**I have known it since about ten past two this afternoon and I did it anyway, and I would like somebody to write down that I knew, because I have watched about four hundred meetings in this district and I know exactly what happens to a thing that somebody knew was not right.**"

"**Then what have you done,**" said Hazel Cray.

"**Eleven answers in a drawer and a question on a door,**" said Ina Mordaunt, "**and about two hundred and ninety children on this roll who live on or below that line, and I have not written that number down and I am not going to, and it is the only figure in this school that everybody knows and nobody can produce.**"

She looked at the end of the table.

"**And the other thing is a line of white emulsion on a gym wall with a date under it in a man's handwriting, and that is the only date this school has, and it is twenty-three inches off a floor, and it cannot be checked, and if anybody tries to check it they have to go and find a man of fifty-five and he will not.**"
