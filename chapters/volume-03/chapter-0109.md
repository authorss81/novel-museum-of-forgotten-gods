# Chapter 0109 — Schedule F, And A Power In A Box

**On Wednesday the twenty-third of June 2025 four photographs were countersigned at the point of making and taken on a wharf about two hours after they were the only photographs anybody in this case wanted, and on the Thursday the twenty-fourth a woman of about forty-eight issued an instrument on a form with a box in it for a period, and at nine o'clock in the morning four people and two surveyors stood on a quay in front of an iron door and did not open it, and the reason was a piece of paper with a number on it, and the paper was right.**

**Schedule F. Flood-emergency access suspension. Numbered, dated, signed, and in force from the twenty-fourth of June 2025 to the eighth of July 2025 inclusive, and there is no appeal in it and no box for a person and no box for a reason other than the one it printed. It is the reason nothing on that wharf can be got at for a fortnight, and it is the reason about nine hundred addresses' worth of paper cannot be counted by anybody before the middle of July, and it is correct.**

---

## And on the Monday morning a Director of fifty-three countersigned four exposures at the point of making, on a telephone, in a corridor, having said on the tenth of June that she would do exactly that and would not enjoy it

Ivo Pell telephoned at about nine on the Monday morning and did not have to explain anything, and the four exposures were made at about eleven past ten on a quay four miles downriver with a woman of fifty-three on a mobile telephone at the other end of it, and the countersignature is on the back of each print in a hand that is not his, and it says the date and the time and the place and the words **COUNTERSIGNED AT POINT OF MAKING**, and it is the first thing this case has produced on a camera since the eighth of December 2024 and it is the right way round and it is thirteen days late.

He took four photographs and they are all of the same wall, and in all four of them the same thing has happened.

"**That is not a line,**" said Wendy Ockley, who was about fifty-four and had one van and four people and had been in the salvage trade for twenty-six years, and who had been on that quay since about seven. "**That is a stain with a top edge on it. That is what happens when water comes up over a coping, goes through a ventilation and settles into the top of a wall as the water goes down. A line is a line. That is a stain that is about a foot above where the line was, and it is going to be there in August.**"

"**And the line is still there under it.**"

"**The line is still there under it, and it is on the inside of the ventilation grille as well, and I can put my thumb on both.**" She put her thumb on it. "**The tide is going to go above the June mark again inside a fortnight, and everybody in this city knows it, and the forecast on Monday said the same thing in a sentence at the bottom that nobody read.**"

He put the camera down.

"**Wendy. I was on this quay on the tenth of June at about half past three and I did not take that wall. I had a camera and I had a condition and I telephoned and was told to come back on the Monday, and the Monday is today, and the thing I came for is a stain with a top edge on it instead of a line.**"

"**Then somebody in a building on a hill has cost you the clean one,**" said Wendy Ockley, **and meant it kindly, and then said, in about four seconds and with total precision:**

"**And the two of you got it right, and I want that said in whatever you write down, because the rule is not stupid. The rule is a man in an office four miles away who has never been on a wet quay being quite sure that whoever says they took a photograph was not asked to take it by somebody with an interest. It is a good rule. It cost you the clean line and it stopped about four hundred exposures that would have been taken on a wet quay at four in the morning by a man with a clipboard. I have seen about nine of those and every one of them was a lie, and I could not have proved one of them.**"

## And the woman of fifty-four who said that is the second person on that quay to say the true thing about a form inside forty-eight hours, and on Wednesday afternoon a fourth person arrived and asked to see a building and was told no

Nobody sent her. That is the sentence that matters and it is the reason what happened on the Thursday happened, and it is about nine seconds long.

Yvette Carrow was forty-eight or forty-nine and had a duty under a schedule and about eleven years in the post and had spent the morning at the district office in Bankfoot with a preliminary view sheet in front of her, and she came out to the low bank at about half past two on the Wednesday afternoon because the preliminary view sheet has a line on it that says *inspect premises at the wharf, district of Ferry Bank, stores and outbuildings, and report on condition of bank*.

Nobody asked her to come to a quay. The sheet asked for a building. She walked about four hundred feet along it and stopped at a wall.

"**How long have you been doing this.**"

"**Six weeks.**

"**Right.**" Wendy Ockley had not stopped working. "**You are going to want to look at the ventilation grilles and the cill and the outside of the doors and the underside of the capping, and I would do it in that order, and I would do it with a torch and not with your eyes, and I would take about forty minutes and not nine.**"

"**Why forty minutes.**"

"**Because if you take nine you will write a preliminary view and if you take forty you will write a different one, and mine is free and yours is not, so do not let me stop you doing nine.**"

They did it in about fifty minutes. The bank between the quay and the store had moved — a stone revetment, and two courses of it gone, and a void behind, and about nine centimetres of horizontal movement in a joint. Nobody had to be told what that meant; both of them looked at it and neither of them said anything for about eleven seconds.

"**Right,**" said Yvette Carrow. "**When does the tide next reach that capping.**"

"**Thursday morning, before noon, on a spring tide, and again on about the eleventh,**" said Wendy Ockley.

"**And the next one after that.**"

"**You have got a table in your car.**"

"**I have got a table in my car and a statutory instrument in my bag and I have to have a view before I can use the second one, and the view has to be in writing and dated, and I will write it tonight and it will be about four lines long.**"

## And the refusal is in writing and it is numbered and it is dated and it has a box in it for a period, and the officer who signed it had not seen a piece of a single document belonging to this case and does not know that any of it exists

> **CITY FLOOD OFFICE**
> **SCHEDULE F — FLOOD-EMERGENCY ACCESS SUSPENSION**
> **Reference: CFO/25/SF/0194**
> **Premises: the bonded store and all outbuildings, yards, quay and revetment adjoining, [wharf], district of Ferry Bank.**
> **SUSPENDED. No person shall enter the premises above described between the date and time below and the date and time below.**
> **FROM: 24 June 2025, 06:00.**
> **PERIOD OF SUSPENSION: to 8 July 2025, 23:59 — *box for a period***.
> **GROUNDS: (a) recorded bank movement at the revetment adjoining the premises, inspected 23 June 2025; (b) forecast tide levels exceeding the June 2025 mark within the period; (c) loss of access for emergency services.**
> **DATED: 24 June 2025.**
> **SIGNED: Y. Carrow, Flood Emergency Powers, City Flood Office, Wharf Street.**
> **This suspension may be lifted early on written application. It cannot be appealed. Applications under this Schedule are not persons and are not recorded.**

"**Box for a period,**" said Elias Venn, reading it in a corridor at about half past nine on the Thursday morning with a man of twenty-five standing next to him, **and then he said, in about nine seconds, and it is the ninth of nine things he has said in four weeks that were correct:**

"**The period is the only box in it. There is no box for a person and there is no box for a claim and there is no box for a document and there is no box for a freeholder of seventy-four who has never been inside her own building. There is a box for a period, and the period is fourteen days, and it runs from a date to a date, and on the eighth of July the door comes off the latch again and nobody in this building has any idea what is going to be behind it.**"

"**And the eight other people who have signed something in this city since December have all left a box for themselves in it,**" said Ivo Pell.

"**Yes. And the reason they left one is that they are not stopping anybody from a building. She is stopping herself.**"

## And at nine o'clock on the Thursday morning six people stood on a quay in front of an iron door and the door did not open, and every one of them had a document, and four of the six documents were correct

They had arranged it on Saturday. The freeholder, her agent, a licensed officer, and two surveyors, at nine o'clock, with a schedule of the contents to be produced. They had a letter from the bond-holder's agent dated the twentieth confirming the attendance. They had a scaffold tower going up on the Friday and a van booked and a shutter to be cut and a bonded-goods seal to be broken in front of a licensed officer.

"**I am not going to cut the shutter,**" said Ama Osei, who was thirty-six and a licensed officer and who had done eleven of these and had never done one on a quay. "**And I want it on my file that I said it at about nine and not at about eleven, and that I said it before anybody argued with me, and that I have been asked to attend and have attended and I am not able to enter.**"

"**You are not able to enter.**"

"**I am not able to enter a suspended premises, and a suspended premises is a premises entered by nobody, and the whole of the point of the bond is that the seal is broken in front of me or not at all, and I cannot be on the quay and not in the building, and there is no version of this where the two things are compatible.**" She put the letter down. "**And I want to be exact about what I am saying. I am not saying they have done anything wrong. They have four documents and all four of them are correct and mine is the fifth and mine says the same thing, and the reason it says the same thing is that everybody in this trade is working to the same rule and the rule does not have a clause in it for a building that a bank is falling away from.**"

"**Is it true.**"

"**It is true. The revetment has moved. The tide is going above the June mark on Thursday morning and again on the eleventh. I have measured the joint myself with a tape and I got nine centimetres and I would not argue with the nine centimetres.**"

"**Then you are on the same side as us.**"

"**I am on the same side as the bank,**" said Ama Osei. "**I am not on anybody's side and I would like it recorded that the two of those sentences are different and that I have thought about the difference for about a minute and a half and I am still not sure which one is true.**"

## And the freeholder read the order on the quay and then asked the only question anybody asked that day, and it was the right one, and it was answered in about eleven words

Prudence Halloway was seventy-four and had stood on her own wharf twice in a week and had not been inside her own building in thirty-eight years, and she read Schedule F standing up, on the quay, in the rain, with a surveyor's clipboard held over it.

"**It says I cannot go in,**" she said. "**I am not going to argue with it. My agent advised that the contents would be scheduled on the twenty-fourth and I have had a scaffold tower standing on this quay since Friday for a building I have not been inside since 1987, and that is my money, and I am not going to say the word outrageous, because I have had a very good week of being frightened and I am not going to insult it.**"

"**What is your question.**"

"**This.**" She turned the sheet round and put her finger on the box. "**Two weeks. Somebody in an office has put two weeks in a box. What happens on the ninth of July if nothing has changed.**"

Yvette Carrow, who had come to the quay at about ten past nine because the sign on the door is on the premises and nobody had told her that was a problem, answered in about eleven words.

"**Then somebody in an office writes a second one, or does not. There is no hearing and there is no appeal and I will be the person who signs it, and I have not decided, and I will not decide on this quay.**"

"**Thank you.**" The freeholder put the sheet down on the surveyor's clipboard, which was the wrong thing to do and which she corrected at once. "**I am sorry. I have no authority to put a city document on your board and I am not going to do it twice.**"

"**It is all right. It is a wet morning and about nine of us are watching and none of us is going to say anything about it later.**"

## And she said the cost out loud, twice, before she signed anything at all on the Wednesday evening, and nobody in that building was going to do that and did not

"**Before I sign this I am going to say what it costs and I want it in the file, and I am not going to be thanked for saying it and I am not going to be argued with either.**" She was in an office on Wharf Street at about seven in the evening on the Wednesday and there were two other people in it. "**It is fourteen days. In fourteen days nobody lawfully enters that quay. Not the freeholder, not a fire officer, not a surveyor, not a bonded-goods officer, not a person from this office. It is a fortnight in which about nine hundred addresses' worth of salvage paperwork sits in an office on that quay and about six hundred and forty tonnes of water sits in a room on that quay, and about a hundred and forty years of somebody's record of who drowned in this city sits in that room, and nobody can touch any of it.**"

"**And the low bank.**"

"**And the low bank, and this is the half that will get quoted. Access to about sixty ground floors on the low bank is suspended from tomorrow morning while the bank is being watched, which means no assessor goes in, which means no claim gets a cause of loss confirmed, which means a household that has already been waiting since December waits a further fortnight for the same visit with a different person.**"

"**Is that necessary.**"

"**On the evidence I have got, at half past five this evening, yes, and I would rather be proved wrong in a fortnight than be proved right in a hearing in November.**" She squared the form. "**And there is a second thing and it is the reason I do not like this instrument and I am signing it anyway. I have got no power in this form to give anybody a reason. I have got no power to see anybody's claim, their tenancy, their letter, their photographs, or anything at all about any person affected. So I am not suspending access to a building. I am suspending access to a building, and I am doing it without ever having to know who is in it, and I have discovered that in about ten minutes that this is the easiest instrument I have ever signed and I would like somebody to explain to me why that is a bad thing.**"

Nobody explained it to her. She signed it at about ten past seven.

"**I know what this is doing,**" she said, to a man of thirty-two who was in the corridor afterwards and had not asked. "**I am doing it. I want that on the page and I want it in your book if you keep one, and I am not frightened of what you are going to write because there is not a line in this form about what you are going to write, and if there were one I would have to decide about it, and I have enough decisions.**"

## And a Director of fifty-three telephoned the flood office on the Wednesday afternoon and was asked one question and answered it and found the answer worse than a refusal

"**She asked whether this Museum held any record of the premises,**" said Anja Ward, **on the Thursday morning, in about nine seconds, on the telephone, to a man of thirty-two who had not asked her anything.** "**I said no. She said thank you. And then I said, and this is the part I have not stopped thinking about, I said: we hold no record of the premises, of the district's flood claims, of the street, or of the people, and we have never held one, and I would be grateful if that were written down anywhere, because in about four years a building that holds nothing is going to be described as a building that was careful.**"

"**She is right.**"

"**She is right and I was not careful and I have been in post nineteen years and there is a great deal of that nineteen years that consists of being careful about the wrong things.**" A pause. "**And she did not say good. She said thank you and she wrote something down and she stopped, and it is about eleven weeks since anybody in this city thanked me for a piece of paper I did not have.**"
