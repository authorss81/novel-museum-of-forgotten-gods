# Chapter 0111 — For The Excess

**On Monday the fifth of July 2025 a claim that had been open since the fifth of June reached the thirtieth day it was allowed, and because the claimant had asked for a review in writing on the Friday, four people sat in a small room on the second floor of a building on Wharf Street from about half past ten in the morning until about twenty past twelve, and in that room a woman of about thirty-four said out loud, in her own words, for the first time in nine years, what the fourth box on page one of a two-page instruction is for, and she said it correctly, and nothing whatever happened.**

**Nobody in that room could do one single thing about it, and she was not rescued, and she is not rescued later in this volume either, and the reason she is the one who is trapped is the reason she is the one who knows. This chapter gives the answer to the question Chapter 110 asked in a hall and nobody answered, and the answer is four lines long and it has been printed since 2011, and it is *for the excess*, and the excess is seven hundred and fifty pounds once per household per event, and nobody in this city has ever had a meeting about it.**

---

## And a claim that has been open since the eighth of June reaches the thirtieth day on a Monday, and a review is a thing a claimant is entitled to ask for, and the room it happens in is a room with a table and four chairs in it

Roisin Mellor had asked for it in writing on Friday the second of July and had been given the Monday, and the reason she had asked for it was not that she had found anything. It was that the letter said thirty days from the date of the letter and she wanted somebody to say the date out loud to her in a room.

"**I have got a grandmother of eighty-two who does not know what day it is,**" she said, on the telephone, on the Friday. "**And I have had seven hundred and fifty pounds in my head since the eighth of June and I would like somebody to tell me in a room whether it is coming.**"

There were four people. Marianne Cope, who had been handling it and who had read the refusal letter out in the same chair on the eighth of June. Sinead Molloy, who was forty-seven and had been in the same handling team for twenty-two years and had never handled that claim and was there because a review is attended by somebody other than the person who made the decision. Roisin Mellor, who was thirty-four. And a man of thirty-two with no post, who was not the policyholder and not the claimant and not an adjuster and did not work for the company that wrote the policy, and **there is no box on the review form for him, and nobody wrote his name down, and he was in the room for an hour and fifty minutes and there is no record that he was ever there.**

"**Are you allowed to be in this.**"

"**I am allowed to be in a room if the claimant asks me to be in it,**" said Marianne Cope. "**That is not a rule, that is four months of me telling you the same thing, and I have still not found it in writing anywhere and I have stopped looking.**

---

## And she said it the way she would say it, which is as a schedule, and a figure, and a place a thing attaches to, and it took her about forty seconds and she did not use the words *excess settlement* once

She had a clean copy of the two pages in a plastic wallet and she did not take it out. She had it in her hand as a shape.

"**Right. I am going to say it and then I am going to stop, and I am going to say it the way I would say it to a contractor, and not the way you would say it at somebody's table.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**A date of loss is for attaching an excess to a household.**" She said it flatly. "**That is the whole of it. Not the bill — you have heard me say that already and I am not going to say it again. The bill does not want a date. The bill wants a list of things and what they were worth, and if you cannot prove what a thing was worth you get nothing on it, and that is a different system and it has its own hardship in it and nobody in this office is paid to care about it. The date belongs to the other one. The date is how an excess gets onto a claim.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**An excess is a flat sum. It is payable once in respect of each household in respect of each event. One event, one payment, one household. The household is the policyholder at an address on the date. The attachment is by reference to a date and not otherwise. And where the date is not evidenced, the settlement cannot be attached and the claim is referred.**" She turned the wallet over in her hand without opening it. "**Seven hundred and fifty pounds. Once. Per household. Per event. There. That is what the box is for.**"

Nobody in the room said anything, and she had not said it in a hall and she had not said it to a claimant and she had said it in a small room to four people on a Monday morning, and it had taken her about forty seconds.

"**You have said that to about four people before.**"

"**I have said that to nobody, because in nine years I have said *the form answers what the field is and you are asking what it is for* to four people and refused the question four times, and on the twenty-eighth of June I said the same sentence in front of nine people and told them it was a wall.**" She put the wallet in her drawer. "**And it was a wall and it was not an answer, and the answer is four lines long, and I have had it since 2016, and I am going to tell you the rest of it and then I am going to be no use to you for about a week.**"

---

## And then she said what it does, and what it does is a thing that happened to a household in a district where the ground floor was a foot under water, and she did not say it gently

"**Now the part that nobody in this building has ever said to a claimant, because it is the part that makes the form look cruel, and the form is not cruel, it is arithmetic, and arithmetic has no opinion.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**A household cannot be attached to anything without a date. So a household that cannot produce a date is not attached. And a household cannot produce a date when the only documents that would evidence it are inside the building. And the building is the thing that went.**" She was looking at the table. "**Your tin was on top of the wardrobe in the front room and the front room has two feet of silt in it and the wardrobe is a shop-bought one from about 2011 and it is on its side now and I am not going to be told otherwise by anybody. A rent letter would have done it. A meter. A bank. A doctor. A tenancy. All of them were in that flat and all of them are in a cupboard under a staircase in eleven inches of water.**"

"**And nobody has done anything wrong.**"

"**Nobody has done anything wrong at any point in this claim, and I want that on your own book and not only in mine, because the sentence I am about to say is going to sound like a complaint and it is not a complaint. It is the truth about who the form is for. The form is for a household whose paperwork moved to a relative's, or to a storage place, or to a skip, or nowhere. It is not for a household whose paperwork was in the water. And the households it is not for are the ones the water went to.**" She stopped. "**That is arithmetic. I have known it since 2016.**"

---

## And she said the thing she had never said out loud in a room, and it is a count and a print run and a date, and there is no conspiracy in it anywhere, and by the time she had said it the room had gone very quiet

"**Four hundred and eleven adjusters, contractors and loss adjusters' clerks in this city were issued that instruction between 2011 and November last year. I send the wallets out. I have sent about two hundred and fifty of them myself since December and there is an archive of the previous four prints in a cupboard on this floor.**"

"**And you have said what the box is for to four people.**"

"**To about four, and I have said it out loud to all of them, and two of them have left the industry.**" She said it without any weight on it at all. "**I did not persuade anybody of anything. I read them section B and asked them what they thought it was for. One said the audit trail. One said the statistics. One said she had always assumed it was to do with the household count. And one asked me what I thought it was for, and I told her, and she has not worked in this trade since 2019.**"

Sinead Molloy said, from the end of the table, without any edge on it at all:

"**I have read page one.**"

"**I know you have. You read it in about 2016 and you told me at the time that section B was elegant and I have never asked you a second question about it and I have thought about that a great deal since the twenty-eighth of June.**"

"**What does it change.**"

"**Nothing,**" said Marianne Cope. "**That is the sentence and it is the whole of this chapter. It is not a secret. It is not a cartel and it is not a cover-up and there is nobody in another building who knows and nobody who does not know. It is printed. It is page two, section B, in four lines, and it has been printed since 2011, and there are four hundred and eleven copies of it in this city, and there has never once been a meeting about it. Not one. In nine years. Not in a room of eleven people, not in a team meeting, not in an away day. Four hundred and eleven people have been handed the thing that decides whether a household is attached to a payment, and not one of them has ever sat in a room and asked a second person what it was for.**"

Nobody improved on it. The woman of eighty-two was at home and did not know any of this was happening.

---

## And a man of thirty-two said the thing nobody in that room wanted, and he said it in about nine seconds, and it was the worst thing he had said in twenty-nine weeks

He had been sitting with his hand flat on the table for most of the morning. He had counted the room twice and had stopped counting the second time.

"**You have known it since 2016.**"

"**Since about March of 2016, and I can give you the month because I remember the meeting it came up at, and I am not going to because it is irrelevant.**"

"**Nine years. You have been the person who knew what that box was for since March 2016, and you have been in this office every working day of it, and in 2016 you said it out loud to about four people, and on Monday the twenty-eighth of June, eleven days ago, a room of nine people asked you the question and you gave them a sentence.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**And nobody asked you until the twenty-eighth of June.**"

"**Nobody asked me because nobody thought of it, and I have gone over it since Monday night and there is one reason and it is not a good one. It is that everybody in this building reads the form as a form. You fill a form in, you look for a date, and the date is there or it is not there, and you move to the next one. And a person who fills in forms for a living stops asking what the boxes are for, and I have been in this room for nine years and I have been the person who did not ask, and I have never once noticed that I had stopped asking, because there was nothing to notice on any particular day.**"

"**And you have now noticed.**"

"**I noticed on Monday night at about eight o'clock in a hall with the windows open,**" she said, "**and it has not got better since, and I am telling you that I am not going to be able to help you with any of it, and I want that said in a room rather than in a corridor at about half past three, because you will ask me again and I would rather you knew now.**"

---

## And nobody in the room could do one single thing about it, and she was precise about what she could and could not do, and the list has six items on it and every one of them is true

"**So. What can I do.**" She put her hands flat, the way people do when they are about to be accurate. "**One. I can assess your claim today and pay it if the item is evidenced. It is not, and it cannot be, and you have known that since June and so have I. Two. I can refer it, which means it goes to a referral officer, and a referral officer has the same instruction I have and a worse diary. Three. I can reopen it if the item is produced, and the item does not exist, and the cupboard under the stairs is in eleven inches of water and the water went out in June and the silt stayed.**"

"**Three items.**"

"**Four. I cannot change the instruction. It is not mine, it is the print, and the print is changed by somebody who owns it. Five. I cannot write anything into a file that explains why the item is missing, because the form has no box for a reason and I have told you that once already and I will not say it again. Six. I cannot pay you the money that the decision has actually cost you, which is seven hundred and fifty pounds, and there is no fund for that, and I have looked, and there is not one in this building or in this office, and I have looked twice.**"

Roisin Mellor had been holding the back of the chair.

"**What about the boxes.**"

"**Which boxes.**"

"**The ones in the hall. Nine of them, on the Monday night, and the fourth one down. I asked somebody to read it out and I did not ask the question.**" And she was not going to ask the question, and she was not going to be asked it again. "**I have been in this since the twenty-eighth of June and nobody has asked me one question about the water, and I have noticed that, and I have noticed that the reason nobody asks me is that there is nothing to ask, and I would like it written down that I have worked that out and that I have not been asked and that I am not complaining about it.**"

"**There is one thing I want to ask you,**" said Marianne Cope, "**and I have never asked it, and I have wanted to since June, and I am going to ask it and then I am going to regret it.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**On the night of the ninth, was there anybody else in that flat.**"

Nobody moved. Roisin Mellor stood up from the chair she was holding and put both hands flat on the table, which she had not planned to do.

"**No,**" she said. "**It was me and my gran and a dog we have not had since 2011.**"

Marianne Cope wrote nothing down, and closed the file, and said:

"**Right. And I am not going to be able to do one single thing with that either, and I have wanted to say that out loud in a room since the eighth of June and I have now said it twice.**"

## And on the Tuesday morning a woman of thirty-four went through the claims that were closing that day with a man of thirty-two who is not a party to any of them, and on the list there was one line where the box had been filled in, and what was in it was a date, and it was not a date

The list was a printout on a single sheet and it went down the page in reference order. Twenty-six claims. Twenty-five of them had item four either blank or filled in with something in the correct shape.

> DL/24/11031 · 22 CUTLER'S RISE · 4 (DATE OF LOSS) — *the 9th dec*

Elias Venn read it twice.

"**That is a date.**"

"**That is a day and a month and no year and no way of knowing which December, and it will be December because there has only been one December. It is a date in the sense that it is a day somebody is certain about, and it is not a date in the sense that the instruction means, and section C says the excess is attached to an event by reference to a date and not otherwise.**" She put her finger on the line under it. "**And look at the other one.**"

> DL/24/11044 · 14 ANDER'S ROW · 4 (DATE OF LOSS) — *9 OR 10 DEC*

"**She is not lying.**"

"**She is not lying. She wrote *or* because the water came up over the wall on one night and the men came for her grandmother on the other and she cannot now tell you which night she lost the sideboard, and that is a household that knows precisely what happened to it and cannot put it in a box.**" Marianne Cope read the rest of the line. "**Reported the twelfth. Contents claim, itemised, not an excess election. Seventeen hundred and forty pounds of household goods, a piano, a sewing machine and a wedding hamper.**"

"**And the excess.**"

"**And there is no excess on this one, because she has gone for the itemised list and not the flat sum, and the itemised list does not want a date at all.**" She went down the sheet. "**Which means that the one line on this list where a woman has put down something that is nearly a date is the one line where the date would not have paid her anything, and every other line on this list is a household that either had the date or did not need it.**"

"**That is the worst of it.**"

"**That is not the worst of it. The worst of it is on the next page and there are about forty of them and I am not going to read them to you.**" She squared the sheet. "**And the answer to the question you asked me on Monday, which was what a date of loss is for, is this. It is for attaching a flat sum to a household. And the households it attaches to are the ones whose paperwork went out of the building in a lorry, and the households it does not attach to are the ones whose paperwork went into the water, and I have not got a single thing I can do about either, and I have said it, and now you know, and I would be grateful if you did not make it a better story than it is.**"
