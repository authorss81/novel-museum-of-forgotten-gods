# Chapter 0131 — The Post Box, And Not A Form

**On Friday the tenth and Saturday the eleventh of September 2025 a single sheet went out to about nine hundred addresses in a registration district of about nineteen hundred tenancies, and the sheet has three paragraphs and no name in the third of them, and the address at the bottom of it for the office's answer is a post box in a wall in Wharf Street, and it is not a form, and a woman of thirty-four said one sentence out loud in a room and then said it out loud a second time, and neither time would she have it typed.**

**The office had been given its paragraph by a minute signed on the thirty-first of July by a woman of forty-seven, and the minute says the paragraph will name nobody, and the office is right, and there is nowhere in this city for the answer to go except a box in a wall, and a box in a wall is the only instrument in this flood that a person cannot be found by, and it is also the only one that a person cannot complain about, and nobody in the room on the Friday had a way of saying those two halves in one sentence and a typist of forty-seven said both of them out loud in about four sentences and would not type the sentence that went with them.**

## And on the Friday morning at about ten past nine a woman of thirty-four read a paragraph out loud in a room above a shop in Fisher Lane, and then read it out loud again, and the second reading was the one that mattered

The sheet was one side of A4 and it had been set in a shop on the lane on the Thursday night for about forty-one pounds, and about nine hundred of them had been on the table in a stack, and nobody in the room had opened the box the copies came in.

> **RECOVERY — DISTRICT OF FERRY BANK. JUNE 2025.**
> **1. The water in this district came on the night of Friday the eighteenth of June and went back on the nineteenth. If your household can give a day and a month on which your own front door stopped being a way in and out for you, write it down and send it to us. A day and a month is enough. Do not guess a year.**
> **2. Nothing on this sheet will be used to decide whether you get anything. There is nothing on this sheet that can be used against you. We are asking because a form in this city has a box on it and a box wants a date and about nine hundred people who were in the water could not produce one, and we would like to know how many of you there were.**
> **3. The City Flood Office has not established whom it may speak for on behalf of the residents of this district, and is asking. The office has not consulted with the residents of this district and will not say that it has. It has arranged a place for replies and the place is a post box in Wharf Street, and you do not have to give your name, and you do not have to write anything on this sheet at all.**
> **IF YOU WISH TO REPLY: write anything on any piece of paper. Put your reference from the top of this sheet on the front. Put it in the post box in Wharf Street. Do not put your name on it if you do not want to. There is no form. There is no box on a form for what you would be writing on.**

Marianne Cope read the third paragraph out loud twice, and between the two readings she took her glasses off and cleaned them on her sleeve, which she had not done since about March, and which nobody in the room remarked on.

"**That is a correct paragraph,**" she said. "**And I wrote two thirds of it on the Friday night and I am going to say the other third out loud, and I am not going to have it typed, and I would like the room to hear it said by a person rather than read off a sheet.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**The City Flood Office has consulted with the residents.**" She said it flatly, the way she reads a box off a form. "**There. That is what the sentence is, and it is the sentence I was going to have typed at about half past two this afternoon, and I have now said it out loud twice, once on Monday in about nine words and once now, and both times I have stopped.**"

Nobody in the room said anything for about four seconds.

"**Say why,**" said Sinead Molloy.

"**Say why she has not typed it,**" said Marianne Cope, "**because you have, on Monday, and you have a better reason than I have, and I want yours on the record before I give mine.**"

"**My reason is that it is untrue,**" said Sinead Molloy, "**and I have been sitting in this building for twenty-two years and I have typed every publication this office has put out since 2019 and I have never once typed a sentence I could not find the source of, and the source of that sentence would be a meeting, and there was no meeting, and I looked.**"

She squared the stack of nine hundred in front of her.

"**And I would like to add the other half to mine, because I have not said it out loud and it is worse than mine. If I type that sentence then in about four years there is a document in a system that says this office consulted the residents of this district in September 2025, and somebody in about 2031 will be asked to produce the consultation, and there is no consultation, and the sentence will have been true at the moment it was typed and false by the time anybody looked at it, and the only person who did that is a typist of forty-seven who was working late.**"

"**That is a good reason,**" said Marianne Cope.

"**It is a clerk's reason,**" said Sinead Molloy, "**and I am a clerk, and you are not, and I would like somebody in this room to notice that the two best arguments we have got for anything in this flood have both come from people whose whole job is the typing and the saying of a form.**"

Nobody improved on that, and Elias Venn wrote it down, and then crossed about two thirds of it out, and left the last sentence.

## And what happens to a post box is the turn of this chapter, and it is not a thing anybody in that room had thought about for one second, and the woman who found it out was a woman of thirty-four who had printed the sheet

Sinead Molloy went down to Wharf Street at about half past three on the Friday afternoon with about forty copies in a plastic bag, because the district office had agreed to take them for posting and the district office had a bag of them and a van and a man who comes on a Friday.

"**You cannot post to a post box,**" she said, at about ten past four, on the telephone, from a pavement. "**I am on a pavement. You cannot post to a post box.**"

"**Say what you mean.**"

"**I mean there is no such thing as the address of a post box.**" She was not raising her voice and she was reading a board. "**If you want to post *to* a place you need a postal address, and a pillar box is not a postal address, it is a hole in a wall with a plate on it. What you have given nine hundred people is: put it in the box, and I will find it, because I empty it. That works. That is not a lie. It works today, and it works because the reference on the front is what gets it looked at.**"

"**Then it is not a lie.**"

"**It is not a lie and it is the first time in four months that anybody in this flood has designed something that protects a person and loses them in the same breath and I did not see it until I was standing on a pavement,**" said Sinead Molloy. "**And I want somebody to hear the rest of it because I have worked out why it does it and it took me about eleven minutes.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**A letter in a post box is protected from every form in this city. There is no data controller. There is no retention rule. There is no subject access request. There is no disclosure duty. If a woman writes her address on a piece of paper and puts it in that box then the office cannot be forced to produce it, cannot be compelled to destroy it, cannot be made to explain who saw it, and cannot be asked what was done with it, and in about four years that will be the only kind of document in this city that a person can put something into and be certain that no system ever has it. That is not a flaw. That is the whole thing anybody in this flood has wanted and I have wanted it since July.**"

"**Then it works.**"

"**It works for about nine hours,**" said Sinead Molloy. "**Somebody empties that box at about six. I have never done it and neither have you and neither has anybody in this room, and it is done on a round by a member of the public with a key and a bag, and the round is somebody's job and not anybody's duty, and they are on it on a Friday and they are not on it on a Saturday. Anything in it after six goes on the van to the sorting office and after that it is a letter, and a letter is sorted, and a letter is put in a bag and taken to a cage and a cage is a place where about nine thousand pieces go through on a Tuesday, and a reference written on the front of a piece of paper is a thing a machine reads or does not read, and the only person in this city who can stand at that box and say which envelope is which is the person on the round, and she is not in this case and nobody has her name and nobody has asked for it.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**So the sheet is asking nine hundred people to write down who was in a building on a night and put it in a hole in a wall with my name on a rota,**" said Sinead Molloy, "**and I am a clerk of forty-seven and I have a holiday and I am not the record.**"

## And on the Saturday morning about eleven o'clock something went into it, and by about half past one there were four things in it, and at about four in the afternoon nobody in this case could say who was allowed to open it

The box is on Wharf Street outside a shop that does alterations and it is about four feet off the pavement and it has a plate on it and a keyhole and a slot, and it was emptied on the Friday at six o'clock by somebody on the round with a key and a bag, and the case did not know who and had not asked and could not have found out.

"**So the answer goes in and nobody reads it.**"
