# Chapter 0108 — The Morning After

**On Sunday the twentieth of June 2025 a man of thirty-two walked about four miles of the low bank on his own, with no list and no instrument and no reason, and on Tuesday the twenty-second he walked to a district called Bankfoot, on the other side of the ring road, where nobody in this case has ever been, and knocked at a door on the ground floor of a building where the water had been in and could not be written to, and that is the whole of this chapter and the going is the chapter.**

**Nobody in this chapter is a villain and nothing supernatural happens in it and the moral floor of this case is not said out loud by anybody, and it is acted on, on a doorstep, at about ten past four on a Tuesday afternoon, in about four hundred words, and what is said at the door is not the floor.**

---

## And Sunday morning is a street of men with bowsers and a man of thirty-two who is not one of them, and there is a joke at about ten o'clock that is the only joke anybody makes that week

The water had gone. That was the first thing and it was the whole of what anybody found cheering about: at about eight in the morning on Ferry Bank the low bank was a street with a smell on it and about nine inches of grey in the gutters and a council bowser at each end and about forty men in orange waistcoats working a road they had not worked before the day before.

"**You are not on the list,**" said the man with the clipboard, at about twenty past eight, without any edge in it at all.

"**No.**

"**Good. Because the list is Thursday's and Thursday's list is a follow-up visit list and it is not a list of people who are all right and it is not a list of people who are not all right, and if you are on it and you are not on it you are going to have a bad morning in about ten minutes.**" He looked at the clipboard and then at Elias Venn. "**Who are you with.**"

"**Nobody.**"

"**Right.**" He did not ask again and he did not write anything down, and that was the entire exchange and both of them behaved correctly.

The joke came at about ten from a man in his twenties standing on a tail lift with a clipboard of his own, and it was about a docket.

"**If anybody finds a good sofa, I am not the man to say no,**" he said, to nobody. "**I have got a book with a weight column in it and if you want a sofa in it you have got to tell me the weight of the sofa, and I do not know the weight of any sofa in this city.**"

Two men in orange waistcoats laughed, and it lasted about four seconds, and then everybody went back to what they were doing, and Elias Venn counted the doors between the end of the street and the wall and got to about forty and stopped counting because he was embarrassed, and nobody saw him do it.

## And the salvage trade came in on the Monday because a crew works a Monday, and a man of forty-four had been telling people for three weeks when it would come, and it came

Two lorries at about a quarter to seven on Monday the twenty-first, crew 1 and crew 2, and Colin Bray in a high-visibility jacket with a docket book under his arm, and a different clipboard-writer on each tail lift, and a man called Ferris from South Sluice with a generator and a pump on a trailer who had been sent because a flood-control station is the only thing in this city that owns a pump that works on a Tuesday.

"**I told them Monday,**" he said, to a man of thirty-two on a kerb, at about ten past seven, in the tone of a man being proved right. "**I said Monday, and I said it in March, and nobody believes a contractor about a date. The Monday is the removal. The Saturday is the clearance. The water is supposed to be in between, and this year it was in and out inside a day, so we have got a clearance we did not do and a removal we did, in that order, and that is not a thing anybody planned.**"

"**How many since Friday.**"

"**Nine addresses, and six of them have something in them, and three have not, and I have written three dockets today that say nothing removed and I could not tell you one thing about the three.**" He put a hand on the tail lift. "**That is the honest state of my trade at seven in the morning on the twenty-first of June and I have said it to about nine people today already and I would like somebody to write it down, and the only person who has offered is a man with a notebook who does not have a stamp.**"

"**I am not going to write it down in a place where anybody can check it.**"

"**Then write it down in a place where you cannot,**" said Colin Bray, "**which is what you have been doing since March, and which is why I keep talking to you, and I am aware of how that sounds and I have thought about it.**"

## And a woman of about forty-three was on that street on the Monday evening and she was in a building on Friday night and nobody asked her about it, and on the Tuesday he went and knocked

He had been on the low bank for two days. On the Monday evening there was a woman standing outside a set of railings at about nine o'clock with a carrier bag who was not waiting for anybody and was not going anywhere, and he asked her one question, which was whether the council had been to her mother's address, and she said it had, and that was the end of it.

And then, at about half past nine, in about nine seconds, without any preamble and while putting a carrier bag into a doorway, she said:

"**There is a building in Bankfoot where the water went in on Friday and there is nobody anybody can ring, and it is not on your list because there is no name on it, and I know that because I have been in it.**"

He did not ask her a question. He asked her a question about how she knew and then he did not ask her a second question, and the second question was the one he wanted, and it is the reason that she is not in this book with anything else in it.

On the Tuesday he got a ward map off a board in a library and found the line, and Bankfoot is on the other side of the ring road, and it is about four hundred addresses, and this case has been in one ward for twenty-seven weeks and has never had a reason to be in any other, and nobody in it has ever said that out loud, and it is the most damning sentence available to me this month and I have written it here instead of in a room.

---

## And a building on Ashgill Row in Bankfoot has a ground floor that was in the water on Friday night, and a door that is on the latch, and nobody can be written to it, and that is a fact about four systems and not about a person

He got there at about ten past four on the Tuesday afternoon. The rain had started at about two and had stopped at about half past three and had left everything about as bad as it was going to leave it.

The building is Ashgill Row 14 to 22: nine flats, a ground floor and two above, brick, about a hundred and ten years old, a railings and a bin store and a strip of grass about two feet deep with a palisade fence round it. The ground floor had a tide mark on the outside of the door about fourteen inches high with a clean line at the top of it, and the mark stopped dead at the left-hand door and went across the right-hand one, and the difference between the two doors was about three inches and the difference was a step.

He knocked. He knocked the way he had knocked at about four hundred doors in twenty-seven weeks, low, twice, on the wood, and then he waited.

Nobody came for about eleven seconds and he counted them and was embarrassed about counting them.

The door was on the latch. He said:

"**I am not from the council and I am not from anywhere. I am on my own and I have got a notebook and I am not writing anything down. I have got three things and you can shut the door at any point, including halfway through the first one.**"

She had it open before the end of the sentence. She was about forty-three and she had a cardigan on over a work top and bare feet and she had a bowl of something in her hand, and she did not ask him who he was, and after about four seconds she said:

"**You are going to want to come in and you are not going to, so say the three things from the step.**"

---

## And the three things are his, and they are the third line of a notebook, and he said them on a step in the rain and he said them properly and he was not better than he had been in May

"**One. I am not going to write your name down. Not today, not in a book, not on a form, not anywhere, and I have got a rule at the top of the first page of this that says a fact a person gives me in a street is theirs until it is in a file, and I wrote that rule in January after a postman told me the truth about something.**"

"**Two. I am not going to tell you that anybody is coming, because nobody is, and I have been on two streets since Friday saying that sentence and about forty people have believed me, and it is not true, and I have stopped saying it.**"

"**And three, which is the one that costs me, and I am telling you it costs me.**" He did not move. "**In about a month I will not be able to say that I came here. That is what the not writing costs. I can be here on this step at ten past four on a Tuesday and in four years there will be nothing anywhere that says a man of thirty-two was ever at this door, and I have decided that that is the correct arrangement, and I have been wrong about about four things in twenty-seven weeks and one of them was in a room with nine people in it and it was my own handwriting, and this is the arrangement that is not wrong.**"

She looked at him for about four seconds.

"**Then say the other thing.**"

"**I am not going to ask you whether there was anybody in this building on Friday night.**"

"**Why not.**"

"**Because if I ask it, then in about four years there is a question in the world with an answer in it, and the answer will be whatever you say, and you will say what you remember, and what you remember will be in a file somewhere with a date on it, and you will be a person in a file that says she was or she was not.**" He put the notebook back in his pocket. "**And I have been the other end of a file. Somebody in a building four miles from here said no to a museum twice about a dead man's name on a card and she was right, and I have never worked out how to say thank you for that to somebody I do not know.**"

---

## And what was said at the door was a carpet and a boiler and a decision about a kettle, and it took about eleven minutes, and it is not the moral floor and nobody said the moral floor

"**The front room's got fourteen inches of it and there's a smell that I will not be able to get out for a year,**" she said. "**And the carpet's gone. Not wet. Gone. It was a carpet from about 1994 and the underlay's a black thing and I've had to pull the whole thing up and the boards under it are black and I have a man coming on Wednesday to see whether the joists are.**"

"**Do you have somewhere to be.**"

"**My mother's at number six and she's eighty-nine and she is sleeping in my front room and neither of us has said a word about it.**" She almost smiled. "**And there's a boiler in the hall that is a boiler from about 1978 and it is not wet, and I have had a man in and he looked at it for about a minute and said it will last the winter, and I have decided to believe him because I cannot pay for a new one and the number for the excess is seven hundred and fifty pounds and I have not got that either, and I do not know who to ring about that.**"

"**There is a woman on the second floor of a building on Wharf Street who is the person to ring.**"

"**Is she the one who decides.**"

"**No. She is the one who will tell you the truth about what decides it, and she will not be able to do anything about it either, and I would rather you had the second one than the first one.**"

They stood there for about a minute. It was not raining any more.

"**Do you want a cup of tea,**" she said. "**I am going to make one anyway.**"

"**No, thank you.**"

"**It is a bad cup of tea and it is hot and you have been standing in the rain since about ten past four.**"

"**Then yes.**"

## And a council contractor asked her name on the path at about twenty past five and she gave him a first name and a street and nothing else, and nobody made a thing of it, and it is the only document that exists about either of them

"**Are you the occupier of fourteen?**"

"**Ground floor. Yes.**"

"**Can I take the name?**"

"**I have given you the number and the ground floor. That is what you need for the follow-up.**"

"**Right. Yes. Sorry.**" He looked at her and then at the door. "**It is the follow-up, it is not urgent, it is Tuesday, and if nobody is here they go to next Tuesday.**"

"**Fine.**"

And that is the whole of the record of 14 Ashgill Row, and it is a first name she did not give and a number and a floor, and it exists on a clipboard in a council office, and it will be destroyed on a retention cycle, and there is no version of this case in which anybody can produce a document in which that woman was asked whether there was anybody else in the building.

## And in a corridor of a temporary office on Ferry Bank on the Monday morning a man of about thirty-four in a high-visibility vest said the sentence that puts a sealed store on the map of this case, and he said it as a fact about his own job and not as a warning

Elias Venn was in that corridor at about eleven on the Monday morning for a reason of his own, and a man called Ferris from the pump crew was in it for about nine minutes waiting for a form, and they were both looking at a wall.

"**You were on a wharf on Friday night,**" said Ferris. "**I was, at about eleven, on the pumps.**

"**I was at the north end about twenty to eleven and then I was on the quay at about one.**"

"**Then you know there is a bonded store on that wharf with the doors shut and a man inside it till Saturday morning.**"

"**I know.**"

"**And you know there is a room in it with about six hundred and forty tonnes of water in it and about four hundred boxes.**"

"**About nine boxes moved,**" said Elias Venn.

"**Nine. Right.**" Ferris turned round and looked at the wall for a second. "**And here is the thing I have been trying to work out how to say since Saturday and I have not got it right and I am going to say it badly. If you cannot establish where a person was on a night, then the only record in this city that says where anybody was on a night is the register the Drowned Registry keeps, and that register is city records of people recorded as drowned in this city's floods, and it is kept because a drowned person's estate could not be settled without a death record, and it is in a building on a wharf that was shut on Saturday morning and has not been opened since.**"

"**And there is no other one.**"

"**There is no other one. Not a copy, not a photograph, not a book, not a family. There is a register in a shut building and about a hundred and forty years of somebody deciding to keep it.**" He got his form. "**And I am a pump operator and I have just told a man with no post the location of a city record on the second day after it went under four feet of water, and I have done that because you were on the quay and because I have been thinking about it since two o'clock on Saturday morning, and I would like it noticed that I thought about it for two days first.**"
