# Chapter 140 — The Week Before

**On Monday the eleventh and Tuesday the twelfth of October 2025 six people did about eleven items of ordinary competent work in two days in rooms above a shop in Fisher Lane and in a laundrette on the low bank, and every one of the eleven items was finished, and not one single thing in either day went wrong, and a man of thirty-two was in both days and was good at both, and everybody in the room said that he was fine, and everybody in the room was right.**

**This chapter spends nothing. No memory gone, no minute lost, no name gone, no time out of the world, no second entry of consequence, and nobody has offered him a word of his own to stop on and nobody is going to. In thirteen days there is a week, and he has written nothing about it, and this chapter is about the thirteen days before a week in which nothing is wrong either, and about a laundrette, and about a rota, and about the fact that he has asked one person in this city to be in a room with him and read back anything he says, and has not told her why.**

**The batch ends here and the case does not end. About nine hundred houses have a printed sheet in them with a post box in Wharf Street in the third paragraph, and about nine of them know about a woman's exercise book, and four brass marks are in a wall that only one man in the county can read, and eleven households can say who was in a room and none of them can say how high it came, and four clauses are in a file in a building on Lantern Row with a word in them that nobody in this case has said twice, and a Director of fifty-three has said a thing that a registrar of twenty-eight wrote in a book and a woman of forty-four has not been told. All of that is running. None of it is stopped. Everybody in this case knows all of it and is putting it in order, and putting it in order is the whole of what a team does.**

## And on the Monday morning there were six people and eleven items and everybody in the room had done this before and the room was over by about one o'clock

The room above the shop in Fisher Lane had a table, a window, a radiator that worked and a kettle that did not, and there were six people in it at about ten, and a woman of fifty-eight with a statutory power had a printed agenda with eleven items on it, and the agenda had been produced by a resilience coordinator of forty-seven who was not in the room and had not been in any room since the twenty-eighth of September and had sent it in with a note saying *I am not convening anything today and if anybody convenes anything I will resign, and I have said that in writing and the minute of the thirty-first of July stands.*

"**One,**" said the woman of fifty-eight, "**and it is the only item that is a decision.**"

> **1. That the district office's reply arrangements for the September recovery page are unchanged; that no correspondence is to issue to the emptying round at Wharf Street; that the schedule prepared on 28 September 2025 is to be destroyed unrecorded; and that this decision is signed by name and not by office.**

"**Read the second half of that out,**" said Marianne Cope.

"**The schedule is to be destroyed unrecorded,**" said the woman of fifty-eight, "**and that is the second half and it is mine and I put my name to it, and I want it said that a decision to destroy a document unrecorded is the one kind of document this flood has produced that nobody can appeal and nobody can be harmed by and I have been a housing officer for twenty-two years and I have never destroyed anything in my life and I have never had to before.**"

Nobody argued with it and it took about four seconds.

"**Two,**" she said. "**The eleven doors on the twenty-third and twenty-fourth of September are not to be visited a second time before the end of the year.**"

"**Say the reason and say it in one sentence.**

"**And you have not asked her for the wallet.**

"**And I am not going to,**" said Hazel Cray. "**And I have not asked her whether she has looked at one of them, and I have not asked her twice, and I did want to, and I want it in the minute if there is a minute that there is a housing officer of fifty-eight who wanted to.**"

There is no minute. There has not been a minute since the fifth of July.

"**Three,**" said the woman of fifty-eight. "**The laundrette.**"

## And he was good at the work, and he was fine, and everybody said so, and one thing he said in the middle of it was about order and nobody remarked on it

He read the queue at about half past eleven on the Monday and it was a claim queue for the district of about forty entries with about forty-odd days against them, and he read it out slowly and in order, and a woman of thirty-four corrected him once on a date and he took the correction and wrote it down, and read on from the right place.

"**Say that again,**" said Marianne Cope. "**Say the bit about the order.**"

"**The queue is in date order and the order is the claim,**" said Elias Venn. "**A household is a person at an address on a date, and a date out of order is not a different date, it is the same date in a different place, and about nine hundred households have a date now and forty of them are on that board, and in about four years somebody is going to work down that board the wrong way round and pay a household for a day it was not in the water and not pay a household for a day it was, and the money is seven hundred and fifty pounds and it is the only money anybody in this flood is going to get.**"

"**That is the whole of the case in nine seconds,**" said Marianne Cope.

"**That is the whole of the case in nine seconds and it is on a whiteboard and not in a form,**" said Elias Venn, "**and I have never once been able to say why the order matters without somebody in the room looking pleased, and I have looked for the reason for five months and I think the reason is that I cannot check order, and nobody in this city can check order either, because there is no form for whether a list of dates is in the right order, and a form for that would be a form for whether a person is a person.**"

Nobody remarked on it. The agenda went on. They finished the eleven items at about one o'clock and everything on the agenda was finished.

Nobody has offered him a word of his own to stop on on either day and nobody has ever offered him one and he has written down four times in five months that he is not going to be given one.

## And on the Tuesday afternoon at about four o'clock he went behind the machines at a laundrette on the low bank, and there is a rota on the counter for the week of Monday the twenty-fifth, and a man of thirty-two has asked one person to be in a room with him in that week and read back anything he says
