# Chapter 0141 — Three Days Of Nothing Going Wrong

**On Wednesday the thirteenth, Thursday the fourteenth and Friday the fifteenth of October 2025 the case did its work, and not one single thing in the three days went wrong, and a man of thirty-two was good at all of it, and by Friday afternoon a room had begun to arrange itself around him in a way that nobody in it could name and that he had noticed on the Wednesday morning, four days before the week it was arranging itself for.**

**This chapter spends nothing. No memory gone, no minute lost, no name gone, no second entry of consequence, and nobody has offered him a word of his own to stop on. In ten days there is a week, and he has written nothing about what is in it, and this chapter is about three ordinary days and about a whiteboard that a woman of fifty-eight put three columns on, and about nine people in a laundrette on the low bank with machines going, and about the fact that the case was in that laundrette on the Wednesday because the case had nothing else to do on a Wednesday.**

**Nobody in this case has asked the woman who owns that building what she has agreed to for three days in the last week of October, and she has not told anybody, and she was not asked on the Wednesday, and she was not asked on the Thursday, and she was not asked on the Friday.**

## And on the Wednesday morning at about ten past ten there were three columns on the back of a laundrette counter written in a laundry pen, and the first thing said about them was that they were not a list

The laundrette is on the low bank and it has four machines and a dryer that is older than three of the people who use it, and it is the only warm dry building within about two hundred metres of nine households whose ground floors have not dried since June. The water came off the walls in the first week of September. The floors in some of them are still cold in the middle of the afternoon.

On the Wednesday morning the case came in at about ten past ten because there was nowhere else to be on a Wednesday, which is what a man of thirty-two wrote down at half past eleven that night and which is the plainest sentence in this chapter.

"**I have got nothing to be doing on a Wednesday,**" he said, "**and there are about nine people in this room who have got nothing to be doing either, and about four of them have been in here every day since the middle of September, and this is the first room in five months that has had all of us in it at once with the door shut and nobody wanting anything.**"

Nobody in the room found that remarkable at the time.

The counter is about two metres long and the back of it is painted a green that used to be white. Somebody who works in the building had written three column headings on the back of it in a blue laundry pen on the Tuesday night, and under them there were about nine rows, and every row had a day of the week in it and a stretch of hours in it and a person against it.

The rota is in the hand of the woman who owns the building. It has no addresses on it. It is not this case's document and nothing in this case has written a word of it, and a housing officer of fifty-eight had looked at it for about nine seconds on the Monday of the week before last and had said *that is a rota and not a list* and had said nothing else about it.

"**Do not read it out,**" said Helen Venn, from behind a machine.

Nobody was going to.

"**I am not saying do not read it out because it is mine,**" she said. "**I am saying it because there are nine rows on it and eight of them have got a name on them and I have had this counter since 2011 and I could tell you which of those eight are not coming in today.**"

She turned a machine off, and then turned it back on, which is the only fidget she has.

"**And the ninth one is mine,**" she said, "**and on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday of the week of the twenty-fifth of October I am in this room from about nine in the morning until about four in the afternoon, and I am not going to be drying anything, and the machines will be going, and I would like that written down somewhere and I would like it written down by somebody who is not me.**"

"**I will write it down,**" said Elias Venn.

"**You will write it down,**" said Helen Venn, "**and you will not say why, and I have already told you that you are not going to say why, and you have already told me that you are not going to tell me in a week either, and both of those were said in this room about eleven days ago and the machines were going then as well.**"

Then nobody said anything about it for three days.

## And a woman of fifty-eight put three columns on the back of a whiteboard on the Thursday and a man of forty-four brought a file of four hundred and six buildings and did not open it, and a claims handler of thirty-four read about forty lines off a card out loud, and everything that was done was done correctly and nobody remarked on any of it

## And on the Friday a man of thirty-two did a thing the room had started doing without him, and he noticed the room doing it on the Wednesday morning and did not say what he had noticed, and it is in this chapter once and it is not in the book in his own hand

He was in the room for the whole of the Friday. He read the board out for about nine minutes on the Friday morning while Marianne Cope went to fetch a file, and he read it in date order and he read it correctly, and when he got to the row for a household on the fourteenth of June he stopped for a second and said that the fifteenth was the date in the woman's exercise book, and everybody in the room wrote that down, and then he asked a question about a row and Douglas Nairn answered it, and then he sat down.

And the thing is this.

On the Wednesday, about twenty past ten in the morning, before anybody had said anything, Elias Venn had stood in the doorway of that laundrette for about four minutes and worked out that a room had rearranged itself around him and that he was not in the middle of it any more.

He worked it out in about eleven seconds and it took him the rest of the morning to be sure of it and the whole of the afternoon to decide not to say it.

What he had worked out was this. On the Wednesday, when Marianne Cope went out of the room at about half past ten to telephone the district office, three people in the room did not stop. On the Wednesday, when he went to the counter to get a dry cloth for a machine that was leaking, the conversation did not stop. On the Thursday, when Hazel Cray asked a question, she asked it of the room and then looked at him, which she has always done, and which he has never before noticed her doing on the first asking and only on the second.

And there is a man of thirty-two who can hold an object, and there is no other person in that room who can hold an object, and a room that has arranged itself around a man it is not asking anything of is a room that has decided what the man is for, and in five months of this case the only instrument that has ever caught anybody has been a record with a name against it, and a room is not a record and nobody can query a room.

He has written down the finding and he wrote it once and he has not written it twice, and the sentence in the book in his own hand is this:

> **The room did it. Nobody in the room did it. On the Wednesday it was a room with nine people in it that had a man in it who could hold a thing, and by Friday it was a room with nine people in it that had a man in it, and the difference took three days and there was no meeting and there is no minute and there is not going to be one.**

He did not say any of it in the room, and on the Friday afternoon at about four o'clock he said something else entirely, which is that a laundry that is also a drying place is a building that has got two jobs and neither of them is on a form, and the woman who owns it agreed with him about that for about nine seconds and then went back to a machine.
