# Chapter 0125 — What A Signature Is For

**On Monday the twenty-third and Tuesday the twenty-fourth of August 2025 the first licences under the second instrument were issued, and a woman of forty-one signed the one line on the form that is for a person, and a man of thirty-two was asked to sign another one and did not, and the argument about what a signature is and is not was had in a room above a shop in Fisher Lane by four people and it is an argument this case has been having since a goods-out docket in October, and nobody improved on it, and the one thing that came out of the room was that nobody could tell a woman of forty-one what her signature was for in a sentence.**

**The instrument is a printed form. It has a box for a period and it has five other boxes and then two more that are for people and one of the two is for a second person, and the second-person box is the only box in the whole of this flood that anybody has ever thought about as a promise rather than as a box, and a man of twenty-five who is not a flood officer explained in about nine sentences what that promise is worth, and then told the man of thirty-two what he had cost.**

## And on the Monday the form came out of a drawer in a building on Wharf Street and it is a form and not a claim, and it has eight boxes and a licence in it, and the eighth box is a promise

> **LICENCE TO ENTER PREMISES COMPRISED IN SCHEDULE F. Reference `CFO/25/SF/0231`.**
> **1. APPLICANT: name / grade or post / body.**
> **2. PREMISES: address, or frontage reference and length.**
> **3. PURPOSE: (a) structural inspection; (b) flood damage assessment; (c) utility works; (d) works required by a duty-holder; (e) other — describe.**
> **4. PERIOD: from (time and date) to (time and date).**
> **5. On completion the applicant shall record entry and exit. The person in receipt of the premises shall sign the receipt line below.**
> **RECEIVED AT THE PREMISES BY: name / relationship to the premises / signature / time.**
> **WITNESS: name / body / signature / time.**
> **6. This licence does not permit entry to any part of the premises not described in box two. It confers no right of entry upon any person other than the applicant.**
> **7. A licence is not a person and is not recorded as one.**

"**Eight boxes,**" said Mara Iles, "**and I have been sitting here for about nine minutes and I have counted them three times and there are eight, and I want that said out loud in a room because everybody who has seen this instrument in the last fourteen days has said *one box* and they have meant one box for a period, and there are seven more, and two of the seven are for people and the other five I have not got to and am not going to.**"

"**Say the important one,**" said Marianne Cope.

"**Number six,**" said Mara Iles. "**It confers no right of entry upon any person other than the applicant. And I have been thinking about that since Monday morning and I have got to say it to somebody, and the somebody is you, and it is this: an officer gets in. A person with a disabled husband does not get in, unless somebody applies on her behalf, and a person cannot be applied for on her behalf, because she is the person, and item two of a claims form is the only identity operation in this business and it is in a different building.**"

"**Then how did she get one.**"

"**She did not get one. She telephoned and she was told what to do and what she was told to do was find out whether a relative or a neighbour or somebody with a duty was willing to be the applicant, and she has been doing that for eight days and she has not found anybody, and she rang me on Monday morning and I am the person who told her that.**" Mara Iles put her hand flat on the form. "**I told her the truth on Monday and I have been sitting here for two days with it, and the truth is that a household cannot be an applicant and a household cannot be a witness and a household cannot be a person in receipt of premises, because a household is a person at an address on a date and this form has got no box for a date.**"

Nobody improved on that for about four seconds.

"**Except that one has,**" said Hazel Cray.

"**It has not,**" said Mara Iles.

"**It has, and I am going to say why and then you can tell me I am wrong.**" Hazel Cray had a folder and she did not open it. "**Box three. Category (e), other, describe. I asked the office on Friday whether a licence could be issued to a person at an address for the purpose of enabling somebody else to enter, and a man of about thirty-one in that building said that the box said *other* and *describe* and that he was not the person who decided what went in it, and then he rang somebody and came back and said yes, and it is in and it took eleven days, and I have got the number of the ring, and I am not going to read the number out.**"

"**Then it closed.**"

"**It closed a box,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and I want to be very careful, because I have spent three days in a school and a kitchen and an office working out what that means and what it does not mean. It means a woman at an address can be the applicant on a licence for her own premises. It does not mean the form has a box for her, because it has not got one and she is in box one and box one says *grade or post* and she has not got a grade or a post and she wrote *none* in the box and the man of thirty-one said that was acceptable and wrote a note, and the note is in your system and not on her form.**"

## And on the Tuesday morning she came, and she signed the only line on it that is for a person, and she asked what it was for, and nobody could say in a sentence

Yolanda Speke was forty-one and had been in a first floor at 18 Ander's Row for nine years and her husband had been in it for eleven and had been in a wheelchair for three years and had come home from hospital on the fourteenth of June.

"**I have got the copy,**" she said. "**And I have read it, which I am told nobody does.**"

"**What did you think of it.**"

"**I thought it was a piece of paper that says a man may come into my hall,**" said Yolanda Speke, "**and that I have to sign something at the bottom to say he did, and that is fair, and that is all I have ever wanted anybody to do in this entire flood, which is to come and then tell me they came.**"

"**That is the whole of what it is,**" said Marianne Cope.

"**Then what is the other line for.**"

Marianne Cope had the form in front of her and she had been in this business for nine years and she had a way of reading a form off a person that made them feel stupid, and she stopped using it somewhere around 2019 and the reason was a review she had to sit through.

"**You have two signatures on that page,**" she said. "**Yours, at the bottom of box five. And one at the bottom of a line that says *witness*. And they are not the same thing and I am going to tell you the difference and then I am going to tell you the part where I cannot.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Yours says that a man came into your hall and you were in the hall. That is a fact about an event and a fact about a person and it is the only line on that form anybody is ever going to look at.**" She turned it round. "**His says that he was also there, and that is all it says, and it does not say what he saw, and there is no box anywhere on that form for what he saw, and in four years that form is in a filing system and the only thing anybody can do with his signature is to go back to the man and ask him what he saw, out loud, in a room, months later, with nothing in front of him.**"

"**That is what a witness is for.**"

"**That is what a witness is for,**" said Marianne Cope, "**and the reason I cannot finish the sentence is that there is a second question and the form has not got a box for it either, and the second question is: what if somebody says you did not let him in.**"

Yolanda Speke looked at the bottom of the page for a while.

"**Nobody has said that to me,**" she said. "**I have been at this house nine years and I have had a man from the council in my hall about nine times and I have signed for two of them and I have never signed for the other seven and I did not know there was a line to sign on.**"

"**There is a line now.**"

"**There is a line now,**" she said, "**and I want to ask the question and I am going to ask it and I would like an honest answer and I do not mind if it is a bad one.**"

"**Ask it.**"

"**What is it for.**"

There were five people in the room and the man of thirty-two was one of them and he had answered a version of this question in nine different rooms in five months and had got a good answer every time, and there is no good answer to this one.

"**I will answer it as a person and not as an office,**" said Marianne Cope, "**and my answer is that it is so that if anybody in about four years says this was never done to this house, there is a person named on a piece of paper who did it, and that person can be asked whether it was.**"

"**And if anybody says I did not let him in.**"

"**Then there is your name on the piece of paper and there is his, and somebody will have to decide which of the two they believe,**" said Marianne Cope, "**and that is the whole of what a signature is and I have been doing this for nine years and I have never had to say that sentence to a claimant and I have said it to about nine hundred officers in training and not once to the person it is about.**"

"**Then that is worth having,**" said Yolanda Speke, and she signed the receipt line, and her name went on a form in a building on Wharf Street and she did not ask to see it afterwards and she was told the reference and she wrote it on the back of her hand with a biro because she had nothing to write it with.

## And on the Tuesday afternoon a man of twenty-five who is a systems technician and not a flood officer said what a second person is worth, in about nine sentences, and then said the thing nobody in the case had said to the man of thirty-two in thirty-seven weeks

Ivo Pell had come in at about two and had been in the room for nine minutes without saying anything, which is what he does.

"**I wrote a condition in this building on the first of December 2024,**" he said, "**and it is in force, and nobody has argued with it, and it is in four pages somewhere and nobody reads it. It says: every photographic exposure of collections material by a member of staff is countersigned at the point of making. Not reviewed afterwards. Not authorised in advance. At the point of making, by a second person, who sees it happen.**"

"**Why.**"

"**Because on the twenty-ninth of November 2024 a man photographed four exposures of a plinth card in this building at about ten past two in the morning, and there was a logged outage on the level-two landing at the same time, and there is no camera on that landing, and the access log shows him and shows nothing else, and for four months the only evidence that the exposures happened was the man's own notebook and about nine hundred words in a shared log saying *a number*.**" Ivo Pell had a folded sheet and he did not open it. "**I have read those nine hundred words. I want you to understand that I have read them, because I wrote the condition, and the reason I wrote the condition is the reason that house had four exposures in it and no second person, and it is not that the man was dishonest. It is that there was nobody to ask.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**That is a second person,**" he said. "**A second person is not a pair of initials. A second person is somebody who can be asked afterwards what they saw, in a room, with nothing in front of them, and whose answer is the only thing that is going to hold anybody's account up. And the second person has to have been there at the moment it happened, because a second person who was not there is a signature and a signature is a signature.**"

"**So the witness line is worth having.**"

"**The witness line is worth having and the form has a box for it and the box is going to be filled in,**" said Ivo Pell, "**and here is the thing, and I have thought about it for two days and I have decided to say it in a room rather than write it down, which is a thing I have never done and which I have learned to do from watching a man of thirty-two do it for five months.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**In about four years that box is going to be filled in by somebody who has been told there is a box. They are not going to be asked what a witness is. They are not going to be trained. They are going to be given a clipboard and they are going to be told to get a second signature on the line, and about nine hundred of those are going to be people who are somewhere else in the building, and none of them is going to know that the whole of the value of the line is that they were there.**" He squared the folded sheet. "**And the day the first one of those is printed off and somebody finds out in about 2031 that the second person on the licence for premises at 18 Ander's Row was a man from a different office who came in afterwards, then the line is not a witness line any more. It is a box with two names in it and it will have been a box with two names in it for years, and nobody will ever be able to say which of the two was lying and neither of them will have been.**"

Marianne Cope said, "**We cannot add a box.**"

"**You cannot add a box and you are right and I have watched four people in three months say no to a form and be right four times,**" said Ivo Pell, "**and there is exactly one thing you can do about a box that is going to be filled in by somebody who does not know what it is, and it is that the second person on the first one is somebody who does know. That is all. You do not change the form. You fill the first one properly and you leave a note about it, and in four years somebody reads the note.**"

He looked along the table at the man of thirty-two, who had been at the end of it for about forty minutes and had said nothing, and who had been asked once, on the Friday morning, by a registrar of twenty-eight, whether he would put his name in box eight of a licence as a second person.

"**And you are the only one in this city who can do that,**" said Ivo Pell, "**and you have been the second person on nothing since the sixth of December, and I have been in this case for nine weeks and I have worked out why, and it is not humility and it is not a rule about the notebook, and I am going to say it once and then never again.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**You have said no to five things in five weeks and one of them was me, and every one of the five has been a thing that would have made you useful, and you have been the most useful man in this case for five months and you have made yourself into a man who cannot be used in the one place where being used is a legal instrument,**" said Ivo Pell. "**I do not think you are wrong. I want that minuted by somebody who does not like me. I think you have decided that the practice at Sillick Lane and the thing on this form are the same instrument, and I think you are wrong about that, and I am not going to be able to convince you of it, and I am going to go and write a note about a witness line and leave it in a folder where it will be found in about 2031.**"

## And the man of thirty-two did not sign, and the reason has not changed since the first of June, and it cost him the only thing he had, and nobody argued with him and a woman of fifty-eight wrote four words on a pad and did not turn it round

"**No,**" said Elias Venn. "**And I said it on Friday to a registrar of twenty-eight before anybody asked me, and I am saying it now in a room with five people in it, and the reason has not changed and it is not that I do not know what a witness is, because a man of twenty-five has just told me what a witness is in front of a claimant and in front of a housing officer, and I have written it down twice this afternoon.**"

"**I saw a knock on a door on the sixteenth of May at about a quarter to eight in the evening and I did not write it down. I have still not written it down.
