# Chapter 0115 — The Instruction, And The Person Who Adopted It

**On Monday the nineteenth and Tuesday the twentieth of July 2025 an answer arrived to a letter a registrar of twenty-eight had written on the fourth of June, and it was a null, and it was minuted as a null, and it said so in a printed form, and the null was in form CI/4 and it named a man of fifty-eight who is alive and has a job and who did not write the sentence everybody in this case has spent seven weeks talking about: he adopted it, in 2011, out of a general claims manual, and he went to work on the Friday.**

**He is not a villain. He has been in post for nineteen years and he is not going to lose it and he was not going to be asked to lose it, and a registrar of twenty-eight has written his name on a piece of paper because that is the only instrument anybody in this case has ever had, and it is the second time in thirty-one weeks that it has caught a person who has not asked to be caught, the first having been a sheet of A4 six days ago with an empty name line on it, and there is no procedure for un-catching either of them and there is no box on the form for it.**

---

## And the letter went on the fourth of June and was due on the second of July and did not come, and nobody chased it for four weeks, and a chasing letter went out on Thursday the tenth and the answer came on the nineteenth, and the answer is a form

The letter was four paragraphs and it asked for three things in order. Was Part 5, section B the product of a consultation. If not, was there a drafting history. If there was not, the name of the person who signed it off.

Mara Iles had put no name of Elias Venn anywhere on it. She had put her job title on it. She had told him on the stairwell on the fourth of June that it would be answered in about four weeks, and she had told him the number of weeks before he could go and count, and both of those had been correct.

"**Twenty-eight days to the second of July,**" she said. "**And then nine days in which nothing happened, and I chased it on the tenth, and it came on the nineteenth at about twenty to ten in the morning, and it is one sheet of paper and it is a form, and the form is the answer, and I have been sitting in this room for about an hour and a half trying to work out what to do with it.**"

"**You have read it.**"

"**I have read it four times and I have not written anything down and I have made two cups of tea.**"

---

## And a null is a result, and a result is minuted, and the first minute anybody in this case has ever had is a printed box on a form that records the absence of an answer

> **CITY FLOOD OFFICE — CLAIMS INSTRUCTIONS. RETURN OF ENQUIRY. FORM CI/4.**
> **Enquiry from: M. Iles, Registrar. Date received: 4 June 2025. Reply due: 2 July 2025. Chased: 10 July 2025.**
> **Subject: instruction to handling staff and adjusters, flood claims, two pages, issued 2011. Part 5, section B.**
> **1. Is the section the product of a consultation? — NO. The section was not drafted for this instruction.**
> **2. Is there a drafting history for the section? — NONE HELD. The section was adopted verbatim from the General Claims Manual (Domestic and Commercial), Part 9, in force from 2009 and not reissued since. A general manual is not drafted in sections and carries no drafting history.**
> **3. The section was adopted into the instruction on 6 March 2011 by N. BRAITHWAITE, technical liaison, policy instructions, Claims Instructions Library, Wharf Street. No other officer adopted it and no other officer is asked about it.**
> **4. The adopting officer has been asked whether he holds any record of the purpose of the section. His answer is appended and is signed and dated.**
> **Form CI/4 is a return of enquiry and is not a record of any person. — issued 19 July 2025, 09:40.**

"**Section three has a name and a date on it.**"

"**Section three has a name and a date on it because I filled in section three,**" said Mara Iles. "**I did not ask for a name. I asked for the name of the person who signed it off, and a man in an office on Wharf Street read my question on the eighteenth of July and typed in the name of the person who adopted the section, because that was the answer to my question inside his own form, and he was not hiding anything, and there is no box on that form that would have let him answer me without it.**"

---

## And the answer is appended, and it is four paragraphs, and it is the best writing in this case that has come out of a company, and it is a man of fifty-eight describing a piece of administrative work he did on a Friday in 2011

Neil Braithwaite was fifty-eight and had nineteen years in post and had been the technical liaison for policy instructions since 2011, which meant that he was the person on that floor who knew what was in the wallets, and he came down to the room at about eleven because the registrar had telephoned and asked him to and because he said he would.

"**I am going to read it out because you asked me to read things out and because I would rather somebody read it than have it handed round,**" said Mara Iles, "**and if I say anything it will be afterwards.**"

> **I was the technical liaison in 2011. I did not write section B and I have never written it. I adopted it from Part 9 of the domestic and commercial manual, where it stood in the same words for about six years, into a new instruction for a product that was expected to run for three years. That is the whole of what I did and there is nothing else in it.**
> **I read Part 5 as a whole once, in 2011. I have read section B since on the occasions when something needed checking, which I would estimate at six times. There is no record of the purpose of the words because the words were not written for this instruction and nobody was asked.**
> **A general manual is written once, by a committee, over about two years, and then it is corrected, and the corrections are dated in the manual's own errata, and Part 9 has not been reissued since 2009. If the words are wrong then the words are wrong in a manual that about four hundred offices use for about nine other products.**
> **The correct place to raise that is the manual's owner. It is not me and it is not you, and I say that as a fact about a building and not as a way of getting rid of you. — N. Braithwaite, 19 July 2025.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**Nine other products,**" said Marianne Cope. She had come in at about half past ten and had been standing at the back with her coat on, because a claims handler has no business in a museum and had said so on arrival and had been allowed to stay. "**Is section B in the manual in the same words.**"

"**Verbatim. Four lines, letter for letter, including the word *evidenced*, which is not a word anybody in this building uses.**"

"**Then the form has been in my office since 2011 and I have sent it out to about two hundred and fifty people since December and it is a photocopy of a page of a general manual,**" she said, "**and I have had it in a plastic wallet, and I have kept the current print and the archive of the previous four in a cupboard on that floor, and I have never once opened the manual.**"

"**Nobody opens it,**" said Neil Braithwaite. "**That is not a criticism. It is four hundred and eleven copies of a photocopy of a page of a book that about four hundred offices use, and the reason nobody opens the book is that the page answers the question the form asks, and it does.**"

---

## And he is not frightened, and he was not invited to be, and he was at work that Friday and he will be at work on Monday, and the room could not decide whether to be sorry for him or to ask him something harder

"**Are you all right.**"

"**I am perfectly all right,**" said Neil Braithwaite, "**and I want to be careful, because I can see what that question is doing and I would rather answer the other one. You have not asked me whether I am sorry. You have asked me whether I am all right, and I am, and I have been in post nineteen years and I am not going to be moved by a form, and if anybody in this city was going to be moved by a form it would be me, because I am the person who puts things in forms.**"

"**Then let me put a harder one.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Do you agree with it.**"

"**No,**" he said. "**And I have not got a better sentence and I have had nineteen seconds to find one and I have not got one, and I would like it recorded that I said no without a reason and that I noticed.**"

Marianne Cope said, from the back:

"**He is right and you have all heard him and none of you are going to say so, so I am going to say the thing that is actually the matter, and it took me two days to get here and it is the ninth thing I have learned this year that I did not know on the first of June.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**The sentence is not his and it is not yours and it is not mine, and there are four hundred and eleven copies of it, and every one of them is a photocopy of page nine of a book that about four hundred offices use for about nine other products. And you have all been asking, for seven weeks, who is responsible for it, and the answer is that there is a man in a room who is responsible for the procedure and the procedure has got an owner in another building that none of us has ever written to.**" She had not taken her coat off. "**And the reason he has just told you that the correct place to raise it is not him is not him getting rid of us. It is the correct place. And I have been doing this for nine years and I have never once sent a question to a manual's owner, and I could do it this afternoon, and I am telling you that I am not going to, and that I have thought about it, and that the reason is that if I write to them and they reissue Part 9 then four hundred and eleven wallets in this city become out of date and nobody who has had one since December will ever know.**"

---

## And a registrar of twenty-eight had to decide what to write down about a man of fifty-eight, and she decided it in about four minutes, and the answer is a name against a date on a piece of paper, and there is no box anywhere for undoing it

She had a card. It was not an accession card and it was not a label and she said so twice while she filled it in.

"**I am not going to put this in the collections system and I am not going to accession it and I am not going to photograph it, and I want that written into the record because in about four years somebody will assume all three.**"

> **RG/26/5. Part 5, section B, instruction to handling staff and adjusters (flood claims, 2011).**
> **Source: General Claims Manual (Domestic and Commercial), Part 9, in force 2009, not reissued. Adopted verbatim 6 March 2011.**
> **Adopting officer: N. BRAITHWAITE, technical liaison, policy instructions, Claims Instructions Library, Wharf Street. Alive. In post. No record held by him of the purpose of the section. Answer of 19 July 2025 appended.**
> **No consultation. No drafting history. No wrongdoing identified. No action proposed against any person.**
> **The section remains in force. It is in the instruction and it is in four hundred and eleven wallets in this city.**
> **M. Iles, Registrar. 19 July 2025.**

"**I have written *alive* and *in post*,**" she said, "**and I have written them because the next person to read this is going to be me in about four years and I am going to want to know whether I am writing about a living person or a historic one, and a record of a living person who did nothing wrong is the hardest kind of document there is and I have never in five years had to make one.**"

"**Why write it at all.**"

"**Because the only instrument anybody in this case has ever had is a name against a date,**" said Mara Iles. "**That is the whole of the reason. It has caught a man who was owed something once. It has caught a bell with a false number on it. It has caught a woman of eighty-two who said yes in a coat in a hall. And if I do not write this man's name then I am only keeping the instrument for somebody worse, and I do not want to be a person who keeps it.**"

"**And you could have written *no consultation* and stopped there.**"

"**I could have, and it is on the form, and the form is on the wall of my office and anybody can read it.**" She squared the card. "**And I have written the name because a name against a date is the only thing in this city that has ever actually caught anything, and because a sentence with no author in it cannot be argued with by anybody, because there is nobody to argue with, and that is the thing that is going to hurt about nine hundred and four claims and it started with a man in 2011 being kind to a manual.**"

---

## And he went back to his floor, and the case has a name and a date and nowhere to put them, and everybody in the room understood the trap before anybody said it out loud

"**There is a thing about this that is worse than the name,**" said Elias Venn. He had not said anything for most of the morning, and what he said next was the ninth time in five weeks that he had said something in a room that turned out to be correct, and nobody argued with it.

"**Say it.**"

"**The man is the wrong one.**" He had both hands flat on the table. "**If he had written it, we would have a person to ask whether he thought it was right, and he would have had to answer, and I would not like it but it would be an answer. Because he adopted it, the sentence has no author, and a sentence with no author cannot be argued with by anybody, and the only person in this city who could change it is the owner of a manual in a building that none of us has the name of.**"

"**That is four times now that you have been right in a room,**" said Marianne Cope, "**and every single time it has been the bad news and I would like you to stop doing it in a way that is so pleased with yourself.**"

"**I am not pleased. I have got a notebook and I have not written in it since Saturday and I am going to write in it tonight for the first time in a fortnight and I am going to write down a name and a date, and I have broken the third line of my own first page to do it, and I did not break it for the woman of forty-three in Bankfoot and I have broken it for a man of fifty-eight who has not asked to be caught.**"

Nobody improved on that either.

It is page nine of a general claims manual, in force from 2009, not reissued, adopted verbatim into a two-page instruction on the sixth of March 2011 by a man of fifty-eight with nineteen years in post, in a job, alive, at work on Friday. He has adopted it, and the answer is in a book in a building that none of us has the name of, and the woman who could write to it in about four minutes has told us she is not going to, and the reason she gave is the correct one, and the reason is that four hundred and eleven wallets would go out of date and nobody who has had one since December would ever know. > **What a date of loss is for. Answered, for the excess, and now traced, and the trace is worse than the answer. A date of loss is for attaching a flat sum to a household, and the sentence that decides it was lifted out of a manual by a careful man who was not asked, and the four hundred and eleven people who were handed it afterwards were handed a photocopy, and not one of them is a wrongdoer, and the instrument that has caught him is a form with three items on it that he filled in correctly, in his own hand, in his own job, on a Friday.**
> **What a legal neighbour is. And what a null is when it is minuted, which is a result, which is the only thing anybody has ever been able to give anybody in this case that was not a worse thing.**
> **And who keeps it. Nobody. And now the section is the sixth. A hardback in a drawer that does not lock. A pad of nine addresses in a locked drawer on Wharf Street. A form in a system that generates an unsigned notice appointing nobody. A sheet of A4 in a folder on a shelf on a hill with a name line left empty on purpose. A hardback of interments in a vestry at Braeswick above a radiator. And now four lines of a manual in a card on a wall on the same hill, with a man's name and a date on it, and a note under them saying that no wrongdoing has been identified, which is the first sentence I have ever written in this book that is a defence of somebody and I have written it about a man I met for fifty minutes and I am not certain it is true.**
