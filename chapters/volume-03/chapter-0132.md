# Chapter 0132 — The District's First Instrument

**On Monday the thirteenth and Tuesday the fourteenth of September 2025 a duty officer of forty-seven with nineteen years in post completed a printed block on the back of a form, in a room at Northglass, in front of four people, and the fourth box is a period, and the period is the whole of it, and the three people watching had no power to stop her and did not try, and she was right, and by four o'clock that afternoon a structural engineer of forty-one had put a level across the same forty feet and proved her right about the ground, and none of that has anything to do with the seven households behind it.**

**This is the first time a status has gone on a frontage anybody in this case can name. The other eight went on frontages nobody could name, and nobody noticed them, which is a thing about a line on a map and not about anybody. This one is on chainage zero plus thirty-one to zero plus thirty-five on the wharf revetment, forty feet, and the case got that off a form on the fourth of August and put it in a book, and about seven households are behind it, and the four people in the room could not name one of the seven, and they had been told the address of the frontage, and it turned out that being told where a line is does not tell you who is behind it.**

---

## And on the Monday morning a housing officer of fifty-eight put a piece of paper on a table and said a sentence she had been carrying for about nine days, and the sentence was that there is no such thing as knowing where a thing is

> **FERRY BANK / WHARF FRONTAGE / CHAINAGE 0+31 TO 0+35. Length 40 feet. The frontages behind it. Referred from `CFO/25/SF/0231`, 15 August 2025.**
> **The above chainage and length were taken from the premises field of a flood-emergency access suspension by a man with no post on the fourth of August 2025 and written into a book of his own that nobody may read. He did not take them from an address. There is no address. A chainage is a distance along a line, and the line is a wall, and the wall is a hundred and forty years old.**

"**Say the thing you have been carrying,**" said Mara Iles.

"**I have been carrying it since Friday,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and it is this, and I want it said in a room and not in a corridor. There is no such thing as knowing where a thing is. A chainage is not a place. A frontage is not a house. Everybody in this building says the word frontage the way you would say the word house and I have been a housing officer for twenty-two years and I have never once let that go and on Friday I found out that it matters.**"

"**Go on.**

"**The instrument named four hundred metres of wall and the frontages behind it. I have spent about eleven days trying to turn four hundred metres into a number of households and I can get you about two hundred and thirty tenancies in a council spreadsheet of four hundred and six buildings, which I cannot run against, and about nine hundred addresses in a book on a quay that I may not have, and about nine hundred claims that are the pay run and not the people.**"

"**And now there are forty feet.**"

"**And now there are forty feet,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and I have had it on a piece of paper since Friday morning and it is the first time in this flood that I have held a thing that is exactly the size of the thing I need, and the reason it is useless is that a line on a map has a length and a direction and a bearing and it has got no people on it, and a man in a room of fifty-five said the same thing to me on the fourth of August and I did not understand it until Friday.**"

Nobody improved on it, and the man of thirty-two wrote down the four words on a pad and turned it round, and the four words were not the four words she had written on the fourth of August and were not the four words she had written on the seventh of September, and this time they were: **a line is not a door.**

## And on the Tuesday morning at about ten past ten a duty officer of forty-seven turned a form round on a table at Northglass and put her pen down next to it and said out loud that she had never once been asked to consider whether it was a good idea, and the reason she said it is that she was not going to get the chance again this morning

Delyth Morgan had the block in front of her and a ruler and a pen and a rubber, and she had ruled the four ruled lines under *period affected* with a pencil before anybody came in, because she always did, and she said that she had been doing it for nineteen years and that it is the only thing about a form that she is proud of.

> **RECORD STATUS — DISTRICT REGISTER. Ferry Bank. To be completed by the district register at Northglass. Not completed by this office.**
> **PREMISES / FRONTAGE REFERENCE .......... FERRY BANK / WHARF FRONTAGE / CH. 0+31 TO 0+35 (40 ft) AND THE FRONTAGES BEHIND IT**
> **STATUS AT (date): ( ) OCCUPIER RECORDED ( ) NO OCCUPIER RECORDED ( ) NOT ASSESSED**
> **PERIOD AFFECTED: from ............... to ...............**

"**Go on from the top,**" said Mara Iles, "**and I am asking because I have been in this building twice since the sixth of June and both times I was shown the front of something, and I would like to watch a duty officer fill in the back of one, and I have asked her to do it in front of us and she has said yes, and I want it on the page that I asked.**"

"**That is fair,**" said Delyth Morgan, "**and I am going to do it in about nine minutes and I am going to say the reason I have agreed out loud first, because there is a woman in this room who has been in my office twice and has not asked me anything and I would like to give her one thing before the end of it.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**I have never once been asked whether it is a good idea.**" She said it without weight, the way a person says a thing they have said to themselves. "**Nine of these since the twenty-fourth of June. I signed the first eight. I signed the ninth on the Wednesday of the first of September, which was a frontage at the north end and I entered the occupier as recorded because a tenancy in a file at the council says there is a person at that address, and I entered the occupier as not recorded on a frontage at the east end because the file says nothing, and I entered not assessed on one where the file says something that is not about a person.**"

"**And nobody asked you.**"

"**Nobody asked me, and it is not my decision, and the block is completed by a duty officer and that is printed on it,**" said Delyth Morgan, "**and I am not going to pretend to be surprised about any of that, and I have a job, and my job is to complete this block accurately when the front of the form has been signed by somebody with a power, and the front of the form was signed on the tenth of August at about ten to twelve by a statutory officer who was right about a joint in a wall, and I have never met her and I have got her name and her minute number and I have got no way of asking her a question about it and I would not if I could, because it is not my decision and she has told me so herself, in writing, on the fourth of August, in a note on a file.**"

She filled in the premises line. Then she stopped.

"**The status is a status at a date,**" she said. "**And the date on this one is the fourth of August, because the referred finding is dated the fourth of August, and the status is what the district register held on that date about the occupancy of this frontage, and what the district register held was: there is no occupier recorded, and there have never been an occupier recorded, and that is because occupancy at a frontage is established from a tenancy, and a tenancy at an address, and a tenancy is not a thing the register holds.**"

"**Then why are you writing it now.**"

"**Because the front of the form came to me on the eleventh and it has been on my desk for three days and I complete it,**" said Delyth Morgan, "**and there is a date in it that has passed and I enter the status as at that date, because a status is a statement about a date and not about today, and if I enter it as at today then I am recording a thing about a period that has already ended and the register would be wrong, and a thing about a period that has ended is a thing a person can check, and I would like this block to be checkable, because it is going to be a bad thing and I would rather it were a bad thing I can be asked about.**"

She ticked the box. She wrote the period in.

> **STATUS AT 4 August 2025: ( ) OCCUPIER RECORDED ( X ) NO OCCUPIER RECORDED ( ) NOT ASSESSED**
> **PERIOD AFFECTED: from 06:00 Sunday 15 August 2025 to 23:59 Thursday 30 September 2025**
> **D. Morgan, duty officer, district register, Northglass. 14 September 2025, 10:26.**

"**There,**" she said. "**And that is the tenth in this county since the twenty-fourth of June and it is the first one on a piece of ground anybody in this room can say out loud, and I have known about that since Friday, because a woman of fifty-eight telephoned me on Friday and asked me one question, and the question was whether the block was on a building or on a line, and I said a line, and she said thank you and rang off.**"

## And four people watched it happen and three of them could have stopped it, and none of the three had anything, and the fourth one had no standing at all and knew it

Nobody spoke for about a minute and a half. The block was face up on a table in a room with a window and a radiator and about four hundred files on shelves behind it, and it was a printed block and it had a form number in the corner and the form number was the same number as the front.

"**Say what you can do,**" said Mara Iles, "**both of you, and I will write it down, and I want it in your words and not in mine.**"

"**I can do nothing whatever,**" said Delyth Morgan. "**This is not a determination and it is not appealable and it does not remove anybody from anything that pays anybody. It goes on the frontage. A frontage is a line on a map and the register keeps lines on maps and has kept them since 1951. When a district's occupied frontages are computed against a boundary review, a drainage programme, an insurance pool, or anything with a number at the end of it, then a frontage whose occupancy cannot be established is a variable and a variable is carried at a value and the value we carry is not occupied. I read that note out in a room on Ferry Bank on the first of September and I read it out again in a corridor on the fourth and nobody has asked me a different question in either.**"

"**And I can do nothing,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and mine is a different reason and it took me longer to find. I have no instrument and no power and I am not a party. And I would like it on the page that a housing officer of fifty-eight with twenty-two years watched a status go onto the only piece of frontage in this flood that anybody has ever been able to name, and that she could have put her hand flat on the table about four times in about nine minutes, and that she did not, and that the reason she did not is that there is no box on earth on which she is a party to a record status and she has known that since the fourth of August because a man with no post wrote it down in a book and a registrar of twenty-eight said it out loud in a corridor.**"

"**And I can do nothing,**" said Elias Venn, "**and I have been asked nothing this week for the second time since the seventh of September and I would like that on the page too because nobody in this city is going to keep a count of it except me.**"

"**What can you do.**"

"**I can say that I put a chainage and a length on a piece of paper on the fourth of August and that it is on the back of a form now, and that I did that, and that I did not know what it was for,**" said Elias Venn. "**And I can say that I have been sitting in this room for about twenty minutes with a frontage that I named, and a duty officer who has never been asked, and a woman of fifty-eight who has no standing, and a registrar who has a duty that is not a power, and every one of the three of them is competent, and the whole of what has happened in the last nine minutes is that a competent person completed a box accurately, and that is the third time in five months that this has happened and it is going to be the mechanism by which about nineteen hundred tenancies lose a district.**"

Nobody improved on that, and the duty officer of forty-seven said, after a while, that she would like it read back to her, and it was read back to her, and she said that was correct.

## And on the Tuesday afternoon a structural engineer of forty-one put a level across the same forty feet and came back and said that the fall across it is about seventy millimetres, and that a fall is not a movement, and that she has known it was a fall since about the eleventh of August, and that nobody has been able to use that for three weeks

Dinah Vassallo had a level and a staff and about forty minutes, and she did the whole of the forty feet in one go because a level does not care how long it takes and she had done four visits since June.

"**About seventy millimetres, from the north end down to the south,**" she said, "**and I have got it twice and it is the same both times, and I want to be exact about what I have found, because everybody in this building has been arguing about a number for two months and I am the one who keeps putting numbers on walls and I would like to say what a number is.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**That is a fall and it is not a movement. A movement is a change and I have got a change on a joint nine millimetres in June and thirteen in August and eleven more at the north end on the first of September, and that is a change, and I have said the rate is the finding. This is not a change. That has probably been there since the wall was built, because a hundred and forty years of a riverbank behind a wall does not stay level, and the ground behind the coping at the south end is about seventy millimetres lower than the ground behind the coping at the north end, and it is lower because the fill was tipped and it has settled and it settled in the first decade.**"

"**So what does that do.**"

"**It does two things and I only want to be responsible for one of them,**" said Dinah Vassallo. "**The one I am responsible for is that you cannot stand a frontage on that. Not a person, not a wall, not a fence, not a line on a map. If the ground under forty feet falls seventy millimetres from one end to the other, then that forty feet is not a place, it is a slope, and there is no version of a district boundary, a drainage programme, an insurance pool, or anything with a number at the end of it that can be computed on a slope, and a register that carries it at not occupied is not being difficult. It is being correct.**"

"**And the other thing.**"

"**The other thing is that it has been available since about the eleventh of August,**" said Dinah Vassallo, "**and nobody in this flood has been able to use it, and I have been carrying it for five weeks and I would like to say why, and the reason is that it is only true about ground.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**On the eleventh of August I took a level across this and it was seventy millimetres and I wrote it in my own book and I have written it in my own book ever since and I have not given that number to anybody, and the reason is the same reason a man of fifty-five has not given a table, and I have never met him and I know exactly what he says because I have been doing the same job for nineteen years and we were on the same training course. A number with a place on it stops being a measurement and becomes a claim about land. And a claim about land in this district, in September, in a flood emergency, is a number somebody can put in a computer.**"

"**So you will not give it out.**"

"**I have given it out,**" said Dinah Vassallo, "**to three people in a room this afternoon, and I have said what it is, and if any of you put it on a piece of paper then in about four years it will be in a system and somebody will be able to query it, and a number that a person can query is a number that gets deleted. I do not mind being wrong about a wall. I mind being wrong about a wall in a document.**"

## And then the woman of fifty-eight said the second half of a thing she said on the first of September out loud, and this time she had got hold of the other end of it, and it did not change, and she said that was the point

She had been sitting at the end of the table for about an hour and a half and she had said very little, and she said it now with her hand flat on the table, which she did.

"**On the first of September I said a thing in that room and I have thought about it every day since and I would like to say it again and this time I have got hold of the other end of it.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**On the first of September I said that there is a household in this flood that nobody is owed anything for, and that it is owed nothing, and that it is not on the docket round and not in a claims file and not on a recovery page and not in a Forum and not in a parish register and not on a school roll, and that its only trace in this city is one line in a contractor's book that says a crew did not attend and removed nothing, and that no body in the room could name it, and that therefore no body in the room could object on its behalf, and that I said that is not a failure of the meeting and that is how the thing is designed to work.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**And it has now happened twice, from both ends,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and the second time we knew the frontage. That is the difference and the difference is nothing. On the first of September we could not find the household. Today we can find the wall, and there are about seven households behind forty feet of it, and I have been the housing officer in this city for twenty-two years and I could not tell you one of the seven, and nobody else at this table could either, and one of the four of us has a statutory power over the water in it and she is not going to use it to ask.**"

"**Say the last part of it.**"

"**The last part of it is that on the first of September I said that and everybody in that room thought it was the terrible thing, and today I have watched a competent woman put a status on a frontage we could name, and it is the same thing, and it is worse, because the first time I was telling you about a household you could not find and today I am telling you about a household you could find and still cannot ask, and I would like it minuted that the difference between the two is that I have a piece of paper in my handbag that says where the frontage is, and I have had it for four days, and it has not helped me once.**"

Nobody in the room could say who the seven were, and nobody had asked, and the block was face up on the table with a name on it and a period in it, and Delyth Morgan put her hand flat on the form for a second, which she had not done, and said that she had checked the arithmetic twice and that the arithmetic was right and that she had checked the *not occupied* box against the register's own holdings and that there was no occupier recorded for that frontage in August, and that she had entered it because it was true, and that she would enter it again on Thursday for the frontage at the north end, which has an occupier recorded, and which she would enter as recorded, and that the two entries together were a district that a computer would settle a boundary against, and that was what the block was for.
