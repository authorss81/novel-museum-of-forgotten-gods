# Chapter 0106 — The Tide

**On Monday the fourteenth of June 2025 a sheet of paper came onto a board in a library in Low Ward with a number on it, and on Tuesday the fifteenth a man of about fifty-five stood on a wharf four miles downriver with a folding rule and a woman of about fifty-five and told Elias Venn what a number on a wall means, and could not tell him whose number it was, and would not try.**

**There is no water in this chapter. The weather on the fifteenth of June was unremarkable and the wharf was dry and the tide was out at about twenty past two in the afternoon, and the whole of what happens happens on paper and in about nine inches of tide line forty years old. The water is in Chapter 107 and nothing in this chapter is a preparation for it.**

---

## And the alert is a printed sheet with a number in metres and a clock time, and it is correct, and the number is a level at a gauge four miles downstream of the district it is about

> **CITY FLOOD OFFICE — RIVER FLOOD ALERT**
> **Reference: CFO/25/131. Issued 14 June 2025, 08:40.**
> **River reach: Cauldwen to Northglass.**
> **Forecast peak level at the Northglass pier gauge: 3.86 m above Newlyn, between 01:00 and 03:00 on 19 June 2025.**
> **Confidence: medium. This alert supersedes the warning at CFO/25/118, which remains a candidate-period notice and is not withdrawn.**
> **In the named districts of Ferry Bank and the lower terraces of Low Ward, residents should act on the warning at CFO/25/118 and not on this level. A level at one gauge is not a level at any street.**
> **Enquiries: Wharf Street. Quote the reference above.**

Gwilym Petherick read it twice with his glasses on and gave it back.

"**It is correct. Every word of it is correct and it has been written by somebody who has stood where I have stood and been asked the questions I get asked.**"

"**Then what is wrong with it.**"

"**Nothing. There is nothing wrong with it at all, and that is the difficulty, and I have been trying to work out how to say the next part to a man in a coat since Monday morning and I have not managed it, and I am going to manage it now by saying the first half and then stopping and you will do the other half, because you are better at that than I am and I have watched you do it.**"

He did not stop.

"**That is three point eight six metres above Newlyn, at the Northglass pier gauge, at about two in the morning on the nineteenth. It is a real number and it was made by a real instrument and the man who made it is good.**" He put a finger on the line. "**And it is about four miles downstream of Ferry Bank, past a bend, and there is about eleven miles of channel between that gauge and the low bank, and a level is not a height and a height is not a level, and the difference between three point eight six at Northglass and whatever happens at Ander's Row is a slope and a channel and a bend, and nobody in this country has printed a table that converts one to the other for that reach, because the Northglass gauge is the funded one and it has been the funded one since 1911.**"

"**And the notice says so.**"

"**The notice says it in a sentence, and the sentence is correct, and the sentence is the reason nobody has got hurt in this city for a hundred and fourteen years.**" He took his glasses off. "**And the notice also says, in the fourth line, that residents in Ferry Bank should act on the earlier warning and not on this level. And that is not a courtesy. That is a body saying out loud that it cannot give you a number for your street and would rather you knew that than had one.**"

---

## And the number on the wall of a house is a measurement if you know what it is a measurement of, and a memory if you do not, and that is the whole of the chapter and it is a professional's sentence and not a god's

They were on the quay at about twenty past two with the tide out and about eleven inches of green on the stonework, and the tide line was where it had been every day of his working life, and Petherick put the folding rule against it and read a number off it and wrote it on a piece of A4 in biro.

"**There. Three point eight six would be about** ***"*** **and that is a level and not a number I have made up, and I will not tell you what it is a measurement of, because that is the whole of your question and I have to answer it in four separate pieces and one of the four is a piece I do not have.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**One. A number on a wall is a measurement if you know what it is a measurement of, and it is a memory if you do not, and there is no third thing, and everybody who has ever looked at a tide mark on a house has pretended otherwise.**"

"**Two.**"

"**Two. The tide mark on the front of these premises is not a mark anybody made. It is forty years of the same level, and I can date it because the wall was re-rendered in nineteen eighty-six and the line crosses both finishes, which means the line is younger than the render and the render is thirty-nine years old. So the line is at most thirty-nine years old and at least one, and I cannot tell you which, and a band of about sixty millimetres at the top of it is lime and it is four years old, and the four years is the only part of that wall I can put a date on.**"

"**And a house.**"

"**And a house. Same answer and it is worse. A chalk line on a wall in a house is not a measurement. It is a memory. It is a person remembering where the water stopped and putting a pencil on the wall, and the pencil is on the wall, and the person is not, and the wall is the only witness, and the wall cannot say whose memory it was.**" He was not looking at him. "**And nobody who does that has ever been asked to say what the level was in any unit, and nobody who reads those lines has ever asked what the wall was measured from, and both of those omissions are the same omission, and it has got a name, and the name is datum, and a memory has no datum in it.**"

"**Three.**"

"**Three. The reason I can give you a level for this wharf and not for Ferry Bank is that this wharf has a gauge with a printed zero on a plate bolted to it, and the plate has a date, and there is a chain of custody behind the zero going back about a hundred and fourteen years.**" He looked up then. "**And Ferry Bank has a flood wall that is older than the district's records.**"

"**How much older.**"

"**I do not know. That is not a figure I am being coy about. The wall is in a 1780s survey with a name on it and no levels in it, and the district's registration records begin in nineteen fifty-one, and there is a document somewhere that joins the two and it is not in this city.**" He put the A4 in his pocket. "**Which means that for a district of about one thousand nine hundred tenancies, there is no printed table that says how high the water has to be at the wall before it is inside. And when somebody asks me that question — and somebody will ask me that question within a fortnight, and they will have a duty and a statutory power and a form with a box for a period on it — I am going to have to say: I can give you the water on the sea, and I can give you the water at a gauge four miles away, and I cannot give you the water on that wall, and the reason I cannot is a piece of paperwork from the seventeen hundreds, and there is nobody alive who is angry about it.**"

## And a woman on the quay said she had a photograph of the December water on her floor with a date on it, and a man of fifty-five told her that a date on a photograph is a fact about a camera

She was waiting for a van. She was about sixty-one and she had a phone in a plastic sleeve and she was pleased about it, and she got it out before anybody asked her for it, and the photograph is a carpet and a skirting board and about an inch and a half of water and a date in the corner of the frame.

"**The ninth of December,**" she said. "**I know it is the ninth because the camera says the ninth, and it is the only thing about that week that I have got.**"

Elias Venn looked at the corner of the frame for longer than he needed to.

"**Can I ask you something and it is a technical question and I am sorry.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Is there anything in the picture that says where it was.**"

"**It is my front room. There is a dado rail and there is a radiator under the window and that is my mother's chair upside down on the table.**"

"**Then you have got two things,**" said Petherick, "**and I want to be careful, because you did not ask me and I am about eleven minutes into your week.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**You have got a date, which is a fact about a camera, and you have got a level, which is the top of that water against a radiator and a dado rail, and the two of them are about four inches apart in the room and about a fortnight apart in the world, and you have put them in one picture and the picture is the only place either of them is.**" He put his own rule away. "**Now. A level is a fact about a building and a date is a fact about a camera. A claim wants a date of loss, and a date of loss is not the date on a photograph, and there is nowhere in this country that anybody has ever written down why the two are different, and the reason nobody has written it down is that it was never necessary until about 2011.**"

She looked at him for a second.

"**Will that photograph get me anything.**"

"**I do not know. I am a river engineer and I have no idea what a photograph does to a claim and I would not guess in front of you.**"

"**That is the first honest thing anybody in this business has said to me since December,**" she said, **and put the phone away, and got in the van.**

---

## And the fourth piece is the one he does not have, and he said so, and then he was asked the question that is the whole of the next four days and he answered it correctly and it was not enough

"**The fourth piece,**" said Elias Venn.

"**The fourth piece is that a conversion table exists.**" Petherick said it flatly, on the quay, with the tide coming back and nothing in his voice. "**In this county. About two pages. In my wallet. It would give you the height of the water at Ander's Row and at a hundred and forty other places, and it is right, and I told a man of thirty-two on Wednesday at a lock house that I am not giving it to anybody, and I am not going to change my mind this week and I would like to be asked again in a different mood in about four weeks.**"

"**Then how does anybody get a number for a street.**"

"**They don't. They send somebody out with a folding rule and a tape and a camera and a clipboard and they stand in a street at about two in the morning and they write down how high it came on that wall, and they sign it, and the document says what the document says, which is *the water reached this height on this wall*, and it does not say a number in metres because there is no datum on the wall, and the document is nonetheless completely true and is worth a great deal to the household and is worth nothing to anybody else.**"

"**And that is why the district can be closed and cannot be opened.**"

"**I do not know what you mean and I have not got a document in front of me and I am going to decline to speculate on a Tuesday afternoon about something I have not read.**" He started towards the van. "**And I am going to say one more thing and then I am going to drive back up the ring road. In about four days somebody with a duty is going to ask me for a level for Ferry Bank and I am going to have to say no, and the reason I am going to say no is not that the water is uncertain. The water is the most certain thing I work with. The reason is that I would be handing over a number with a place on it, and a number with a place on it stops being a measurement and becomes a claim about land, and I have watched a flood wall come down on a good survey and I am not going to be the man who printed a height for a street that has not got one.**"

Three, and they are in three hands, and one of the three is a document he has promised to make me ask about twice.**
