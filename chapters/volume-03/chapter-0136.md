# Chapter 0136 — Two Days

**On Monday the twenty-eighth and Tuesday the twenty-ninth of September 2025 a woman of fifty-two who empties a post box in Wharf Street at six o'clock every evening had nineteen pieces of other people's paper in a cage in a sorting office in about nine days, and the case wrote her a letter on the Monday and did not send it on the Tuesday, and the reason is in this chapter and the reason is not that anybody did not want her to have it. And on the Monday afternoon, in a room above a shop in Fisher Lane, four people said out loud why nobody in this case has said in a room what is going to happen on Friday, and the reason was not restraint either, and the chapter gives both of those reasons properly because they are the only two people in this flood who have been asked to write a letter and have not sent it.**

**The first of October is two days away at the end of the Tuesday. A woman of eighty-one keeps a hardback of about a hundred and fifty ruled pages in a drawer that does not lock on purpose, with no names in it, about nine gaps in it, about four hundred more things to come, and a fourth line on the first page that says a thing nobody in this case has said out loud in five months. She is referred to in this chapter and she is not in it, and her book is referred to and neither of its dates is printed here, and that is deliberate and it is not a courtesy.**

## And on the Monday morning a postwoman of fifty-two came to a room above a shop on Fisher Lane because she had telephoned an office twice and had been put through twice, and she had a bundle in a bag and she wanted somebody to take it

"**I have not come to give you anything,**" said Winnie Okoro, before she sat down. "**I have come to say that I have got six envelopes in a cage with your reference on them and about thirteen other things that are not letters, and that I have done this job for eleven years and I have never had anybody in this flood telephone me before, and the two times I telephoned the office your colleague told me to ring back after the second post, and I have rung back after the second post twice, and I would like somebody to say what I am supposed to do on a Friday.**"

"**Say the whole of it.**"

"**The whole of it is that I am not allowed to hand you anything.**" She had the bag on the table and she had not opened it. "**A pillar box is not a postal address. There is no such thing as posting to one. So what is in that box is not a letter to you. It is a letter to nobody, and I am the person who empties it, and I have got a bundle, and the bundle is in a cage on a van, and on Monday it goes on a van and on Tuesday it goes to the cage and on Wednesday morning it gets sorted, and a piece of paper with a reference on the front of it is a piece of paper to a machine.**"

"**And you identified the six.**

"**I identified the six because I read the front of every one of them, and I have read the front of every one of them since the Friday, and I put the six in a bundle of their own and I put a rubber band round it, and that is not a process. That is a woman of fifty-two doing a job properly with a rubber band.**"

"**You kept a bundle.**"

"**I have kept a bundle,**" said Winnie Okoro, "**and I want to say to you before anybody says anything else that the rubber band is the whole of the safeguard and I know that, and I would like you to understand that I did it and not that you asked me to, because you did not ask me to, because you cannot ask me to, because there is nothing to ask me with.**"

Nobody improved on that.

"**Say what you came for.**"

"**I came because on the Tuesday of the twenty-first I telephoned and I got a man who said there was no way of asking for the contents of a box and he was correct, and then he said *send it to a post box* and I said the thing I am saying now, and he said he understood and would raise it, and I have not heard from anybody since and it is seven days, and I have a bundle and a rubber band and a Thursday coming, and a rubber band is not a plan.**"

"**What do you want.**"

"**I want somebody to write down that six people put a piece of paper in a hole in a wall because you told them they could, and that I read all six fronts, and that I put a band round the six and not round the other thirteen, and that on Wednesday the six go on a van to a cage, and that in about four years nobody will be able to say who read them,**" said Winnie Okoro. "**I do not want the paper. I have never wanted the paper. If somebody had given me a form in June asking me to record what went into that box I would have said no, and I would have said no for the same reason you do not write a list of addresses, and I have read the minute, because it is on a wall in the room you work in, and item one of it is the reason.**"

## And the case wrote her a letter on the Monday afternoon, and did not send it on the Tuesday morning, and the reason is four sentences long and a registrar of twenty-eight said them out loud and put them in her own book and not in a minute

The letter was about a page and a half and it was correct. It said what the office was, what the box was, what the reference was, that the office was not able to ask for the contents of a pillar box, that no process existed by which the six could be returned to the writer's office, and that the office was sorry.

It also said: *in order that our office may be able to confirm, in due course, the manner in which material received at that box has been handled, we should be grateful if you would complete and return the enclosed schedule.*

There was an enclosed schedule. It had one column and it was headed *item* and it had about nine ruled lines, and nobody in this case had designed it, and nobody in this case could have designed it, because it came out of a system in about four seconds off a template that has been in use since about 2016 and is used for about nine other things.

"**That is a form,**" said Hazel Cray, at about four o'clock on the Monday.

"**That is a schedule,**" said Sinead Molloy. "**And I wrote the covering letter and I wrote the first three paragraphs of it and I am not going to say it was a form because it is not, it is a covering letter, and the schedule came out of the system on its own and it is on a template that has been in use since about 2016, and if the covering letter were not sent the schedule is not sent, and there is a mechanism in this building for that and it is called a bin.**"

"**Say the whole of the objection.**"

"**The objection is a man of thirty-two wrote a sheet with a box on it that said *your address* and put his own name under it in a hall in August, and I signed a minute on the thirty-first of July that said no list of addresses was to be made, circulated, printed or entered into any system, and a woman of fifty-one typed three hundred and eighty of those addresses into a school office in sixteen years and told the room about it in about nine seconds, and a woman of sixty-eight gave a list to a woman forty miles away in 1986 and the woman worked from the front and never got to 1990.**" Hazel Cray had her hand flat on the letter. "**I am not objecting to this because of the minute. The minute is about addresses. I am objecting to this because a woman of fifty-two has done the only safe thing anybody has produced in this flood, which is put six envelopes in a bundle with a rubber band and not tell anybody, and the only thing anybody in this case can offer her in return is a piece of paper with her name on it and about nine ruled lines and a column headed *item*.**"

Nobody argued.

"**Then what do we do.**"

"**We do not send it,**" said Mara Iles, on the Tuesday morning, at about ten past nine, and she had not slept and she had said the whole of it out loud twice. "**And I am going to say the reason and it is four sentences and I want them in my own book and not in a minute, because a minute is read out to a person who was not in the room and this is about a person who was not in this room.**"

"**Say them.**"

"**One. We do not send it because a schedule with a column headed item, filled in by a postwoman, is a record of what other people put in a box, held by her, and she would be the only person in this city holding one. Two. She did not ask to be a record and she told us in a room on Monday morning that she would have said no to a form, and gave the reason, and the reason is the reason we have been using for five months. Three. And if we do not send it then she has no record either, and that is the same thing that happened to a woman of sixty-nine who asked whether she could give a card back and was told she could not, and a woman of about forty-three who was put on a schedule in a hall and has never been asked. Four. So the choice is between making her a record and leaving her unprotected, and both of those are the choice this case has been making at every door since the first of June, and I am not going to pretend the third door exists, and it does not, and I would rather that the choice was made in a room and minuted by somebody who has to live in it than that it is made by a bin.**"

"**Then it goes in the bin.**"

"**It goes in the bin,**" said Mara Iles, "**and the decision is signed by three of us and one of us is the housing officer and one of us is the registrar and one of us has no post, and I want the three names on it and not the office, because a decision not to write to a person is a decision about a person and a decision about a person ought to have a person on it.**"

## And on the Tuesday morning the reason nobody in this case has said in a room what is going to happen on Friday was said out loud, in about four minutes, and it was not restraint, and there were two reasons and they turned out to be the same reason

"**I want to get this on the page before Friday and I want to get it on the page properly,**" said Elias Venn, "**and I want to say first that I have asked for nothing seven times and I am not going to make the eighth a notice, and I have not been up the hill and I am not going to be, and the reason is that she has said in front of nine people that she is not going to be asked, and a notice would be a sentence, and a sentence about that date would be a document, and a document about that date would in about four years be read out to a woman of eighty-one by somebody with a duty who has never met her.**"

"**Then say the reason nobody has said it,**" said Hazel Cray.

"**Two reasons and they are the same reason and it has taken me four months to notice that they are the same.**"

"**Say them.**

"**The first is that this case has four instruments and every one of them is a document, and the whole of what this case believes is that a document can be checked, and if four people sit in a room on the Monday and say out loud what is going to happen on Friday then there is a fifth thing in this case that is not a document and it is a sentence, and a sentence in a room is the only instrument in this flood that nobody can strike through and nobody can correct, and I have watched a woman of sixty-nine say in a room that she stood in her hall with the door open for four hours and there is no form for it, and I know exactly what a sentence in a room is worth and I know exactly what it costs, and it is the same thing, and it is about four hours.**"

"**That is a good reason,**" said Mara Iles.

"**The second one is that we do not know,**" said Elias Venn.

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"**Say that again,**" said Hazel Cray.

"**A man of fifty-two drafted a boundary on a wall on the twenty-fourth of April with a bolt pen, and he did not sign it, and the fifth line on it says that nobody signs it and nobody keeps it and it is on the wall, and that line is now wrong on one sheet on purpose and is staying wrong, and a registrar of twenty-eight said out loud in a hall on the twenty-eighth of June that she was not going to take it off. A woman of eighty-one chose the same date for the inside front cover of a hardback about a fortnight later. And a woman of forty-eight with a statutory power noticed on the sixth of August that neither of them has ever said in a room what the date is for, and said in front of a room that she is leaving it, and she is the fourth person in the case to notice and I am the fifth and I have noticed seven times and asked for nothing seven times.**"

"**And the second reason.**"

"**The second reason is that nobody in this case knows what the date is for,**" he said. "**And if four of us sit in a room and say what it is for then we are inventing it, and inventing what a date is for is the one thing this case must not do, because the whole of what we have ever caught anybody with is a record, and a record of what a thing was for is the one thing in this case that is not a record of anything.**"

"**Write that down,**" said Mara Iles. "**In your own book. Not in a minute. I am not going to minute that, and if anybody else minutes it I will find out, and the reason I am not is that a minute is read out to a person who was not in the room and there are two people on this island who would be read it and neither of them is in this case.**"

"**I will write it down,**" said Elias Venn, "**and the reason I will write it down and not say it in a room is the third line of my first page, and the reason the third line of my first page exists is a rule I made in December about a thing I can be got at over, and it turns out that a rule about a thing you can be got at over is also a rule about a thing you can be got at over in a room, and I noticed that on Monday and I have been carrying it about five months without knowing it.**"

## And the book is referred to and neither of its dates is printed here, and the reason is that a book in a drawer has two days in it that disagree with each other and a repair pass has not chosen between them, and a writer who printed either of them would be making a fact

It is a hardback, about a hundred and fifty ruled pages, and it cost a pound and ten, and it is in a drawer that does not lock, and the drawer does not lock on purpose and the person who told this case that it does not lock on purpose said so in a hall in front of nine people in June and was not asked about it afterwards.

There are no names in it. There are about nine gaps in it. There are about four hundred more things to come.

The fourth line on the first page says this, and it says it in the keeper's own hand, and it is the only line in the book that is addressed to anybody:

> **if you are reading this and you are not me, then you are the second person, and that is the only reason it exists**

"**I want that read out,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and I have asked twice and I have been refused twice, and I have been refused by a man of thirty-two and not by her, and I would like it understood that I know the difference.**"

"**It has been read out to me in a room by a registrar of twenty-eight,**" said Elias Venn, "**and it is the only sentence in five months of this case that is a rule about a document and not a rule about a person, and there is a man of twenty-five in this city with a condition of his own that says a second person is somebody who can be asked afterwards what they saw, and he wrote it in four pages in December and nobody reads it, and her fourth line is in a hardback on a shelf on a hill in a village and nobody reads that either, and the two of them are the same sentence written by two people who have never met and about four hundred years apart.**"

"**Then say the thing that is actually the trouble with it.**"

"**The thing that is actually the trouble with it is that there are two days in it that do not agree,**" said Elias Venn, "**and I am not going to say either of them, and I have said that in a room on Monday and I am saying it again, and the reason is not a courtesy. One of those two days is earlier than the other one and about four hundred words of records, in about nine places across this case, say the later one. A repair pass has to choose between two days and not between two statements, and it has not chosen, and the day it chooses, about nine files change, and I am not going to be the man who writes a day into a book before a repair pass has picked it.**"

Nobody argued with that either.

"**About four hundred more,**" said Mara Iles.

"**About four hundred more things to come,**" said Elias Venn. "**And I have noticed in about five months that she has never once said four hundred days, and about four hundred entries, and about four hundred things that are going to happen. She says things to come. And I do not know what she thinks is coming, and I have not asked, and there is a woman of forty-eight in a car on a ring road in July who set me a condition to see whether I could work out a thing without asking, and I have not worked this out, and I am not going to write the word down until somebody asks me in a room when it is not a question I have to answer.**"
