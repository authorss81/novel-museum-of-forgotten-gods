# Chapter 0127 — A Box On The Back Of The Form

**On Monday the thirtieth of August, Tuesday the thirty-first and Wednesday the first of September 2025 this case read a piece of paper it had been holding for three months and was wrong about it, and the wrongness was a reversal of a reading and not of a monster, exactly as the last one was. For three volumes the Registry has been read as a body that keeps the names of people this city's water has drowned, so that an estate can be settled. That reading is not wrong. It is one half. The other half is that the same body, with the same care, using instruments it has had since 2019, can take a house out of a register without ever knowing or asking who is in it, and has been doing so on four hundred metres of frontage since a woman of forty-eight put a box in a form and made it wider.**

**She was right about the bank. On the Wednesday the wall moved again, and the thing that carries the horror is not that anybody lied. The form is one sheet. It is printed on one side. It has a box for a period on the front and it has a box for a period on the back, and the two boxes are the same size, and the woman who has signed the front of it for six years signed the back of it in June without ever having turned it over.**

## And on the Monday morning a registrar of twenty-eight asked for a blank form, which is the least remarkable request anybody in this city makes, and got one, and the reason she asked for two is that the office had sent her a photocopy of a one-sided thing

The request went in on the Friday and it came back on the Monday at about half past nine, in an envelope, with a note on it.

> **Specimen, Schedule F, current issue. One side. Photocopied 30 August 2025. A complete specimen is enclosed; the copy was made from the office's own file and the reverse of the copy was blank in the file, which is not evidence about the form, since paper of that age has a habit of losing ink on one side. — Flood Emergency Powers.**

"**A complete specimen,**" said Mara Iles, "**and the office told us in writing, in a note on an envelope, that the reverse of their own photocopy was blank, and that they do not know whether that is because of the form or because of the paper.**"

"**And you asked for two.**"

"**I asked for a specimen twice in the same letter on Friday, because I wanted a second one to destroy, because I have been in this job for five years and I know what a piece of paper becomes, and I wanted the second one so that the first one could go on a table and be looked at.**" She put the two sheets down side by side, back up. "**I have not shown the second one to anybody yet and I am not going to until Tuesday, and I have had it in a drawer since about ten past ten this morning and I have not slept and I have had four goes at the telephone.**"

Elias Venn looked at the back of the sheet for about a minute and a half.

"**It is printed,**" he said.

"**It is printed.**"

"**It is not a memo. It is not a note. It is printed on the same press with the same furniture, and it has a form number in the corner, and the form number is the same number as the front.**"

> **RECORD STATUS — DISTRICT REGISTER. Ferry Bank. To be completed by the district register at Northglass. Not completed by this office.**
> **PREMISES / FRONTAGE REFERENCE ..........**
> **STATUS AT (date): ( ) OCCUPIER RECORDED ( ) NO OCCUPIER RECORDED ( ) NOT ASSESSED**
> **PERIOD AFFECTED: from ............... to ...............**
> **This block is completed by the district register. It is not a determination and it may not be appealed. It is a status. A person may hold a status and a person may hold two statuses at once.**

Nobody said anything for about eleven seconds and the eleven seconds were not disbelief.

"**Read it again,**" said Marianne Cope, "**and this time read the bottom line as though it were printed for somebody in particular, because I think it is.**"

"**A person may hold a status and a person may hold two statuses at once.**" Mara Iles read it flatly. "**Which means a household can be recorded as occupied by the register on a Tuesday and recorded as not occupied by the register on a Wednesday, and both of those are true, and the register does not think that is a problem, and it is printed on the form as a reassurance, and the reassurance is for the person filling it in, and the person filling it in is not us.**"

"**Then who is it for.**"

"**I have had two answers today and I do not like either of them,**" said Mara Iles. "**The first answer is that it is for the officer at Northglass who completes it, and it is a reassurance, because a duty officer filling in a block by hand does not want to be told by a colleague that the block is contradictory. And the second answer is that it is for anybody who comes in later with a question, and the reassurance is: yes, this is possible, and here is a form that says so, and you were not misreading anything.**"

"**Which is it.**"

"**Both, and it does not matter,**" said Mara Iles, "**and the thing that matters is that the form has told a stranger on the second side of a piece of paper that a person may be two things at once, and has not troubled itself to say which two.**"

## And on the Tuesday at about nine o'clock, in a room above a shop in Fisher Lane, the reading changed, out loud, and it was not a person who changed it, it was a piece of stationery, and eight people were in the room and every one of them had a duty

Rukhsana Iqbal had come in for the last hour on the Friday of the sixth of August and had chaired a question. She came in at nine on the Tuesday because the block on the back of a form is a thing that affects her district and she is the person who convened the meeting that said no list of addresses is to be made, circulated, printed, or entered into any system.

"**Then read it out,**" she said, "**and then somebody is going to say what it is, and I am going to rule on it, and I want it said now that I have read it and I am not going to pretend I have not.**"

"**I am not going to be the one who says what it is,**" said Mara Iles. "**I have been reading it since half past nine yesterday morning and I have written two pages and I have thrown both of them away, because both of them said what it is, and what it is is a reading, and a reading is the thing this case has got wrong three times.**"

"**So say the reading.**"

"**Say it slowly and let somebody stop you.**"

Mara Iles put the specimen face down on the table, which Marianne Cope had done with a plastic wallet in June and which Yvette Carrow had done with a printed sheet on the sixth of August, and nobody remarked on it and everybody noticed.

"**I am a registrar. My whole trade is that a person is only ever on a page if somebody put them there and a page is a page and not a thing.**" She turned it over. "**And I have been reading a body of this city for three months as a body that keeps things. That is what it is for. A drowned person's estate could not be settled without a death record, and a death record has to be somewhere, and this is somewhere, and it has been for about a hundred and forty years, and everything I have found in it in three months has been a body refusing to be wrong — a card not taken, a note in a public schedule that names nobody, a list it would not produce, a form it would not reissue.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**It has never been a body refusing to be wrong in order to keep a person. It has been a body refusing to be wrong in order to keep a record. And a record is a thing that goes on a side. And I have read every single thing that body has done in three months as a thing done about a person, because everything it has done has been about a person, and I never once asked whether there was a side of the document I was not seeing.**"

She put both hands on the table.

"**It is not only preserving old names. It is using flood emergency powers to take people out of the register, and it is doing it with a box for a period, and the box for the period is on the back of a form that a woman of forty-eight signs, and the box on the front and the box on the back are the same size.**"

Nobody said anything for a while and then Yvette Carrow said the thing that made it a reversal and not an accusation.

"**Show me the back of one I signed.**"

## And the woman of forty-eight who is proved right about the water in the middle of this volume found out what she has been signing, and she is not a villain, and nobody in that room explained to her why it is a bad thing, and she said it herself twice in July and nobody wrote it down

They got the file up from a drawer on a floor above a shop in the same lane, and there were four Schedules in it for this district, three of them hers and one of them from the previous year, and she spread them out on a table in the order she had signed them.

"**I have never turned one over,**" said Yvette Carrow.

"**You have never turned one over,**" said Mara Iles, "**and I have read the front of four of them across three months and did not ask for the back, and neither did a claims handler of thirty-four, and neither did a woman of forty-seven who convened a meeting about a district, and the reason none of us asked is that the front of the form is complete. It has three grounds. It has a premises. It has a period. It has a no appeal. It is a finished document and it is about water.**"

"**I have said the thing about this before,**" said Yvette Carrow, "**and I said it in a corridor in June to a man who had not asked, and I said: I have no power in this form to see anybody's claim, their tenancy, their letter, their photograph, or anything at all about any person affected, and so I am not suspending access to a building, I am suspending access to a building without ever having to know who is in it, and I have discovered that in about ten minutes that this is the easiest instrument I have ever signed and I would like somebody to explain to me why that is a bad thing.**"

"**Nobody explained it to you.**"

"**Nobody explained it to me,**" she said, "**and I have thought about that for eleven weeks and I have a better sentence now and it is worse. I did not need anybody to explain it to me. I asked for an explanation because I wanted to be told I was wrong, and nobody in this case was going to be rude to a statutory officer, and a woman of fifty-one in a corridor was rude to me about something else and I have not stopped thinking about that either.**"

She picked up the June instrument by its corners, the way people pick up a document they are about to be careful with.

"**This is a period and it runs to the eighth of July and I signed it at about ten past seven on the Wednesday evening having said the cost out loud twice, and it says on the front of it that the form contains a box for a period and no other box, and applications under this Schedule are not persons and are not recorded, and that sentence is true, and that sentence is on the front, and it is the sentence that stopped me from asking about the back, and it is the most accurate sentence on the form.**"

"**Why is it accurate.**"

"**Because it is a statement about the front,**" said Yvette Carrow. "**There is no box on the front for a person. So an application is not a person. That is true. And I have read it about four hundred times and I have read it as reassurance, and it is reassurance, and it is also, and this is the part I have not been able to put down for four days: it is a warning, and it is addressed to me, and it says that on the other side of this piece of paper the definition of a person changes.**"

Nobody in the room took that further and the woman of forty-seven who had convened the meeting about a district said so.

"**I am going to rule that we stop,**" said Rukhsana Iqbal, "**and then I am going to rule that we do not stop, and I am going to give the reasons in that order, because the second ruling is the honest one and I want the first one on the record as a thing that happened.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**First. We stop, because the block on the back of that form is a thing being done to about two hundred addresses behind four hundred metres of wall, and we do not know who they are, and I signed a minute on the thirty-first of July that says no list is to be made or printed, and this is a printed thing about addresses and I am the person whose minute it offends.**"

"**And the second ruling.**"

"**We do not stop,**" said Rukhsana Iqbal, "**because the wall moved four millimetres in seven weeks and it moved again on Monday, and if I stop on a Tuesday morning because a form has a second side then I have done a thing I have told this room three times in five weeks I would not do, which is put a document in front of the water and ask the water to wait.**"

Nobody improved on that and Marianne Cope said out loud, for the record, that she wanted it in the minute that the resilience coordinator had said the thing she had said about a cost first, in July, with a different cost, and that it was the same sentence.

## And the woman from Northglass was forty-seven and had nineteen years and she was not a villain, and she said the true thing, and the true thing was worse than the false thing anybody might have expected her to say

Delyth Morgan had come from Northglass and had brought the district register's own guidance note, printed, dated in April, and she had read it out in about four minutes and had not once been interrupted.

"**A status is not a determination,**" she said. "**It does not remove anybody from anything that pays anybody. It does not go on a claim. It does not go on a tenancy, and it does not go on a rate, and it does not go on anything a person can see. It goes on the frontage, and the frontage is a line on a map, and a line on a map is not a house and is not a person, and the district register keeps lines on maps and has kept them since 1951.**"

"**What is it for.**"

"**It is for computation.**" She put the note down. "**When a district's occupied frontages are being computed against — for a boundary review, for a drainage programme, for an insurance pool, for anything with a number at the end of it — then a frontage whose occupancy cannot be established is a variable, and a variable is carried at a value, and the value we carry is not occupied. That is not an act against anybody. That is arithmetic.**"

"**And when it is a flood emergency.**"

"**Then the arithmetic is very bad,**" said Delyth Morgan, "**and it is bad for a reason that has nothing to do with anybody in this room, and the reason is that a flood emergency generates events, and an event generates claims, and a claim attaches to a household, and a household attaches to an address, and a frontage with four hundred open claims on it is a frontage that a computer will not settle a district boundary against, and everybody who has ever tried has ended up with a district that cannot be drawn.**"

"**So the status is used to make a district drawable.**"

"**The status is used to make a district drawable,**" said Delyth Morgan, "**and it has been used nine times in this county since the twenty-fourth of June, and I have signed eight of them, and I will sign the ninth on Wednesday, and I would like somebody in this room to understand that I have never once been asked to consider whether it is a good idea, because it is not my decision, and I have a job, and my job is to complete the block accurately when the front of the form has been signed by somebody with a power.**"

"**Who chooses which frontages.**"

"**The front of the form chooses the frontages,**" said Delyth Morgan. "**And the front of the form is four hundred metres of wall and the premises behind it, and the person who signs the front is the person who decides, and she does it on a Tuesday, and nobody has ever told her that a status is attached to each address behind the premises, and I have not told her, because she does not decide the status, and I do not decide the status, and the office does not decide the status. The block is completed by a duty officer. That is printed on it. That is what it says.**"

Yvette Carrow sat down, which she had not done.

"**Then I am not the villain in this,**" she said, "**and I have been the villain in this for four days and I would like it recorded that I have been wrong about myself for four days in the correct direction, and that a woman of forty-seven has just told me in a room that I have no power over this, and that is the second time anybody in this case has told me that and the first time I believed it.**"

"**You are not the villain,**" said Hazel Cray. "**You are the woman who made the form wider. And I want that said differently, because I have been in this flood since the first of June and I have watched a man of forty-four in this building be right about a column of numbers for three months, and I have watched a woman of fifty-five with a rule be right about a height for three months, and neither of them is a villain, and this is the same shape, and the shape is a person doing their job with a box in front of them.**"

## And on the Wednesday afternoon the bank moved again and she was right about that too, and the second half of the reversal came out of a room with eight people in it and it is a household, and nobody in the room can say who it is

On Monday afternoon a contractor appointed under the second instrument reported that the coping above the movement joint at the north end had dropped about eleven millimetres since the fourth of August and that the revetment had taken a set. It is a stone revetment, it is a hundred and forty years old, and the wall behind it is older than the records that describe the district.

"**So it is going,**" said Yvette Carrow, "**and I was right, and I would like it minuted that I was right about the bank in a room where the bank was the least of the news, and I have now been right about the water four times since June and the water has never once been the thing anybody is fighting me about, and I have started to find that I do not know what to do with being right.**"

The second half came at about half past four on the Wednesday, in the same room, with six people left in it, and it came from Hazel Cray and it took her about ninety seconds and she had it in her pocket since Tuesday.

"**Then let us say what is actually going to happen,**" she said. "**A status goes on a frontage. A frontage is a line on a map and a line on a map is not a house and is not a person. Which means that if a status is put on the frontages behind four hundred metres of wall, then every household behind those frontages stops being a household in one register and stays a household in every other, and I have been the housing officer in this city for twenty-two years and I have never seen that done deliberately to anybody, and I have seen it done accidentally in about four hundred cases, and the reason it happens accidentally is that two systems were built by two bodies at two different times for two different purposes and nobody was ever in a room with both of them on the table.**"

"**And is that the harm.**"

"**No,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and I am going to say the next part and I have been not saying it since Tuesday afternoon, and the next part is that we cannot say who is harmed, and that is not because the answer is difficult.**"

She put her hand flat on the table, which she did.

"**There is a household in this flood that nobody is owed anything for. It is owed nothing. It has claimed nothing. It is not on the docket round, because the docket round is the households that called a man with a clipboard, and it did not call a man with a clipboard, and the only trace of it anywhere in this city is one line in a contractor's book that says a crew did not attend and removed nothing, which the contractor himself has twice called the best line in his book and worth nothing. It is not in a claims file. It is not on the recovery page. It is not in the Forum. It is not in the parish register, because a parish register is a record of people who died. It is not in the school roll, because a school roll is a record of children, and the child in it may not live there.**"

"**So who is it.**"

"**I cannot say,**" said Hazel Cray. "**And that is the whole of the finding and it is the reason the finding is a finding and not a complaint. There is at least one household in this flood that is owed nothing, is owed nothing by anybody, and is not on any list of people who are owed something. And it is behind that wall, because the wall is four hundred metres long and it goes through the middle of a district, and there is no way on this earth of knowing which frontage it is at, and therefore there is no household in this room that can stand up and object to a status being put on it.**"

Nobody in the room could say who it was.

Elias Venn was the fifth person in this case to have noticed, since the twenty-eighth of June, that a man of fifty-two and a woman of eighty-one put the same date in two places and neither of them has ever said out loud in a room what happens on it, and on Wednesday the first of September he noticed it a sixth time, because the block on the back of the form has a period in it, and a period ends, and the first of October is thirty days away, and a status that expires is a status that was only ever a status.

He did not ask. He wrote it down afterwards and nobody asked him for it and he did not offer it, and the sixth time is the same as the first five.

Yvette Carrow was the fourth person in the case to notice that neither of those two has ever said it, and she had said on the sixth of August, in that room, that she was leaving it, and on Wednesday the first of September she was still leaving it, and she said so out loud to nobody in particular at about ten past five, because a statutory officer who has noticed something and is leaving it should at least say that she is leaving it.

## And the man of thirty-two was asked nothing on the Wednesday and decided something, and the decision was in a corridor above a shop in Fisher Lane at about six in the evening, and nobody asked him and nobody in this case will ever be able to say that he was asked

The room above the shop in Fisher Lane was let by the hour and on the evening of Wednesday the first of September it was empty and had a grey box in it with twenty-two things in it and no maker and no date and no material and no provenance and no place.

"**Read the third line of your first page,**" said Mara Iles, from the doorway, without coming in.

"**Do not write it down.**"

"**Do not write it down,**" she said, "**and I have not come in, and I want to say one thing to you from this doorway and then I am going home, and it is that a box with nothing written on it is the only object in this case that is not a record, and that there is no name in it and no reference and no form and no collection number and no card, and that the only reason it exists is that a branch manager of fifty-one would not have thirty-one unknown objects in her corridor, and that you are the only person in this city who may hold it.**"

"**Somebody has to look for the household.**"

"**Yes,**" said Mara Iles.

"**Then I have got no other instrument.**

"**You have got twenty-two things that nobody owns and that nobody can be asked about, and a grey box, and about eleven seconds at a time, and you have said no to a knock five times in five weeks and you are about to do the opposite of a knock and you are doing it because a form has no box for it,**" said Mara Iles, from the doorway. "**And I am not going to tell you not to, and I want you to notice that I did not tell you not to, and I want you to notice that I knew about it on Friday and I have not said a word about it for three days, and I would like both of those to be in your book and neither of them to be an excuse.**"

He did not write it down. That was the fourth line, and it held.
