# Chapter 0133 — Who May Speak For A Community, And It Is Not Settled

**On Saturday the eighteenth and Sunday the nineteenth of September 2025 a room on the first floor of a building on Wharf Street had about eleven people in it, and a woman of sixty-one who had come down a hill three times in about eight weeks asked the question she had said in advance she was not going to ask, and nobody answered it, and she told the room she was not going to answer it before anybody had finished trying, and then the room got on with about nine items of work and got every one of them right.**

**This is the last day of a movement and a movement has closed twice in this volume and both times on a question and not on an event. The other one asked what a date of loss is for, in a church hall in June, and nobody answered it, and the answer has since turned out to be seven hundred and fifty pounds once per household per event, and nobody said it in the room. This one asks who may speak for a community, for the second time in this case, six weeks and a day after the first time, in a different room, with more people in it, and nobody answers it, and the woman of sixty-one who gave an answer last time that was a way of behaving and not a person gives no answer at all and says so before anybody has decided whether that counts.**

## And the room is on the first floor of a building on Wharf Street, and it has eleven people in it, and four of the eleven are here for no reason anybody could put in a sentence, and that is the first thing said about the room

It had been a meeting room since the second week of June and it had eleven chairs in it and a table that took eight comfortably, and there were eleven people in it at about half past two on a Saturday afternoon, and Rukhsana Iqbal had said in a car on the Friday that there would be eleven and that she was sorry about it.

"**I have tried for a week to get this to eight,**" she said, "**and I could not, and I have stopped trying, and I am going to say the thing that is actually true about the number, which is that four of the eleven are here because they heard about it and four of the four are here because they are frightened, and I have put those two facts in that order because it took me about nine seconds to work out which way round it goes and I have had a week, and I would like it minuted that the housing officer for this district is one of the eleven and did not ask to be, and that if she had asked to be I would have said no.**"

Nobody argued.

The other four were a district nurse of about forty-four who had come because she had been asked and could not get away, a man of sixty-one who chaired a tenants' association on two streets and had asked to come and had not asked what about, a woman of thirty-six who ran a childminding place in a ground floor on a street off the low bank, and a parish clerk of about fifty who had come up from the river with a bag and had been given a chair and had not been given a job.

And a woman of thirty-four who ran a claims team, and a woman of forty-seven who typed publications and would not type one sentence, and a woman of twenty-eight who was a registrar on a hill, and a man of thirty-two with no post, and a woman of sixty-one who had come down a hill and had not told anybody she was coming.

Hesta Rell put her hand flat on the table, which she did.

"**I am going to ask the question,**" she said, "**and I told the room on the seventh of September that there was a question I had not asked and that when it was asked somebody should know that I had said it was not now. That was eleven days ago and I have thought about it every day and I have decided that the reason I said it was not now was not that it was not now. It was that I wanted to be in a room when I asked it and there is only one room in this flood that I come to and I was waiting for the right Saturday.**"

"**Then ask it.**"

"**I am not going to answer it,**" said Hesta Rell, "**and I am telling you that now, at the top, before anybody has worked out that I am going to, so that nobody in this room spends the rest of the afternoon trying to get one out of me.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**Here is the question. When this room has finished today, in about four years, and somebody from an office that has been reorganised twice wants to know what this room said — who is it that they ask?**"

## And nobody answered it, and two people tried, and both of them said a thing about a document and neither of them said a thing about a person, and the woman of sixty-one let about four seconds go by before she said the reason

About four seconds. Then Rukhsana Iqbal said, "**A minute. The chair minutes it and the minute is signed and the minute is a document and anybody may apply for a copy of it, and the application is answered by a person in an office and not by anybody in this room, and in about four years the answer will be that the minute of the meeting of the eighteenth of September 2025 records the question and does not record an answer.**"

"**That is correct,**" said Mara Iles, "**and it is the second time in this case that a minute has said a thing like that, and the first time was a telephone conversation taken by a registrar of sixty-eight at twenty-three minutes past ten on a Friday night in August by a woman who was not in the room, and I have been in this flood for five months and I have never once been in a room where the correct thing and the useful thing were the same and I would like somebody to say whether that has ever happened.**"

Nobody said it.

"**The reason I am not answering,**" said Hesta Rell, "**is that I have been asked this question three times in my life. Once in 2011 by a magistrate's clerk. Once in 2019 by a woman from a council. And once in June in that room, where I said the one who is there when it is inconvenient, and then I said why that was not an answer, and about eleven people in that room have carried the reason about with them for six weeks and I would like it to be written down that I said it was not an answer before anybody had decided it was not one.**"

"**Say the reason.**"

"**Every answer I have ever given that question has been about a person,**" said Hesta Rell. "**In 2011 it was about a woman in a wigwam at the top of the hill who is dead. In 2019 it was about me, and I was right about me for about a year. In June it was about whoever happened to be the one who had to be in a hall at a bad time. And every one of those three people is gone, or has stopped coming, and a thing that has to be carried by a person is not a practice. It is a rota.**"

"**And the fourth one is the Last Cup,**" said Rukhsana Iqbal.

"**The fourth one is four names on a door frame, and I have had about nine people want those four names since 1994 and eleven more since March, and I have said no every time, and the reason I say no is that a list of names at an address on a date is a household, and a household is a thing that can be attached to a schedule, and a schedule pays a flat sum to a household, and four people who open a door on a Tuesday have no form and no reference and no office and no way of being summoned, so there is nothing to refuse you with.**"

"**You have never said that in a minute,**" said Mara Iles.

"**No,**" said Hesta Rell, "**and I have said it out loud in about eleven rooms since March and it is the reason there is no minute of the Last Cup anywhere in this city, and that is a thing I have got exactly as I wanted it, and the cost of it is that when a room like this one needs a thing from a village nine miles upriver, there is nothing in any system anywhere that can be produced, and I have not got a better offer.**"

Nobody answered the question. It stood in the room from about half past two until about ten past four, and in that hundred and ten minutes the room did about nine items of work, and got every one of them right, and nobody in it settled anything, and one woman in it went out at about half past three and came back at four and had not said anything while she was gone.

Nobody asked her.
