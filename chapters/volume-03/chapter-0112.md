# Chapter 0112 — The Weight Column

**On Wednesday the seventh and Thursday the eighth of July 2025 a woman of about thirty-four with a duty in a flood claims office asked a salvage contractor of forty-four, lawfully, in writing, for forty-nine docket pages, and got them in about nine minutes, and did four hours of arithmetic on them, and the answer was that about nine of the nine hundred December lines are a number that is not what a lorry carried, and that the reason is not that anybody lied, and that not one of the nine can be prosecuted and every one of the nine is owed seven hundred and fifty pounds.**

**The same week pays out for a real loss and refuses a real loss, and it does both of them at the same two addresses, and nobody notices, because one of them is in a column with a weight in it and the other one is in a column with a date in it. And at twenty-three fifty-nine on Thursday the eighth of July a suspension of fourteen days runs out on a quay four miles downriver, and a form arrives, and the building does not open.**

---

## And the only way anybody lawfully gets a page of a salvage docket book is by paying a claim that the page supports, and the claim that this week was a real loss, and nobody in this chapter had to break anything to get the paper

The request came out of Wharf Street on the Wednesday morning as a letter and it is four lines long, and it is not a favour and it is not a fishing expedition, and Marianne Cope wrote it in about ninety seconds and then sat looking at it for a while.

> **To: C. Bray (Salvage) Ltd.**
> **Re: claim references DL/24/10988, DL/24/11002, DL/24/11019 and forty-one others, contents and clearance costs, event of 9–19 December 2024.**
> **We require the contractor's docket for each attendance at the above addresses, in the form in which it was completed at the time, together with the yard's weight ticket for the corresponding load. This is a routine verification of a claim we are paying and is made under the claims verification provisions of the policy. Please treat this as a formal request rather than a telephone call.**

"**That is the whole of my authority,**" she said. "**It is not a power to demand anything else and I have not got one and I would not use it if I had.**

## And the man of forty-four said yes in about four seconds and gave the reason he had said no four times in seven weeks, and the reason a stranger gets refused and a claims office does not is that a stranger was going to use it and he has never once in nineteen years known what anybody was going to use a page for

"**You have a stamp.**"

"**I have a form,**" said Marianne Cope, on the telephone. "**And I am going to tell you the whole of why I am asking, because you would rather have the true reason. I am not looking for a fraud. I have no function that looks for a fraud. I have got four hundred claims open and about forty of them have come to the point where the fourth box on page one is the thing in the way, and I have known what that box is for since Monday morning and I have had two days to do one single thing about it and I have not, and the answer is that it is for attaching a flat sum, and every one of the forty is a household that cannot evidence a date, and not one of the forty has anybody in her house who is dishonest.**"

"**Then what is it.**"

"**It is four hours of arithmetic. I want to know whether the money on one side of my desk and the money on the other side of it come out of the same machine, and the only way to know is to look at the number that is on the paper.**"

The pages came on the Wednesday afternoon by email as scans, and she would not have wanted them any other way.

"**He sent them in about nine minutes,**" she said. "**He has never sent a page to anybody outside his trade and he sent me forty-nine, and he told me why and I am going to tell you why, because it is the same reason he has been saying no to you since June and the reason is correct both times and it is correct in opposite directions and I have been sitting here for two days turning that over.**"

"**What did he say.**"

"**He said: you are a customer of the business. If it goes wrong in four years, the first thing anybody will want is a page of my book with a stranger's handwriting on it, and I have never let that happen, and you are the first person who has ever asked me for something in a form.**"

"**And the money you are owed.**"

"**He told me the same afternoon, unasked, and it was in the room for about four seconds and neither of us did anything with it.**" She closed the folder. "**He is owed money for December across about forty addresses by a managing agent whose head office is a floor above a man who took a call in February and said the same thing, and it is not been paid since the eighth of January, and there is no forum in this city in which a small contractor can say that out loud to anybody with a duty, and I am the woman with a duty, and he knew that when he told me, and I could not do one single thing about it, and he did not appear to expect me to.**"

## And a docket book has seven columns and six of them can be checked from a kerb and the seventh is the money, and the seventh is a number in hundredweights that a man with a clipboard wrote down from what a woman said on a step, and there is no return anywhere in the trade that says what came out of a house

The scans were grey and the block capitals had come out very well. Dermot Slade had filled them in on a tail lift in a wet week with a biro that had a clip on it, and every page of them was the same seven columns.

> **SALVAGE DOCKET — CREW 2. No. SB/F/12/0xxx. Address: [ ]. Date: [ ]. Crew: 2 (Slade, K).**
> **ITEM 1–3. ESTIMATED REMOVED — [ ] cwt / [ ] qtr / [ ] lb. DISPOSAL: yard, Ferry Bank. TICKET NO. [ ].**
> **CLIENT SIGNATURE: [ ]. CREW SIGNATURE: D. SLADE.**

"**This is the December book,**" said Marianne Cope. "**Nine hundred and six attendances between the ninth and the nineteenth. I have forty-nine of them and the yard tickets for the corresponding loads, and I am not going to do all nine hundred because I do not have all nine hundred and because if I did nine hundred I would stop being a person and start being a proportion.**"

"**You did forty-nine.**"

"**I did forty-nine and that is because forty-nine is what I can hold in my head as forty-nine.**" She put her finger on the seventh column. "**And look at what is on this. There is no scale in this column. There has never been a scale. The yard weighs the lorry before and after and the yard's number is the number the yard tells us, and the yard does not know what was in the back and has never once asked. So the number in the docket is the client's number, and the client's number is what a person said on a step to a man who did not have a scale and did not have the time and had a Saturday.**"

"**And the yard's ticket.**"

"**The yard's ticket is the difference in the weight of the lorry. And the yard's ticket is a fact. And the yard's ticket is about eleven feet away on the same page from the fact and nobody in the trade has ever put them next to each other, and I have now put forty-nine of them next to each other.**"

## And about nine of them do not agree, and the reason is not that anybody lied, and every one of the nine is a household, and not one of the nine is lying about having lost something

She did the arithmetic on a pad and she did it twice and she did it a third time on the second afternoon with a different method, because she said on the first afternoon that a person who gets a surprising number ought to be suspicious of it before anybody else is.

Nine lines.

"**Nine out of forty-nine,**" she said. "**Which is about one in five and I am not going to give you nine hundred because I have got forty-nine and because if I had nine hundred and one in five it would be about a hundred and eighty and a hundred and eighty is a number that would be on a wall by Tuesday.**"

"**What is a one in five.**"

"**It is a docket where the number in the seventh column is a long way above the number on the yard's ticket for the same load on the same day.**" She turned the pad round. "**And I have read all nine and I have the addresses and here they are, and I am going to read them to you, and every single one of them is a household that lost something on the ninth of December, and not one of the nine is lying about having lost it, and I want that said before the number.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**Eight Cutler's Rise. 14 Ander's Row. 2 Cutler's Rise, which is the one you know about, and the one where the grandmother was got out at two in the morning. 31 Cutler's Rise. And four more and I am not going to give you the street of a woman who has been through this.**"

"**That is a list.**"

"**It is nine addresses and it is in a pad in a locked drawer and it is not going anywhere, and I have been writing address lists in a pad since 2016 and I do not know one single thing about any of the nine, and I did not go and find out, and I want to be exact about why, because the reason is not a rule.**" She closed the pad. "**If I find out about them then I know about them, and the second I know about them they stop being a line in a column and they start being nine women, and then I cannot do the arithmetic without it becoming a decision, and I am not the person who decides, and that is the whole of my post.**"

## And what a claims handler says about a number that is wrong by a great deal, on a Wednesday afternoon, in an office, and it is not a police sentence and it is not a moral and it is the truest thing anybody says about the salvage trade in twenty-nine weeks

"**Say the part about the money.**"

"**The money is paid on the docket, not on the yard,**" said Marianne Cope. "**The client is reimbursable for clearance and removal costs, and the contractor invoices the weight, and the weight is reimbursed at a rate per hundredweight, and the rate is the same whatever came out of the house. So if the docket says twenty-two hundredweight and the lorry actually carried nine, the household is paid for twenty-two and the contractor is paid for twenty-two, and neither of them is paid anything for the thirteen that was never in the lorry, and the thirteen is not saved by anybody, it goes into a claim settlement, and the claim is closed, and the file goes in a box.**"

"**And the household is not being paid twice.**"

"**The household is not being paid twice and I want that said because I have read four things about this flood written by people who think that is what has happened. What has happened is that nine households have been paid for between about four and about nineteen hundredweight of goods that was never in a lorry, and that money is not stolen from those households, it is stolen from a settlement pool, and the pool in December was about four hundred claims deep, and the four hundred waited.**"

"**And the nine.**"

"**And the nine cannot be prosecuted, and I am going to give you the four reasons in the order a person would give them, and every one of them is correct.**"

She counted them off on her fingers without looking at them.

"**One. Nobody lied to the contractor. The contractor wrote down what the household said, and the household believed it, and a household that has just watched two feet of silt come through a front room is not going to weigh a piano. Two. Nobody lied to me. There is no claim form for the salvage weight; the weight is not on my form at all, it is on a contractor's book, and it reaches me only after it has been paid for. Three. There is no offence. For a man to have committed something there has to be an act and a purpose, and the act is a man writing down what a woman said, and the purpose was to get to Monday. Four, and this is the one that matters. If I pursue this, I am pursuing nine households, and not one of the nine can be charged with anything, and every one of the nine is owed the same seven hundred and fifty pounds that the rest of this flood is owed and that about forty of them are still not able to claim.**"

"**So it is nine people who are owed money who have been overpaid on a different line.**"

"**It is nine people who are owed money, and eight of the nine have never had the flat sum, and every one of the nine lost something real, and the number on the docket was written by a man who was told it by a woman who had just lost a piano.**" She put the pad down. "**I am not going to be the person who rings a fraud line about that, and I want it understood that I have thought about it for two days and not for nine seconds.**"

## And the same week is paying one real loss and closed another one, and both of them are on the same floor of the same building, and both of them are on the list of nine

"**There is a claim on my desk that I am paying this week,**" she said, on the Thursday morning, before the quay and after the arithmetic, "**and it is itemised, and it is a piano and a sewing machine and a wedding hamper, and it is seventeen hundred and forty pounds, and I am paying it on photographs and on a list the claimant wrote herself in her own hand, and the woman has not been paid since December and she is being paid on Friday.**"

"**And the address.**"

"**14 Ander's Row.**" She said it flatly. "**Which is the second of the nine, and it is the one whose fourth box says *9 OR 10 DEC* in a hand that was doing its best, and it is the one I told you on Tuesday morning that would not have been paid the excess for anything, because she went for the itemised list, and so the date was worth her nothing, and she is being paid on Friday anyway.**"

"**So the two things happened at the same address.**"

"**The two things happened at the same address on the same claim, and I only found it out on Wednesday afternoon at about ten past three because I read the addresses on the pad out loud in the order on the pad and the second one on the pad is the third one on my closing sheet from Tuesday.**" She had both files on the desk and she did not open either of them. "**A household was over-reimbursed on a clearance invoice by between about four and about nineteen hundredweight, on the ninth of December, on the same morning that the same household wrote two dates in a box and sent the form in without choosing one, and neither of those two people knew the other one existed, and the money that went into the first one came out of a pool that four hundred claims were standing in, and the money that the second one is about to be paid comes out of the same pool.**"

"**2 Cutler's Rise is the third address on the pad, and it is also the flat where a woman of thirty-four and a grandmother of eighty-two were got out at two in the morning on the tenth of December, and it is the flat that closed on Monday morning because item four was empty.**" She squared the two files and did not put them in the same pile. ## And at about eleven o'clock on the night of Thursday the eighth of July a period of fourteen days ran out on a quay four miles downriver, and a form arrived, and four hundred feet of iron doors did not open, and nobody can say who owns the decision

They had gone back at nine o'clock in the morning, the whole of the same party, at the same time, on the same notice, because the surveyor and the freeholder's agent had written to say that a schedule of the contents was to be produced and the schedule had not been produced.

Ama Osei had not needed to be asked. She was there at a quarter to nine with the bond-holder's authority in a plastic wallet and she had come because somebody with a duty had to be on the quay at nine in the morning on the last day of a period.

"**I am going to say the thing I have to say and then I am going to stop,**" she said, at about nine minutes past nine. "**The period of suspension expired at twenty-three fifty-nine last night. I am in possession of a printed notice of that, generated by the office's own system, and it is on a clipboard, and I have countersigned it, and it is the only document in the history of this quay that anybody wanted.**"

"**So the doors are open.**"

"**The doors are shut, and I am not able to open them and it is not because anybody has said no.**" She turned the clipboard round so that everybody on the quay could see it.

> **CITY FLOOD OFFICE — notice of expiry of scheduled access restriction.**
> **Reference: CFO/25/SF/0194. Period of suspension ended 8 July 2025, 23:59.**
> **The premises are no longer subject to a scheduled access restriction. No further restriction has been scheduled. No person is appointed by this notice.**
> **Generated 8 July 2025, 00:04. Not signed.**

"**It is not signed,**" said the freeholder's agent.

"**It is not signed because there is nothing in the system to sign it,**" said Ama Osei. "**The period ended. An expiry is not a permission, and a permission is not a power, and a power to enter a bonded store is not a permission to enter a bonded store, it is a different instrument, held by different people, in a different building, and it has to be applied for.**"

"**How long does that take.**"

"**I do not know. I have never had to find out, because in eleven of these there has never been a period that ran out with nobody in the building who wanted to know.**"

The surveyor took a photograph of the notice and asked for the reference twice. The freeholder asked, in almost the same words she had asked on the twenty-fourth of June, and got almost the same answer.

The why is that nobody ever designed a piece of paper for the morning after, and a morning after is not a period, and it does not have a box in it, and there are about four hundred boxes on the other side of those doors and about nine hundred addresses of salvage paperwork in an office along this quay that I have been told I am also not able to enter.**"
