# Chapter 0118 — A Number, And Who Is Allowed To Have It

**On Friday the thirtieth and Saturday the thirty-first of July 2025 a body of about eleven people met in two rooms of a temporary office on Ferry Bank to decide one thing, which is whether anybody was going to make a list of about nine hundred addresses, and the numbers in front of them were about nine hundred salvage dockets, about forty claims waiting on a box, and a district of about one thousand nine hundred tenancies whose flood wall is older than the district's records, and a hardback of interments that somebody had refused in writing to release as a list.**

**Nobody in either room is a villain and everybody in both rooms is competent and one of them is going to be right about a list and wrong about a person, and the person is a housing officer of fifty-eight who has been careful in this case for two volumes and who is going to be right in about nine sentences and wrong in about eleven, and the person she is wrong about is a woman of seventy-nine who has never been named in this manuscript and who said no to a crew in her own house in June and was right.**

---

## And a body has to decide what a list is for before it makes one, and the reason it has to decide is that three bodies have each already started one, and the chair has convened it and has said the cost out loud first, and it is the fifth time anybody in this case has done that and she has heard of the other four

Rukhsana Iqbal was forty-seven and had been a resilience coordinator for the district for six years and had convened the meeting because it was her job and because three offices had each told her, in writing, that they were producing something, and because a week before somebody in an office on Wharf Street had told her that a parish registrar upriver had refused to give a list and had refused in writing and had put the refusal on a wall.

There were eleven people in a room with a whiteboard and a window onto a car park, and the whiteboard had four numbers on it, and none of the four was a total of anything.

"**Before we start. I am going to say what this meeting costs and then I am going to say what I have been told this is about, and then somebody else is going to say what it is actually about, and I am not going to interrupt them.**" She uncapped a marker. "**What it costs is a morning of eleven people's time and about two hundred pounds of room hire, and if we decide to do nothing today then I will be the person who decided to do nothing, in writing, with my name on it, and I have never put my name on a *nothing* before and I have decided that I will.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**What I have been told this is about is that there are about nine hundred addresses in a salvage book and about forty claims that have come to a point where a box on a form is the thing in the way, and that both of those numbers are real, and that the two of them might be the same nine hundred people, and that if they are the same people then a household is a thing in this city that exists in three places and in two of those places it is a row in somebody's book and in the third it is a schedule nobody can attach.**"

Nobody had put that on the whiteboard and four people looked at her.

"**I have not written that number down,**" she said, "**and I have asked for it to be minuted, and there is a woman of sixty-eight upstairs who is going to tell me in about four minutes that I have got it wrong, and I have told her I would let her.**"

---

## And the four numbers on the whiteboard are four different kinds of number and three of them are nobody's business, and the person who wrote them on a whiteboard on the Friday morning said so before she wrote them

> **1. About nine hundred addresses in salvage dockets, event 9–19 Dec 2024. Source: a contractor's book. A book, not a list. Held on a quay four miles downriver.**
> **2. About forty claims at the point where item 4 (date of loss) is the obstacle. Source: a claims office. A handler's pencil number.**
> **3. Ferry Bank (registration district): about 1,900 tenancies. Source: the district. Its flood wall is older than the district's records, and no printed table says how high the water has to be at that wall before it is inside.**
> **4. Braeswick parish: a hardback of interments, about a hundred and forty years. Not released as a list. Refused in writing, 22 July 2025.**

"**Write on the top of it,**" said Rukhsana Iqbal. "**In pencil, in a large hand: these are four numbers and they are about four things and if anybody joins them up in a document I will resign.**"

Marianne Cope said, "**I would like to be on record as the person who would have to resign first, and I am not resigning, and the four numbers are the four numbers and I have never joined them up and I have refused twice to turn nine hundred and four claims into a number of households.**"

"**And number two is mine,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and it is about forty and it is a pencil number in the back of a plastic wallet in an office on Wharf Street, and the woman who holds it has told me the number herself and has told me she will not give it to a wall. And I am going to say the thing that has brought us all into this room, and then I am going to be wrong about a person, and I would like to be wrong about it in front of you rather than afterwards.**"

---

## And the argument for not making a list is made by a housing officer of fifty-eight in about nine sentences and every word of it is correct, and the alternative she proposes is better than anything else in the room

"**A list of nine hundred addresses in a district that is under flood emergency powers is not a list,**" said Hazel Cray. "**It is an instruction with no verbs in it. Every address on it is a place a person can be found at, and there are three live instruments in this city that work by address — a suspension on a quay, a follow-up visit list that goes on a clipboard, and a distribution of door-to-door leaflets that a contractor runs on a Monday. I have watched what a list does in this city in twenty-two years and the answer is: it decides who gets a knock, and the knock is not the worst thing, and the worst thing is that a person who is on a list has been on a list, and in about four years there is a record that says she was, and nobody can say who put her there.**"

"**Then what do you want.**"

"**One sheet. One person holding it. No circulation. A name given out only in answer to a specific question about a specific address, and the name of the person who asked, and the date, and no reason recorded, because the form has no box for a reason.**" She said it as a professional and not as a proposal. "**I have run the weekly unpaid-bills queue out of the back room of a branch library for nine weeks and that is the same shape and it works, and the reason it works is that there is one queue and one person and the person is the queue.**"

"**That is a good answer,**" said Rukhsana Iqbal. "**Say the rest of it before somebody says something clever, because I have got a document here that says the same thing in about four words from a woman of sixty-eight and it is dated the twenty-second of July and it is in my bag.**"

---

## And the hardback of interments is refused in a letter of four sentences and the letter is read out and it is the correct answer and nobody in the room improves on it and the chair puts the letter in the file

> **Braeswick parish. 22 July 2025.**
> **You have asked for the register of interments for the parish. The register is a book and it stays on its shelf. A page may be copied for a person and a date. A list of names will not be made for anybody.**
> **The reasons are in the book and the reasons are about a family in 1990 and I am not going to write them out for a district office.**
> **A. Shand, registrar of burials, Braeswick.**

"**Four sentences,**" said Rukhsana Iqbal, "**and one of them is a refusal and two of them are an offer and the third one is a boundary, and the chair's ruling is that the offer is accepted for the specific question, and that if anybody in this room asks for a list in writing I will answer it myself in writing and not refer it.**"

"**Is that a decision.**"

"**It is a decision, and it is the only one anybody is going to make today, and I am making it as the person who chairs and not as the person who owns the ground.**" She put the letter on the table face up. "**And I want it minuted, and I am aware that there is no minute, and that there has now been a chair in this case who has said the same sentence twice.**"

---

## And the list, if it is made, has to be made out of the salvage book, because the salvage book is the only source that has an address and a date on the same line, and that is the moment a housing officer of fifty-eight is right about a list and wrong about a person

"**Then we have got three numbers and one of them is the only one with an address and a date on a line,**" said Hazel Cray. "**Nine hundred and six attendances between the ninth and the nineteenth of December, each with an address, a date and a crew. That is not a list of households and I am not going to pretend it is, and it is the only thing in this city that says a household was at an address on a night, and if we are going to have anything at all then that is the only place it can come from.**"

"**It is also a contractor's book and it is on a quay nobody can get at.**"

"**It is also the only one. That is the argument and I am making it before somebody else makes it and I am aware that I am about to be wrong about somebody and I have known that since Tuesday night and I have come anyway.**"

"**Say the part where you are wrong.**"

"**The book records attendances. A household is on it if a crew went to it. On the ninth of December there was a woman of seventy-nine at a house in Ferry Bank who came out onto her step in a cardigan and said no to a crew in her own house, and she said the reason before anybody asked her for it, and the reason took about forty seconds, and she was right and everybody on that kerb knew it.**" She had not looked up. "**And the docket for that house says one line and the line is *declined by client, nothing removed*. And the contractor has said, in a room, that that is the best line in his book and that it is worth nothing. And he was right, and I have agreed with him, and I would like it recorded that I have now found the one thing in this entire flood that the best line in his book is good for, and the thing is this: it is the only record in the city that says that household was at risk and did nothing.**"

Nobody in the room said anything.

"**So if I build the sheet off the docket round then she is on it, and she is on it because she said no, and if I do not build it off the docket round then I have got four hundred claims and a pencil number and a district, and no address on a date anywhere, and about nine hundred people who are not on anything.**"

"**So which is it.**"

"**That is the whole of the argument and I have not resolved it and I am not going to, and I am going to say the thing I actually think, which is that a household that refused a crew in its own house in June is not a household that needs to be found by anybody in August, and I am probably right, and I have been in this flood since the first of June and she is the first person who has got the whole of it right in about forty seconds and I am going to put her name on a sheet.**" She put the marker down. "**And I am aware that I have just argued in about nine sentences that a list is an instruction, and in about eleven that the correct list is the docket round, and that the docket round is the households who called a man with a clipboard, and that the households who did not call a man with a clipboard are the ones who had a go at the door on their own terms in a fortnight when they were frightened, and I do not have a column for that, and neither does anybody else, and I have been a housing officer for twenty-two years.**"

---

## And the man of forty-four said the thing about his own book, and it is the only moment in either room where anybody says what the list is actually made of, and nobody improved on it

Colin Bray had been invited and had said that he was not a flood body and had come because somebody with a duty had written to him, which is the third time in six weeks that a person with a duty has written to him.

"**I have got about nine hundred and six lines in a book and I have said in a room and to a man with a notebook that I cannot tell you how many of them are wrong, and I have not got that number, and I am not going to have it by Monday.**" He had not brought the book and he did not offer. "**And on Thursday a woman of thirty-four in an office four miles from here did four hours of arithmetic on forty-nine of my lines with my permission and she has got about nine out of forty-nine and none of the nine is anybody cheating, and she is right, and I am not going to say anything this morning that takes that away.**"

"**Then what are you here for.**"

"**I am here because she is going to say that a line of mine is evidence of an event, and it is, and a line of mine is also evidence of a person who was frightened, and I have been carrying both of those in the same column since December and I have never had anybody ask me about the second one.**" He looked at the whiteboard. "**And I have got one line in nine hundred and six that says a crew did not attend and removed nothing, and it is the best line in the book, and I said that in a hall in June and I was not asked a question about it, and there is a woman of seventy-nine behind it and I have never met her, and I am not going to be introduced to her, and if your list has got her on it then somebody is going to knock on that door in August and they are going to be the second person in six weeks and they are going to have a clipboard.**"

"**Then take it off the list.**"

"**I cannot take it off a list I have not got, and if I could I would be doing it for the wrong reason, and the wrong reason is that I do not want the knock,**" said Colin Bray. "**I would want it off because she said no in about forty seconds and she was right and there is no form that takes a knock back.**"

## And the decision was made on the Saturday morning at about eleven, and it was a *nothing*, and it was signed by the woman who convened the meeting, and it is the first document in this case that somebody has put their own name under a decision to stop

> **MINUTE. Temporary flood office, Ferry Bank, 31 July 2025, 11:05.**
> **Present: eleven. Apologies: two.**
> **1. No list of addresses is to be made, circulated, printed, or entered into any system of the district, the flood office, the Museum, or any contractor.**
> **2. If a specific address is asked about by a person with a duty, it may be answered from a single sheet held by one named officer, and the name of the person asking and the date are recorded, and no reason is recorded.**
> **3. The sheet is to be built from the salvage docket round and from nothing else, and the officer holding it is H. Cray, housing officer, and she will hold it and not delegate it.**
> **4. The parish register is not available as a list and the refusal of 22 July 2025 is accepted and is not to be appealed.**
> **5. The four numbers on the whiteboard are not to be joined in any document.**
> **Signed: R. Iqbal, resilience coordinator, 31 July 2025.**

"**I have put my name under a decision to stop,**" she said, "**and I have never done it and I have been told twice today by a woman of thirty-four not to convert a set of claims into a proportion, and I have written five hundred and something words and about four hundred of them are somebody else's sentence and I have said so on the page, and if anybody in four years reads this minute and finds a number that does not belong in it, then the chair of this meeting wrote it.**"

"**What has been lost.**"

"**Nine hundred people,**" said Hazel Cray. "**Nobody can now ask about nine hundred people. Not you, not me, not the flood office, not the family of a man of ninety-one. There is one sheet, in a drawer, in this building, and it is a list of the households who called a man with a clipboard, and about nine hundred people who did not call a man with a clipboard are on nothing, and they were on nothing before today and they are on nothing now, and the only difference is that today there is a piece of paper saying that on purpose.**"

"**That is a good minute.**"

"**That is a good minute and a bad morning,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and I have been in this flood since the first of June and I have never once been able to do the thing I was trained to do, which is to get a list of who is in a building. And I have now written down a rule that says we are not going to, and I have signed it, and it is the first rule I have written in twenty-two years that I expect to be argued with, and I am going to be argued with, and I am not going to take it back.**"
