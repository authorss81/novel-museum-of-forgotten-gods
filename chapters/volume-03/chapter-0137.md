# Chapter 0137 — Friday The First Of October

**On Thursday the thirtieth of September, Friday the first of October and the small hours of Saturday the second, a boundary on a wall behind a row of houses on Sillick Lane stopped, and a hardback of about a hundred and fifty ruled pages in a drawer that does not lock stopped on about the hundred and ninth page, and nobody appointed anybody, nobody decided anything, and nobody in that case has ever said in a room what either of those two things is for.**

**The date was chosen twice, by two people, about a fortnight apart, in about April and May of this year, and neither of them has ever said in a room what happens on it. A man of thirty-two noticed it seven times since the twenty-eighth of June and asked for nothing seven times. He noticed it an eighth time on Thursday the thirtieth, at about eleven o'clock at night, in a notebook, in his own hand, and he wrote down beside it that this was the last entry the notebook would make about that day before the day arrived, and he kept that promise in the only way it can be kept, which is by saying nothing on the day.**

**It is a dull chapter on purpose. Nine people stood in a yard in the rain in the afternoon looking at a wall, and three people were in a kitchen for six hours, and the only words anybody said in the kitchen were *It is Friday* at about ten in the morning and *Right* at about four in the afternoon, and both of them were said by a man of thirty-two, and neither of them was a question.**

---

## And on the Thursday afternoon a woman of eighty-one walked out into a yard with a hardback under her arm and about nine people followed her out, and nobody had arranged any of it and none of them had been told, and two of them had come for a cup of tea and one of them had come for a bus

It had been raining since about two in the afternoon and it was the kind of rain that gets into a yard and stays there. The wall is the brick flank of the old mill yard at the back of Sillick Lane, about nine feet high, and it faces the backs of nine houses, and the kitchen windows of six of them look at it.

The writing on it is about five feet off the ground and it is in white emulsion and it was put on with a roller and a bolt pen in about eleven minutes on the twenty-fourth of April 2025 by a man of fifty-two who lives at number 4 and who has never signed anything in his life and who was not asked to.

> **1. A boundary. Not a house. Not a street. A line and a period.**
> **2. Set on this wall on the twenty-fourth of April 2025. Not signed. There is a line for a name and it is empty and it is staying empty.**
> **3. This boundary is a date and nothing else.**
> **4. A date of the first of October 2025 at the end of it.**
> **5. Nobody signs it. Nobody keeps it. It is on the wall.**

Pinned to the wall a foot to the right of it, at about the same height, is a sheet of A4 with the same five lines on it in a different hand, and the fifth line on the sheet says this:

> **5. Somebody signs it. Somebody keeps it.**

A man of about thirty-four who lives at number 8 made that sheet in about eleven minutes one evening in May because he thought people wanted to know what the wall said. It is wrong. It is wrong because of a woman of eighty-one at number 6, who keeps a copy of the wall in a hardback on a shelf in a kitchen, and because a registrar of twenty-eight said out loud in a hall on the twenty-eighth of June 2025 that she was not going to take it down, and she gave her reason, and nobody improved on it.

The reason was: **it is wrong, and it is on a wall, and if it comes off then in about four years there is one account of the fifth line and it is on a wall in a yard where you cannot get to it in the wet, and a woman of eighty-one is going to be asked whether the fifth line says what she thinks it says.**

"**She is right and it is staying there,**" said Hazel Cray, who had come up on the Thursday in a car and had not wanted to and had come, "**and I have not said that out loud in this yard before and I am saying it now because in about four years somebody is going to be in an office with a file and the file is going to have a picture of that wall in it, and the picture is going to be taken from about ten feet away in the dry, and it is going to have both sheets in it.**"

Nobody said anything about that.

"**Has anybody said what happens tonight.**"

Nobody said anything about that either, and that turned out to be the shape of the whole two days.

---

## And the reason nobody said it is the reason a room above a shop gave on Monday, and on Thursday at about eleven o'clock at night a man of thirty-two noticed it for the eighth time and asked for nothing for the eighth time, and wrote down beside it that it was the last time

The kitchen at number 6 is about eleven feet across and it has a table, two chairs, a gas cooker with a kettle on the back of it, a dresser with three plates on it and a lamp, and a window that looks at the wall, and a drawer in the dresser that does not lock and has never locked and is not going to.

There were nine people in and around the house over two days. Two of them came for a cup of tea. One of them came for a bus. One of them was thirty-eight and had a child in the house next door and came at ten and went at eleven and did not say anything to anybody, because the woman of eighty-one was writing. A woman of about sixty-four from number 8 had not come for any of those reasons and did not say which.

> **Thu 30 Sep, 23:30. Forty-two weeks and four days since the sixth of December. The practice is thirty-eight weeks and three days old. The bell has said nothing for two hundred and ninety-one days and the panel on its case is three hundred and seven days old.**
> **The first of October is tomorrow. I have noticed eight times now that a man of fifty-two and a woman of eighty-one put the same date in two places and neither of them has ever said in a room what the date is for, and I have asked for nothing eight times, and the eighth was at about eleven o'clock tonight in this kitchen with the kettle on and a woman of eighty-one writing on about page eighty-four, and I did not ask, and I am writing down that this is the last entry this book will make about that day before the day arrives, and I am not going to break that, and whoever reads this in about four years and finds that I stopped writing about it on the thirtieth of September will know that it was on purpose and it was not because I had run out of anything.**
> **And the weekday, derived, and I am writing down that I derived it, because the rule is that the date is the real date and the weekday is the real weekday with two days put on the front of it, and the real first of October in the real world is a Wednesday, and the first of October in this case is a Friday, and I have got that right in a book and I have got it right in my head, and the only person who is going to say a weekday out loud about that date in the next eleven hours is me, and I am going to say it once, and then I am going to shut up.**

---

## And on the Friday it was a Friday, and the words in the kitchen were *It is Friday* at about ten in the morning and *Right* at about four in the afternoon, and neither of them was a question, and about nine people stood in a yard in the rain and nobody said a word

It was a Friday and it was the first of October and nobody in the kitchen said so.

Vera Pilling was eighty-one. She had been thirty-one years in a flat at the mill and eleven years in this one and she had a bad knee and she was going up and down stairs about nine times a day and nobody in the house tried to stop her and nobody in the house helped her either, because in June she had said a thing in a hall in front of nine people and the thing was that she was not going to be asked.

She made tea at about half past eight. She had a hardback on the table. It had been on the table since about half past eight and it had been open at about the forty-first page from the back and it stayed open there for about five and a half hours.

Nobody wrote anything down except her, and nobody asked her anything, and at about ten o'clock she said the first thing that anybody said.

"**It is Friday,**" said Elias Venn, who was sitting at the table with a cup of tea he had not asked for, and he said it because she had the date written and had her hand near the pen and had stopped.

And that was all that was said in that kitchen until about four o'clock in the afternoon, and Elias Venn said both things, and the first was the weekday and the second was *right*, and neither of them was a question, and the man has said no to eight things in about nine weeks and this is the ninth, and it was not a refusal, it was a man not asking a question he had noticed seven times and an eighth.

The rest of it was done in the way a thing is done when nobody is in a hurry about it. She turned a page. She ruled nothing. She drank her tea. At about eleven she went out into the yard with the book under her arm and about nine people were in the yard because it was raining and because it was the afternoon and because nobody had told any of them, and two of them had come at two o'clock because a man at number 4 had put his head out of his back door and said *are you coming down* to nobody in particular, and that was the whole of the organisation.

They stood in the rain and looked at the wall. Nobody read anything out. Nobody said anything at all until about four o'clock except a woman of about sixty-four who said *well* at about half past two, and what she meant by it has never been established and she has never been asked, because the practice of asking has been in this case for five months and it has not been turned on a wall.

The practice at Sillick Lane had three rules and no form under it. It ran from three o'clock until you could not read a number off a door. It had been running since the beginning of June and it was not running on the first of October, and the reason it was not running on the first of October is that the date at the end of the fifth line had arrived, and nobody had decided that, and nobody had arranged it, and the man of fifty-two who put the wall on in April was not there and did not know that it was the day and has not been asked and will not be asked.

"**I am not going to be the one who asks him,**" said Elias Venn, at about four o'clock, in the yard, to nobody, and a woman of about thirty-eight who was standing next to him heard it and has repeated it since to about four people.

---

## And the entry is one hundred and forty-one and it is on about the hundred and ninth page of a book of about a hundred and fifty, and there are about forty-one pages left in the book, and the book did not run out, and a woman of eighty-one has known since about the eleventh of July that the arithmetic does not work

At about four o'clock she came back in, and put the book on the table, and turned it round so that it was facing the man of thirty-two, and it is what is on this page and there are no names in it anywhere and there never have been.

> **141. 1 October 2025. — this is the last one.**

That is the whole of the last entry in a hardback of about a hundred and fifty ruled pages that cost a pound and ten, and there are no names in it, and there are about nine gaps in it, and the arithmetic has not worked since about the eleventh of July, because about four hundred more things to come do not fit into about forty-one pages whichever way you cut it, and she has said in a kitchen to about three people in about five months that she is going to run out of book before she runs out of things to write down, and she has never once used the word *will*.

The fourth line on the first page is the only line in the book addressed to anybody, and it has not been touched since the book was bought.

> **if you are reading this and you are not me, then you are the second person, and that is the only reason it exists**

"**Four hundred more,**" said Elias Venn, looking at the entry, and then he stopped, and then he said, "**Right.**"

And then he said nothing else, and at about five o'clock he made a second cup of tea and put it in front of her and she drank it, and at about half past five he took the two cups to the sink and washed them, and the woman of eighty-one sat at the table with the book shut in front of her and her hands flat on either side of it, and nobody put a hand on the book, and nobody asked her what the four hundred were for, and nobody has asked her since.

---

> **Fri 1 Oct, 23:30. Forty-two weeks and five days since the sixth of December. The practice is thirty-eight weeks and four days old. The bell has said nothing for two hundred and ninety-two days and the panel on its case is three hundred and eight days old.**
> **It has happened and it is not an event and I am going to write that down first because everybody who reads this in about four years is going to want there to have been one. A boundary stopped. A register ended. Nobody appointed anybody. Nobody decided anything and nobody organised anything and a man of fifty-two who put a wall up in April with a bolt pen was not there and did not know and has not been asked, and a woman of eighty-one who keeps a hardback in a drawer that does not lock was not in charge of anything and has not asked to be and has said so in a hall in front of nine people in June.**
> **The only words said in a kitchen for six hours by anybody were mine. *It is Friday* at about ten in the morning, because she had the date written and had her hand near the pen and had stopped. And *right*, at about four in the afternoon, when she turned the book round. I have said no to eight things in nine weeks and I said nothing to a woman of eighty-one for five and a half hours today and I want that written down as the ninth and as the first one that was not a refusal, because I have been keeping a count since June of things I have not asked and this is the first one where the not-asking was the whole of the work.**
> **AND THE BOOK DID NOT RUN OUT. Entry one hundred and forty-one, on about the hundred and ninth page of a book of about a hundred and fifty, with about forty-one pages left in it. Four hundred more things to come and they do not fit and she has known since about the eleventh of July. That is the whole of it and I am not going to improve on it and I want it in a book of my own before the end of the month: a thing stopping is not a thing finishing. A date of loss is a date on which a household has demonstrably stopped being a household, and there is paper for every one of those, and the paper is why, and the second one is a date at the end of a line on a wall and there is a hardback for it and the hardback is not full and it has stopped anyway. Five months of this case have been about a box, and I have not once seen anybody look at the box and ask what it would be like if there were no need of it.**
> **And the fifth line is wrong on a sheet pinned a foot to the right of the right one, and it is staying wrong, and a registrar of twenty-eight said so in a hall on the twenty-eighth of June and gave the reason, and the reason is that a wall in a yard where you cannot get to it in the wet is the only account of that date anybody has got, and in about four years a woman of eighty-one is going to be asked whether the fifth line says what she thinks it says, and a sheet that is wrong is a second account, and I would not have understood that in June and I have thought about it every day since the Monday and I have not improved on it.**
> **About four hundred more to come. To come. Not four hundred more days and not four hundred more entries and not four hundred things that are going to happen. Four hundred more things to come, in a book with no names in it, kept by a woman of eighty-one who has never used another word in my hearing, and there is a book nine miles upriver with dates down the left and what was done in the middle and nothing on the right and no names in it either, and a registrar of twenty-eight read that one in July and said it was better than anything the Museum produces, and I do not think anybody in this case has said out loud that the two books are the same object twice, and I am not going to be the first, and there is about a month left in which somebody might.**
> **The moral floor was not said and it is said zero times at full length in forty-six chapters. It is a fourth line on a first page and a fifth line on a wall and a book that is not full. I have had it in my mouth about five times since June and every one of the five had a room. That is not restraint and it is not a rule. It is that I have watched nine people stand in the rain in a yard for two hours not say one word, and a boundary that a man drew in eleven minutes with a bolt pen did not need a sentence, and I have gone round this flood for five months looking for the instrument and the instrument is a thing nine people did not say, and I have written that down twice this year and I am writing it down a third time and I am going to stop.**
> **The bill goes out. The name comes back.**
> **What a date of loss is for. Not asked this week, and I did not ask it, and the day has come and gone and I did not ask it, and I am not going to now, and a woman of thirty-four said it out loud in a church hall in June to about forty people and the answer was for the excess and it has taken me five months to understand that the question was not mine.**
> **What a legal neighbour is. And what a book is when it is not full and stops anyway, and what a wall is when nine people stand in front of it in the rain for two hours and one of them says *well* and nobody has ever asked her what she meant.**
> **And who keeps it. Nobody, and there are twenty-six now, and the twenty-sixth is a hardback shut on a table in a kitchen with about forty-one pages left in it and about four hundred more things to come and a fourth line on the first page and a name line on the second page that has always been empty and is staying empty, and the twenty-fifth is a letter that was not sent with three names under it, and the twenty-fourth is a pad with the number eleven on it, and I have noticed eight times and I have asked for nothing eight times and the eighth was on Thursday night at eleven o'clock and it was the last one, and I said in this book on Thursday that it would be the last entry this book makes about that day before the day arrived, and it was, and I have kept it, and there is nothing to add to it, and I am not going to.**
