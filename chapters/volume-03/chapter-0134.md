# Chapter 0134 — A Mark On A Wall

**On Monday the twentieth and Tuesday the twenty-first of September 2025 a man of about fifty-five with a folding rule and a laminated sheet in a buttoned pocket came off a bus on the ring road at twenty past eight in the morning, uninvited, unasked for, and unannounced, and he did not come to be asked anything, and four people watched him set four brass marks in a hundred and forty-year-old wall over two days and read four numbers into a book of his own and give the numbers to nobody.**

**This is the first instrument of the movement that is not a document, and it can say one thing, and the thing it can say is where the water stood. It cannot say who was behind it, and the man who set it said that out loud on the second afternoon in about nine seconds, and a registrar of twenty-eight said the other half of the insufficiency out loud in a room on the Monday, and the two halves together are the whole of what this case has for the next three months: a height, and a household, and not one of us can say what hour it was.**

**Nobody converted a height into a level. Nobody asked him a question about a pattern. The answer to a question this case has carried since the spring is still a number beside a knot, and it is not in this chapter, and the man with the rule was not asked for it and did not offer it, and the reason he came is his own and he gave it and it was not the reason anybody would have wanted.**

## And he got off the bus at twenty past eight and there was nobody there to meet him, and that was the arrangement, and he had made it

He came down the ramp with a folding rule in his hand and a bucket and a spanner and a lump of cement in a plastic basin under his arm, and there were four people on the wharf at twenty to nine and none of them had been told he was coming.

"**You are the rule,**" said a woman of thirty-six with a steel tape who had done eleven of these.

"**I am the rule,**" said Gwilym Petherick.

"**Nobody told me you were coming.**"

"**I told nobody I was coming,**" he said. "**I am not here because anybody asked me and I want that said on whatever it is you are going to write down, in those words, before I put a spanner anywhere, because if it turns out afterwards that this was the case asking me then I will put the spanner in the back of the van and drive off and you will not see me again until 2031, and I have said that out loud twice before and both times I did it.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**I have been coming to this wall since the fifteenth of June,**" said Gwilym Petherick. "**I came in June because there was a tape in the water and a joint that had not moved since 1985. I came in July and August because a joint went from nine to thirteen and then a coping dropped eleven more at the north end, and I have a book with those in it and the book is the only place they are. And I am here this week because the second of your instruments ran out at twenty-three fifty-nine on the thirty-first of August and nobody renewed it, and there is now a section of about four hundred metres of a hundred-and-forty-year-old revetment with nobody's name on it, and I am the only person in this county who walks walls.**"

"**Nobody renewed it because there is no form for the morning after,**" said the woman of thirty-six, "**and a morning after is not a period.**"

"**Then I am not the only person in this county who stands on a wall nobody has claimed,**" he said, "**but I am the only one with a rule, and I have got about nine years of my own readings, and I would like to put four marks in it this week while there is a fortnight of ordinary low water, and then I will go away and you will not see me until somebody needs a number, and that is the whole of my business and it is not generous and it is not clever and I would like it minuted that I said it out loud on a wharf at twenty to nine in the morning.**"

Nobody minuted it. He did not appear to expect it.

## And four marks, and the numbers are the only numbers in this flood that one man in the county can read, and that is the design and it is deliberate and he said so before he did it

A survey mark is a small brass bolt with a crossbar and a point, set flush in mortar in a wall, with a level on it that does not move, and the level is written in a book belonging to the man who set it, and the book is in a van.

There were four of them, and they went in at chainage zero plus nothing, zero plus one hundred and thirty, zero plus two hundred and sixty, and zero plus three hundred and ninety, and he set them himself, and it took most of Monday and about an hour on the Tuesday, and the only thing he used a spirit level for was the bolt.

"**Then nobody can read those four numbers.**"

"**Nobody in this city can read those four numbers,**" said Gwilym Petherick. "**That is on purpose. That is the entire reason I set them. He put the book on the wall, face down, and squared it with his thumb, and left it there for about a minute and a half while two of them looked at the wall instead of at it.

"**You may look at it if you want,**" he said, to a man of thirty-two who had not asked. "**I am not asking you to.**"

"**No,**" said Elias Venn.

"**No,**" said Gwilym Petherick. "**And I want to say that that was quick and I want to say the other thing as well, because I have watched you not ask me one question across about three months and I have counted it, and the reason you gave me in the summer was that you had no standing, and that was true and it was not the reason.**"

"**Say the reason you think it was.**"

"**I think you have already had one warm thing come out of an object this year,**" said Gwilym Petherick, "**and you believed it, and it put a woman's name into a column in a hall, and there is a line through her name on a public schedule now and it is legible for ever, and you have not asked me for a number in September and I would like to say that I do not think it is standing. "**That is the reason.**"

"**That is the reason,**" he said. "**And I have offered you a book nine times since the fifteenth of June and you have not taken it and I am not going to ask again, and the offer stands, and there is no deadline on it, and that is the difference between me and about nine other people in your flood.**"

Nobody improved on it. Ivo Pell, who was twenty-five and had come out on the Monday to be useful and had been useful twice, asked the one question he was entitled to ask, and it was not the table.

"**Who is the second person on a mark.**"

"**There is no second person,**" said Gwilym Petherick. "**A mark is a place with a number on it. It is about the wall. It is not about the house behind the wall and it is not about the man in the house, and there is nothing in the county that can put a person on a wall, and I have looked, and I have looked for about nine years and not for this.**"

"**Then the number is only in your head.**"

"**The number is in a book in a van, and the book is a job and not a document,**" he said, "**and the second it is printed it stops being a job and becomes a thing that was true in 2016, and I have said that to about four people since March and I am not going to say it a fifth time because a man of twenty-five has just heard it and he has a condition of his own that somebody wrote in four pages in a building on a hill, so he knows what a second person is for.**"

Ivo Pell said that he did, and that it was not the same instrument, and that he had not said it was the same and would not, and that the mark was the better instrument of the two and that this was the first time in about four months he had been in a conversation where somebody was right about his own thing and he was not going to argue.

## And on the Monday evening a registrar of twenty-eight said the whole of what this case has and does not have, in about forty seconds, and refused to speculate about the rest of it, and the reason she gave is the reason this chapter ends on

"**And when the number comes out, it will be a number beside a knot, and it will be in a corridor, and it will be mine, and nobody will have said anything.**"
