# Chapter 0113 — The Ground Floor, And Four Words

**On Monday the twelfth and Tuesday the thirteenth of July 2025 a registrar of twenty-eight went into a ward on the other side of the ring road that this case had been in exactly once, in the rain, twenty days before, and asked a woman of about forty-three a question that nobody in this city has ever asked a person, and the woman answered it in four words, and the registrar then had to decide what to do with four words, and what she did with them is the second floor of this case coming off the ground.**

**She is not named. Nobody asks her name. She is not asked about the flood, not asked about a claim, and not asked about the woman she went in to get out, and nobody in this chapter prepares anybody for anything. The moral floor of this manuscript is not said out loud by anybody in these two days, and it is not said by the man, and it is not said at length by the woman, and the page says so in his own hand at the end of it because in twenty-nine weeks that is the only place he has been able to put it.**

---

## And the reason a registrar came is a material question and not a moral one, and she had it in her head for nine days before she said it out loud, and it is this: there is no line on a card for a person in a building on a night, and there has never been one, and she has looked

Mara Iles had been in the case for five years and she had spent the first three of them believing that a record corrected violence.

"**I have got a job this week and it is not a favour and it is not a favour to anybody,**" she said, on the Friday morning, on a stairwell on the fourth floor of a building on Civic Hill, to a man of thirty-two who had asked her on Wednesday what he could carry. "**The Museum has been asked twice this summer by a body with a duty whether it holds any record of the premises, of the district, of the street, or of the people, and the answer is no, and the answer has been given twice by a Director who was correct both times. And I have been sent to write down what the Museum would hold if it did hold something.**"

"**That is a very short answer.**"

"**It is a short answer and it took me nine days and it is this. A card wants a thing, a maker, a date, a material, a provenance and a place. A card has got a line for the person who made it and a line for the person who gave it and a line for the person who bought it, and every one of those lines is somebody's *name*, and every one of those lines is somebody who has agreed to be on it, and there is not one line anywhere on a card for a person who was in a room on a night and did not agree to anything.**"

"**So the Museum cannot hold a person.**"

"**The Museum cannot hold a person who has not asked to be held, and a person in a building on a night has not asked, and if the person has asked then the person is on a claim and the claim has a reference and the reference is in an office on Wharf Street and not in this building.**" She had a folder and it had nothing in it. "**And there is one household in this flood that somebody has told me about, in nine seconds, on a kerb, and nobody has ever asked her anything, and I would like to go and ask her one question and write down the answer.**"

"**Which question.**"

"**I do not know yet,**" said Mara Iles. "**And I have not asked you for advice and I am not going to, because if I ask you what to ask then the answer belongs to the two of us and I want it to belong to her.**"

---

## And 14 Ashgill Row is nine flats and a ground floor and two above and a railings and a bin store and a strip of grass about two feet deep, and the door was shut, and it was not on the latch this time, and the door was not on the latch and the person who opened it had not been expecting anybody

They went at about half past two on the Monday afternoon. There was no rain. The tide mark on the outside of the ground-floor door was still there and had gone the colour of weak tea and had a chalk line drawn through it in about four places, by somebody, in a different hand, and the mark stopped dead at the left-hand door and went across the right-hand one, and the difference between the two doors was a step.

Mara Iles knocked the way she had been told to knock and waited and did not count.

The door opened about four inches and stopped.

"**Yes.**"

"**I am not from the council and I am not from the offices on Wharf Street,**" said Mara Iles. "**I am a registrar. That means I keep records for a building on a hill, and I am not here to ask you anything about the water and I am not here about a claim. I am here because I have got a question and it is one question and I am going to say it before you decide anything and you can shut the door at any point including in the middle of it.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**And the man with me is not writing anything down and he has not got a form, and if you want him to wait on the step he will.**"

The woman looked at the man on the step for about four seconds.

"**You told me you would not come in,**" she said.

"**I did not come in.**"

"**You have been on that step twenty days ago and you told me three things and then you told me a fourth thing after them, and the fourth thing was that you were not going to ask me whether there was anybody in that building on Friday night.**" She had not opened the door any further. "**So somebody has finally come to ask me that and it is a woman with a folder.**"

"**I have got a folder because I write things down,**" said Mara Iles. "**That is all the folder is.**"

---

## And the going is a carpet and a set of joists and a boiler, and none of it is the chapter, and the chapter starts when the woman says a thing about the telephone

"**The man came on the Wednesday,**" she said, from the step, with the door still four inches open. "**About the joists. And he was honest with me, which I did not expect, and he put a light under the floor and he said the boards are not joists, the boards are boards, and the joists underneath are sound, and the two of them are the reason my mother's bed is still in my front room rather than in a hotel.**"

"**That is good news.**"

"**It is good news that costs about eleven hundred pounds and I have not got eleven hundred pounds, and I have got a hundred and ninety, and that is a different sum and it is the only one.**" She shifted. "**And the other thing is that I rang the woman you gave me.**"

"**Wharf Street.**"

"**I rang her on the twenty-fourth of June. I have got the date on my phone because I looked for it afterwards and I did not write it anywhere else.**" She said it without any weight at all. "**I told her about the boiler and she asked me whether I was in the flat on the night of the eighteenth and I said yes, and she did not write it down, and then she told me that the money is seven hundred and fifty pounds and it is once, and that I have to be able to give them a date on a piece of paper, and that I have not got one, and that she is very sorry.**"

"**What did she say about the eleventh-hundred-pounds.**"

"**She said that eleven hundred pounds is not hers.**"

Elias Venn said nothing, which is what he had come to do.

"**And she was right, and she was not able to do one thing about it, and I have spent three weeks being extremely angry with her for not being able to do anything, and she has a job and I do not, so I have got off the telephone.**" The door came another inch. "**Do you want to come in. I have got a cup on and it is a bad cup.**"

---

## And the question was asked in a front room with the boards up and the underlay black, and it was asked in the plainest words anybody in this case has ever used for it, and nobody wrote it down first

Mara Iles sat down on a chair that had a chair under a dust sheet on it. The boards were up in the front room in four places and there was a smell in the room that was going to be there until the autumn.

"**I am going to ask you one question and I have thought about how to say it for nine days and I am going to say it badly.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**On the night of Friday the eighteenth of June, was there anybody in this building besides you.**"

Nobody in the room said anything. The woman was sitting on an upturned crate and she did not answer straight away, and the pause was not a refusal, and everybody in the room understood that it was not a refusal, and nobody said so.

"**And I looked properly,**" she said.

Four words.

"**Say that again for me,**" said Mara Iles, **and did not write them down yet.**

"**And I looked properly.**"

"**That is four words and it is not a yes or a no and I am going to be honest with you about what I think you have said, and you can tell me to stop.**" She had a pen in her hand and it was not over the paper. "**I think you have said that you went into that building and you looked and there was nobody there. And I think you have said that you have thought about it for three weeks and that you are certain. And I do not think you have said anything at all about anybody who was there, because there was nobody, and you are telling me that you are sure of it and not that you were told it.**"

"**I looked properly.**"

"**Yes. That is what I have said.**" Mara Iles wrote, and then read it back, and it was this:

> **Statement made by a person at 14 Ashgill Row, Bankfoot, on 12 July 2025, and read back to her in the same words.**
> **Question asked: whether there was any other person in the premises on the night of 18 June 2025. Answer given: "And I looked properly."**
> **The person has stated that she is certain of this.**
> **Recorded by: M. Iles, Registrar. Not a claim record. Not collections material. Not a copy of anything held by any office.**

The woman read it twice.

"**You have written *found*.**"

"**I have.**"

"**Take it out.**"

"**Say what to put in its place.**"

"**There was nobody there.**" She said it flatly. "**You have written that I looked and that I did not find anybody, and that means I went looking in a building where there was a person, and it means the not finding is the thing that happened. And I did not go looking for anybody. I went into a flat at about half past one in the morning because I was awake and the water was up and I could not see anybody from my own front room. And there was nobody. That is not a search and there is nothing in it to find.**"

Mara Iles put her pen down and then picked it up again.

"**All right.**"

"**And do not put my name on it.**"

"**I am not going to.**"

"**You are going to have to put something on it.**"

"**I am going to put a date and a place and a reference number of a form I made in this room this afternoon, and I am not going to put your name on it, and there is a line on the form for a name and I am going to leave it empty, and the form is going to say that it is empty, and that is the first time in five years I have left a line empty on purpose and I have thought about it for nine days.**" She wrote for about a minute. "**Do you want to read it back now or on Tuesday.**"

"**Now.**"

---

## And a registrar of twenty-eight has to decide what to do with four words, and she took two days, and the decision is a sheet of A4 in a folder on a shelf in a building on a hill, and it is not a card and it is not a photograph and it is not in a claim file, and here is why each of those three was refused

"**A card,**" she said, on the Tuesday morning, in a conservation office with the door shut. "**There is a woman in the label room who gave me her surname once and told me that you can card a bell and you cannot card a rope, and she looked at the form twice, and I have been a registrar for five years and she has been right about that for longer than I have been anything.**"

"**A person is not a rope.**"

"**A person is worse than a rope, because a rope does not read its own card back.**" She had the sheet in a clear sleeve. "**And I am not photographing it, for the reason that a man of twenty-five gave on a wet quay in June and which I have written down myself now: there is not a line in this form about what you are going to do with a picture, and if there were one I would have to decide about it, and I have enough decisions.**"

"**And a claim file.**"

"**And a claim file, and this is the one I want to put in my own book because it took me two days.**" She put her hand flat on the folder. "**There is a schedule, in every claim file in this flood, with a line on it in a column headed *held by claimant / held by office*, and the line says: any record of the number of persons in the premises on the date of the event — none held, no form is used to obtain it. And on Monday night a form was used. By me. On a doorstep, in my own hand, with a question I wrote myself.**"

"**And you could not send it in.**"

"**I could send it in and it would be refused, and it would be refused correctly, because there is a decision date and a claim reference and a policy number and a settlement, and there is no line in any of that for a woman of about forty-three who has not claimed anything and has not asked to claim anything and has lost a carpet and a boiler and eleven hundred pounds' worth of joists and has not got a date.**" She squared the folder. "**And if I sent it in and it were accepted it would become item eleven on a schedule, and item eleven is a line about a person and it would be in a file in a building in a room with about nine hundred other files, and in about four years somebody would want to know who she was, and there would be a name against a date at the top of a page, and she has not given me her name and I did not ask her for it and I am not going to.**"

"**So it is a sheet of A4 in a folder.**"

"**It is a sheet of A4 in a folder on the second shelf of a cabinet in a registrar's office in a museum on a hill, and the folder is not in the collections system, and the cabinet does not lock, and the sheet has a date and a place and a question and four words and the fact that it was read back.**" She held it up. "**And I have written a reference number on it, and the reference is mine and not the Museum's, and it is in the form *RG/26/4*, and the twenty-six is because it is the fourth thing I have recorded in this case and the first one I have recorded about a person.**"

"**Is it in the schedule of collections anybody may ask for.**"

"**No, and it must not be, and I have checked, and the answer is that the schedule of collections is a schedule of collections, and a person who has not asked to be held is not a collection, and if I put it in there then the Museum becomes the only building in this city that can produce a record of a person in a building on a night, and I would be building the instrument and I am not going to be the person who builds the instrument.**" She shut the folder. "**And I know what that sounds like, and I know that a woman who has fought two offices in this city about a man's name on a card would have something to say about me, and she has never told me what it is, and I have looked.**"

---

## And on the Tuesday morning she rang the number and put the woman on the line and read her the four words and the corrected sentence and did not ask her a single question about the case

"**I have a sheet with four words on it and a sentence underneath them, and I am going to read you both and then hang up, and if you want me to stop I will stop.**"

"**Go on.**"

> **Question asked: whether there was any other person in the premises on the night of 18 June 2025. Answer given: "And I looked properly." The person has stated that she is certain of this. Recorded by M. Iles, Registrar. Reference RG/26/4.**

"**And that is the whole of it and there is nothing else on the sheet and there is no box for a name and I did not write one.**"

There was a pause of about nine seconds on the line.

"**You are the first person who has asked me anything,**" the woman said, "**and I want to say one thing about it and then I am going to hang up because I have got a man coming about the boards at four o'clock.**"

"**Say it.**"

"**You are the first person who has asked me anything and you are the first person who has written down what I said and then read it back to me so that I could hear what I had said. And I have not got anything for you and I do not know what I would do if I had.**" The line went.

Mara Iles wrote the time of the call on the outside of the folder, in pencil, and did not put the time in the minute because there is no minute.

That has been the second floor of this case since April and it has been said in about nine mouths and it has been in a document since the eighth of June as a line on a schedule in a column, and on Monday afternoon a registrar of twenty-eight asked the question out loud in a front room in Bankfoot with the boards up, and a woman of about forty-three answered it in four words, and a sheet of A4 exists.**

Nobody in those two days said it. The registrar did not say it. I did not say it. > **What a legal neighbour is. And what a registry is, when the person it is about has not asked to be in it, and how a form can be made by a person with a duty in about ten minutes on a doorstep and be refused by every office that ought to want it, and be correct to be refused by all of them.**
> **And who keeps it. Nobody. A hardback in a drawer that does not lock. A pad of nine addresses in a locked drawer in an office on Wharf Street. A form in a system that generates an unsigned notice at four minutes past midnight appointing nobody. And now a folder on the second shelf of a cabinet that does not lock either, in a building on a hill, with a reference number in a private series and not the Museum's, and a name line left empty on purpose. Three drawers that do not lock and one that does, and I am the only person who knows about all four, and the one that locks is the one with the least in it, and that is not a system and it is not going to become one.**
