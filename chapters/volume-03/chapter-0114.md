# Chapter 0114 — A Page Is Not A List

**On Friday the sixteenth and Saturday the seventeenth of July 2025 three people went nine miles upriver to a parish called Braeswick, where a registrar of burials of about sixty-eight with forty-one years in post keeps a hardback of interments in a vestry, and were asked for a list, and were given a facsimile of one page, on the grounds that a page is not a list, and the reason for that is the manuscript's own argument given to a woman of sixty-eight with a form in her hand and eleven refusals behind her.**

**She is not a keeper of secrets, she is not a villain, and she does not know what is in a bonded store on a wharf thirteen miles downriver, and nobody tells her in this chapter, and the reason nobody tells her is said on the page and it is the best reason anybody gives in these ten days. And on the Saturday morning, in a church hall with the windows open, a reading is put on a table with a day on it, and the day is not this one.**

---

## And a river parish upriver is a reach and a lock and a church and about four hundred people, and a registrar of burials is not a registrar of anything else, and the two of those are not to be put in one sentence

The Braeswick reach was a bend with a lower lock on it and a weir above that and a church on the bank above the weir with a churchyard that had been moved twice, and the parish ran from the lock up to the top of a hill and about four hundred people lived in it, and the nearest post office was in the next village.

"**Before we go in,**" said Mara Iles, in the van, at about half past ten in the morning. "**I am going to say a thing that will sound like a correction and is not. I am a registrar. I keep records for a museum. She is a registrar of burials for a parish and she has kept the register of this parish for forty-one years, and there is no relationship whatever between her book and mine, and I am not here as an officer of anything and I have no authority in this building, and I have to say that out loud to her face inside four minutes or I will spend the rest of the day being somebody I am not.**"

"**And if she asks what a museum registrar does.**"

"**Then I tell her, and I tell her the truth, which is that I keep a record of about nine hundred objects and that about four of them are about people and none of those four are the people themselves.**"

"**That is a strange thing to say to a registrar of burials.**"

"**It is, and I have thought about it for a week, and I am going to say it, because she is the first person I have met in this case who is paid to write down that somebody died and I would like her to know what I actually do.**"

---

## And the question was asked in a vestry with the door open and a dehumidifier on and it was asked in about nine seconds, and it was not asked in a way that anybody in the room enjoyed

Averil Shand was sixty-eight. She had been registrar of burials for the parish of Braeswick for forty-one years and she had a hardback book on the shelf behind her, about four inches thick, and the shelf was a shelf in a vestry with a radiator under it and a dehumidifier on the floor with a tank in it that was about a third full.

"**You have come for the list.**"

"**We have come for a list,**" said Elias Venn. "**And I have got to say the rest of it before you answer, because in about four weeks I have watched a man refuse a thing and give the reason and it was the correct reason and it was worse than the rude one, and I am not going to do that to you.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**We cannot give you a reason that would satisfy anybody, and I am not going to invent one. The truth is that nine hundred households on a low bank nine miles downriver cannot say who was living at their address on a date, and there is a book in a shut building on a wharf that is the city record of everybody this city has ever recorded as drowned, and it has been under water since the nineteenth of June, and nobody can get at it, and we have found out in the last three weeks that there is a second question and the second question is whether there is any record at all that a person was alive.**" He put his hands in his pockets so that it was clear there was nothing in them. "**And somebody said that there is one, and it is in this building.**"

She looked at him for a while.

"**There is one,**" she said. "**And I have never told anybody that there is one, and I am not going to be asked for it twice, and you are the first person who has come up the hill in six months to ask me for it and you have told me the reason before I have said no, which nobody does.**"

"**You are going to say no.**"

"**I am going to say no, and then I am going to give you a page, and the page is not a list, and the two of those are not the same thing and I am going to be very careful about how I say it, because I have had this conversation eleven times and the eleven of them were all in the other direction.**"

---

## And a page is a record of a burial on one day at one churchyard, and a list is a set of names anybody can read in order, and she has been asked for the second for forty-one years and has given it eleven times and has lost a great deal by it

"**A page,**" said Averil Shand, "**is a record of what happened on one day at one churchyard. It has a date at the top of it. It has names down it. The names go down it in the order they came in, which is not alphabetical, because I enter them as they come in, and so a page has a shape that a list has not got: you can see what order things happened in and you can see where the day stopped.**"

"**And a list.**"

"**A list is a set of names anybody can read in order, and anybody is the whole of the problem. A list is a thing with a beginning in the middle and no end, and once it is out of my hands it is out of my hands in a way a page is not, because a page can be accounted for. I can tell you that the page for the ninth of December exists and that there are four interments on it, and I can give you that page, and in four years I can still tell you what is on every other page and I can tell you that none of them is missing.**" She put her hand flat on the hardback. "**A list cannot be accounted for. The first time I gave one away it was in 1986 and it went to a woman about forty miles away who was doing her family history, and she was looking for a man who drowned in 1949, and the list was in date order, and 1949 is a page in the middle of a book about four inches thick, and she was working from the front, and she did not get to it, and she told the family he was not there.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**I found the entry four years later on the fourteenth of June 1990. He is on the page for the eleventh of March 1949. His name is there. His age is there. It took me one afternoon to find it and it had been there for forty-one years and nobody had ever looked at the page because everybody had been given a list.**" She squared the book. "**And the family wrote to me and I wrote back and the letter came back, and that is the whole of what happened, and no one in that family ever knew that a mistake had been made by a woman who was doing something decent, and they have a different story now and they have had it since 1990 and it is theirs and it is not mine to take.**"

"**And the other ten.**"

"**The other ten were better than that and worse than that, and I am not going to do all eleven, and two of them are in my minute book with the dates in and I have not got a column in the minute book for what I lost.**" She looked at Mara Iles. "**You are the museum one.**"

"**I am.**"

"**And you have written something down already, because you have had your hand on a bag since you came in.**"

"**I have written down the date of your dehumidifier tank being about a third full, which is the only useful thing I have learned in this room.**"

"**Then write this as well.**" Averil Shand took a sheet of A4 out of a drawer, put it face down, and turned the multifunction printer on. "**I am not going to let anybody photograph that book. Not you, not him, and not a man from the water authority who came in 2014 and stood in front of it for about nine minutes with a telephone in his hand. A photocopy of one page is a different animal from a book and I can account for a page, and I am going to account for this one.**"

---

## And the page for the ninth of December 2024 has four interments on it, and the fourth of them has nothing in the column that is for a stone, and the thing in the column is eleven words long and it is about money

> **BRAESWICK PARISH. REGISTER OF INTERMENTS.**
> **9 December 2024. No. 1, KIRK LANE. No. 2, CHURCH ROW. No. 3, WEAVER'S COTTAGE, UPPER BRAESWICK. No. 4, THE PADDOCK, NETHER BRAESWICK.**
> **1. M. RUTHERFORD, 79, of 1 Kirk Lane. No memorial.**
> **2. A. PENHALIGON, 6 months, of Church Row. Ashes.**
> **3. W. H. TOLLEY, 84, of Upper Braeswick. Committal.**
> 4. E. SOWERBY, 91, of The Paddock, Nether Braeswick. *No memorial to be provided. Family cannot.*

Mara Iles read the fourth line twice and then gave the sheet back to the woman who had made it.

"**I have kept records for five years,**" she said. "**And that is the first line I have ever read that tells me about a family in this city that could not pay for a stone, and it is a line in a column headed for a stone, and the column is doing a job about a person and it does not know it, and I am a person who spends five years a week designing columns, and I have never once asked a column what it was actually for.**"

"**Then you know what to do with it,**" said Averil Shand.

"**I know what to do with it and it is nothing.**"

"**That is what I have said to eleven people and I have never said it well and you have said it in four words on a Saturday morning, so I am going to stop.**"

Elias Venn was standing at the vestry door and had not said anything for eleven minutes, which was the longest he had gone in this case without speaking and the only person in the room who knew it.

---

## And the man who ties a knot in a line every spring is two hundred yards from that church and has known the woman of sixty-eight for forty years, and neither of them has ever asked the other one a question, and the reason they have not is a wall

Emrys Tasker had a kettle on in the lock house and did not offer anybody anything until he had said what he had come out to say, and what he came out to say was about the churchyard wall.

"**It is the fourth time it has been down,**" he said. "**Nineteen fifty-four, and the sixties, and about 1979, and again in 2011. And every time it goes, the graves go, and every time the graves go they get put somewhere else, and every time they get put somewhere else there is a page missing out of that book and it is not lost, it is on a page, and the page is in the book, and the book is on a shelf.**"

"**Which page.**"

"**That is what I have been trying to say to her for eleven years and she has never once asked me the question, and I am not going to ask it now.**" He put a cup down. "**And I have been trying to say what she says since about 2014, in a worse way, and I said it to the water authority and to a man from the council and I have never said it to anybody who was writing anything down, because I did not think anybody would write it down.**" He did not look at the man by the door. "**So that is the whole of my part, and I am conceding it to her, and I am not conceding it to the two of you, and I would like it written down in whatever the two of you are writing in.**"

"**I did not ask for a list,**" said Elias Venn. "**I have been in this case for thirty-one weeks and I have never once asked anybody for a list, and I have said on the page twice that a list of houses is worse than a list of people, and I have got a notebook with about nine days in it and no names in it.**"

"**You want something recorded,**" said Elias Venn. "**On a page, or in a list.**"

"**I want a knot in a line,**" said Emrys Tasker. "**Which is the same thing, and is the reason I have got one and she has got a book.**"

## And the thing nobody told her about is a shut building thirteen miles downriver with about four hundred boxes in it, and the person who decided not to tell her is a registrar of twenty-eight, and the reason she gave is about eleven seconds long and nobody improved on it

It was Mara Iles, on the Saturday morning, in the church hall with the windows open, when the talk had got to a hundred and forty years.

"**May I put something on the table that is not mine,**" she said, "**and it is a reading, and it is not a burial and it is not a list.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**The Museum holds a schedule of collections that anybody in the public may ask for, and in the summer there is a note in it at the entry for one plinth, and the note was added on the twenty-eighth of May, and it names no person, and the note says *Municipal Works*.**" She put a sheet of paper on the table. "**And there are two things in that building that nobody has read. There is a hand on an index card from 1902. And there are two entries in the day book for the week of the casting. And neither of those has been read by anybody, and I have not read them, and I am not going to read them in a corridor.**"

"**Why not.**"

"**Because a null has to be minuted as a null, and a null that is minuted at four o'clock on a Tuesday by two people who have come in from a corridor is not a null, it is a rumour with a date on it.**"

Averil Shand had been listening with her head on one side.

"**You have said that three times now and I do not think you have noticed that all three of them are about the same thing,**" she said. "**You will not read a thing in a corridor. You will not have a list. And you have made a facsimile of a page. It is the same decision three times and you have made it for different reasons each time and every one of the reasons is correct.**"

"**What is the decision.**"

"**The decision is that a thing is read in a room, by people who are all in the room, on a day that is written down, and a note is kept, and the note is kept by somebody with a duty and not by the person who is frightened of it,**" said Averil Shand. "**And if the answer is that there is nothing on the card, then the note says there is nothing on the card, in those words, and it is dated, and it is signed, and in about four years a person in a room can read it and know that somebody looked.**"

Mara Iles wrote for about half a minute and then pushed the sheet across the table.

"**Then I am asking you to chair it.**"

"**I am sixty-eight and I have never chaired anything except a church meeting about a boiler.**"

"**You have kept a minute book for forty-one years,**" said Mara Iles. "**That is the whole of the qualification and I have watched about nine people in this case say that they have no standing, and every one of them was wrong about that in the same way.**"

## And a day was written down, and it was not this one, and the sheet that says what is to be read and who is to read it and what happens if there is nothing on it went into a bag and the bag went into a cupboard in a vestry with a dehumidifier under it

> **TO BE READ, in a room, on Thursday 19 August 2025, at ten o'clock in the morning, in the registrar's office, Civic Hill.**
> **1. The hand on the index card of 1902. 2. The two entries in the day book for the week of the casting. 3. The note at the entry for plinth thirty-four, which is already public, which names no person, and which is not read aloud and is not part of the finding.**
> **To be present: M. Iles, Registrar, and such other persons as are told they are required to attend. Two witnesses. A note will be kept. If there is nothing on the card, that is the finding, and it will be written in those words, and dated, and signed.**
> **The book is not to be read in a corridor.**
> **— A. Shand, registrar of burials, Braeswick, 17 July 2025.**

Nobody in the hall said anything about the nineteenth of August, and nobody said anything about who else would be told they were required to attend, and the man of thirty-two did not write it down in the notebook he was not carrying, and it is written on a sheet of A4 in a bag in a cupboard in a vestry with a dehumidifier under it and that is where it is.
