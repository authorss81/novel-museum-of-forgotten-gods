# Chapter 0120 — Who May Speak For A Community

**On Friday the sixth, Saturday the seventh and Sunday the eighth of August 2025, three days, a room above a shop in Fisher Lane with the windows open because it had been a hot week, a question was asked out loud by a woman of forty-eight with a statutory power, and nobody in the room could answer it, and a woman of sixty-one was asked it and gave an answer that was a way of behaving and not a person, and the chapter is three days long because the question and the room do not fit into a day, and the movement ends on a question and not on an event.**

**There are two minutes in this case now and there were none in it in June, and the second one is four lines long and three of them are about the question, and a registrar of sixty-eight made it upriver from a telephone at about eleven at night into a minute book that belongs to a parish. And on the Sunday morning a man of thirty-two carried two pieces of paper back across a city to a shopkeeper's counter in Fisher Lane, where they had lain unread since December, and told a woman of sixty-one the truth about a thing she has been owed since the fourth of June, and she asked him one question and he could not answer it either, and that is the end of Movement 2, and the paper is not a file and it is not a document and neither of the two things at the end of it is the thing at the end of the last one.**

## And the reason the question came up is a paragraph in a public document that has to be written in September, and the paragraph is about a community, and to write it the office has to know whom it is allowed to speak to, and the office has four names and none of them is a household

Yvette Carrow had come with a printed sheet and had put it face down on the table, which is something Marianne Cope had done with a plastic wallet in an office on Wharf Street in June, and nobody remarked on it and everybody noticed.

"**I am going to put a thing on the table and then I am going to ask a question and I am not going to answer it myself, and I have given that instruction to myself this afternoon and I am going to keep to it.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**The City Flood Office has to publish a recovery page for the Ferry Bank district in September. It is a statutory publication and it goes on the district's own site and any printed copy may be taken by anybody. It will have a heading and a paragraph about the water and a paragraph about the recovery and a paragraph about the community.**" She turned the sheet round. "**And the third paragraph wants a sentence beginning *the Recovery Forum was consulted by*, and the Forum has four names on it, and the four names are a councillor, a parish clerk, a man who chaired a meeting in a church hall in June, and a woman of eighty-one who keeps a hardback at number six Sillick Lane that nobody asked her to keep.**"

"**Four names,**" said Marianne Cope.

"**Four names, and not one of them is a household, and I have looked for about an hour and I could not get past that, and I have been doing this for eleven years and I have never once not been able to get past something.**"

"**Then what is the question.**"

"**The question is this,**" said Yvette Carrow. "**Who may speak for a community.**"

## And nobody in that room could answer it, and it went on the air at about nine o'clock on a Friday evening in a room above a shop in Fisher Lane, and it stayed unanswered for about eleven seconds, and then somebody started guessing
