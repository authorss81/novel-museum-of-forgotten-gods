# Chapter 0103 — Two Crews And A Docket Book

**On Saturday the fifth of June 2025, from about half past seven in the morning, two lorries with tail lifts stood in a street on the low bank in a district called Ferry Bank, and about two hundred people watched from upstairs, and a man of forty-four called Colin Bray wrote on a clipboard in a docket book in block capitals because it was going to be wet by eleven, and every line of it was about a weight in hundredweights that nobody weighed.**

**Nobody in that street had anything to do with a god and nothing in it came back. It is a trade. It arrived ten days before a warning that said the water might come, and it is paid for by weight, and the people paying for the weight are the people whose houses have already been under it once.**

---

## And the reason it is a Saturday is that a crew works a Saturday and a docket is written while the work is happening, and if the water comes on Monday then Monday is the removals and Saturday is the clearances

Colin Bray had been in the salvage trade for nineteen years and had two crews, four vans, a weighbridge at a yard eleven miles off the ring road, and a docket book per crew, and he had been in about nine streets on Ferry Bank since the Thursday before, and about nine hundred addresses were in his book from December, and the December ones were closed and the June ones were open.

"**People ask me why I am here before the water,**" he said. "**It is because of the weight. We get paid by the tonne, and we get paid by the tonne when there is a loss, and a lorry that takes a bedroom out of a house on the fifteenth to a yard on the sixteenth is worth nothing to anybody on the fifteenth, and worth a great deal to everybody on the nineteenth if the water has been in it.**"

"**So you are betting on the water.**"

"**I am booking capacity eight days in advance against a forecast, which is what everybody in this trade does, and if the water does not come I have four vans and two men on a Saturday for nothing, and so has the household, because she has given a lorry a room and there is no form in this city that gives a room back.**"

Nobody had told him there was a warning on a board. He had it on a printed sheet from the City Flood Office, reference CFO/25/118, in a plastic wallet, with the other nine hundred sheets of paperwork he carries, and he had read it.

---

## And he pays everybody on a Friday and he is owed fourteen thousand pounds himself, and he said both of those things in about nine seconds, in the street, unasked, and nobody made either of them into anything

"**I pay my lads on a Friday,**" said Colin Bray. "**Every Friday, whatever I have. I have missed two this year and I told them before I missed them and both times I told them why and neither time I told them it was somebody else's fault, because it was my own, because I put two vans on a street in March that I should not have put them on.**"

"**And what is in the way this week.**"

"**Fourteen thousand one hundred and eighty pounds,**" he said. "**Outstanding since the eighth of January. A managing agent in an office in this city owes it for December across about forty addresses and has not paid a penny of it and has told me four times that it is with their head office. Their head office is a floor above a man who took a call from me in February and said the same thing. So I have four vans and six men and no money, and I am working a Saturday, and I am telling you that because you have not got a stamp and because I would rather somebody knew than did not.**"

Elias Venn had a notebook and a week of days in it and no means of doing one single thing with any of it, and he wrote the figure down, and it was the first figure he had written in a book of that kind in about a month and he noticed himself doing it and did not stop.

"**You have just written down an outstanding balance of a small contractor in a book you are the only owner of.**"

"**I have. And I will not be able to prove it in four years, and it is the first thing I have written down in a month, and I am telling you that instead of pretending I did not.**"

---

## And the docket book has seven columns and six of them can be checked from the kerb and the seventh is the money

He let a man of thirty-two read a page. That was deliberate and it cost him nothing and he did it about nine seconds after meeting him, and the reason was that a man who stood on a kerb asking questions all morning was going to ask somebody else by lunchtime and he would rather it was him.

> **SALVAGE DOCKET — CREW 2**
> **Docket no. SB/F/06/0441**
> **Address: 31 Cutler's Rise, Ferry Bank. Date: Sat 5 Jun 2025. Crew: 2 (Slade, K).**
> **Client: named at door. Authorised by: client, in person.**
> **ITEM 1 — Ground floor, front room: carpet, two armchairs, sideboard, chest of drawers.**
> **ITEM 2 — Ground floor, rear: gas cooker, upright freezer, eight boxes personal.**
> **ITEM 3 — Hall: runner, two pictures, umbrella stand.**
> **ESTIMATED REMOVED — 14 cwt / 7 qtr / 0 lb.**
> **DISPOSAL: yard, Ferry Bank. TICKET NO. left blank.**
> **CLIENT SIGNATURE: __________  CREW SIGNATURE: D. SLADE**

"**Six of those seven I can stand on this kerb and tell you are true,**" said Colin Bray, "**because a man does not take a gas cooker out of a house and then put it back, and because the address is the address and the date is today. I cannot tell you one thing about the seventh. Not one.**"

"**Fourteen hundredweight.**"

"**Seven hundred and fourteen kilos,**" said Dermot Slade, who had been standing at the tail lift for about ten minutes without saying anything, and who was thirty-four, and who wrote in block capitals because it got wet. "**That is what he said on the step. He said there was a carpet, two chairs, a sideboard, a chest, a cooker, a freezer and eight boxes and he said fourteen hundredweight, and I wrote fourteen hundredweight, because I am not going to put a scale on a carpet in a front room on a Saturday morning for a number that goes on a docket.**"

"**Is there a scale.**"

"**There is a weighbridge at the yard and it weighs the lorry, and the yard weighs the difference, and the yard's number is the number the yard tells us and the yard has never once asked us a question.**" He put the clipboard down. "**I write what the client tells me and I do not ask them to prove it, and if I asked them to prove it I would be doing a different job on a Saturday for less money, and the job I do is a job.**"

"**What is in it for him.**"

"**Nothing at all, if the water does not come,**" said Colin Bray. "**He has paid nothing, and he has got a boiler, a freezer, a sideboard, a chest and eight boxes of his own mother's belongings in a lorry, and he has got a signature he did not have to give, and there is no form in this city that takes a lorry back. He has been done over by a man with a clipboard and a good forecast and he has paid nothing for it, and he is not the man who should be cross with me. I am.**"

---

## And the first refusal of the batch was a woman of seventy-nine who said no to a crew in her house, and she was right, and nobody in that street argued with her

She had come out onto the pavement in a cardigan at about ten past eight with her grandson and she said no from the top of the step, and she said the reason before anybody had asked her for it, and the reason took about forty seconds.

"**I know what you are. You have come about the warning on the board and I have read it, and I have read the bit at the bottom with the number on it, and I have been in this house nineteen years and I know what happens on the low bank.**" She did not come down. "**You are not clearing anything. You are filling in a docket about my house in case it floods, and the docket pays you for what you take, and I have had one of your lot in December and I got a cheque for the freezer and nothing at all for the sideboard and I have not seen a piece of furniture since and I am seventy-nine next year.**"

"**That's a fair go,**" said Dermot Slade.

"**It is not a fair go and it is not unfair, it is a bet, and you are having it and I am not allowed to.**" She put her hand on the door frame. "**If the water does not come, I have given a lorry my mother's things, my cooker, my front room and my sideboard, and there is no claim in the world for a thing I chose to give away. And if it does come, then I have given it away anyway and the water has had the rest, and either way I am out of a house and in a lorry with a docket number on it instead of a house with a door on it.**"

Nobody said anything for about four seconds, and then a man of twenty-five with a folding rule and a camera on a strap said, to nobody in particular:

"**She is right, and I want it written down that the trade is paid by the flood, and that everybody standing on this kerb knows it, and that the woman on that step has understood the whole of it in forty seconds on a Saturday morning and has got it exactly right, and that the only person here it does not suit is him.**" He nodded at the contractor. "**And the reason it does not suit you is that if she is right and the water does not come then you have done a day's work for nothing and she has done it for less, and both of those are true at once and neither of them is anybody's fault.**"

"**No,**" said Colin Bray. "**It is my fault. It is entirely my fault and I would like it recorded as such by a man with a notebook, because in about four years somebody is going to stand in a room and say nobody could have known, and I could, and I did, and I sent two vans out anyway because I have four vans and six men and no money.**"

---

## And a man of twenty-five had a condition of his own from the first of December 2024, and it cost him a photograph on a wet street, and he made a joke about it and it was the first joke anybody made that week

Ivo Pell was in Ferry Bank at seven in the morning for a reason that had nothing to do with the water, which was that the Museum's loading bay is on a slope and the drainage invert at the low end is about nine inches lower than the drawing says and he wanted to see where the water would come in if it came in, and there was a bay in the yard opposite the Sillick Lane branch library and he had booked a car and the car was late.

He had a camera. He had wanted a photograph of the docket book open on the tail lift, because he had got into his head overnight that about nine hundred dockets with one column in them and the money on that column was a thing that could be *counted*, and he wanted the page.

"**You cannot have it,**" said Dermot Slade, before anybody had explained anything. "**It is my book and it goes back to the office. You can read it standing here.**"

"**I would like a photograph of one page.**"

"**No.**"

"**Understood.**" Ivo put the camera away, and then took it out again, and put it away, and the second time was slower. "**And I am not going to photograph it with somebody's permission either, and I want to be exact about why, because I have been asked about this and I have not got it right once.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**On the first of December the Director put a condition on me and I asked for it, and the condition is that every photographic exposure of collections material by a member of staff is countersigned at the point of making. Not afterwards. At the point.**" He put his hand flat on the camera strap. "**And a docket book in a salvage contractor's book is not collections material, and I know that, and I have known that since the second of December, and I would like somebody with a duty to say it in a room so that I can stop thinking about it at seven in the morning on a Saturday. And until somebody says it, I am going to treat it as if it covers everything I point a camera at, because the last time I did not, four photographs of a drawer went into a police file and the last time after that, I had to be the one who found the wrong day of the week in nine pages that somebody else had been relying on.**"

"**So no photograph.**"

"**No photograph. And the reason I cannot photograph it today is not the condition, it is Dermot has said no and he is the only person here who can say yes.**" He took the folding rule out of his pocket and looked at the lorry's tail lift. "**Three rules and a kerb, and I am the rule-holder.**"

Then he laughed, which nobody else on that street did, and it was the joke he makes when he is frightened, and it took about two seconds and it was not good and it was not meant to be.

"**I will transcribe it. Slade, will you write me the one column out on the back of a docket and let me photograph that.**"

"**I'll write it and you can do what you like with it, and if anybody asks me I wrote it down at the man's request on a Saturday and I was not paid for the writing.**" Dermot Slade wrote fourteen hundredweight, seven hundred and fourteen kilos, on the back of a docket, in block capitals, and Ivo Pell photographed that, and countersigned it himself in the bottom corner with his own initials and the time, because there was nobody else there to do it and he wrote underneath, in the same hand, **COUNTERSIGNATURE UNAVAILABLE — ONE MEMBER OF STAFF PRESENT** and looked at it for a second and did not take it out.

"**That is going to be a problem for somebody in about four years,**" he said.

---

## And what a man of thirty-two could have had that day was a page of somebody else's book, and he asked for it, and said no to it, and told the street why in about nine seconds

He asked at about twenty past ten, in front of the two crews, and it was not a good ask and he knew it was not a good ask before he had finished the sentence.

"**Can I have a copy of any page of that book.**"

"**You can have a photograph of the page, no,**" said Dermot Slade.

"**Then can I have a sheet with four or five dockets on it in your hand.**"

"**I can give you one off the back of a docket with my writing on it and I have just given it to him, so no.**" The contractor had not looked up. "**And I will tell you the reason, and it is not a policy, and it is that if there is ever an inquiry into these nine hundred dockets I am going to want to be able to say that nobody outside this trade has ever had a copy of a page, and you have asked me for one on a Saturday morning within about two hours of standing on this kerb, and I would rather you had asked in a fortnight and been told the same thing by somebody with a duty.**"

"**That is correct and I knew it about four seconds after I started the sentence.**"

"**Then why did you do it.**"

"**Because there is a column in that book that nobody in the world can check, and it is the only one I have ever been inside of, and I have got a book of my own with about nine days in it, and this morning is the first morning in a month where I have wanted to put a document in it and I did not, and I would rather have said the sentence out loud on this street than have it sitting in me until November.**"

Nobody on that street had any idea what he was talking about and none of them said so, and the two lorries went back up the road at about eleven, and about two hundred people watched from upstairs, and one docket had been written with a number in it that nobody had weighed, and one docket had been written with a word in it that nobody would ever be able to weigh.

---

## And the one honest line in that book is the line where a crew did not attend, and it is the only line in it that anybody in this case has ever been able to point at, and it is not evidence of anything

Colin Bray tore the page off at about ten to one and put it in a plastic wallet, because a docket that has been torn off is a docket that has been accounted for, and he said so while he did it.

"**Every page that comes out of that book is accounted for,**" he said. "**Attended, fourteen hundredweight. Declined by client, nothing removed. Book closed and returned, nothing removed. That is the only vocabulary in it. There is no box in this book that says the number is wrong, and there never will be, because if there were one, somebody would fill it in.**"

"**And the declined one.**"

"**The declined one is the best line in the book and it is worth nothing.**" He put the wallet in the van. "**A crew did not attend at number forty-one and removed nothing and that is true and it is on a piece of paper and there is no form anywhere in this city that says what ought to have been in that column instead. If that woman had been robbed tonight, the docket would read exactly the same. So it is a good line and it is the only one, and I have had it eleven times since December, and I have not got the faintest idea what any of the eleven are worth.**"

---

> **Sat 5 Jun, 23:30. Twenty-five weeks and six days since the sixth of December. The practice is twenty-one weeks and five days old. The bell has said nothing for a hundred and seventy-four days and the panel on its case is a hundred and ninety days old.**
> **Two crews, a contractor of forty-four, a docket book with seven columns, and a weight column that a form asks for and nobody on earth can check, and the money is on the weight. Seven hundred and fourteen kilos. I have written it down in a street on the low bank at about ten past ten on a Saturday morning and I have told a man and a woman and a man of twenty-five and about two hundred people watching from an upstairs window that I wrote it down and that I will not be able to prove it in four years, and I said that out loud on purpose, because a thing I write down and do not say is a thing about me.**

The trade is paid by the flood. Everybody on that kerb knew it and the only person it does not suit is the man who sent the vans.**

He said that would be a problem for somebody in about four years. He is twenty-five and he is right and it is going to cost somebody something and not him.**

Nobody in this case has any instrument for that. That is correct. I knew it about four seconds into the sentence. I asked anyway, because there is a column in that book that nobody in the world can check and it is the only one I have ever been inside, and because in November I am going to be asked what I did about it and I would rather have said the sentence on a street than have it sitting in me. That is not the same as being right and I have written down that it is not the same as being right.**
> **The bill goes out. The name comes back.**
> **What a date of loss is for. Not asked this week. But I stood in a street and heard a woman say, without ever having heard the words excess or schedule, that a docket pays you for what you take and not for what was there, and she has been here nineteen years and she is seventy-nine next year, and she is right, and the two of those are the same fact in a street and on page two of a two-page instruction.**
> **What a legal neighbour is. And what a salvage crew is, which is a book with seven columns and a number in one of them that is a man's guess and a man's signature, and a man of twenty-five who was told no by the only person who could say yes and did not go and find somebody else.**
> **And who keeps it. Nobody. A docket book per crew goes back to an office on a Saturday and a page comes out of it into a plastic wallet, and the only line in nine hundred that anybody in this case can point at is the line where a crew did not attend, and that line is worth nothing, and the man who wrote it said so, in a street, and was right.**
