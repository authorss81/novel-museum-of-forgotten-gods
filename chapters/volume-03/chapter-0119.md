# Chapter 0119 — What The Form Does Not Ask

**On Tuesday the third and Wednesday the fourth of August 2025 four professionals with four different jobs sat down at one end of a table in a temporary office on Ferry Bank with a two-page instruction in front of them and went through the nine boxes on page one, one at a time, out loud, in order, and each of the nine decided something, and every one of the nine decided it about a person, and by about four o'clock on the Tuesday there were ten things on a sheet that no box in the form asked for, and none of the four of them would write a form, and the reason they would not is the reason this whole manuscript is.**

**There is no form for whether there was a second person and there is no form for a household's membership on a date. Both of those have been said out loud in this case for about four months. Neither of them is said in this chapter. The chapter is nine boxes and ten gaps and what four competent people do when they are asked to find a box for something that has never had one, and one of them is asked to do the thing nobody can do, on the same morning, and he says no, and the no is correct.**

---

## And the reason the four of them are in a room with a form is not that anybody has discovered anything, and it is that the settlement of a district cannot be computed without knowing who was in it, and about a third of the way through box seven a registrar of twenty-eight said the sentence that made them stop going through the boxes and start counting what was missing

They had a table to themselves at the end of a room in a temporary office on Ferry Bank, because the room was being used for something else until four o'clock and then it was not.

Marianne Cope, thirty-four, flood claims. Mara Iles, twenty-eight, registrar. Hazel Cray, fifty-eight, housing officer. Yvette Carrow, forty-eight, flood emergency powers. And a man of thirty-two with no post, who was there because a housing officer had said on the Friday that he was useful and had then explained in about nine seconds why he was not.

"**Say that again, because I have written it down,**" said Elias Venn.

"**I said you are useful. I also said that there is no form on earth on which you are a party, and that in about four years there is going to be a meeting about a settlement and at that meeting there will be a question about a street, and at that meeting the only person in this room who can walk up a street and knock on a door is a man with no post, and there is no box for him and I have looked.**" She squared the folder. "**I am not asking you to do anything today. I am telling you what you are for.**"

"**And I am saying no to it,**" he said, "**before anybody asks, and I have said no to seven things in thirty-three weeks and I have said no to eight counting a booking, and the reason is the same reason as the last one and it is not modesty: the day I am the man who knocked on the four hundredth door and got asked what is on a street, then in about four years the practice at Sillick Lane and Cauldwell Buildings are the same place, because I will have put myself there, and the difference between the two is a man of thirty-two who was careful and a man of thirty-two who was useful, and I do not know which of those is worse and I have decided I do not want to find out.**"

Nobody argued with him, which is the correct treatment, and Hazel Cray wrote four words on a pad and turned the pad round so that everybody could see it: *not asked. says no first.*

## And boxes one to three decide who opened a claim, who wrote their name on it, and whose policy it was, and every one of those three is a person who has agreed to be on the form, and that is the only reason those three work

> **1. CLAIM NOTIFICATION AND OPENING.**
> **2. CLAIMANT DETAILS (name of claimant, address of claimant).**
> **3. POLICY AND PERIOD OF COVER.**

"**One,**" said Marianne Cope. "**It decides who is allowed to start the thing. A notification can come from a policyholder, a mortgage lender, an agent, an occupier, or a person who found the property. It cannot come from a neighbour. It cannot come from a person who was in the building. There is no box in item one for a person who was in the building and there is no route by which they can start a claim about themselves, and about nine hundred people in this flood who did not ring a helpline are in a category the form does not have.**"

"**Two,**" said Mara Iles. "**It decides that the person who fills it in is the person it is about. And a name at an address is a household, and a household is a person, and that is the only identity operation in the whole of this business and it is in box two, and every other box takes the result of box two as given.**"

"**Three,**" said Yvette Carrow. "**It decides whose money it is. It is a period of cover, and a period of cover has to be somewhere, and the somewhere is a company, and the company has no idea there is a street. And I will say the thing that nobody has said in either of the last three rooms I have been in: item three is the only box in this form that can be read by somebody who has never set foot in the district, and it is the box that decides everything, and the man or woman who fills it in does not have to know a single thing about the water.**"

"**That is three boxes and three people who agreed to be on the page,**" said Mara Iles, "**and I want to be very careful about what I am about to say, because the next four boxes are harder, and I do not want the room to think I am building to something.**"

## And box four is the date, and the date has been answered, and the fifth and sixth boxes are the settlement, and the seventh is the contents, and by the seventh box a registrar of twenty-eight has stopped reading and has started counting

> **4. DATE OF LOSS (mandatory — see Part 5, section D).**
> **5. BASIS OF SETTLEMENT.**
> **6. EXCESS.**
> **7. CONTENTS — itemised schedule, or election under section 5.**

"**Four,**" said Marianne Cope. "**It decides whether a household existed at an address on a day. It is a date, it is mandatory, it is the fourth box, and it is for attaching a flat sum. It is answered, it was answered on the fifth of July, and it is a hundred and forty words to explain and I have explained it four times in five weeks and I am not going to explain it again.**"

"**Then go on,**" said Mara Iles.

"**Five decides which of two systems you are in. Six decides how much. Seven decides what was actually in the house, or elects the other system and gives up on that.**" She put her finger on the seventh. "**And seven is the one that has no form behind it either. An itemised schedule wants a thing, a value, a date of purchase if you have one, and a receipt if you have one. And the receipts were in the room. And a piano has no receipt and a wedding hamper has no receipt, and a woman wrote her own list in her own hand on the back of a bill and that is what got her seventeen hundred and forty pounds, and that is correct, and I have paid about nine hundred of those since December and about nine hundred of them are a person writing down what they lost from memory on a bad day.**"

"**From memory.**"

"**From memory, in a wet room, and the form wants a document, and the document is the list. And the list is not evidence. It is a person's recollection and it is the only evidence anybody has got and it is being relied on constantly and nobody has ever said out loud that that is what is happening.**"

"**It is happening on the other side of box two as well,**" said Mara Iles. "**Box two is a name written from memory. The whole form is a person's memory, typed.**"

"**Then that is a speech, and I have had about nine of those in five weeks and I do not want one today,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and I want to say the thing I actually came to say, which is about eight and nine, and then I want us to do the counting, because we said on Friday that we would do the counting.**"

## And boxes eight and nine decide when money goes and when a file shuts, and a housing officer of fifty-eight says the thing about nine that four professionals have not been able to say for five weeks, and it is not a moral and it is a form

> **8. PAYMENT.**
> **9. CLOSURE.**

"**Eight decides who gets paid, to an account, at an address, and it is the only box on the form with a payment reference on it, and everything before it exists in order to produce that reference.**"

"**Nine decides that nothing more will ever happen to this claim,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and here is the thing. There is no box for whether a household still exists. I have been a housing officer for twenty-two years and I have watched about nine thousand claims close on box nine in this city, and a claim closes when the settlement is made, and a settlement is made when the file is complete, and a file is complete when a person has stopped asking.**"

"**That is a sentence and not a form.**"

"**It is a sentence about a form and I have got a duty, and I am going to give you the fact under it, and then we are going to count.**" She put both hands flat. "**There are seven households living in four flats in a building off Low Ward on a weekly rent with a term to the thirtieth of November, and none of the seven has a tenancy, because there is no tenancy and there is no landlord's clause that makes one, and the owner of freehold has said in writing that she is not a party to any arrangement between the residents. So: seven households, seven addresses, seven rents paid weekly into a counter, and not one document in this city exists that ties any of those seven to any of those seven addresses, and if a flood claim came in from any of them tomorrow, box two could be filled in and box four could not, and the reason box four could not is that there is no tenancy to produce and there never was.**"

Nobody said anything for about four seconds.

"**Seven households,**" said Elias Venn. "**I know about seven households. I have not said anything about them since the first of June.**"

"**You have not said anything about them because you told a housing officer in a corridor on the first of June that you would not be in that building, and that was correct, and I have thought about it about nine times since.**" She did not look up. "**I am now telling eleven people in a room, and you are one of them, and you are the only person at this table who is not a professional, and that is not an accident and I have not arranged it.**"

## And they counted, and the counting is the whole of the chapter, and it took about an hour and forty minutes, and it produced ten items, and every one of the ten came out of a box and not out of anybody's opinion

They used the back of a printed sheet from the flood office and they wrote in columns and they did not once use a word that was not attached to a number on a form.

> **THINGS THAT DECIDE WHETHER A HOUSEHOLD WAS A HOUSEHOLD AT AN ADDRESS ON A DATE, AND THAT NO BOX ON THE FORM ASKS FOR. Compiled 3 August 2025 from the nine items of page one, in order. Nothing below is a view.**
> **1. Whether the person filling in box two is the person who was there. (Cope)**
> **2. Whether anybody else was at that address. (Cope)**
> **3. Whether the household had moved its paper out of the building before the water. (Cray)**
> **4. Whether the address was occupied at all, as against vacant, in a district where a vacancy rate exists and is not on this form. (Cray)**
> **5. Whether a person at that address could be traced at all. (Iles)**
> **6. Whether anybody has ever asked. (Carrow)**
> **7. Whether anybody was inside the premises on the night. (Carrow)**
> **8. When a household stopped being a household, as against when it lost something. (Cope)**
> **9. Whether a person has agreed to be on the page. (Iles)**
> **10. Whether a form for any of the above exists anywhere. (all four: no)**

"**Ten,**" said Marianne Cope. "**And I have put a one in the last line myself, and there is nothing above it that is a view, and every line came out of a box, and the two that are mine are the two I have been sitting in front of since the eighth of June.**"

"**Then that is the second floor of this case and it is on a sheet in about an hour and a half,**" said Elias Venn, "**and nobody in this room is going to say the sentence about it, and I am not going to say it, and I have a rule in my own book about it and I have kept the rule for thirty-three weeks and I am not breaking it in a room on a Tuesday afternoon.**"

"**I have a phrase,**" said Marianne Cope. "**And it belongs to two women who are not in this room, one of whom said it to the other in a corridor in 1994, and it is not mine and I have never been introduced to either of them, and I am not going to use it, and I want that on your page.**"

"**It is on my page,**" said Elias Venn, "**and it has been on my page every week since the fourth of January and it says the same thing you are about to say and I am not going to write it either.**

## And the fourth professional was asked the question that the other three could not answer, and she answered it, and the answer was that the one instrument in this city with a box for a person in it is not the one that has the person in it

"**I have been sitting here for two hours and I have not said anything and I have a thing and I am going to say it and then I am going to go back to a bank,**" said Yvette Carrow. "**I have got the only instrument in this city that can lawfully stop anybody entering a building, and it has a box for a period and no other box, and I signed it on the twenty-fourth of June and it was the easiest thing I have ever signed.**"

"**That has been in two books already,**" said Hazel Cray.

"**Then you know the second half and I am going to say the first half properly, because nobody has. The reason it was the easiest thing I have ever signed is that I have no power in it to see anybody's claim, their tenancy, their letter, their photograph, or anything at all about any person affected.**" She had not raised her voice at any point in two hours. "**So I am not suspending access to a building. I am suspending access to a building without ever having to know who is in it. And the form for a household's claim has no box in it for who is in a building. And the form for who is in a building has no box in it for a claim. And those two forms are the whole of what this city has got, and they do not touch, and the gap between them is item seven on that sheet and I have put my name against item seven because I have got a statutory power over a quay and not one square inch of power over a person.**"

"**What would close it.**"

"**Nothing I can write,**" said Yvette Carrow. "**I have been about four minutes on that question since Monday and the answer is that the two forms would have to be the same form, and they belong to two bodies, and the two bodies have a boundary between them that is about a hundred and forty years old and is on a wall somewhere, and I have not got a power to move a boundary and neither has anybody in this room.**"

## And on the Wednesday morning they decided four things and three of them were about stopping, and the fourth was about a sheet of paper in a drawer, and nobody wrote a form

They decided that the sheet of ten items would not be circulated. It would be held by one person, it would be shown to somebody with a duty on request, and no copy would be made, and the reason given was recorded.

"**The reason is in a sentence and I am going to say it and then I am not going to improve on it,**" said Mara Iles. "**A form becomes a standard, and a standard is read by people who are not in the room, and about four hundred and eleven people were handed a photocopy of a page of a manual in 2011 and none of them ever read note four, and that is the shape of it and we have the letter with the name and the date on it in a folder on a hill.**"

"**And I have got one line on this side of it,**" said Marianne Cope, "**and it is that if the ten items are ever written on a form, then item eight of the form will be a date, and the date will be compulsory, and somebody in about eleven years will refuse a claim on it.**"

"**Nobody is writing a form,**" said Hazel Cray, "**and I want to say why, and it is not a principle. If it is a form then it has an owner, and the owner is a body, and the body will put it in a system, and the system will have a mandatory field, and in about four years the field will be the whole of it, and there will be nine hundred claims with the field filled in by somebody who guessed.**"

"**And one line from me and I am going home,**" said Yvette Carrow. "**The ten items on that sheet are the best work anybody has done on this flood in five weeks and there is not one of them that I can put in an instrument, and the reason there is not one is that every single one of them would have to be answered by somebody standing in a wet room.**"

Elias Venn had not written any of it down, and he said so, and nobody asked him to, and it was the first time in thirty-three weeks that he had been in a room where a piece of paper was produced and had been the one person in the room who did not take a copy, and he noticed it, and he wrote it down that night and only that.
