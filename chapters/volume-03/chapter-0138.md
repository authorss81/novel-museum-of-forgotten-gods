# Chapter 0138 — A Second Book

**On Monday the fourth and Tuesday the fifth of October 2025 a woman of fifty-seven with a bad ankle and an exercise book from a shop on Ferry Bank for sixty pence came across a counter in an office on Wharf Street and offered it to the City Flood Office, and it is the best document anybody has produced in this flood, and the office cannot have it, and cannot refuse it, and the two of those are the same refusal and it took about eleven seconds and it is the shape of the next three months.**

**She is not a professional and she is not a villain and she is not a device. She is a woman who could not produce a date in June when a man with a clipboard asked her for one in a wet front room, and he wrote something on the clipboard and he did not write down that he had not got it, and she has spent about four months trying to give that date to somebody. By Tuesday evening the book has forty-one households in it and there is no form in this city that can take it and no person in this flood who is permitted to say no to it, and a registrar of twenty-eight said the sentence that has to be said to about nine hundred people over the next three months and then said out loud that she does not know how she is going to.**

## And on the Monday morning at about eleven o'clock a woman of fifty-seven put an exercise book on a counter and said that it was the only thing anybody had asked her to do since June, and a claims assistant of twenty-nine asked the only question that mattered, and nobody in that building could answer it

The queue was four deep and Sheila Kearney was not in it. She came to the side of the counter with a book under her arm and a photocopy in her hand and she waited until the last person had gone and then she put the photocopy down first and did not open the book.

"**Is this the right office.**"

"**It is the claims office for the district.**"

"**Then I have brought you the wrong thing for that counter,**" said Sheila Kearney. "**I have brought you a thing that is not a claim and I am not claiming anything, and I have had three people tell me that I am not claiming anything and I have come anyway, because there is nobody else in this city to give it to and I have had it since the fourth of June.**"

Marianne Cope came out from behind a partition with about nine seconds' warning, because somebody at the counter had telephoned her and said there was a lady at the side with a book and did not want to make a fuss about it.

"**Say the first line of it,**" said Marianne Cope.

"**I have not written one,**" said Sheila Kearney. "**I have written about forty and I could not think of a first line, and I have thought about a first line for about four months and there is not one, because it is not about anything. It is what happened in about forty houses and it is not a claim and it is not a survey and it is not a report and I am not a professional and I am not asking anybody to do anything with it.**"

Marianne Cope picked up the photocopy.

> **HOUSE. WHEN THE WATER CAME IN. HOW MANY OF US THERE WERE. WHO WAS IN THE ROOM. HOW HIGH. WHO TOLD ME.**
> **9 Sallyport Row. 19 June. 5 of us. Me, my husband, my mother, a boy of 14 and a girl of 11. Room: front room and back kitchen. About 2 feet on the front step, up to my knee in the back kitchen, and it went down the back of the cooker which is the highest thing in that room. Told by me. I was in both rooms.**
> **11 Sallyport Row. 19 June. 2 of us. Both of us were upstairs. The woman next door came down about half 2 and says it was at the door.**

"**There is no heading,**" said Marianne Cope.

"**There is no heading,**" said Sheila Kearney, "**and there is no name at the top of it either, because I have not written any names, and that was on purpose and I want to say why and then I want to say the other thing and the other thing is that it took me a week to work out the no-names and I did it because of a dog.**"

"**Say the dog.**"

"**In June a woman on this street put her sister and her sister's two children and a dog into a box on a form because there was nowhere else to put them,**" said Sheila Kearney, "**and I was not there and I heard about it from two people, and I sat on that step and thought about it for about a week, and what I worked out is that the moment I write a name in that book then that book is a list, and a list goes somewhere, and about four hundred people have been asked for a date on this low bank and about four hundred people have got a piece of paper saying they were asked and did not get an answer, and the answer is going to be in a form, and this is not a form, and I am not going to help build one.**"

"**Then what do you want.**"

"**I want you to have it,**" said Sheila Kearney, "**because in June a man stood in my front room with a clipboard and asked me for the day my door stopped working and I could not tell him, and he wrote something on the clipboard, and he did not say out loud that he had not got it, and he went, and I have had four months of it. And I want somebody in a building to have a piece of paper that says how high it was in about forty houses, because you have all been telling each other since June that nobody can produce a number, and about four hundred people can produce a number, and they are producing it at about one a day, and they are producing it on their own, and nobody has asked them and nobody will.**"

The claims assistant of twenty-nine, who had been in post eleven months and whose name was **Cerys Price**, said the thing that ended it, and she said it in about four seconds and she was right and she was not being difficult.

"**Who is the data controller.**"

Nobody in the room said anything.

"**Say the question again,**" said Marianne Cope.

"**Somebody has to be responsible for this,**" said Cerys Price. "**Not us. Somebody. Somebody has to be the person who is answerable for it, who can be asked by one of these forty-one households what we did with it, who has to give it back if they ask, who has to destroy it if they ask, who has to lose their job if they lose it. If it comes into this office then this office is the data controller of forty-one households' recollections of the eighteenth of June, and every one of those households can in writing ask us what we hold and we will have to answer them, and we will have to answer them in about a fortnight, and we will have to be able to say which bit of it is theirs.**"

"**And if it does not come in.**"

"**Then it is a woman's exercise book in a ground floor and nobody in this city is responsible for it,**" said Cerys Price, "**including her, and that is worse, and I am not being clever. I have been in post eleven months and I did a data protection course in July and that is the whole of what I know and it is enough to know that we cannot hold it and not enough to know what to do about the fact that we cannot.**"

## And on the Monday afternoon six people in a room worked out that nobody in this case may touch that book and nobody in this case may say no to it, and the two halves of that sentence arrived about four seconds apart, and neither of them is a rule anybody wrote down

The room above the shop in Fisher Lane had six people in it by half past four and the book was not in the room, because the book was on a table on a low bank in front of a woman of fifty-seven who had gone home at about twelve with her arm through it, and nobody had followed her and nobody had asked her for it.

"**Say it in order and do not let anybody improve on it,**" said Mara Iles.

"**One,**" said Hazel Cray. "**Nobody in this case touches that book. Not the office, not the case, not a register, not a schedule, and not him.**"

"**Agreed.**"

"**Two,**" said Mara Iles, "**nobody in this case says no to it. Nobody. Nobody argued with either half and it took about four seconds and nobody noticed the four seconds, and Elias Venn noticed them, and has written down that he did.

"**Say why the first one is not a rule,**" said Marianne Cope.

"**Because it is the best document any of us has ever been given,**" said Mara Iles. "**And I have spent five months saying that a document can be checked, and that is true, and it is the only reason I am a registrar, and a woman of fifty-seven has just produced a document that says how high the water came in a back kitchen up to a person's knee and in a front room about two feet, and it is checkable, and it would be checkable by anybody who went to that house and put a rule on the cooker, and there are about four hundred of them and about a hundred and forty people in this city who own a rule.**"

"**Then say why the second one is not a rule.**"

"**Because saying no to it is a thing we would have to do to a person,**" said Mara Iles, "**and we have not done one single one of those since the first of June, and I have said no to about eleven things in five months and every one of the eleven was a rule and not a person, and I have been waiting since about the eleventh of July for a person to come and ask me for a thing, and she came, and I am not going to be the person who says no to her, and if I say no to her then she stops, and if she stops then there is nothing.**"

"**And what happens on Friday.**"

"**On Friday there is a recovery page in about nine hundred houses saying that the office has not established whom it may speak for and is asking,**" said Mara Iles, "**and about nine hundred people will read that sentence and about nine of them will find out this week that a woman on a low bank has a book, and about four hundred of them will already be in it by December, and there is nothing anybody in this case can do about that, and there is nothing anybody in this case can say to stop it, and it is going to happen, and it is the first thing in this flood that is going to happen because of something we printed.**"

"**Then that is the finding.**"

"**That is the finding and it is about four sentences and I have been getting it wrong for four months,**" said Mara Iles. "**A form wants a number out of a person who is standing in a wet room on a bad day, and we have spent four months proving that the form is right and the person is right and the two of them cannot be in the same sentence. And then a woman with a bad ankle and an exercise book for sixty pence has taken the whole of the argument and put it in a place where it works, and it works because it has a heading and it does not have a heading, and it works because it has forty-one households in it and no names, and it works because the only thing it asks of anybody is that they write down a number, and about four hundred people have been waiting four months to be asked for one.**"

Nobody improved on that.
