# Chapter 0101 — The First Of June

**The term at Cauldwell Buildings began at ten past nine on Tuesday the first of June 2025, in a corridor on the first floor, with a set of four keys on a ring and a folder, and the folder had four pages in it and the fourth page had four flats on it and a rent in pounds and a weekly frequency and a name against each of the seven households who were going into them.**

**⚠ THE CLOSE OPENS ON A WOMAN OF ABOUT FIFTY-EIGHT WITH A FOLDER AND NOT ON THE MAN OF THIRTY-TWO, AND THAT IS A WIDENING AND IT IS DECLARED HERE SO THAT A READER IS NOT SURPRISED BY IT. Elias Venn is not in that building at all until about three o'clock in the afternoon, and the reason is on the page in about nine seconds: he asked, and he was told no, and the reason he was given for the no was the correct one, and he has never yet been on the wrong side of that particular fact. The first chapter of this volume is the first chapter since the sixth of December 2024 in which the man who is in twenty-six weeks of notebooks is not the man anything is happening to, and the only reason that is true is that somebody in a corridor asked him a question and he said no to the answer as well as to the question.**

---

## And a corridor on the first floor at ten past nine in the morning is the least remarkable room in this case, and seven households are about to move into four flats, and neither of those facts is a mystery

Hazel Cray had been a housing officer for twenty-two years and she had told four people in this building that she was going to be straight with them before she said anything else, and she said it again on the first of June because it was a different morning.

"**Before anybody says anything else. This is a commercial arrangement between seven households and an owner of freehold, and there is no discount, and there is no priority, and nobody in this corridor has been given a home, including me, and I am going to say that once a month while I am in post and you may check that I have said it.**"

"**Second thing, and I am saying it now because in three weeks somebody will ask me why I let the first be the first and not the day each of you got your key.**" She turned the folder round. "**All four rents are due on the first of June, and two of you will be paying rent this morning for a flat you have not slept in, because the term begins on the first, and I could have made it the Monday after each of you got the key, and I have not, because four different dates on a fourth page is worth less to all of you than one date, and I have thought about it for two days and that is the reason.**"

There were two sets of keys on the ring and neither set had been used yet. In flat 1 there were two households, in flat 3 there were three, in flat 2 there was one, and in flat 4 there was one, and that is how seven people came to be in four flats on a first floor off Low Ward.

"**A hundred and twenty pounds a week between you, all in,**" said the woman of about fifty who had the rota in a plastic wallet and had not come for the rent. "**And that is the whole of the money and I am not going to pretend it is more than that.**"

"**It is four flats, and the rents on the fourth page are thirty-two, twenty-nine, thirty-four and twenty-five a week, and that is a hundred and twenty, and I have done that sum in a corridor about nine times since May,**" said the man of fifty-two, from the back of the corridor, in the voice he used.

"**It is four flats, and I have not made an arithmetic error, and I did not put those two sheets of paper there by accident in May, and we had that conversation in a corridor and I am not having it twice.**"

---

## And at four o'clock in the afternoon two of the four flats were still empty, and the housing officer said so in the corridor, and nobody contradicted her

She said it to the landlord, who had come up the stairs at about half past three for the first time since the twenty-seventh of May, and she said it with the folder shut.

"**Two of these are empty and they are going to be empty all week, and I am not going to describe that as a problem, and I would like it written down that I did not describe it as a problem.**"

Enid Skelham was sixty-eight and she lived in the ground-floor flat and had let the four of them weekly since about 2009 and had three empty flats on the first floor and had had three empty flats since about December. She looked at the two sets of keys on the ring and did not take either set.

"**They are mine,**" she said. "**I have handed them over and that is all I have done and that is all I am going to do.**"

"**That is correct,**" said Hazel Cray. "**And I want to say why it is correct in front of the seven of you, because in about four years somebody is going to ask why the landlord of a building handed over four sets of keys and then went downstairs.**"

The owner of freehold did not look at the man of fifty-two and did not look at the woman of about fifty and she answered a person who was not in the corridor.

"**Clause seven, on page two.**" She said it the way a person says a thing they have read more than once. "**The owner is not a party to any practice, arrangement, custom or agreement between the residents. And the residents are not parties to anything the owner does in the building. I asked for that clause in 2011 because I have had a shopkeeper downstairs tell me what my tenants are doing at my stairs and I did not like it, and I have never been up here with any of you, and I am not going to start, and clause seven is the reason I do not have to.**"

Nobody improved it. That is what the clause is for.

---

## And the two clauses are in force from the first hour, and neither of them has been tested by anybody, and one of them has a name on the fourth page and the other one has a date on the third

Clause fourteen is on page three and it is one line and it was the housing officer's and the owner agreed to it in about four seconds and the owner has never read it since.

> **14. Any one resident of the group may end the group's occupation on fourteen days' notice without affecting the occupation of the others.**

"**I asked for that one and I will tell you why and then I am going to stop,**" said Hazel Cray. "**Because I have had three empty flats since December and I did not want a group of seven that cannot leave. A group that cannot leave is a group that stops paying in April.**"

"**And who in this corridor thinks that clause is going to be used in June.**"

Nobody answered, and the housing officer did not press it, and the man of fifty-two wrote nothing down, because nothing was written down in that corridor either. There is no minute of the first of June. The register does not begin until there is something to put in it and the register is not that.

The woman of about fifty from the sheet looked at the fourth page for a while and then said the thing that had been sitting in the corridor since May.

"**Two of the nine said no. They are not on this page. They are on a form in an office with DECLINED on it and a date against it, and one of them did not give a reason and I wrote NO REASON GIVEN and read it back and she said it was right.**" She put a finger on the page. "**That is the whole of the difference between them and the seven of you and it is a box in my office, and I have sat with that since May and I cannot make it any bigger than a box.**"

"**It is a box and a duty,**" said the housing officer.

"**It is a box and a duty and somebody who was prepared to be told no and to write down that they were told no, and I am not going to stand in this corridor and call that a victory, because somebody will.**"

---

## And the fourth page says the thirtieth of November at the bottom of it, and the housing officer is the only person in that corridor who can say what happens the day after

"**Term six months,**" said Hazel Cray. "**First of June to the thirtieth of November, and the thirtieth is a Tuesday, and it is in the contract and four people will sign it and I have the counterfoil.**"

"**And then they are yours again,**" said the woman of about fifty.

"**On the first of December they are mine, and I will let them to whoever I like, and I have not decided who, and I am telling you that I have not decided and that it is not a plan and that I will not be asked to have one before November.**" She closed the folder. "**That is the correct position and I have had it since the twenty-seventh of May and I have not improved on it and I am not going to.**"

Somebody said the word *home* in the corridor at about ten to four and the housing officer turned round.

"**Say that again and say it at somebody who has moved in,**" she said. "**Nobody in this building has been given a home by anybody, and the difference between a home and four flats with a landlord in them is not a matter of opinion, it is the fourth page, and the fourth page ends on the thirtieth of November.**"

---

## And on the board by the stairs there was a river notice, on a printed sheet, with a number on it, from a body with a duty

It went up in the stairwell of that building on the Friday before, and the same sheet went up in nine other stairwells in the same district, and on the noticeboard of the branch library in Low Ward, and outside the laundrette where a woman of thirty-two's mother worked, on tape, at eye level, with a drawing of a wall in the top left corner and a box in the bottom right corner with nothing in it.

> **CITY FLOOD OFFICE**
> **RIVER FLOOD WARNING — CANDIDATE PERIOD 12 JUNE TO 22 JUNE 2025**
> **Areas: Ferry Bank (registration district) and the lower terraces of Low Ward.**
> **Reference: CFO/25/118. Issued 30 May 2025. This notice is issued under the powers of the City Flood Office and is not a flood. It is a warning that a flood is possible in the period above. Residents in the named area should prepare ground floors, move paper and electrical goods above floor level, and check on neighbours who do not post.**
> **A flood alert will follow, with a level, if a level is forecast. Nothing in this sheet should be used to decide whether to enter or leave a building.**
> **Queries: Flood Office, Wharf Street. Quote the reference above.**

Nobody in that stairwell read it as anything but a piece of paper about the weather, and that was correct, and it was the first thing any of them had been given by anybody in twenty-five weeks that came from a body outside the ward with a duty attached to it.

---

## And at three o'clock he was in a room above a shop in Fisher Lane, and he was the only person in it with a notebook, and he did not use it

He had asked Hazel Cray on the Friday afternoon whether there was anything he could do on the first, and she had said no in about nine seconds and given him a reason, and the reason was correct.

"**There is nothing you could do here that four adults with keys could not do, and if you were in this building on the first of June then in about four years there is a man in a room who can say the practice at Sillick Lane was in Cauldwell Buildings on the first of June with him in it, and he would be right.**"

The room above the shop in Fisher Lane was let from three o'clock until you could not read a number off a door, which in the first week of June was about nine, and the shopkeeper on the corner had let it eight times now and had asked nothing eight times and had told nobody what it was for. The boundary went on the wall at three. It is five lines, and the fifth line says *nobody signs it, nobody keeps it, it is on the wall*, and that line has been on a wall in this city since the twenty-fourth of April and is now wrong on one sheet on purpose and is staying wrong, and nobody in that room proposed to change it.

There were six people in it. Frank Dunning had not taken his coat off. Vera Pilling had a cloth bag and did not take anything out of it. Mavis Ludlow was on the step with the knee, and had said her own name out loud at three of the four doors she had done in May and was not going to say it at four this week because the light was better on the other side.

And Elias Venn was in a room with a piece of A4 on the wall and a notebook in his coat pocket with a rule at the top of the first page in three lines, and the third line is *do not write it down*, and he read it at half past two in an empty room and then did not write anything down for five and a half hours, and that is the whole of what he did on the first of June.

---

## And the thing that made it different was not the room. It was that nobody asked him anything, and he had been asked nothing seven times in five weeks in May and had not known what to do with it

He was on the step at about half past three and two people asked him whether he was all right, and nobody asked him a question, and at about twenty past four somebody said *your kettle's on the boil if you want one* and that was the whole of it.

"**You have been standing by that door for about an hour,**" said the woman of about fifty, "**and nobody has said anything to you about anything.**"

"**No.**

"**Then you are doing it right.**" She did not look at him while she said it. "**There are five of us on this street who know what you are and none of us is going to say it in a room, and there is a man of thirty-two standing by a door who has not said it, and that is not a coincidence, and if you had said it you would have been asked about it for a year.**"

He did not answer that and she did not want one.

---

## And at about a quarter to nine, at the last door on that side, a woman who had lived there for nineteen years said out loud that she did not know what a number was for

She had the warning in her hand, folded in four, because it had come through the door of the ground floor in a bundle with two leaflets about a boiler and she had put it on the hall table and then picked it up again.

"**It came on Friday,**" she said. "**And there is a number on the bottom of it and a place to ring, and it says about the water, and I have read it about four times and I cannot work out what the number is for. It is not a claim. I have not got a claim. It is just a number.**"

"**That is the first piece of paper anybody at this door has ever been handed,**" said the man of fifty-two, **and he did not go in and he did not say a name and he stood on the step and let her shut the door.**

"**Twenty-five weeks,**" said Frank Dunning from behind. "**Twenty-five weeks and we have had nothing from anybody. And now there is a number.**

"**The number is for them,**" said the woman of about fifty. "**It is not for us and it is not for this wall. And that is the whole of what I have got.**"

Nobody said anything else on that side. The boundary came off the wall in one piece at about twenty to nine and the tape came off the plaster and left a clean rectangle, and the pound came out of the envelope and the shopkeeper took it and said the room had been let for one day.
