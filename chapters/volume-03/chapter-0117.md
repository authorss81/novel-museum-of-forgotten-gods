# Chapter 0117 — A Shrine Record, And Somebody Who Asks A Question

**On Monday the twenty-sixth and Tuesday the twenty-seventh of July 2025 a woman of sixty-one came down a hill from a river parish to a room above a shop in Fisher Lane, and she was the first person in thirty-one weeks of this case who is not a municipal employee, and she was the first person who asked Elias Venn a question instead of answering one, and she asked it in six words and he did not have an answer and did not pretend to.**

**Her question is *is a place allowed to say no.* It is not a mystery and it is not a trap and she is not a mystic. She keeps a shrine. The shrine has caretakers and there is a list of the caretakers and it is a list of people and she will not give it to anybody and she gives the reason in about nine sentences and the reason is this manuscript's own argument in a different mouth. A registrar of twenty-eight is in the chapter and reads the shrine's own book and the reading does not go well. Neither of them uses the word they have both been avoiding since January, and the page says so in his own hand at the end.**

## And she came because a registrar of sixty-eight sent her one question in an envelope before she sent a facsimile of a page, and the question was whether she recognised a name, and the name was a man of ninety-one who had nobody

Averil Shand had written it on the Friday before and posted it on the Saturday and it had come back on the Monday with four lines in it, and one of those four lines was a telephone number.

"**I wrote to her because I have always done it and because I have never once done it in forty-one years,**" said Mara Iles, in a room above a shop in Fisher Lane at about half past three on the Monday afternoon. "**If I am going to give a page of a register to a stranger, and I did give it to a stranger, then the person who gave me the information about the fourth interment on it is a person whose information I am about to reproduce, and she is not the vicar and she is not the family and she is the person who telephoned me on the eighth of December and told me a man of ninety-one had died at The Paddock with no next of kin inside four miles.**"

"**And she answered.**"

"**She answered one line of the four and she wrote the other three herself, and one of the three is that there was nobody else, and one of the three is that if the page is going anywhere she is to be asked first, and the third is a telephone number.**" She put a sheet of paper on the table. "**And I wrote back and said she was to be asked first, and she has come, and she is in the room, and she has not asked who I am.**"

She had come at about three o'clock and she was sixty-one and she had a coat over her arm and she had not put it on the chair.

"**You are the one who asked,**" said Hesta Rell. "**Not the man. The other one. I could tell from the way you stood up.**"

## And she keeps a shrine called the Last Cup in a cottage at the top of a hill in the same parish, and it is a room with a door in it, and there is no god in the room and there is nothing on any plinth in this city, and what happens there is that four people open a door on a Tuesday and sit with whoever has been brought

"**Say what it is, because you are going to write something down and I would rather it was right,**" she said. "**It is a room with a door and a table and a list on the wall, and the list is of the four, and one of the four is me, and there have been four for about nineteen years and before that there were three, and I have the names of every one of the four since 1994 and about nine more before that from a book my predecessor kept.**"

"**What is done in the room.**"

"**Somebody who is dying and has nobody goes and sits with them.**" She said it the way a person says a thing they have said about nine hundred times. "**That is all. There is no ceremony in it and there is no word in it and there is nobody in it who is a priest or a minister or anything. A person is brought to a door and two of us go and we sit with them, and we sit with them for as long as it takes, and one of us washes them, and one of us tells the house if there is a house, and if there is not a house then one of us walks about four miles to the register and gives the information.**"

"**And the register.**"

"**And the register.**" She looked at the registrar. "**Which is why your woman of sixty-eight wrote to me, and I have signed nothing, and there is a man of ninety-one on a page in a book because I telephoned a parish registrar on the eighth of December and told her that he had died, and she wrote what I said in a book, and that is the only reason there is anything anywhere that says he existed, and he was ninety-one, and nobody had come for him for about four days, and I am not going to tell you the rest of that because you would put it in a book.**"

Nobody said anything at all for a while.

"**Do not put that in a book,**" said Hesta Rell. "**I am not being coy. I am telling you where the line is and the line is there and I have been walking round it for about nine seconds waiting for you to walk round it too.**"

## And there is a list of the caretakers on a wall, and it is a list of people, and she will not give it to anybody, and the reason she gives is the argument of this case said by somebody who has never read this case

"**You want the list.**"

"**I would like to know who they are,**" said Mara Iles, "**because I have spent five years building records of things and there are about nine people in this case who have told me that a record is the only safe place for a person, and I would like to see one that has been built on purpose by somebody who does not want it to exist.**"

"**No,**" said Hesta Rell.

"**You have not asked why.**"

"**I do not need to and neither do you, and you have known why since the fourth of January and so have I, and you have not said it either.**" She put her hand flat on the table, which is a thing she did and he noticed it and did not write down. "**Four names. That is all it is. And I am going to tell you why I will not give them to you, and I have never said it out loud to anybody who wanted them, and I have had about nine people want them since 1994 and eleven more since March, so this is the first time I have said it to somebody who is going to write it down.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**A list of names at an address on a date is a household.**" She said it flatly and she said it slowly. "**That is the definition and it is not mine, it is yours, and everybody who has ever done anything in this city has got it. A name at an address on a date is a household, and a household is a thing that can be attached to a schedule, and a schedule pays a flat sum to a household, and that is the whole of your flood.**" She put two fingers on the table. "**And I am not giving you four names so that you can put four names in a folder in a building on a hill, because on the day somebody comes with a statutory power and a box, the only thing that will be in that box is a list, and the only list will be mine, and the four names will go in it whatever I want, because the four of us have no form and no reference and no office and no way of being summoned, and there is nothing to refuse you with.**"

"**Then say what protection you have.**"

"**A door on a Tuesday and a table, and four people who are not employees of anything, and that is the whole of it and I am not going to pretend it is more than that, because it is not more than that and a woman of thirty-one said a thing to a woman of fifty-four in a corridor in 1994 and I have never met either of them and I have thought about that sentence more times than I have thought about most things.**"

Nobody said that sentence, and nobody said it, and it was not said at length by anybody in that room on the twenty-sixth of July 2025.

## And she asked Elias Venn a question, and it was six words, and it is not a mystery and it is not a trap and he did not have an answer and did not pretend to, and the reason he did not have one is the whole of thirty-one weeks

"**You are the one who has been doing the writing,**" she said. "**Not her. You. And I have read what the woman in the shop tells me, which is not much, and I am not asking about your book, and I want to know one thing and then I am going to be quiet for about an hour.**"

"**Ask it.**"

"**Is a place allowed to say no.**"

Nobody in the room said anything. It went on the air in a room above a shop in Fisher Lane at about ten past four on a Monday afternoon and it was six words and it was not about a claim and it was not about a bell and it was not a mystery and there is no instrument in this city that would answer it.

"**I do not know,**" said Elias Venn.

"**Say the next part.**"

"**There is no next part, that is the answer, and I am not going to dress it up.**" He had his hands flat on the table and he had stopped counting the room. "**I have been refused four times in thirty-one weeks by people, and every one of the four was right, and a person refusing you is a thing I have got a shape for, and I know what to do with it, which is to go away and not come back. And nobody has ever refused me a place. I have been in a parish vestry, a church hall, a claims office, a room above a shop, a lock yard, a car park, a kitchen in Fisher Lane and a flat on the first floor of a building with a landlord in it, and every single one of them let me in, and I have never once had to stand outside a building and be told that a building would rather I did not come in, and I have not got a shape for that, and the shape I have not got is the one I would need.**"

"**Say the last part.**"

"**And I have been on about four hundred doorsteps since January asking people to be on a record, and I have not once asked a building whether it wanted to be, because a building cannot answer, and I have written in my own book that a building cannot answer, and I wrote that down because it was convenient, and you have been in this room for forty minutes and I have not written one word down and I have not had a single one of my nine seconds to myself since you sat down.**"

Hesta Rell looked at him for a while.

"**That is the first honest thing anybody has said to me about it since March,**" she said, "**and I am not going to thank you for it, and I am going to tell you the other thing.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**And I have got a second question and I am not going to ask it.**"

Nobody moved.

"**I have got a second one and it is worse than the first and it has to do with a room with about eleven of us in it who do not agree about anything, and I am not going to ask it today, and I am telling you that I have got it, and I am telling you that I am not asking it yet on purpose, and that is not a threat, it is that I would like about three weeks of watching a man not know the answer to the first one.**"

## And on the Tuesday morning a registrar of twenty-eight read a book in a room at the top of a hill, and the reading did not go well, and the reason is that the book is better than anything the Museum has ever made and she cannot put it in a card

The room was on the first floor of a cottage with a stone stair and no stair carpet and a cold radiator that had been on since somebody came in. There was a table, and a lamp, and two chairs, and a list of four names on a sheet of paper taped to the door frame, and there were about eleven other sheets of paper in a box on the shelf, and the sheets were the same four lines long with a different week on each.

"**What am I looking at.**"

"**A book. It is not the parish's and it is not anybody's. It is ours and it has been ours since about 1988 and it is the only thing about that room that is a record.**" Hesta Rell opened it on the table. "**Dates down the left. What was done in the middle. Nothing on the right.**"

> **Tue 2 Mar. Sat 6 Mar. Tue 9 Mar. Sat 13 Mar. Tue 16 Mar.**
> **Door opened. Sat with. Sat with. Sat with. House told. Register told.**

"**There are no names in it.**"

"**There are no names in it at all and I did not design it that way as a protest. I did it that way because I have watched what happens when a name goes in a book like this one, and I have watched it twice, and both times it was not done to hurt anybody.**"

"**Then what was it.**"

"**The first time, one of the four wrote a name in beside a date because she thought it would help, and it did help, and in about a year the four of us could not have a conversation about a Tuesday without somebody being frightened, because a name on a page is a person who can be spoken about, and then the person was spoken about.**" She turned the page. "**And the second time was a house. A house can be named. A house is a household in every system in this city and there is a form about a house and it has a box for a date, and a house with a name on it and a Tuesday in it is the exact shape of the thing that has been ruining this flood for seven weeks.**"

Mara Iles read two pages and then stopped and put her hand flat on the open page, which was the gesture of a woman of sixty-one at a table and she noticed it and did not take her hand back.

"**I have to tell you something and I am not going to enjoy it,**" she said. "**This is better than anything the Museum produces. And I am a registrar and I can say that as a professional and I have said it about about nine objects in five years. It has more dates in it and more of what was done than anything in a collection file I have ever opened, and it is more careful, and it is in a better hand, and it is on better paper for the purpose.**"

"**Thank you.**"

"**And I cannot put it in a card,**" said Mara Iles, "**and I have not been able to since about half past ten this morning and I have tried four times, and here is why, and it is the reason I read it badly. A card wants a thing, a maker, a date, a material, a provenance and a place, and I can fill in five of the six. And the maker is a problem, because the maker is the four of you and none of the four has agreed to be the maker of a record, and the place is a room, and I have got nothing for a room, because there is no line on a card for a room, and I have known that since 2019 and I have never once had to put my hand on it.**"

"**Then put your hand on it.**"

"**I have put my hand on it,**" said Mara Iles. "**I have put my hand on it about four times in this room and I have a form in a drawer on a hill that I made on a doorstep nine days ago, and I am not going to tell you about that form today, and I am not going to tell you why, and the reason I am not going to tell you is that you would ask me a question about it and I have not got an answer, and I have not got an answer because I have had nine days and I have asked nobody, and that is the thing I have done wrong this week and I have not told the man and I am telling you.**"

## And on the Tuesday afternoon she asked one question too many about a person, and she was told the truth, and the truth was correct, and she did not like it, and that is the reading that did not go well

"**Two. Do not print the book.
