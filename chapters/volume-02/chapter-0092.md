# Chapter 0092 — A Signature Is A Hook

There is a hall at 88 Sillick Lane with a tiled floor and a mat on it and a stair going up with a newel post and a handrail on one side only, and a table against the wall under the place where the post would have been in a house built for a bigger family, and on the table on Thursday the thirteenth of May 2025 there were two plastic wallets, a kettle that had just boiled, and a sheet of A4 with five lines on it and a date on the end of it.

**The second wallet had been put there on the Monday by the man of fifty-two, who had come back on his own at about half past four and had copied the wall out onto a fresh piece of A4 in the same bolt pen and had not signed it and had said that he was not going to be asked to sign it.**

**Nobody had asked him.**

---

## And a registrar of twenty-eight came to a hall on a Thursday afternoon and asked nine people a question, and said the answer was allowed to be no before she asked it

Mara Iles had asked to come. She had not come as the Museum and she said so in the first eleven seconds, standing in a doorway with her coat still on.

"**I am not here as the building. There is nobody from the building with me and there is no document in my bag, and if anybody asks me afterwards what I did today I will say I was in a hall in Low Ward on a Thursday afternoon, and that is all of it that is true.**"

"**Then why are you here,**" said Frank Dunning.

"**Because there is a piece of paper on that table with a date on the end of it and about nine people who are relying on it, and I am a registrar, and a registrar's whole trade is a name against a date, and I have spent five years telling people that the only thing anybody can act on in this country is that.**" She took her coat off. "**And I am going to say the cost out loud before I ask the question, because that is what I do and because in April a man of fifty-two wrote a document nobody had asked him for and changed his mind at the end of it, and I would like this room to know that I know that happened.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**Here is the cost. A name against a date on a piece of paper is the strongest thing in this country and the only instrument in this case that has ever caught anything. That is not a figure of speech, it is four or five separate things that have happened in nineteen weeks and every one of them worked. And the same property is a hook. In about four years that sheet is in a file, in a drawer, in a room, in an office, in a building, and there is a person's name on it and a date and an address, and somebody in that building has never met the person and their whole function is that drawer.**"

"**Then don't,**" said somebody at the back.

"**I cannot not. I have thought about it for nine days.**"

---

## And the question was one sentence long, and she asked it nine times, and she said out loud that she would not be writing the answers down

"**Two things and then I will stop. One. This sheet says on the last line that nobody signs it, and I am asking you to sign it anyway, and the last line is on the sheet and I am not taking it off, and if you sign it the last line is wrong and it will stay wrong on the paper. Two. You may say no, and I am not going to ask you why, and I am not going to write it down, and I am not going to count it, and if you say no then there is nothing anywhere that says you were asked and nothing anywhere that says you said no.**"

"**That is convenient for you.**"

"**It is convenient for you, and it is the only thing I have got, and I have said so, and I am not going to be thanked for it because it is not a favour, it is the third rule and it has been in a plastic wallet since the eighth of January and it is not mine.**"

Then she asked.

---

## And the first person she asked was a woman of sixty-one of number twenty-two, and she had read the whole thing twice before she came in, and she was the first signature in this case in twenty-one weeks

"**It is Mavis Ludlow. Twenty-two. Thirty-one years in that flat and before that the one above it with my aunt. I was a cook at St Anselm's until 2019 and I have got the knee.**"

She had the sheet in front of her and she had read it, and the woman of about fifty from the sheet said she had watched her read it, twice, on the Monday, and had offered to read it out loud and had been told no.

"**I have read it. I want one question answered and then I will do what I am going to do.**"

"**Ask it.**"

"**What happens to the signature afterwards.**"

It took about nine seconds and it was given to her without a pause and without any attempt to make it better than it was.

"**It goes in that wallet on that table. In about four years it will be in a file in a room in a building, because that is what happens to paper, and it will not be in this hall, and nobody will be able to take it back, and your name will be on it and the address and the date and a thing you did on a Saturday afternoon, and I cannot tell you which building and I would not tell you if I could, because if you knew the building you would start being careful about this and the whole point of it is that you are not careful about it.**"

"**That is the worst thing anybody has said to me in about two years.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**And I am going to sign it anyway.**"

"**I would like you to tell me why, and you do not have to, and if you do not tell me I am going to have to write *the reason was not given* on nothing, because there is nowhere to write it, and that is going to be in my own head for about four years.**"

"**I will tell you.**" Mavis Ludlow took the ballpoint off the table, which was the ballpoint Frank Dunning had had in his hand for two hours and had not used. "**In December I got a letter. It came to number twenty-two. It was not addressed to me, it was addressed to the house, and it was about something to do with a review, and I still have it, and it is in a drawer, and it is a piece of paper that knows where I live and it does not know who I am.**" She signed. "**That is the whole reason. I have lived at twenty-two for thirty-one years and there is not one piece of paper in this city that has got my name on it and that address on it. And if I sign this then there is one. And I know what that is, because I have been in a queue in a library back room for six years watching what happens to people whose names go into fields, and I am signing it anyway, and you have told me what it costs and I have understood the cost.**"

The second wallet went back on the table with a name and a date on it and nothing else on the line.

---

## And two more signed and two said no, and nobody wrote down which was which, and the not-writing took about nine minutes to explain

Frank Dunning said the thing about the fourth rule.

"**Somebody is keeping it. And now there is a name against it and a date, and the sheet is in my wallet and my wallet is on my table and the day I stop, it stops, and that has been the arrangement since the fourth of January and it is the correct arrangement and it is the worst arrangement in the world in that order.**"

"**That is the second time,**" said the man of fifty-two.

"**It is the third,**" said Frank Dunning, "**and your woman at the back counted, so it is three.**"

---

## And then a man of fifty-two said the sentence that the whole of this case has been walking toward since the fourth of December, and he said it in about eleven seconds, and nobody interrupted him

"**You are all treating that as a trap and it is not a trap. It is the only thing that works.**" He did not get up. "**Read the last line. *Nobody signs this. Nobody keeps it. It is on the wall.* That is a boundary with a date on the end of it and no names on it, and it is worth nothing, and it can be torn up by anybody who walks past it, and every single thing that has ever caught anything in this case has had a name against it. Nine names in a district's field. A dead man's name on a comparison sheet. A woman's name on a public minute. A card with a man's address on it. A record of issue with nine entries in it. All of them worth more than that wall, every one of them, and every one of them got somebody hurt, and I am not going to pretend otherwise to a room.**"

"**Then what.**"

"**Then signing is not agreeing.**" He put his finger on the last line. "**That is the difference and it is the whole of it and it took me twenty-one years of this street to get it. A signature on that sheet does not mean I agree with it. It means that on the thirteenth of May 2025 a person called Mavis Ludlow was in this hall and did a thing at number twenty-two, and that paper is now the only record in this city of either. And the day somebody comes with a clipboard — in four years, in nine — they cannot come to my door about a rumour, because there is a piece of paper with her name on it, and a piece of paper with her name on it is a thing you have to answer. A thing you have to answer is worth having. I have watched about nine people have nothing to answer with.**"

"**That is the first time anybody in this case has said that a name on a document is a good thing,**" said the man of thirty-two. "**In twenty-one weeks.**"

"**Then it is about time somebody said it and I am not paid and I am fifty-two and nobody asked me.**"

---

## And then the woman of about fifty-four, who has refused this Museum three times and was not asked about anything on the Monday, said the whole of the rest of it

"**I have not been asked to sign anything and I am not going to, and I want it noticed that nobody has asked me, including the woman with the satchel, including him, and including you.**" She was looking at the registrar. "**I have refused that building three times and the fourth thing that happened in this case is that it did not ask me a fourth time, and that is the entire difference between it and every other institution in this city, and I have been waiting since the ninth of April to see whether anybody would do that again.**"

"**I was not going to ask you,**" said Mara Iles.

"**I know. That is why I am still talking.**"

"**Do you want to say why you are not signing.**"

"**No.**"

"**All right.**"

---

## And a registrar of twenty-eight then said the thing that made the room go quiet, and it was about a list

"**I have been a registrar for five years and I have put about four thousand names into fields and I have never once thought about a piece of A4 with three names on it in a plastic wallet, and I want to say why, and the reason is that a piece of A4 is nothing and a field is something.**" She did not touch the sheet. "**A field goes into a system. A system has a custodian, a retention, a disclosure rule and about four hundred people who can read it. A piece of A4 goes in a drawer. **Signing this does not create a record. It creates the possibility of a record, and the possibility is worth more than nothing and worth less than a record, and anybody in this room who tells you that three names on a sheet of A4 in a hall in Low Ward is a register has not been in my office.**"

"**So it is not the same as the nine blanks.**"

"**It is nothing like the nine blanks and it is not going to stop the nine blanks and I would like that said in this hall before anybody goes home feeling better than they have any right to feel.**"

"**Say the other thing then,**" said the man of fifty-two.

"**The other thing is that three names on a piece of paper in a drawer is the first thing anybody in this case has produced that goes the right way. Everything else we have found in twenty-one weeks is a name coming out of a field. This is a name going into a piece of paper in a room that a person chose, and a piece of paper in a room that a person chose is the only container in this city that nobody has a jurisdiction over, and I would like that noted and I would like it noted that I am not going to pretend a plastic wallet is a system.**"
