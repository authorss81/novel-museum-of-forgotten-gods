# Chapter 0089 — Checked By More Than One Person

The label room is on the first floor and it has a card cutter, a drawer of sleeves, a bin, a stool, and a lintel dome over the doorway that covers the door and the cutter and about four feet of the floor and nothing else, and on the morning of Friday the twenty-third of April 2025 somebody who had not been asked for anything in six years stood under it and looked up at it before she sat down.

**Nobody in that room had asked her. She had been asked, in writing, on Friday the eighteenth, by the systems technician, on a piece of paper with a purpose on it, and she had said yes on the Tuesday and she had come on the Friday in her own time and had refused a bus ticket twice.**

---

"**You have to understand what you have asked me to do,**" she said, at about ten past ten, standing in the doorway with her bag still on.

"Tell me."

"**You have asked a records officer to check a man's handwriting against a source. That is my whole job. I have been doing it for six years and nobody has ever asked me to do it for anything except my own audits, and the audits are about whether I kept what I was supposed to keep and not whether what I kept was right.**" She put the bag down. "**The difference between those two things is about eleven years of my working life and I did not know that until you asked me on Monday.**"

---

**There were four people in the label room and a woman of about fifty-five at the cutter with a card she had not been asked to cut, and a camera, and nobody made an exposure.**

Mara Iles had come because she is the Registrar and clause 6 is hers in practice. Anja Ward had come because a Director's written authority is required and a Director who signs something ought to be in the room when it is used. Ivo Pell was the company. The man of thirty-two was there on a printed card with the word WITNESS in a box, in company, not before eight.

"There is a camera," said the records officer.

"**There is a camera over the door and the cutter,**" said Ivo Pell. "**It does not cover the drawers and it does not cover the middle of that table, and I have checked it twice, and I am not going to check it a third time in front of you because that would be the third time I have done it and I do it every time I use this room.**"

"Then somebody is going to have to stand where it cannot see."

"**I would rather nobody stood anywhere,**" said Fenn from the cutter, without turning round. "**I will put a card on the lens and it will say what it is for and the date on it and I will sign it the way I sign the others, and there is no exposure being made so there is nothing to countersign, and the condition says countersignature at the point of making and nothing is being made.**"

"**That is correct and it is in Ivo's condition of the first of December and I have read it about nine thousand times,**" said Fenn, "**and put the card on.**"

---

## And the first twenty minutes were about a notebook, and the second of them was about why the obvious person was not in the room

It was on a table in a cardboard folder and it had a rubber band on it and it was nine pages and it had cost thirty-five pence and the last page had been written on the eleventh of April.

"**That is the only copy,**" said Elias Venn. "**There is no other. It is not in a file. It is not filed anywhere, deliberately, under clause six, because it was not given to this building and nobody has ever asked me for it and I have never offered it.**"

"And on the twenty-fourth of February a solicitor told you that is a hole in the case with a person standing in it," said Anja Ward.

"**He did. In a committee room, in about nine seconds, and I have written it down and I have not torn it out, and he was right, and everybody who has said anything to me about it since has said it was the correct arrangement, and I have been carrying the comfortable one for eleven weeks and so has everybody else in this building.**"

"**So why is Mara not doing it.**"

Anja Ward asked it and it was the best question anybody asked that morning and the man of thirty-two answered it in about four seconds and then a registrar of twenty-eight answered it properly.

"Because she is in the case," said Elias Venn.

"Because she has already read one of them," said Mara Iles. "**He handed me a hundred and fourteen pages on the eleventh of December and I read it in twenty-two minutes and asked no question and took no copy and wrote a minute with a date on it that he made me give him so he could not move it. I am the only person alive who has read a hundred and fourteen pages of his handwriting and I am not going to be asked to read nine more, and not because it is a burden.**"

"So who," said the records officer.

"**You,**" said Ivo Pell. "**You are the only person in this city whose entire professional function is checking a hand against a source, and nobody has ever asked you to do it for anybody but yourself, and you have been doing it for six years and you have never once been asked whether the thing you kept was right.**"

---

## And then he read it out loud, in order, for an hour and ten minutes, and a woman with a pen checked it, and there were four things in it she could check

He read all nine pages. He did not summarise them and nobody asked him to and he has said since that it was the first time in four months that he had read a thing out loud and that the only thing he noticed while he was doing it was how tired he got.

She had brought two things. She had brought her own record of issue, which is nine entries in six years in her hand, eight of them her own retention audits and the ninth a museum, on the nineteenth of January. And she had brought a diary and a wall calendar for the months of December and January, which she had taken out of her bag and put face down and said she would not use them until she had to and she had to four times.

She checked the dates. She could check them, because a date is a fact about a document and every entry in that notebook that carried a date also carried a thing the date was about — a print run, an acknowledgement, a form, a hearing notice, a card somebody had cut.

"**That one's right. That one's right. That one's right and I can tell you why it is and you could not.**"

At about twenty past eleven she said, "**Stop.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**Read the line above this one again.**"

He read it again.

"**What is the date.**"

"**The third of March.**"

"**What is the day of the week written next to it.**"

"**The eighteenth of March.**"

"**Read me what is written.**"

"**I have written *Tue* next to the eighteenth of March, and I have written underneath it that the hearing is at half past ten in the morning and that I am not on notice for it. I took the date and the day off the letter of the nineteenth of February. I have never seen that letter since and I have never written the day of the week down twice in my life.**"

---

**And the records officer of about fifty put her pen down on the table and said the sentence that is the whole of the chapter, and it took about eleven seconds, and nobody in the room recognised that anything had happened.**

"**Then the date is right and the weekday is wrong.**"

"**The eighteenth of March 2025 is a Thursday,**" said the systems technician.

"**It is a Thursday,**" said the records officer, "**and I have not looked, and I am not going to look now, because I have been doing this for six years and I can do it in my head, and I have been wrong about it about twice in six years and both times it mattered so I checked afterwards.**" She turned the calendar round anyway. "**It is a Thursday. I have been right and I have not looked, and that is not a skill, that is a thing I have believed about myself for twenty years.**"

"Where did I get Tuesday," said Elias Venn.

"**Off a letter,**" said the records officer.

"Off a letter."

"A weekday in a document is not a fact about the world. **A date is a fact about the world. A weekday is a fact about whoever typed it.**" She said it the way she said everything, which was once and without repeating it. "**There is no arithmetic in a weekday. I have never once in six years checked a weekday against a source, and neither has anybody in this room, and I do not know a single person in this city who has, because we all think we can do them in our heads. And that letter was typed by a competent person in an office who got it from a diary that was right about the date and about everything else, and there is a weekday wrong in a nine-page notebook and it has been there for eleven weeks.**"

---

"**Who typed the letter,**" said Anja Ward.

"**A firm of solicitors, on the nineteenth of February, and it is on the file, and the date on it is right and the weekday on it is not, and there is nothing in that worth anybody's attention except that it has been sitting in a notebook in Low Ward for eleven weeks and nobody found it.**"

"He copied it," said Elias Venn.

"**He copied it, and he did not check it, and he wrote it down because it was in front of him, and nobody has ever in the history of the world asked a person to check a weekday,**" said the records officer, "**and it survived in a man's notebook for eleven weeks because there was nobody in this city in a position to ask you whether the days of the week were right, and now there is, because I am about fifty years old and I do this for a living and you have asked me to.**"

---

**And then she said the second half of it, and she said it to the man of thirty-two and not to the room, and it took about nine seconds and it is the only thing anybody said in that room that changed what anybody else could do.**

"**Which means it could have been anything.**"

"I beg your pardon."

"**That line could have been anything. A date could have been anything. A name could have been anything.**" She put her pen flat on the table, and it is the first time in this case that anybody has put a pen down the way a registrar puts a hand flat. "**You have written nine pages in a hand that about nine people in this city can read, and for eleven weeks the only person who has read all nine of them is you. And I have checked four lines in seventy minutes and one of them is wrong, and the wrong one is a day of the week, and a day of the week is the easiest thing in the world for a person to be wrong about and the easiest thing in the world for a person to be *right* about wrongly.**"

"That is not possible," said the man of thirty-two, and then stopped, and then said: "**Say that again the other way.**"

"**If I can be wrong about a day of the week off a wall calendar, then anybody can be wrong about a day of the week in a notebook, on purpose or by accident, and the only way anybody finds out is if a second person reads it.**" She did not raise her voice and she was not slow. "**I have been asked for one thing in six years and it came from this building in January, and I did not know then that it was going to be the only thing anybody ever asked me and that I was going to be the only person in this city who could check a man's handwriting. And I would like it noted that nobody thought to ask me for twenty weeks, and I would like it noted that it was not because anybody was unkind to me. It is because there is no form for *is there a second person*.**"

---

## And they telephoned Morningside at about half past eleven and then Irving Quay, and neither call was about a person

The conciliation service reissued the letter of the nineteenth of February on Wednesday the twenty-eighth with the weekday corrected and the date untouched and a line at the foot that said the weekday on the original was wrong and the date on the original was correct. **The wrong one is not destroyed. It is on file, and the right one is on top of it, and both are dated, and that is the first time in this case that anything has been corrected at source and versioned, and it is a day of the week.**

The solicitor rang at about twenty past twelve and said four things and did not apologise for any of them.

"**The weekday in my letter is wrong and the date is right and I have had the letter typed by two people and the second one typed it off the first, which is what everybody does. Nobody in this court has ever been harmed by a weekday. It survived four weeks in a nine-page notebook because nobody has ever asked anybody to check a weekday, and I include myself, and I have spent nineteen years telling witnesses that their evidence cannot be tested and I have had a letter of my own in a notebook for eleven weeks that nobody tested, and I am not going to draw a moral out of that on a Friday morning.**"

"What will you do," said Anja Ward.

"**I will write to the service and ask them to correct it, which they have done, and I will ask that the correction be on the file, which it is, and I will put in my next letter to you a sentence saying that a weekday is evidence and that anybody relying on my correspondence should check them, which is a sentence I would not have written in February and which I am writing in April because a records officer of about fifty put it in a room.**"

---

## And at about twenty past one a card was cut in the label room and it is on a front desk in a public lobby and it names nobody at all

Fenn cut it in about four minutes and did not put a name in the maker's line and did not put a name anywhere else, and there is a signature on it and the signature is an office.
