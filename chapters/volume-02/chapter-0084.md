# Chapter 0084 — Four Lines On A Step

The hall at 88 Sillick Lane has a table in it, a mat on the floor in front of the door with its worn side facing into the house, a plastic wallet on the table with a sheet of A4 in it, and a kettle that is on about twice a day, and at three o'clock on the afternoon of Monday the twenty-second of March 2025 there were about eleven people in it, which is two more than there had ever been, and one of the two was a registrar of twenty-eight who had never been to Sillick Lane in her life and had written four lines on a sheet of paper in her own hand and got the man whose sheet it was to change the date on it.

**The practice was eleven weeks old. It had begun on Monday the fourth of January at about half past three in the afternoon because a woman of eighty-one stood on the step of number eighty-eight and said a man's name out loud, and she was not in the hall on the twenty-second of March and would not be until the middle of April, and that is the first fact of the afternoon and it is the reason about half of what follows happens.**

---

She had not been let in since Wednesday the thirteenth of January, which is the morning the Hall of Former Faiths reopened on Civic Hill and the morning a woman of eighty-one went into a clinic, and she had been told on Tuesday the twenty-sixth of January, by a registration officer of thirty-one with a satchel, **you go back to the house, not next year, this spring**, and nobody has asked her since, and there is a date on a piece of paper in a clinic file which is the middle of April, and the middle of April is a weekday, and she is eighty-one, and she is the only person in this city who can say anything about a man called Walter Naylor and she has not been asked.

Frank Dunning said that in about nine seconds when somebody asked him why she was not there, and he said it standing by the table with his hand flat on it, and nobody had asked him because they had all known.

"**She's in a clinic since the thirteenth of January and she's coming back in the middle of April and if anybody in this hall has a different date they can say it now.**"

Nobody said a different date.

"**Right,**" said Frank Dunning, "**and I'm not going to say anything else about it, and there's a reason and the reason is that she's the only one of us who's paid for this with her body and the rest of us have paid for it with a Tuesday, and I'm sixty-five and I'm not going to stand in a hall and talk about a woman of eighty-one who isn't in it.**"

---

## The four lines were read out on the step at about twenty past three and the door was open the whole time

Mara Iles read them. She had them on a sheet of her own paper and she did not have a folder and she did not have the four lines on the sheet in the plastic wallet, because they are not on it — they are on a separate piece of paper in a registrar's own hand with a date at the top, and the date says **any day after 21 March 2025**, and Frank Dunning had changed the twenty-first to the twenty-second in February because on the twenty-second his tenancy has still got more than a month to run and he is still the man with the table.

She stood on the step, which is the step of number eighty-eight, which is a council house with a hall that is used for meetings, and she did not go inside, and she read it out at the volume of a person who has decided to be heard by a street.

> **The Museum of Quiet Saints holds no record of this. It will not be given one. A court may ask whether we saw it. What we saw, if we are asked, is a person standing on a mat and saying a name aloud at a door. Nobody will be named. Nothing will be described. The Museum will say that and no more.**

It took about twenty-five seconds and the street is two hundred yards long and has eleven letter boxes on it and about nine flats on the east side and the numbers do not run in order, and a woman with a pushchair was going towards the water and she stopped, and a man on the other side of the road did not stop, and about four people came out of the doors they were behind, and one of them was about eighty.

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds afterwards.

"Who is it for," said the woman with the pushchair.

"**For a court,**" said Mara Iles. "**In about four years, if there is one, about a landlord, about something that happened on a street. Somebody will write to this building and ask whether we saw anything. This is what we will say we saw, and it is all of it, and there is no name in it and no house in it and no date in it.**"

"And if they ask you what you saw on a particular day."

"**Then I will say a person stood on a mat and said a name aloud at a door, and that is true, and it will be in a transcript, and there will be nothing else in the transcript, and I will not be able to improve on it in four years, and I have tried four times.**"

"That's the whole of it."

"**That is the whole of it. It is four lines and it is the first thing I have ever written in my life that is a promise to say nothing, and it is the reason a man of sixty-five agreed to it, and the reason is that a person who is told *nobody will be named* believes it, and a person who is told *we will say what we saw* does not.**"

---

## And then the man with the table had to decide whether to hand it out, and he decided in about four seconds, and the decision was not what anybody expected

"**Right,**" said Frank Dunning. "**That's read. Now — I said on the twentieth of February that I'd hand it out if I thought it should be handed out, and I have thought about it since then, and I'm not going to tell you how long, and here it is. I'm not going to hand it to anybody who hasn't put their hand out.**"

"**Why not,**" said the woman of about fifty from the sheet.

"**Because if I hand it to everybody then in about four days it is a leaflet, and a leaflet is a thing a building gives out, and a thing a building gives out is a thing a building is the author of.**" He did not look at the registrar. "**And I have read your minute, because a Director read it out to me in this hall on the twentieth of February, and it says this museum is not going to be the author of anything about this street. If I hand that paper to everybody on this street then in a year there is a hundred and forty bits of paper with your four lines on them in Low Ward and a person in about four years says *everybody had that*, and the answer is yes everybody had that, because a man in a hall handed them out, and then your minute is a piece of paper that was given away.**

"**So it's a hand out. If you want it, put your hand out. I'm not going to ask anybody whether they want it, because asking is a form, and there is no form.**"

About four people put their hands out and he gave those four a sheet each and he did not count them afterwards, and somebody said *how many took one*, and he said:

"**I have counted things at a gate for about forty years and I am not going to start at sixty-five on a Monday afternoon, and if anybody in this hall has a piece of paper with four lines on it then that is how many took one.**"

---

## And then he asked them, and it took about four minutes, and it is the reason the second half of April is going the way it is

Nobody had asked him to. Nobody in the hall had expected it. The registrar of twenty-eight had come up the hill with four lines and a date and had expected to read them and go down the hill again, and she has said since that she was not ready for what happened after the reading and that she has not told anybody she was not ready, and that this is the first time in five years she has been not ready for a room.

"**Right,**" said Frank Dunning, at about ten to four, to about eleven people in a hall. "**I've been running this for eleven weeks and I have just read four lines off a step and I want to ask everybody in this room one question and I want everybody to know I'm asking it and I want the answer to be allowed to be no and I am not going to ask anybody why.**"

"**Go on,**" said the woman of about fifty.

"**Do you want to be in this.**"

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"**Right. That's the question and it's the only one. Not do you want to come on Wednesdays. Do you want to be in it. There's a difference and I did not know there was a difference until about four minutes ago when I realised I had been treating them as the same thing for eleven weeks and I run a gate for a living.**"

"**Elias. Do you want to be in this.**"

"I have never been in it," said the man in the coat. "**I have stood in this hall about nine times and I have never once stood on that mat and I could not, and that is not modesty and it is not a refusal and it is a different thing and I have not got time to explain it here.**"

"That's not a no."

"**That is a not-yes and it is the whole of what I have and I would rather you had it accurately than generously.**"

"**Fair. Who else.**"

And that was how it went, and it took about four minutes, and about nine people said something, and **not one of them had been asked before.**

---

**What came out of it, in about four minutes, in a hall in Low Ward, on a Monday afternoon, was this: that nobody who had ever stood on that mat had ever been asked whether they wanted to be in it, and that every one of them had thought, at some point in eleven weeks, that there was something they had said yes to, and that there was not, and that they could not find it, and that not being able to find it had not worried any of them until about ten to four that afternoon.**

The woman of about fifty said it first and she said it flatly.

"**I have been in this since the eighth of January. I have never been asked. I have assumed about four times that somebody, somewhere, had decided I was in it, and I have never once been able to say who, and I have not wanted to know, and I have been telling myself that not wanting to know was modesty.**"

"**It is not modesty,**" said Frank Dunning. "**It is that there is nothing to be in. And I have known that since the twenty-eighth of February, when I said in this hall that the minute somebody is keeping it there is a fourth rule, and the fourth rule is somebody is keeping it. And I have not got a fourth rule on that sheet and I am not going to put one on, because I have been in this hall about nine times and I have watched about nine people find out what they think about themselves on a doorstep, and I have not got a single thing to say about any of them, and I would not swap it for a form.**"

"So what was the yes to," said somebody at the back.

"**There wasn't one. That is what I have just found out and I found it out at ten to four this afternoon in my own hall with about eleven people in it, and I have been doing this for eleven weeks and nobody asked me, and I am the man with the table.**"

He put his hand flat on the table the way a registrar does and the way Hattie Bramm does, and he said the last thing of the afternoon, and it took about nine seconds.

"**So somebody is going to have to ask us properly. Not you. Not a museum. Not a clipboard. Somebody with a job. And they had better be able to hear a no, because I have just found out that nine people in this room did not know they were in anything, and if we had found that out in six weeks' time instead of eleven, in a room with a landlord's programme in it, one of us would have said yes to something we did not understand and we would have been the reason it went wrong.**"

---

**And at about ten past four the light went off the front window, and at about ten past half past somebody said the number off a door and that was the end of it, and the door was shut at about ten past, because that is rule two.**

Nobody who was there had been asked whether they wanted to be in anything. That had been true for eleven weeks, and on the twenty-second of March a man of sixty-five asked nine people in his own hall, in front of a registrar who had never been to the street before, and got nine answers, and two of them were not yeses, and one of the not-yeses was a man of thirty-two who was not allowed into a building four miles away and who has never once been on a mat in his life.

**And a woman of eighty-one was not in any of it, and there is a date in a clinic file, and it is a weekday in the middle of April, and she is the first person who ever did any of it and nobody has asked her one question in eleven weeks, and there is no form for that either.**
