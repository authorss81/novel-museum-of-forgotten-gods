# Chapter 0077 — What the Practice Cannot Carry

The hall at 88 Sillick Lane has a table in it, a mat on the floor in front of the door with its worn side facing into the house, a plastic wallet on the table with a sheet of A4 in it, and a kettle that is on about twice a day, and at ten past three on the afternoon of Saturday the twentieth of February 2025 there were about nine people in it and a Director of the Museum of Quiet Saints standing in the doorway in a coat with her hands behind her back.

**The practice was six weeks and five days old. It had begun on Monday the fourth of January 2025 at about half past three in the afternoon because a woman of eighty-one stood on the step of number eighty-eight and said a man's name out loud and it went round the street like that. It ran from three o'clock until you could not read a number off a door. There were three rules on the sheet and the third of them was in a different hand from the first two and had been there since the eighth.**

> **1. You knock first. Then you say the name.**
> **2. It runs from three o'clock until you can't read a number off the door.**
> **3. Anybody taking part may stop at any point, and nobody's asked afterwards why.**

**Nobody in this case has ever asked Frank Dunning a question about any of it. He has said so himself, twice, to a registrar and to a Director, and he has said that it is the best three weeks he has had in about nine years, and he is about sixty-five, and he is not frightened and he is not reassured, and there are about nine names on the sheet in four hands and about a dozen lines under them with a date and a time against each, and it is a piece of A4 in a plastic wallet on a table in his hall, and it dies with him.**

He was asked a question on the twentieth of February, in that hall, by a Director who had come down the hill on a bus and paid a pound, and she asked it properly and she said the cost out loud first, and the cost took about ninety seconds and it is on the page because the man she was asking could not have done anything else with it.

"The cost is this and I am going to say it before I ask. **If I ask you for the sheet, and you give it to me, then in about four years there is a piece of paper in a museum in this city with the names of about nine people on it, and one of them is a woman of eighty-one who was taken to a clinic on the thirteenth of January, and one of them is a postman who works Thursdays, and neither of them has been asked, and I would be the person who asked.** I wrote to a woman of fifty-four in December and offered her a pound and a bus and no questions and she came and spent a morning on a rope and a woman of twenty-eight signed a minute on the twenty-seventh of January that this Museum will not hold a record of anything that happens in this hall. **So I have already decided the answer in a room on the fourth floor and I have told the world and you have read about it in a newspaper.**"

"Then you've come to give me something."

"I've come to ask you whether the Museum can be useful to you, which is a different question, and I have about four minutes, and if the answer is no I would rather have it now than in March."

"**No.**"

And then he gave the reason, and nobody had asked him for it.

"Not no to the sheet. **No to all of it.** No to the sheet, no to a copy, no to a list, no to a register, no to a rota, no to anybody coming round here on a Wednesday with a clipboard because I'm perfectly aware that's what a clipboard is for. **No in about four seconds and I've been waiting since December for somebody to ask me so they could tell me I'd said no.**"

"**Then you have it, and I did not need to ask, and that is the correct outcome and it is the outcome this Museum minuted on the twenty-seventh,**" said Anja Ward. "**And I am going to say the thing I have come to say and it is not the same thing and I would like you to know it isn't.** You have about nine people on a sheet on a table. There are about four hundred and twelve tenancies in nine blocks on a programme in this ward and a term end between the seventeenth of February and the twenty-fourth of March, and I am not able to tell you that none of that reaches you, because I do not know which block you are in, and your agent would and I would rather ask you than ask her."

"That block goes in on the first of April," said Frank Dunning. "**I knew in November. There's a woman at number twelve who's been telling everybody since about September and she was right about all of it, and nobody wrote it down, because it's not the sort of thing you write down. My tenancy's the twenty-second of August. Nobody's said it out loud to me and I've said it to about four people and they've all said the right thing, which is *oh*, and then gone to their dinners.**"

"**Your term ends on the twenty-second of August,**" said the Director, "**and nobody is going to renew it, and I am the first person in a position to say so, and I have said so, and I would like it on your sheet and not on mine.**"

"**You can have it on my sheet,**" said Frank Dunning. "**That's what the sheet's for. That's a fact about a house and a date and it's the only kind of thing that's ever gone on it. Write it down. It's the biggest thing anybody's put on there and it isn't even a person.**"

---

**And then, at about ten past four, a registrar of twenty-eight said the other half, and the other half is the whole of the afternoon, and she is not Elias Venn, and he was in the room and said nothing about it for fifty minutes, and Ivo Pell looked at him twice.**

"**You cannot be a register, and you cannot be a rota, and I want to say why in about a minute and it is not the reason anybody is expecting,**" said Mara Iles, "**and it is not because of the sheet and it is not because of privacy. It is rule three.**"

"Rule three."

"**Anybody taking part may stop at any point, and nobody's asked afterwards why.** That is a *permission*. It is the only permission in this entire case. There is not another one. Every other thing anybody in this case has produced in ten weeks is an instruction or a refusal or a determination, and an instruction cannot be abandoned at any point without a form, and **a rota is a duty, and a duty that a person can stop at any point without a form is not a rota, it is a suggestion with a list on it.**"

She put her hand on the table, next to the wallet, and did not touch it.

"So when somebody in about four months says *let us roll this out* — and somebody will, and it will be a good-faith person, and it will probably be me in a bad afternoon — **the reason it cannot be done is on your sheet, in a different hand, and it has been there since the eighth of January, and it is the only thing in this business that nobody has proposed to change and everybody has accepted without examining.** You cannot scale a permission. There is no version of a form with a box in it for *anybody taking part may stop at any point and nobody's asked afterwards why*, and if there were, this would be a programme, and the day it is a programme about nine people on a pavement are about nine people in a programme."

"And the Museum's part," said the Director.

"**The Museum can hold the mat.**" She said it flatly. "That is a real sentence and it is the whole of what this building is going to be able to offer the people on this street, and it is a two-pound coir mat with a manufacturer's mark in a language nobody here reads, and about nine of them have been sold in this city since the fourth of January and nobody's job is to know that, and if we lend them one then it goes on a schedule inside four months and it gets counted on a Friday by somebody whose job it is, and we did that once already in December and it cost us a gallery and about ninety people on a Friday the eighth of January with a pound each.

"**And the Museum cannot hold the knock.** That's the sentence. That's the other half and it is not a failure and it is not modesty, and I would like it written down: **this institution can put a mat on a schedule and it cannot put a knock on a schedule, because a knock is not an object, and there is no accession number for one, and there is no box in this building or in this county or in this country, and the only reason nobody has put one there in four hundred years is that nobody has ever needed to, and about nine people in Low Ward need to.**"

---

**And then a systems technician of twenty-five asked the question that has been in this case since the fourth of December and that nobody has ever asked in a room, and he asked it badly, on purpose, and it took about nine seconds.**

"Where has to be," said Ivo Pell, "**for this to be the same thing.**"

"Say that again."

"**Where does a practice have to happen for it to be the same practice. Because there's a mat down by the wall at about number forty and it's the same three rules and nobody wrote a new sheet, and about nine of us stood on it, and I have been frightened of that since the eighteenth of January. **And it works at number forty because number forty is on Frank's sheet. And it would not work in Ferry Bank and it would not work at the end of Broad Street and it would not work in a school, and the only reason is a piece of A4.**"

"Whoa," said Frank Dunning.

"I'm not saying it's a problem. I'm saying nobody in this room knows the answer and everybody in this room has an opinion, and about four of us have got the same wrong one." He put the mug down on the sill. "**A mat has got no date of permanence because you can buy another one for two pounds. A tenancy has got a date of permanence and it's got a landlord and a term and a document. And a registry in Northglass has got one box for a place and it's for a burial and it wants a date. So if somebody in March asks me what a practice needs, I have got two answers and they're not compatible and I am not going to be the one who picks.**"

"You will not be the one who picks," said Mara Iles, "**because it is not a systems question and it has never been a systems question, and every person in this room has been avoiding it for ten weeks for different respectable reasons.**"

"Which is?"

"**Because the object is two pounds and the place is a tenancy, and the practice is standing on the object, and if it turns out to be the object then this hall is furniture and there are nine mats in this city, and if it turns out to be the place then this is a street with a landlord and a cycle date and a document, and a thing with a document in it is a programme.**" She squared the sheet on the wallet without touching it. "**And I am not going to say which, in this hall, in front of the man who keeps it, in February, because I said in a corridor on the twenty-second of January that this institution has a category for the first and no category for the second and I have not found a reason to take it back.**"

The hall was quiet for a moment, and about four of the nine people on the sheet were standing at the door with their coats on, waiting to see whether it was finished.

---

**And then Frank Dunning said something to Elias Venn that nobody else in the hall heard, and it is on the page because Elias Venn has refused three things in ten weeks and this is the fourth and it is the only one that was not about evidence.**

"You've not got one, have you."

"No."

"**A word. To stop yourself with.**" He did not make it a joke and he did not make it kind. "**I've watched you stand in my hall for about fifty minutes and you haven't said one word, and I know a man who doesn't say anything, because he's worked out that the room goes better.**"

"No," said Elias Venn. "**I haven't got one and I am not going to have one.**"

"**Right.**"

"It belongs to the street."

"It does not belong to the street," said Frank Dunning, "**and don't get that wrong, and don't tell anybody in a museum that. The street doesn't own it. **I** own it. It is on my table and I wrote it out and it is my rule and if I stop running it there is no practice, and nobody is going to take it off me and nobody is going to keep it for me and that is the correct arrangement, and I am sixty-five and I am telling you that a rule that belongs to a street is a rule that belongs to a council, and I have seen what a council does with a room.** So you can say what you like in a corridor. It is mine."

"I understand."

"**Then that's the only thing I have got to say and I have said it and I am going back to the kettle,**" said Frank Dunning.

---

**And the last thing that happened in the hall at 88 Sillick Lane on the afternoon of Saturday the twentieth of February 2025 was that a registrar put a date on the sheet, in ink, at the top, in a hand of her own, and asked the man whose sheet it was whether she might.**

Mara Iles wrote it out and turned it round.

> **TO BE READ OUT, AT THE DOOR, IN THE HALL, ON ANY DAY AFTER 21 MARCH 2025:**
> **The Museum of Quiet Saints holds no record of this. It will not be given one. A court may ask whether we saw it. What we saw, if we are asked, is a person standing on a mat and saying a name aloud at a door. Nobody will be named. Nothing will be described. The Museum will say that and no more.**

"That's all it is."

"That's all it is and it is four lines and it has got no name in it and it is the first thing I have ever written in my life that is a promise to say nothing."

Frank Dunning read it twice.

"**The twenty-second of March,**" he said. "**Not the twenty-first. On the twenty-second of March my tenancy's still got six weeks to run and I'm still the man with the table.**"

"**Then the twenty-second of March,**" said Mara Iles, "**and I'll come up the hill and I'll read it out on the step, and Mr Dunning will hand it out if he thinks it should be handed out, and I am not going to take it off him.**"
