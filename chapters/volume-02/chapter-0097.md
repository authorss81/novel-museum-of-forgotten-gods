# Chapter 0097 — From The Bottom Of A Card

The schedule of collections of the Museum of Quiet Saints is the one document in that building which a member of the public may ask for, and anybody may ask for it, and no charge is made, and the request is written on a form at the front desk, and the form has a box for the requester's name and address and a box for the date and a box for what the requester wants to see, and the form is filled in at the counter in a hand and given to a person behind the counter.

**It was filled in on Wednesday the nineteenth of May 2025 by a woman who is not in this case and has not been named anywhere in it, and she gave her reason in the last box, and her reason is that she is writing a history of the ward and would like to know what the ward's own museum thought was worth recording, and that is a better reason than any professional in this case has given for asking this building for anything in twenty-three weeks.**

---

## And a registrar of twenty-eight could not produce it, and the reason she could not produce it took about nine seconds, and nobody in that building argued with her about it

"**I have to hand this to a Director,**" said Mara Iles, at about half past ten on the Wednesday, "**and I am not going to pretend to anybody that what I am going to ask her for is a favour.**"

"**What are you asking for.**"

"**I am asking her to let me put a note in the schedule at the entry for plinth thirty-four, and the note is going to say that the maker's line on the public card for that object was struck through and dated on the twenty-fifth of February 2025, that the name struck through is the name of no body, and that the correction is beneath a requester's panel which this Museum has no standing to remove. And I am going to have to say to the Director that a note in that schedule is a thing I cannot take back, because that schedule is the one document here that a stranger can ask for, and every word I put in it is a word I am putting into a stranger's hands on purpose, and I have spent five years learning that this is the only door in this building that does not lock.**"

"**Say the rest of it.**"

"**The rest of it is that the correction is a document that nobody in the world can read. It is under a panel. It has been under a panel since the twenty-seventh of November, which is a hundred and seventy-three days ago, and the panel is the requester's and the requester was asked about it in a public room on the twenty-first of January and declined in a public room on the same afternoon, and there is no standing anywhere in this building or in this county to take it off, and nobody is going to.**" She put the form down on the table. "**And I am the person who will have to write it down, and I have said that sentence out loud three times in two months and I am about to say it a fourth time and it is the reason I am nervous.**"

---

## And the Director read it twice, and said no to about a third of it, and gave the rest, and the third of it she said no to was the part about the body that does not exist

Anja Ward read the note twice at about half past eleven on the Wednesday and did not say anything for about nine seconds, which she does not do.

"**The word *untraceable* goes.**"

"**Why.**"

"**Because I did not look, and neither did you, and neither did anybody, and a note in a public document that says a name is untraceable is a statement about a search that was not made. We looked for a body. We did not look for a person who typed it.**"

"**Then what goes in its place.**"

"**That the name is the name of no body known to this Museum.**" She turned the paper round. "**And a second thing goes. *Removed at the request of a member of the public.* It goes because it is not true. It was taped on by her, and it stays because she asked, and those are different and one of them is a favour and the other is a refusal and I will not have a favour in a public document wearing a refusal's clothes.**"

"**And the third thing.**"

"**The third thing stays and I am going to say why and then I am not going to say anything else about it.**" She squared it. "**The note is to record that the public card for that object carries a name that is the name of no body, and that the card has carried it since the object was accessioned, and that this Museum has been aware of it since the thirtieth of December 2024 and did nothing about it for seven weeks. That is in. It is ugly and it is correct and if I leave it out then this note is a public document that is tidier than the truth, and I have sat in a boardroom for nineteen years watching a building get into trouble by being tidier than the truth.**"

"**All right.**"

"**And there is a fourth thing I am not putting in and I am telling you that I am not putting it in, and it is that in about four years somebody will read that note and assume the Museum corrected the card because somebody made a fuss. It is not in because the requester of the panel must not become part of this Museum's record of itself, and she has refused this building three times and the fourth thing that happened in this case was that this building did not ask her a fourth time, and I am not going to undo that on a piece of paper that a stranger can ask for.**"

---

## And then on Friday the twenty-eighth of May at about one o'clock in the afternoon a registrar of twenty-eight read the bottom of a card out loud, in a public room, in front of eleven people, and did not touch the panel

The Hall of Former Faiths was open from ten until five and the doors were propped and about a hundred and twenty people went through the front door between ten and four and about forty of them went into the Hall. **The mat was on the floor on its plastic sheet inside the cord line with a card on a wire stand that said *no name available* in a finisher's own hand, and there was a bell in a case at the east end on plinth thirty-four, with a staple repair in its shoulder and the fracture open under the staple, and on the front of that case there was a sheet of A4 at a slight angle with tape on it that has gone the colour of weak tea.**

The woman who had filled in the form on the Wednesday was in the room. She had a notebook and she is not in this case and she was not asked to come and she came.

Mara Iles stood at the east end, beside the case, and she read the entry out of the schedule of collections, from the top, and she read it at the volume she uses in a hearing, and she took about a minute and a half, and the entry is a description of an object and it is correct in every line of it.

Then she read the note.

> **PLINTH 34. Note added 28 May 2025.**
> **The maker's line on the public card for this object has been struck through and dated 25 February 2025. The name that was struck through is *Municipal Works*, which is the name of no body known to this Museum, and it is the only name that has ever been on that line, and it has been on that card since the object was accessioned.**
> **This Museum has been aware of the fault since 30 December 2024.**
> **The corrected card is beneath a panel affixed to the case. The panel is the requester's. This Museum has no standing to remove a requester's panel and has not attempted to. No photograph of the correction has been made and none will be.**
> **A finding card was cut on 25 February 2025 and is held at the front desk. It names no person.**

And then she read the last line of the note, which was the one she had fought for and won and was still not sure about.

> **The schedule of collections also contains at its second entry an item that is not an object. That entry is dated 4 February 2025 and is not withdrawn.**

Nobody said anything. The woman with the notebook wrote for about nine seconds and then looked up and said, **"Is that the whole of it?"**

"**That is the whole of it,**" said the registrar. "**And I have read it from the bottom of a card, in order, and the bottom of a card is the fourth line, and there are four lines on that card and three of them are about an object and the fourth one is a name that is the name of no body, and it has been on a public card in this room for about a hundred and twenty years, and it is the fourth line, and that is the entire mechanism. It is not that the lie is old. It is that the lie is at the bottom.**"

---

## And nobody touched the panel, and nobody asked the requester about anything, and the panel is a hundred and eighty-two days old

That is the fact of the afternoon and it is the whole of it.

**The panel was not lifted. The tape was not peeled. The card underneath was not read by anybody, including by the registrar, including by the Director, and the two of them were standing six inches from it, and the correction is still under it, and it is the most carefully kept document in this building.**

The woman with the notebook asked whether she could photograph the panel, and the Registrar said that a card on a public exhibit in a public gallery is not collections material for the purposes of the building's own rule, and that photographs of the room were fine, and that the person in the case underneath the panel was not to be photographed because no photograph of that correction has ever been made and none is going to be.

"Can I see the finding card?"

"**It is on the front desk. It has been on the front desk since the twenty-fifth of February and it is in a plastic sleeve and it names nobody, because a name on a card in a public room is a hook, and this building has said that sentence out loud in a public room and meant it, and the woman who cut it has cut about four hundred cards in this room.**"

---

## And then in a corridor at about twenty past two a woman of fifty-four said the sentence, in eleven words, and nobody claimed it

She had not been asked to come and had not been asked anything and was not asked anything.

"**You put a false name in a public document in front of a stranger.**"

"**We did.**"

"**And a stranger can ask for that document again in about four years and get it, and it will say the same thing, and it will not say a name, because there is no name in it.**" The woman of fifty-four was looking at the floor of the corridor. "**It is that a hole in a card lets whoever comes next fill it in.**"

"**That is a label-room finisher's and not mine,**" said Mara Iles, **and then stopped, and then said:** "**No. I am not going to do that. That sentence was said in a corridor in this city in 1994 and by a registration officer on a doorstep in February and by that woman on the ninth of April, and I have said it in my own office to a colleague twice, and I am not going to claim it and I am not going to give it away. For a house that no system has got a name for.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**You have put a hole in a card with a date in it,**" she said. "**That is the whole of the difference and it is the only thing anybody in this case has done about a false name in twenty-three weeks. A hole with a date in it is a hole somebody made on the twenty-fifth of February 2025. A hole with no date in it is a hole anybody can walk into, and you have put the date on it, and the date is the only thing you have that I did not already say in a corridor in 1994.**"

## And the hand that wrote the fourth line is not named, and that is a decision, and it was made on the Wednesday afternoon in about four seconds

"**For the last time,**" said the registrar, to the woman with the notebook, who had asked. "**Is the name of the body, if there is one, on a piece of paper anywhere?**"

"**There is a hand on the 1902 index card. There is a day book for the week of the casting and there are two entries in it for the label room. Neither has been read by anybody since February because there has been no reason to read them, and if you read them this afternoon you will either find a name or you will not, and if you find a name it is a name of somebody who was nineteen in 1902 and is dead, and it will be in a public document in four years, and a person will be able to point at it and say that the museum decided that a man was a liar.**"

"**So it is not being read.**"

"**It is not being read today, and it has not been read, and if it is read it will be read by a registrar on a stated day with a minute, and not in a public room on a Friday, and the finding will be recorded either way including the null.**" She closed the folder. "**And I have not been asked to read it and I have not read it and I would like that written down somewhere that is not a public document, and it is now written down here, in a corridor, and that is all.**"
