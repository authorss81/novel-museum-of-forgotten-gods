# Chapter 0100 — A Register Somebody Has To Keep

The room was let on Monday the thirty-first of May 2025 from half past three until six, with five pound notes in an envelope and no name on the booking, and the shopkeeper on the corner has now let it five times and has asked nothing five times and told nobody what it is for.

**There were about eleven people in it and there was a piece of A4 on the wall that had been on that wall since the twenty-fourth of April, and a table with a cloth bag on it that had not been in the room before, and a woman of eighty-one who had brought the bag.**

---

## And the first thing that happened is that the boundary was read out, all five lines, at the same volume, and the fifth line is still the truest sentence anybody in this case has written

"**You know the rules,**" said the woman of about fifty from the sheet, and she read it, because she has read it every week since the fifteenth of May and she is the person who has always taken it down.

> **This is what we do.**
> **1. You knock first. Then you say the name.**
> **2. It runs from three o'clock until you can't read a number off a door.**
> **3. Anybody taking part may stop at any point, and nobody's asked afterwards why.**
> **And it goes on until the first of October. On the first of October we stop, and then we will decide whether to start again. Nobody has to promise us anything and we are not asking anybody to.**
> **Nobody signs this. Nobody keeps it. It is on the wall.**

Three names are on a copy in a hall in Sillick Lane. **Nobody is keeping that piece of paper, and nobody has ever been asked to, and the fifth line has been completely true for every day of the twenty-five weeks in which it has existed, and nobody in that room proposed to change it.**

---

## And then the woman of eighty-one opened the cloth bag, and there is a book in it, and it is a hardback notebook of about a hundred and fifty ruled pages, and it cost a pound and ten, and it is not complete

She put it on the table and she did not open it and she let a man of fifty-two open it, and he turned it round so that everybody could see the inside of the front cover, and on the inside of the front cover in a hand that had been taught in a school in about 1938 and had got worse since, and in ballpoint, and dated:

> **KEPT BY V. PILLING, 6 Sillick Lane.**
> **FROM 31 MAY 2025 UNTIL THE FIRST OF OCTOBER 2025.**

"A name against a date," said the man of fifty-two, **and did not say anything else about it, and sat down.**

---

## And then she said why, and it took about a minute, and she had been asked by nobody and had told nobody

"**I am eighty-one and I am not a registrar and I am not a records officer and I am not anything. I have got a clinic sheet with four lines on it and one of the lines is a length of time. So I am going to say the reason and then I am going to read you the first page and you will decide for yourselves whether it is a stupid reason.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**In April a woman of yours asked me to write a man's name on a card about a house and I said no twice, and the reason I gave was that in about four years somebody will come to that door with a clipboard and a woman in that flat will have to say no about a man she has never heard of.**" She put one hand flat on the book. "**And I have thought about that every day for six weeks and I have worked out what I actually meant, and what I meant is that I am the only person in this room who knows exactly what a piece of paper does to a person after it has been made. That is the whole of my qualification. It is not a skill and it is not a job and it is not a virtue. I was the only one of you who had been on the other end of a record, and I did not like it, and I have kept a copy of a pay sheet in a hand of my own in a tin since November 1974 because of it.**"

"**And why not somebody else.**"

"**Because everybody else in this room wants it to be a good thing.**" She said it without any weight. "**The man of fifty-two has been waiting since the twenty-eighth of February for somebody to make him say a fourth rule and he has still not been asked. The woman with the rota wants the rota to be something. The man with the notebook wants a document. And I do not want a good thing. I want somebody who has been on the wrong end of a piece of paper to be the one holding it, and there is one of those and she is eighty-one and she is not going to be here in four years, and I have said that out loud and I would like it written down.**"

---

## And the first page is four lines of what it is, and the fourth line is the whole of the argument of this volume, and the second is the reason it is allowed to have gaps

> **This is a record of what was done on a Saturday.**
> **It is not a list of people and it has no names in it except mine on this page and the name of the street in the headings.**
> **Anybody may stop at any point and nobody is asked afterwards why, so where somebody stopped, there is a gap, and nobody is to fill a gap, ever, and a gap is not a fault and it is not going to be counted.**
> **If you are reading this and you are not me, then you are the second person, and that is the only reason it exists.**

The man of thirty-two read the fourth line out loud twice and nobody improved it and nobody asked who the second person was.

"**That is the whole of it,**" said Vera Pilling. "**Four lines, and I had them drafted in my head for a fortnight and they are not clever and they are not mine either. **A rota cannot hold a permission, because a rota has to say why people stopped and nobody is allowed to ask, and a book that cannot hold the permission is not much of a book, and it is the only kind that is correct.** That is a registrar's and I heard her say it in a room in March and I have written it as mine and it is not mine, and I am eighty-one and I am not going to worry about that now.**"

---

## And she said her terms out loud, and there were four of them, and one of them is a refusal, and nobody argued with any of them

"**One. It is mine. It lives at number six in a drawer and it does not lock and I have decided that on purpose. Two. Nobody reads it without me and I am not going to be asked to say yes and I am not going to be asked to say no and I will say the same word to both. Three. The day I stop, it stops, and it does not go to the Museum and it does not go to a council and it does not go to a solicitor and it does not go to a man of fifty-two, and I am not appointing anybody, because appointing anybody is how the fourth of January turned into the twenty-eighth of February.**"

"**And four,**" said Frank Dunning.

"**And four is the refusal and it is the only one that matters and I have saved it. **There are no names in it.** Not the nine. Not the hundred and forty. Not a person's name at a door, and not the year that was wrong, and not whatever is in a file four miles away with a woman's name on it. It is dates, and times, and door numbers, and how many stopped. And I have been told that is useless and I have been told it by a man of thirty-two who is right, and it is not useless, it is the only version of this that does not put a name in a drawer in a flat in this city, and I would rather have a small thing that is correct than a big thing that is a list, and I have been on a schedule of interim payments since December with about fifty lines on it and every one of them is a document and not one of them is me.**"

"**Then what is it for.**"

"**It is for the fourth of January,**" she said. "**If anybody in this city ever wants to know whether a thing happened on a street, there is one piece of paper that says so, in a drawer, with a date on it, and it is not a list of anybody, and it will be there on the first of October, which is the day the boundary stops, and that is the same day on purpose because after the first of October there is nothing to keep and everybody knows it.**"

---

## And the first entry is on the page, and it is the twenty-ninth of May, and it is four lines long

> **Sat 29 May. Three o'clock to about ten past seven. Fourteen doors. Nine households.**
> **Nobody stopped and then somebody did, so: two stopped. Gaps left.**
> **The boundary went up on the noticeboard at the library and came off at twenty to six and left a clean rectangle.**
> **Weather: no.**

"**It is not complete,**" said Vera Pilling, "**and I am going to say why on the page and then I am going to stop talking about it for the rest of my life.**"

On the third page, in the same ballpoint:

> **This is not complete and it cannot be made complete.**
> **It begins on the thirty-first of May 2025 and there is nothing in this book about December 2024, or November, or any of the about nine households that were in the flats on the east side of that street in November and are not now, and there is nothing in this book about them and there is not going to be anything in this book about them, because you cannot take a list of a thing nobody kept, and because a book that began on the thirty-first of May is not a record of December and anybody who says it is wants it to be one.**
> **And the gaps are the other half of it. There are about nine of them in three weeks and there will be about four hundred in four months, and one of them is a year that a woman said out loud and a man of seventy-four says is wrong, and it is not filled in, and it is not to be filled in, and a gap is a person who stopped and it is the only honest shape a stopped person has.**

---

## And then somebody asked whether this was a religion, and the woman of eighty-one answered it, and it is the last thing she says

"**Is this a religion.**"

"**No,**" said Vera Pilling. "**There is nobody in this room who has to believe anything, and nothing has come back, and nobody has been given a house, and the flats on the east side of that street are still empty and there are still about nine of them and there is still nothing in this city that says what they were except a piece of A4 in nine hands with a wrong year on it. I am keeping a book. I have not been given a miracle and I am not owed one and I would not know what to do with one, and I have been on this street since 1949 and I have seen a great deal come back into this ward and none of it stayed.**"

"**Then what is it.**"

"**It is a book,**" she said, **and shut it, and put the elastic band round it, and it went into the cloth bag, and the bag went on the table under the piece of paper on the wall.**

---

## And four miles away, in a public room on the first floor, a bell said nothing, and the whole of the institutional position is a list of things that did not change

**It has not sounded since four minutes past eleven on Sunday the thirteenth of December 2024, which is a hundred and sixty-nine days ago.** The Hall of Former Faiths was open from ten until five and about four hundred people went through the front door. Nobody in that building has a duty to listen for it and nobody in that building heard it, and a note in that building has never been a record, because the day book at the front desk is the record of issue and return and it has one line per device and a printed head that says so, and nobody has ever written a sound in it and nobody ever will.

**The fracture in the bell's shoulder is open under the staple. The card on the front of its case is under a panel of A4 and tired tape that has been there since Friday the twenty-seventh of November 2024, which is a hundred and eighty-five days old, and the panel is the requester's, and the requester has not been asked anything today and has not been asked anything since the twenty-seventh of April, and nobody in that building has the standing to lift it and nobody is going to.**

**Annexe D is permanent. Annexe E is in force and is stricter than condition four. The Director's condition two failed on the twenty-third of December and on the twenty-ninth and has not been amended and is not going to be.** The Hall is open and the rope line is where it has been since a clerk of works re-hung it in November 1935, and the rope is not on the schedule, and it is furniture, and nine carried patterns stand on open plinths inside it, and a box of minutes on the ground floor at the back of that building says the word CIRCULATION about a route from one door to another door, and a registrar stood in that store on a Thursday in February and refused to claim the rest of it, and she was right.

**And the fourth line of the card under the panel says *Municipal Works*, which is the name of no body, and which went into a public document on the twenty-eighth of May at about one o'clock in the afternoon, in a schedule of collections that any member of the public may ask for, and nobody has been named, and the hole has a date in it, and nothing in this city has moved in either direction tonight.**

---

## And at ten to six the room was given back, and the boundary came off the wall, and the woman of eighty-one went down about fourteen stairs with a cloth bag

Frank Dunning said the thing he has said three times, in the same words, at the door, and it is the last thing said in that room for the last time:

"**That is the correct arrangement and it is the worst arrangement in the world, in that order.**"

The boundary came off the wall in one piece. The tape came off the plaster and left a clean rectangle, and the woman of about fifty from the sheet said it would come off and it did, and nobody looked at the rectangle for longer than it took.

**The pound came out of the envelope and there was six pounds in it and the shopkeeper took it and said the room had been let for one day and shut the door.**

The bag went down the stairs after her. And this is the whole of what there is: a room let by the hour, a wall with a clean rectangle on it, a boundary that nobody signed and nobody keeps, and a hardback notebook of about a hundred and fifty ruled pages in a cloth bag going down about fourteen stairs to a flat at number six, where it goes in a drawer that does not lock, with a name and a date on the inside of the front cover, and about nine gaps in it, and about four hundred more to come, and nobody is going to fill them in.
