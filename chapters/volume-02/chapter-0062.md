# Chapter 0062 — The Comparison

On Monday the eighteenth of January 2025 there were about a hundred and forty people through the front door of the Museum of Quiet Saints between ten and four, and about sixty of them went into the Hall of Former Faiths, and the mat was still on the floor on its plastic sheet inside the rope, and nobody had touched it for nineteen days, and the card beside it still said *no name available* in a finisher's hand, and **nobody had asked about it, and that had now been true for a week in a room that a hundred and forty people a day walk through.**

That is not a mystery and it is not a failure. It is a Monday, and about sixty people went into that room because it has nine carried patterns on open plinths in it and a bell under a permanent classification, and a card on a wire stand at knee height is not a thing anybody stops at.

**The thing that came out of the clause seven query was the correct and worst possible result, and it arrived in an envelope with the Museum's own address on it, hand-delivered by a district courier at about twenty past nine on the morning of Friday the fifteenth, and it is on the page because it is four lines long and it is the first document anybody in this case has ever obtained on purpose.**

> **E. TOFT, records officer.**

**That is a name this building has had since the twenty-fourth of December, in a plastic wallet, on a card that came back marked undeliverable.** Frank Dunning has a name and an address on a piece of paper in a rented room in Low Ward because a woman who has been at that counter for nineteen years took it out of a box and gave it back across a counter for no reason at all, and it cost her nothing and it cost him nothing and it took about ninety seconds, and it was the single luckiest thing that has happened in this case.

And it took four minutes to produce and it was not posted, because the original of a clause 7 answer stays in the file with the query, and the copy went by district courier on the Friday, and the reason for that order of events is that she said it on the telephone on the Wednesday and it is the correct order of events.

And the query cost four minutes, and it told them nothing they did not have, and **both of those facts are correct and neither of them is a disappointment, and everybody in the systems office on the Monday morning understood it before anybody had finished sitting down, and then nobody said anything for a while.**

"Aye," said Ivo Pell. "Well. That's the answer."

"It's the answer to a question nobody needed the answer to," said Mara Iles. She had her reading glasses off, which she did on the eighth of December and on the eleventh of January and on no other days that anybody in that room could remember. "**You have spent a clause on a name that was in a plastic wallet. That is not nothing. That is the first time in this entire business that a document has come back with a person's name on it because a person asked for it. But it is not evidence of anything, and if it goes in a file it goes in a file as a null, and I want it minuted as a null, and I want the minute to say what it cost.**"

"Four hundred and ninety minutes of somebody's time, and it closes a clause that's been open since 2011."

"**Write that in those words. I am not being unkind. I am being the person who has to read this in March when a man in a wig asks why this Museum spent a fortnight of a young man's working life finding out a thing it already had in a plastic wallet.**"

**And then Ivo Pell said the thing that turned the Monday over, and he said it to a screen, and he said it the way a man of twenty-five says a thing he has been carrying round a building for nine days.**

"**That's one line.**"

Nobody said anything.

"The clause says *any single line.* One. Not one at a time, not one per form, not as many as you like — one. And I've used it, and it's gone, and it's the twentieth of January, and the next one I could write would be about the second of December and it would be a different date and it would be a different line and it would take the same four minutes and it would be a piece of paper with a name on it, and I could do eight more of them, one a day, for eight days, and at the end of it I would have eight names and eight dates and **I would still not have the file.**"

"The file is the point," said Mara Iles.

"**The file is the point and the clause does not give anybody the file. The clause gives one line. And one line tells you what a house was called on a day. It does not tell you what this Museum was told, and it does not tell me what I am looking at, and it does not tell me when.**"

He turned the screen round. The block was on it. About nine hundred lines of Museum systems and the threshold group at the bottom of it with four hundred and twelve rows, and the row for 88 Sillick Lane with nothing in the occupancy field, exactly as it had been on the twenty-first of December, because it is overwritten every morning at about a quarter past four and whatever was in it yesterday is not anywhere in the world.

"That block has no history," said Ivo Pell. "**It is not a record. It is a wire. And I have known that since the twenty-first of December and I have been sitting on it for a fortnight telling myself I had a clause.**"

**And the thing nobody in the systems office had thought of, which took Mara Iles about nine minutes at a desk with a pen and a printed terms of reference in front of her, was not a law and was not a form and was in a paper that had been on the table since October.**

"I can direct an inquiry to obtain a document, and then the Museum has to go and get it, and if the Museum cannot get it that is a fact about the Museum, and either way it is a document and it is in a file with a chair's name on it." "Then what is it."

"It is a subscriber writing to another subscriber's supplier under an agreement both of them are already in, asking for the file as issued, on a stated date range, for a stated purpose, endorsed by an inquiry that has six items in its terms of reference and has so far determined one of them." She picked the pen up again. "**It is the least dramatic instrument in this city and it is the only one that has ever worked and it took three days to find.**"

**The letter was written on the Monday morning, signed on the Monday at about eleven, and put with a courier at about two, and the Director said the cost out loud before she signed it, in a room, and had it minuted in that order, because that is what she does and she was not going to stop doing it in January.**

"The cost is this and there is only one item of it. **If we ask for the file as issued, in writing, on a letterhead, with a purpose and a date range, then a records officer four miles away is going to write down that this Museum cannot read its own history, and that sentence is going to be in a file in her building, and she may show it to us, and we cannot stop her, and it is not a hostile act and she will not intend it as one.**"

"And the second cost," said the sub-group member of sixty-four.

"There isn't a second cost. There is one cost and it is mine and it is about my signature and not about the Museum." She did not put her hands behind her back. "I have spent nineteen years not having a sentence about me in this building and in twenty-four hours I have acquired one, and I am signing it, and I would like it recorded that I said the cost before I signed and that I knew what it was on Friday."
