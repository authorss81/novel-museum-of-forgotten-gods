# Chapter 0071 — The Slot

The rota was a sheet of A3 in a plastic sleeve on a wall in a corridor in Northglass, and it was printed, and it had been printed in November, and there were four names on it in a column and eleven dates down the side in another, and one of the dates had a time in a box beside it, and the box said **11:00**, and the date was the sixth of February 2025, which was a Saturday.

Nobody had ever stood in front of it. That is not true — about four hundred people a week walk a corridor in that building and most of them look at a wall they have looked at before — but nobody in this case had ever stood in front of it, and the man who put a name to it on the nineteenth of October 2024 had done it on a printed form in a different building and had been given the date by a woman with a pencil on the back of her own copy who would not send it.

**Elias Venn read the rota at about twenty past ten on the morning of Saturday the sixth of February 2025, on notice, in company, not before eight, and it took him about eleven seconds, and then he stood in the corridor for about four minutes doing arithmetic he had no business doing, which is what he does.**

There is no emergency provision. That is not a refusal and it is not a gap. It is a line on the same sheet, under the dates, in a smaller face, and it reads: *Out-of-cycle inspection: none. A matter requiring inspection before the next listed date will be listed at the next listed date.* **And it has said that since about 2011, and a woman of about forty-five had written it out on a form to a Director on the twenty-third of December in one line, and nobody had ever asked her to.**

He was in the building because a piece of paper said he would be. Not a Museum notice, not a witness card with the word WITNESS in a box, not an item-six direction with a chair's initials on it. **Form A/3, lodged by registered post on the twelfth of December 2024, eleven stamps on the acknowledgements, requester: E. VENN.** It is the only form in that office with a box on it for a period, and the period is how long a determination is meant to last once there is one. **A/3 asks the Registry to determine whether a stated thing falls inside the schedule and, if it does, to hold that determination for a stated period.** He asked for twelve months. He wrote the thing in himself, on the second page, in about four words, and it was a description and not a name, and there is a line at the foot of page two that asks what a thing is, and he read that line twice and then filled it in, and that line is the reason the acknowledgements came back with eleven stamps on them instead of two. He is the only person in this entire case who has ever walked into a room in this city because his own name was on a piece of paper asking for it, and he has been in a rented room in Low Ward since the eighth of December 2024, and the difference between those two facts is about nine weeks of a case that has never once managed to put him in a room.

He noticed it on the stair, at the second landing, and then he made himself stop noticing it, because he is thirty-two and he counts things when he is frightened and he was embarrassed by the counting by the third floor.

---

**The office was at the end of the corridor and it had a door that was open and a chair in it that had been a chair in about 1961, and a table with a telephone on it, and a tray, and a mug.**

She was about forty-five and she had a cardigan on over something with a collar and there were about four trays of paper on the table with the bottoms of them showing, and she was reading, and she did not stand up, and she said:

"You're the A/3."

"Yes."

"Sit down. The other one is behind you."

Mara Iles had come in behind him with a folder and had stopped in the doorway.

"S. Tully," said the woman at the table. "I know who you are, you're the letter of the twenty-second of January, and you're on the page of it, so I've read it twice, and I want to say the first thing before anybody says anything else, which is that I wrote back and it went on the Friday afternoon and I have checked twice that it left, because I've been in this office nineteen years and I have never had to check a letter of my own twice." She put the page down. "You're late."

"We know," said Ivo Pell. He had a folder and a mug of his own. "That's our fault and not the post's. We're the systems contact on the subscriber and the subscriber has to be identified before anything about a file is said in this room, and we couldn't get here for ten because of a bus, and I've said so on the form."

"You've said it on the form."

"Box eleven."

"There's no box eleven."

"**There's a box eleven,**" said Ivo Pell, "**and you have read about four thousand forms and I have read about four, and it is the only instrument anybody in this business has ever been allowed to use twice.**"

That was the whole of the first four minutes and nobody in the room argued with any of it. It is the first time in nine weeks that three people from the Museum of Quiet Saints have been in a room with the fourth person and the fourth person has been right about all of it in the first two minutes.

---

She read her own sentence back at them, out loud, from the acknowledgment of the thirtieth of December. She did it deliberately, she had clearly practised it, and nobody had asked her to.

"Thirty December. Ref QA/CR/1426. *This schedule has no classification for activity observed outside a museum context. Your proposal cannot be listed against a classification. It is not a Schedule 12 proposal and it is not a Schedule 9 proposal.*" She turned the page round and put it face down on the table. "**And then: *No inspection slot is available before 6 February 2025. There is no emergency provision. I am not sorry.***"

Nobody said anything.

"**I've had that read back to me twice since December**," she said, "**and both times it was by somebody in your building who was pleased with themselves about it, and I would like it recorded this morning that I wrote it, I stand by it, and I have never had anybody in a room say the words *I am not sorry* back to me with the punctuation until this morning.**" She looked at Mara Iles. "You did that in a committee room, didn't you. Somebody told me. I don't know who and I don't want to know."

"I did it in a committee room on the fourth of December," said Mara Iles, "and I was quoting you and I got the punctuation right because it was on the page."

"Then we're both all right. Move on."

---

**Here is what came back on the Saturday, and it came back on paper, twice, and both pieces of paper have this date on them, and neither of them was arranged by anybody.**

The first was four lines and it answered a letter of the twenty-second of January, and it was addressed to the Senior Conservator and Accession Registrar of a public museum on a hill and it is the shortest correct document anybody outside that building has produced this winter.

> **QUIET ACCORD REGISTRY. To: M. ILES, Senior Conservator and Accession Registrar, Morrowgate Museum of Quiet Saints. Re: your letter of 22 January 2025, ref QA/CR/1426.**
>
> **1. The answer to your question is the first of the two you offered. This schedule has no box for it.**
>
> **2. I will set out why, at more length than the question requires, because you asked a real question and this office has had very few of them and I would like the reason to travel with the answer.**
>
> **3. The schedule I work to describes things. A proposal under it must name the thing. Schedule 12 carried object; Schedule 9; a fixture; a group. There is no category in it for an activity, and there is no category in it for an activity that happens in a doorway rather than in a room, and there is no category in it for an object that has no maker, no date of manufacture, no place of manufacture, no collection line, no accession number and no series, and which is not accessioned, and which is on a plastic sheet in a public gallery on a floor, in front of a cord line.**
>
> **4. There is one category in this schedule for a place, and it is `PLACE OF INTERMENT`, it requires a date of permanence, and I am not going to use it on a door mat.**
>
> **5. So: there is no object in your file. You have been asking this office to classify something, and the reason it cannot be classified is not that it is a strange object. It is that it is not an object, and a description of it is not a classification of it, and a card in a museum is a description.**
>
> **S. TULLY, assessor. 6 Feb 2025. Countersigned: A. ROTH, assessor.**

**And the second thing is the answer to a form, and it is four lines longer than that, and it is the answer to a form that was registered and posted on the twelfth of December with eleven stamps on the acknowledgements and a period of twelve months in the box for a period, and it was unanswered for nine weeks, and it went out of time at nine o'clock that morning.**

> **QUIET ACCORD REGISTRY. To: the requester, Form A/3, lodged 12 Dec 2024, registered, eleven acknowledgements, period sought: twelve months.**
>
> **1. This reply went out of time at 09:00 on 6 February 2025. It is issued late. There is no penalty and you could not claim one and I have not looked for one.**
>
> **2. The period sought on the form runs from the date of a determination. There is no determination in this matter, and there has never been one, and there cannot be one while there is no box, per the above.**
>
> **3. The request is therefore not refused, and not granted, and not capable of being answered. It has not begun. The twenty-eight days have not started. The form is not late; the thing it asks about has not happened.**
>
> **4. The only thing on that form that can be started is the listing of the question. I am listing it. Determination days are held and are public. The next one is Saturday the sixth of March 2025 and the assessor is the assessor for the district, which is me, and a listed question is decided on a stated day with a stated assessor in the room, and I would like it minuted in your file that this office has never listed a question off a form that had never been a determination, and that I have checked the schedule twice and that there is nothing in it that stops me, and that I have done it because you asked me a question on the twenty-second of January that was not a classification question and I would like there to be one thing in this file that is not a classification question.**

That is the whole of it. Two answers on one morning, in one room, in Northglass, at about twenty past eleven on Saturday the sixth of February 2025, and neither of them is the answer anybody in this case wanted, and both of them are answers, and the second one has a date on the end of it, and the date is the sixth of March, and the date is not a deadline.

---

At about twenty-five to twelve, a woman of about forty-five turned round a form on a desk and read a name off a screen. It was the only time in nine weeks that a document in this case produced a person's name because of Elias Venn, and it took four seconds.

"E. Venn," she said. "Requester. Let me get the identification right, because you are the only person in this room whose name I have to look up and it is the only one I have had to look up, and I have a box for it and the box is *identity of requester* and it is the only box on the whole of this office's intake that has ever been used."

She put a printout on the table and turned it round.

> **MUNICIPAL REMITTANCE BUREAU — fixed positions, schedule of 16 September 2024, ninth entry.**
> **VENN, E. — post no. — hours administered by the bureau. Cost centre M.Q.S. 4. Grade — . Status: active (ungraded).**
> **Remittance: monthly, first working day. Unbroken.**

The room went quiet for a moment, and it was not quiet because anything had been proved. It was quiet because a man of thirty-two was looking at his own name in a field with his own address on it, in a building forty miles from a rented room in Low Ward, and the field was populated, and it had been populated for four months, and it was a payee field.

"That's you," said Ivo Pell.

"That's me."

"**It's populated.**"

"It's populated," said Elias Venn. "It's been populated since the sixteenth of September. It comes on the first of the month and it comes in an envelope with no envelope in it and it has not stopped once, including in January, and the post that pays it does not exist and the grade is blank and the entry is *active* and there is a bureau in this city that has been paying a man who cannot walk into the building that owes him the money, and it has been doing it correctly, every month, for four months, **and nobody has ever queried a line, and the woman four miles away who keeps the log of every time somebody has ever asked her for something has never been asked about this one.**"

He said the last part because he wanted it said in a room with a Registrar in it, and Mara Iles was writing, and she wrote it, and that is the whole of what he got out of the morning.

S. Tully looked at the printout for a moment longer than she needed to.

"**That is the nearest thing to a yes this office has produced in nineteen years and it is not mine and it is not the Registry's and it is a payroll field four miles from a counter on Marsh Lane,**" she said. "**And I am going to say the thing I have to say about it in a room, once, and then I am not going to say it again, and you may quote it. A person's legal neighbour is not a thing the Registry grants. It is a thing that other systems happen to agree on, and they agree on it by writing a name in a field, and none of them ask the person, and the moment any of them stops — for any reason at all, including a payroll post that closes — the person is not a legal neighbour of anybody, and there is no form for that, and I know because I have looked, and I am the person who would know.**"

Then she said: "I said I would not say it again, and I have not, and I am not going to."

---

**And the last thing that happened in that room, at about ten to one on Saturday the sixth of February, is that a registrar of twenty-eight read her own letter of the twenty-second of January back into the file of a form she had not written, on paper, as a supplementary statement, because the form had a box for it and nobody had ever used the box.**

"Box nine," said Mara Iles. "*Supplementary statement by another person in the matter.* Nobody has ever put anything in it. I have just read four lines of my own letter into it and I have signed it and dated it and I would like the Registry to note that it came from me and not from him, because it does and it has to, or it is nothing."

"Noted," said S. Tully, and wrote it down, and did not hurry, and then she put two pens in a tray and squared the tray, which is a thing a person does when a morning has gone a particular way.

"**Right. Go on home, all three of you, it's a Saturday and I've got a determination on the eighth of March and I have not started it.**"

---

**Elias Venn got the tram back at ten past one and wrote nothing down until half past eleven that night, at a table in a rented room in Low Ward, on the fourth page of a book with nine pages in it that cost thirty-five pence, and there is a rule at the top of the first page in his own hand in three lines and the first of them is *a thing I can be got at over is a thing about me*, and he has not torn anything out since the twenty-seventh of November.**

He had a pound in his pocket. He has had a pound in his pocket since the sixth of October and it is a different pound every time and he has never once written down what he spent it on, and there was a pound in his pocket on Saturday the sixth of February 2025 and there was a pound in his pocket on Wednesday the third of February 2025 and both of them came out of a municipal envelope that comes on the first of the month at cost centre M.Q.S. 4 for a post that no longer exists.

> **Sat 6 Feb, 23:30. I have been in a room today because a piece of paper had my name on it. That is the whole of what happened and it took nine weeks and eleven stamps and I want it written down before I do anything clever with it.**
> **She was about forty-five and she has been an assessor for nineteen years and she has never been spoken to by anybody in my building, and she read her own sentence back at us with the punctuation, and she said she had never had that done to her before today, and then she wrote down a date. The sixth of March. Saturday. And it is not a deadline. It is the first date anybody in this case has given me that is a date and not a time running out.**
> **And the answer to Mara's letter is what I asked it to be. The schedule has no box for it. And then four lines underneath it that I did not ask for and that have been sitting in a file in Northglass since about eleven o'clock this morning: there is no object in our file. She has written a description. We have written a description. A card is a description and a schedule is a classification and this building has been holding up a card at a registry for nine weeks.**
> **And she has got one box for a place and it is for a burial and it wants a date of permanence, and she would not use it on a door mat, and she was right and she was funny about it, and I have not been in a room with anybody in nine weeks and I have been in one for three hours and two of those were about me.**
> **And the other one. The form. The answer is that it has not begun. A period runs from a determination, and there is no determination, and there cannot be one while there is no box. So the form is not late, and not refused, and not capable of being answered, and the twenty-eight days never started, and I have been carrying that form since the twelfth of December as though it were a clock, and it was not a clock, it was a door that had never been built.**
> **And a payroll field in a bureau forty miles from here has had my name in it since the sixteenth of September and it is the reason I am a legal neighbour of Low Ward, and I have spent nine weeks being frightened of name fields and the one that is doing me any good is mine, and nobody asked me about it, and the woman in Northglass has said the sentence I have been trying to say since the sixth of December and she has said it once and has said she is not going to say it again, and she is a statutory officer with no evidence and she is right.**
> **What a name field is for. I have that, and I wrote it on Friday, and today a woman said the other half of it out loud in a room and did not know she was saying my sentence, and the other half is that a person is a legal neighbour because about nine systems agree about them and not one of the systems has ever asked.**
> **And nobody in the building knows what to do with *this schedule has no box for it*. I can feel it. I wrote it down on a card at about four o'clock in a corridor and the registrar looked at it and said *that is a refusal* and I said yes and she said *then we have nothing*, and we have got that. I have had that sentence in a drawer in my head for nine weeks as a thing that had come to a door and gone away.**
> **Something has to be wrong with that. I have not got it and I am not going to have it by the end of the week.**
> **The sixth of March. Saturday. A day, with a woman in it.**
> **The bill goes out. The name comes back.**
> **And then, at about ten to one, in a room, from a form with a box on it that nobody has ever used: box nine, supplementary statement by another person in the matter.**
> **A record with two names on it. She put hers on it and mine is already on it and neither of us wrote the other one, and it is the second time in nine weeks that this has happened in this case, and the first time was a minute about having seen something, and I did not understand either of them and I understand this one.**
