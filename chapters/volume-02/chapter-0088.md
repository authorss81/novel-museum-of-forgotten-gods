# Chapter 0088 — Where Has To Be

The room was booked on a form on Wednesday the fourteenth of April 2025 at about eleven in the morning by the Senior Conservator and Accession Registrar of a public museum, and the form is a conciliation service form, and it has four boxes, and one of them is *room*, and one of them is *purpose*, and the purpose as written was **to establish whether a practice requires a location**, and the room was on the first floor of a building in Morningside, and it had a table in it and about nine chairs and a window that gave onto the same yard as the hearing on the eighteenth of March.

**It was on the Friday the sixteenth, at two o'clock, on notice, in company, not before eight, and there were four people in it, and one of them had come because a letter to this Museum had gone past the office of a firm of surveyors and letting agents and she had read the envelope, which is the same reason she gave in February and which she has said twice is the whole of it.**

Nobody minuted anything, and that was the finding, and it was found in the room, on the Friday, by the person with the least power to decide it.

---

The systems technician of twenty-five asked it properly, and it took him about four minutes to set it up, and he had written it out on a piece of paper and he did not read from it and he has said since that he wrote it out because on the twentieth of February he asked it in a hall, badly, on purpose, and got nowhere.

"**Right. Three parts and I have written them down and I am not going to read it.**"

"Go on."

"**One. Where does a practice have to happen for it to be the same practice. Two. What is the instrument that holds it there. Three. Who is the person who says.**" He put the paper face down on the table. "**On the twentieth of February I asked the first of those in a hall and I did it in front of about nine people and about four of us had the same wrong answer and I was one of them. I am asking all three. I am asking them in a room with a purpose on a form, and there is a letting agent in it, and there is a registrar in it who has been asked that question twice and refused it twice, and I am not going to let this go until somebody writes it down.**"

"It is not going to be written down," said Mara Iles.

"**Then it is going to be said in a room by somebody who is competent, which is the second-best thing, and I have had nineteen weeks of first-best.**"

---

## The letting agent answered two of the three parts in about nineteen minutes and would not be drawn on the third

"**Part one, and I have said this before and I am going to say it once and then I am not going to say it again in this city.**" Sonia Boulton had the book under her arm and she did not open it. "**A practice with a shop in it is portable. You can buy the thing it happens on for two pounds, and about nine of them have been sold in this ward since the fourth of January, and I know that because I have driven past a shop in Fisher Lane and seen the shelf, and nobody's job is to know it. And that is the whole of what makes this thing dangerous and I said it in a public room in February and about four people wrote it down and none of them wrote down the half of it.**"

"Which half."

"**That a practice with a landlord in it is not portable, and cannot be, and that is not a moral, it is a fact about what a practice needs, and I will not be drawn on it and I would like that on the form.**"

"**You will not be drawn on what.**"

"**On whether it is a thing or a place. I have been asked that three times now — by a Director in a hall, by a systems technician in a committee room, and by a registrar who refused to answer it in this very room in February. It is the same question and it is a good question and it is not a question a letting agent should answer, because the two answers are both true of my client and I do not get to choose which one you use.**" She put her hand on the book. "**If it is a thing, you can buy another one, and you have, and there are nine, and one of them is in a museum and cannot leave because the only person in this county who can countersign its removal is a nine-year-old. If it is a place, then it has an address and an address has a landlord and a term and a date, and I have about four hundred and twelve of those documents in an office four miles from here and I am not going to give you one of them, and you know that, and it is the correct position and I have been right about it since December.**"

"Then somebody has to answer it for you," said Ivo Pell.

"**No. That is the second time somebody has tried that today and I am going to say the thing that stops it happening a third time: there is no version of that answer where it comes from me. If you want it answered then it has to be answered by whoever is going to be wrong about one half of it, and there is exactly one person in this room like that and she knows it.**"

And everybody in the room looked at the registrar, and she said no.

---

## And the registrar said the object-or-place question out loud for the first time in a room with a professional in it, and then refused it, and her reason took about nine seconds

"**Right. I am going to put it in a sentence because it has been in about nine rooms in nineteen weeks in about four different versions and it has never once been in a room with a letting agent in it, and that is not an accident, that is everybody being polite to each other.**"

She did not get up and she did not put her hands behind her back.

"**Is what these people are doing a thing, or is it a place.**" She put one hand flat on the table. "**If it is a thing it has a manufacturer and a price and a size, and about nine of them have been sold since the fourth of January, and one of them is on a plastic sheet in a public gallery four miles from here and cannot leave this building. If it is a place, it has an address, and an address has a landlord and a term and a date, and about four hundred and twelve of those documents exist in this ward and not one of them is ours and the Museum's own rule from the twenty-seventh of January is that we hold no record of what happens on that street, so the two answers are not compatible and somebody is going to have to be wrong about half of it. That is the question. It has been the question since the fourth of December.**"

"Answer it," said Ivo Pell.

"No."

"**You put it in a sentence in a room with a letting agent in it. You put it the way I have never been able to put it. Say which one.**"

"**No, and I am going to give you the reason, and it is the only reason I have and I have been keeping it for two months.**" She turned and looked at him. "**I am the person who will have to write it down.**"

---

**And then she said the rest of it, and it took about a minute, and nobody interrupted her, and it is the sentence that everything else in April has been standing on.**

"**If I answer that question in this room then in about four years there is a document in this building with a registrar's initials on it that says what the practice at Sillick Lane is, and it will say it in one word, and that word will be in a sentence in a hearing, and a hearing is a public place. And a person will be able to point at it and say *the museum decided*. And the museum will not have decided anything. A registrar will have written down the answer to a question she was asked, in a room, on a Friday, and that is the whole mechanism, and it is exactly the same mechanism as a card, and I have been on both sides of it since November and I am not going to be on this side of it.**"

"Who is going to answer it, then."

"**Nobody in this room.**"

"**Then the question is not answered and it is a question that has now been asked in front of a letting agent, and she has refused to answer it, and you have refused to answer it, and I have asked it and I am twenty-five and I have a systems day and I am not the person.**"

"That is correct," said Mara Iles. "**And I would like the form to say that, and the form has a box for it, and the box is a free text box about four lines high, and I am going to write four lines in it on Friday afternoon and it is the only thing this room will produce.**"

---

## And the answer to the other two parts was given in about a minute and a half by a registrar who had been carrying it since the twentieth of February

"**There is no instrument,**" she said. "**And there is not supposed to be one, and I would like that written on the same form, and it is the whole of my answer to parts one and two and I have had it since the twentieth of February and I have not said it out loud until today.**"

"Why not."

"**Because saying it out loud in a hall in front of a man with a table makes it look like a finding about him, and it is not a finding about him, it is a finding about forms, and I did not want a man of sixty-five to be told by a museum that what he does has no instrument, on a Monday afternoon, in his own hall, when he had put a date about his own tenancy on a sheet of A4 on the twentieth of February and told us it was the only kind of thing that has ever gone on it.**" She squared the paper face down on the table. "**I was wrong. He could have taken it. He would have taken it in about four seconds and he would have said *fine* and gone and put the kettle on. And I did not ask him, because I did not want to be the woman who tells a man of sixty-five that his hall is not a place.**"

---

**And then she said the thing that is the answer, and it took about forty seconds, and it is the sentence the whole of this case has been walking toward since the fourth of December, and it is not a discovery and it is a definition, and it came from a registrar who has spent five years in a building that exists to put names on cards.**

"A practice is a permission. **Anybody taking part may stop at any point, and nobody's asked afterwards why.** That is the third rule. It has been on a sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet since the eighth of January in a different hand, from a person who has never been asked about it and does not want to be. And a permission cannot be located. **You cannot put a permission in a place and keep it there, because a place holds things and a permission is a thing you are allowed to stop doing, and the person who is allowed to stop is the person who holds it, and they are on a step, not in a building.**"

"Say the last part again."

"**They are on a step, not in a building. That is the whole reason there is no form and there is not going to be a form and there ought not to be one. If somebody builds a form, then on the day the form is filled in somebody is going to want to know why a person stopped, and rule three is a promise that nobody will ask, and the form would end it.**"

"**There is no instrument because a permission is not the kind of thing instruments are for, and I have been a registrar for five years and I know every instrument there is, and not one of them can hold a promise to say nothing.**"

Nobody in the room said anything for about nine seconds.

"Then the third part of my question," said Ivo Pell. "**Who is the person who says where it has to happen.**"

"**Nobody. There is nobody. And that is the correct answer and it is the answer I gave in a hall in February and was not listened to, and I would like it on a form on the first floor of a building in Morningside on the sixteenth of April 2025.**"

---

**There were two mats in that room on the Friday afternoon at about four o'clock and neither of them was on the schedule of collections.**

The first was the one that had been down by a wall at about number forty since the eighteenth of January, which had been rolled up against the wall in March because nobody had been there, and which came out of a cellar in about eleven seconds when somebody asked whether it was still usable. **The second was bought on the Thursday at a shop in Fisher Lane for two pounds by four people who put a pound each into an envelope, because there is no form for a two-pound mat and nobody was going to put one on a minute, and there are now about eleven of them in this ward and nobody's job is to know.**

And on the Friday afternoon at about half past four, on a street that is not on a sheet, in about nine minutes, a mat went down against a wall by somebody who has stood on two of them, and nobody recorded it, and nobody in this case asked her to, and nobody has told the man with the table yet, and the first rule is *you knock first, then you say the name*, and the second thing about a mat is that it does not care which door you go to first, and a person who has been on two of them is the only person in this city who knows that about two pounds of coir and nobody has ever asked her.
