# Chapter 0079 — The Card Under the Panel

On the morning of Thursday the twenty-fifth of February 2025 the panel went back onto the case at plinth thirty-four, and it was ninety days old, and it had come off once, on the thirtieth of December, for about three hours, and the tape was tired, and the woman who printed it had asked that it stay and had been asked about it in a public room on the twenty-first of January and had said no in a public room on the same afternoon, and nobody in this building has the standing to remove a requester's panel, and nobody tried.

Underneath it the card was different, and the difference is four struck lines and a paragraph, and it is on the page because it is the only correction this institution has made to a public card in about a hundred and twenty years and it will never be read by anybody.

> **HAND BELL.** *Unpitched at rest, no inscription, no figure, no devotional content. Larger than life-size, used for sounding, worn hollow inside the lip where it was held. Cast 1902. Staple repair, fracture remains visible. Quiet Accord classification Annexe D permanent, to be displayed, under no circumstances to be removed from public view by a museum acting alone.*
>
> ~~*Recovered on the closure of a textile works, Low Ward.*~~ **STRUCK 25.2.25. Not true. No such works; none casting.**
> ~~*Acq. 4471 / B.*~~ **STRUCK 25.2.25. Not a reference of this Museum in any form. No file under it or any form of it.**
> ~~*Municipal Works, unremarkable industrial object, retained for completeness of the Low Ward series.*~~ **STRUCK 25.2.25. *Municipal Works* is the name of no body. It is in the field where a maker's name goes. The Low Ward series opened in 1961.**
>
> **CORRECTION, 25 February 2025.** The three lines above are untrue. There is no maker. There is no collection line. There is no accession number. This Museum has held a bell under the name of a body that does not exist for about a hundred and twenty years and did not know it until 30 December 2024.
> **M. ILES, Senior Conservator and Accession Registrar. Correction read and initialled: F., label room.**
> **This correction is beneath the panel. It is correct. Nobody will read it. That is the requester's condition and this Museum accepted it in a public room on 21 January 2025.**

**Fenn cut it on the Wednesday afternoon and dated it for the Thursday and did not put her own name in the maker's line, because there is no maker, and the first name she has written on a card in about forty years is in the line underneath the description where the correction goes, which is not a field the form has and which she had to invent at about eleven o'clock at night on the twenty-third of February, and she has said she is not going to be thanked.**

"**You can card a bell,**" she said, at about half past ten on the Thursday morning, in a conservation office, to a registrar and to a man in a coat. "**You cannot card a rope. There is no line on the form for it and I have looked twice and I have been doing this for a long time. It is not a thing. It is a fitting. Nobody makes ropes on a bench with a stamp in the middle, there is no date on one, there is no place on one, and the day somebody prints a card that says what a rope is, that rope is an object and it goes in a case, and then it is furniture with an accession number, and I have seen a bin of nine in the store and I have never once wanted a number for one of them.**"

"Agreed," said Mara Iles. "**Then the answer is that we have a date and no instrument, and I am going to live with that, and the date is in the box.**"

---

**The box is on the ground floor in a store at the back, behind the loading bay, under a bench, and it took about nine minutes to open, and four people were in the room, and the fourth person was on notice and in company and not before eight, and one of the four held the key and one of them cut the correction and one of them had asked for the box in writing under item six of the terms of reference of an inquiry, on Monday, with a stated purpose, and a Director had signed the request, and none of those four people is a systems technician.**

**The systems technician had said in January, on his knees, on the floor of a public gallery at about a quarter past eleven in the morning, that he could have looked, and that nobody in this building was going to be allowed to open a box of minutes from 1935 on the strength of a systems technician with a Saturday systems day, and that if somebody let him he would have to write a minute about it, and then it would be an event, and a thing stops being a rope the second time somebody writes a word about it in a room with a date in the minute.**

**He was right. He was not in the room. He had not been asked and he did not ask. What had happened instead is that a card needs a date and a card is a registrar's job and a registrar cannot put a date on a card that has no line for it, and the only way a date gets into this building out of a box from 1935 is by a document with a chair's initials on it, and the document was signed on the tenth, and it went to a board sub-group on the eleventh, and on the twenty-fourth a board trustee of about seventy had said the word *hook* in a minute and meant it, and on the twenty-fifth a registrar walked down to a store with a key board holder, a label room finisher, and a man in a coat.**

**He is the only person in this building who would have known what to look for. Being right about the rope in January has cost him the room, and nobody in that room mentioned it, and he has not been in the store and he was not asked to be, and on the Thursday afternoon he was on the fourth floor doing nine jobs with a Saturday systems day and he did not come down.**

---

Mrs Farrier opened the box. She has done the key board for fourteen years and nobody in fourteen years has asked her a question about a physical object, and she did it in about a minute with a key off a board and put the key back on the board in the right place, which she says is the whole of her job and always has been.

"That's the 1934 to 1936," she said. "**Thirty-one's the 1929 to 1931 and I wouldn't have that one either, and the 1937 to 1939 is on the shelf behind you and it's got water in it, and nobody's asked for that in fourteen years and I wouldn't take it out for a Director.**"

"How many boxes."

"**About forty and they're all minutes and about half of them are works and the other half are things like *approved the cleaning of the stairs* and *agreed the milk contract*,**" she said, "**and I have said that sentence out loud now and I have been doing it for fourteen years and nobody has ever asked me, and I am not complaining, I am just saying it has been said.**"

The September 1935 book is a hardback about two inches thick with a quarter of 1935 in it and the entries are in a clerk's hand and about nine tenths of one page is *water: flushing of the second-floor lavatory, extension sought*, and then there is a page in November.

> **4 NOVEMBER 1935. HALL OF FORMER FAITHS.**
> **The cord line is to be re-hung, the western portion having perished at two stanchions. New line to be of the same appearance and no heavier. The join to be made as the join the old line was made.**
> **Stated purpose: CIRCULATION. The exhibition closed on the 26th and attendance over the six weeks was 11,400. The line is to be moved four feet to the east, the full length, to widen the route from the north door to the south door, which was found to be fouled at the third stanchion on the last three Saturdays and again on the closing day.**
> **The line is not to be moved for the protection of the objects. It is not on the schedule. It is furniture.**
> **Approved: J. H., Clerk of Works.**

**It is one paragraph. It is about circulation, and about a route from one door to another door, and about a line that fouled at the third stanchion on three Saturdays, and about a body of about eleven thousand four hundred people over six weeks, and about a join that was to be made as the join the old line was made, and about a thing that had perished at two stanchions and was replaced with something of the same appearance and no heavier.**

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**Well,**" said Mrs Farrier.

---

**And then a man of thirty-two standing in a store at the back of a building he is barred from read a line of it out loud, and it is on the page because it is the last time in this case that the rope and the thing a person said about the rope in a public gallery in January were in the same sentence, and they are the same sentence, and they are ninety years apart.**

"Fourteen years ago a man in a hall said *a rope is a printing press with a walking speed*, and he said he had tied about forty thousand of them, and he was right, and I wrote it down and said that none of the four people we had asked had answered about this rope."

"You've not said that in a room," said Mara Iles.

"**No. Nobody asked me to, and I have been waiting eleven weeks to be asked, and I have just said it instead, which is a thing I have been told about four times by people in this building, and I have done it anyway.**"

---

**And the Registrar read the second half of the page and then she put her hand flat on the bench and said the thing that keeps the whole of this out of a god, and she said it to a store, and there were four people in it and one of them was not Museum staff.**

"The minute answers what the cord is for," she said. "**It is in a clerk's hand in a box that has been under a bench for about ninety years and it says *CIRCULATION* in capitals and it says the route was fouled at the third stanchion on the last three Saturdays. It also says the join is to be made as the join the old line was made, which means somebody in 1935 knew what the join was and wrote down that the new one was to be the same, and in January a woman of fifty-four found a sheet bend in the middle of the floor and said it took somebody four seconds in good light, and a systems technician counted the lays and said it was a sheet bend and not a fisherman's and that the difference is four inches of spare cord.**"

"That is a small thing and it is a real thing and it is the whole of the rope," said Mara Iles. "**The rope is two ropes and a join somebody made in 1935 because a route was fouled, and it is a cord line in a room where about eleven thousand four hundred people came in six weeks, and it is marked *furniture*, and it is not on a schedule, and it is in a bin upstairs, nine of them, and it is the only object in this building that has never wanted anything from anybody.**"

"And the ninth," said Mrs Farrier.

"Say that again."

"**I have been in this store fourteen years and there are nine of them in that bin,**" she said, "**and the room upstairs has got nine things on plinths and about two hundred and twenty and twenty-two cards saying why the other plinths are empty, and I have never once put those two nines next to each other and I am not going to do it now because I have got a key board.**"

Then she went up.

---

**And a registrar of twenty-eight said the last thing in the store, and it took about eleven seconds, and it is on the page because it is the only time in eleven weeks that anybody in this case has said out loud what the discipline is.**

"**I am going to say one thing and then I am going to stop and I want it minuted somewhere, and I do not care where.**" She did not look up from the page. "**I have watched two people in eleven weeks want an answer out of a room. One of them is a Director who held a meeting in a gallery without an answer and said so. One of them is a man in a coat who stands at the end of tables. And they are both tired, and both of them have been reaching for something in this building since October, and the reason they have is that this is the only building in this city where anybody can put a name on a card, and it is very hard to be in it and not ask it to say something.**

"**A clerk of works wrote *CIRCULATION* in 1935 and it is the right answer to one question. It is not the answer to the other one, and the other one is what a cord line is doing in a room with nine small things in it that people stand in front of and stop at, and nobody in this building has answered that, and nobody is going to answer it in a store at the back of a building on a Thursday morning, and I would like whoever reads this in four years to know that a professional looked at it and did not claim it.**"

---

**And the box went back under the bench and the key went back on the board in the right place, and the typed extract of the November 1935 entry went into a file with a chair's initials on it, and there is no card anywhere in this building that says what a rope is for, and there never will be one, and the date is in a file and the answer is in a file and the thing in the bin upstairs is furniture.**

**And the ninth of the nine carried patterns on open plinths in a public room on a rope line about a hundred and forty years old was attested, in the same book, in a different hand, in a list of the room's contents that had been folded into the back of the minute for September 1935, and it said nine, and it gave no maker and no date and no accession number for any of them, and it is the first time in the history of this building that the number nine has been written down next to the things, and it was written down by a clerk who had been asked to inventory a room for a works schedule and had not been asked anything else.**

**Nobody in that store worked out what that means. It is a count, in a clerk's hand, in 1935, of the things a cord line was hung in front of, and it has been nine ever since, and nobody in about a hundred and forty years has asked what it is a count of, and the answer to that is not on the page and was not said in the store and is not going to be said on a Wednesday in February.**

---

> **Thu 25 Feb, 23:30. The panel is back and the card under it is right and nobody will ever read it and I have written the whole of the card out twice tonight because I wanted it on this page more than I wanted it on a card, and that is a thing I noticed myself doing and did not stop.**
> **The card is corrected. Three lines struck through, dated the twenty-fifth, a paragraph underneath, her initials and Fenn's. It is under a piece of A4 with tired tape on it and it is going to be there in 2040 and somebody is going to lift the tape and find a dead body's name struck through and a date, and that is what a correction is for, and a woman of fifty-four told a Director that in a public room in January and she was right and it is the only argument in this case that I have never once wanted to argue against.**
> **And the box is open. It was open for about twenty minutes. Mrs Farrier had the key and Fenn was there and the Registrar asked for it in writing on Monday under item six with a stated purpose and a Director signed it and none of us is a systems technician.**
> **Ivo was not there. He is the only person in that building who would have known what to look for, and he said so himself in a gallery in January on his knees and he was right and he has been right about the rope since the twenty-first of January, and being right about it has cost him the room, and nobody mentioned it and he did not ask, and he was on the fourth floor on the Thursday doing nine jobs and he did not come down. I want that written down in his favour and against nobody, and I am the man who wrote it, and I would like somebody to check it one day and find that it is true, because he has been right about a rope in public for five weeks and has had a room every time he has been right and has been in none of them.**
> **And the minute says CIRCULATION in capitals and it is about a route from the north door to the south door that fouled at the third stanchion on three Saturdays, and about eleven thousand four hundred people over six weeks, and about a join to be made as the join the old line was made. And in January a transfer clerk with a clipboard on his knee and forty thousand of them said a rope is a printing press with a walking speed, and I wrote it down, and the sentence in the minute and the sentence in his mouth are the same sentence ninety years apart, and they are true, and I have had both of them for five weeks and did not put them together until a Registrar read a paragraph out loud in a store.**
> **And it says THE LINE IS NOT TO BE MOVED FOR THE PROTECTION OF THE OBJECTS. IT IS NOT ON THE SCHEDULE. IT IS FURNITURE. Ninety years. In capitals. Before I was born, before the bell was cast a name that does not exist, before the mat, before any of it.**
> **And a woman who has done the key board for fourteen years said there are nine ropes in a bin and nine things on plinths and she has never put the two nines next to each other and she is not going to start now because she has got a key board, and she went up, and she has said the only sentence anybody has said in this case that everybody in the room believed instantly.**
> **And a list folded into the back of a September 1935 minute says nine. No maker. No date. No accession number for any of them. A clerk inventorying a room for a works schedule. And the number has been nine ever since and nobody in a hundred and forty years has asked what it is a count of, and I have wanted to ask all day and I did not, and the reason I did not is that Mara said the sentence first and it was better than mine would have been.**
> **What she said: that clerk of works answered one question and it is the right answer to that one, and it is not the answer to what a cord line is doing in a room with nine small things in it that people stop at, and no professional is going to answer that in a store on a Thursday morning, and she wanted it written down that a professional looked at it and did not claim it. She asked for it to be minuted somewhere and did not care where and I am putting it here because here is where I have got.**
> **The bill goes out. The name comes back.**
> **What a name field is for.**
> **And the one that has not moved in eleven weeks and will not move this week, and which a clerk of works in 1935 answered for half of it and a registrar in February refused to answer for the other half: what a rope is for. And the answer so far is a route from one door to another door, and that is most of it, and it is not all of it, and I have decided to stop being the person who is going to finish the sentence.**
