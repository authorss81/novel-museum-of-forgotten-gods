# Chapter 0096 — A New Location, And It Is Not A Win

The offer is four pages and it has a landlord's name on it and a date at the end of the term, and it was put in front of nine households on Thursday the twenty-seventh of May 2025 by a housing officer of about fifty-eight who has a duty, who asked each of them separately, and who told all nine of them, before she asked any of them, that the answer was allowed to be no.

**The building is Cauldwell Buildings, off Low Ward, and there are four flats on the first floor and one of them has been empty since December and two of them have been empty since about the middle of April, and the owner lives in the ground-floor flat and has let the four of them weekly since about 2009 and her name is on the fourth page and she is sixty-eight.**

---

## And the housing officer said the cost out loud before she said anything else, and it is the first thing anybody has said in this case about a house that anybody wanted

"**Before anybody says anything else I am going to say what this is and what it is not, and I am not going to do it in a way that makes any of you feel better than you are going to feel by Saturday.**" She had a folder and she did not open it. "**This is a commercial arrangement between nine households and an owner of freehold, and it is being offered at the weekly rent she has been charging, and there is no discount, and there is no priority, and there is nothing in it for either of us except that she has three empty flats and you have nowhere in this ward to be on the first of October. That is all of it. I have never been anything but straight with anybody in twenty-two years and I am going to be straight with you.**"

"**Say the rest of it,**" said the man of fifty-two.

"**Four flats.**"

The room took about four seconds to do that.

"**There are about nine households in this room that have nowhere to be on the first of October and there are four flats, and I have not made an arithmetic error and I am not going to, and I am not going to sit here and work out which four. **What I am going to do is ask nine people whether they want to be in it, and then put the answers on a piece of paper, and then everybody in this room will see the answers at the same time and nobody will be picked.**"

---

## And the offer has a term and a date and a clause about the street, and the clause about the street is there because a registrar of twenty-eight put it there

The term is six months. It begins on the first of June and it ends on the thirtieth of November, and the fourth page says so in a line at the bottom, and there is a rent in pounds for each of the four flats and a weekly frequency and a name against both.

"There is a clause on page three," said the housing officer. "**Any one resident of the group may end the group's occupation on fourteen days' notice without affecting the occupation of the others.** It is not usual and I asked for it and the owner agreed to it in about four seconds and I can tell you why, which is that she has had three empty flats since December and she does not want a group of nine that cannot leave, because a group of nine that cannot leave is a group of nine that stops paying in April.**

"**And a clause on page two,**" said Mara Iles, who had been asked to look at it and had said yes the week before and had not wanted to. "**The owner is not a party to any practice, arrangement, custom or agreement between residents. The residents are not parties to anything the owner does in the building.**"

"**That is unusual as well.**"

"**That is not usual at all and I would like it kept, and I will tell you why in about nine seconds: in about four years somebody is going to ask this landlord whether a practice at a door in Low Ward is a covenant, and the answer has to be that the landlord has nothing to do with it, and it can only be that if it is written down now, and if it is not written down now then in four years it will be a matter of somebody's memory and it will be contested.**" She closed the folder. "**And it is not a favour to her. It is the only way a landlord is ever protected from a street.**"

---

## And nine people were asked nine times, and seven said yes and two said no, and both of the noes are on paper, and that is the first refusal in this case that is

The housing officer did it in a corridor of the building on Cauldwell Row, in about four minutes each, and she did it the same way nine times, and she wrote down what she was asked to write down and she asked first whether she might.

"**I have to write your answer down, do I want it written down, and if I write it down then your address is on a form in an office and the form is a record. That is what it is. You can say no to the writing as well as no to the offer and both of those are allowed and I will not ask you why twice.**"

Both of the two said yes to the writing.

One of them gave a reason and the reason was four sentences and it was this: she has paid rent in this ward for twenty-two years and she is not going to live in a building where four flats are shared between seven households and the rent is weekly, because a weekly rent can be stopped in a week and a monthly one cannot, and she would rather be somewhere she can be turned out of in a month than somewhere she can be turned out of on a Friday.

The second one gave no reason at all and the housing officer wrote **DECLINED — NO REASON GIVEN** and read it back and the woman said it was right.

**And a registrar of twenty-eight stood in a corridor and watched two refusals be written down and said the thing that nobody else in that building was going to say.**

"**That is the first refusal in this case that has ever been written down as a refusal.** There is a board in a flat in Ferry Bank with seven of them on it and eight requests, and a woman of seventy-eight who says a thing with nothing in it is not a refusal, and she is right, and the reason it is true for her is that nobody with a duty ever asked her on a form. **The difference between that woman and the two in this corridor is a box.**"

"**It is a box and a duty,**" said the housing officer.

"**It is a box and a duty and somebody who is prepared to be told no and to write down that they were told no, and that is not a small thing and it is not a victory and I want it said before somebody in this corridor starts calling it one.**"

---

## And then somebody asked whether it was good news, and the answer was given four times, and it was the same answer four times, and it took about a minute

"**Is this good news.**"

It was asked by somebody who has a right to ask, because they have been in this case since the fourth of January and they have been owed something by it.

"**No,**" said the man of fifty-two.

"**Say the whole of it.**"

"**It is not good news and it is not bad news and it is the only thing anybody in twenty-four weeks has been able to put in front of nine people and have all nine of them see at once, and I am going to say the rest of it and I am not going to be gentle.**" He did not have a paper in his hand. "**We are choosing between a street where about nine flats are empty and there is a piece of A4 in nine hands with two gaps in it saying which years, and a building where a room is somebody's landlord's. That is the choice. Those are the two things. And the second one is not a home and it is not a community and it is four flats and seven households and a woman of twenty-two years' standing who has just said she would rather be able to be turned out in a month than on a Friday, and she is right about that, and it is not a small thing to be right about.**"

"**And the money,**" said the woman of forty-four.

"**And the money is not answered by this and nobody has said it is and I am not going to let it get answered by this.**" She was on her feet, which she always is. "**I have been owed money by nobody since 2006 and this does not change that and it does not pay for it. If anybody in this corridor thinks four flats and a weekly rent answers nineteen years of rent, they have misunderstood me, and I would rather say that now than in November.**"

"**Then what does it do.**"

"**It means seven of us are not scattered across four miles in October,**" she said, "**and that is all it does, and all it does is worth having, and I am not going to be nicer about it than that because I have been honest with the nine of you since April and I am not going to start.**"

---

## And a registrar said the date out loud and the room noticed that the two dates on the table are not the same date

The boundary on the wall in a room above a shop goes until the first of October, because a man of fifty-two chose that day, and it is not a covenant and nobody has signed it.

The tenancy in Cauldwell Buildings ends on the thirtieth of November, because the owner of freehold chose that day, and it is a contract and four people will sign it.

"**There are two dates in this case and they are fifty-nine days apart,**" said Mara Iles, "**and neither of them is the date anything ends, and anybody who tells you this arrangement resolves anything is wrong. On the first of October a practice on a step stops, and nobody has to promise us anything, and on the thirtieth of November four flats become available to a landlord who will let them to whoever she likes, and the question comes back into this room and it will be a worse question because there will be nine people in it who have just done it once.**"

"**And the money,**" said the man of fifty-two.

"**And the money.**"

Nobody in that corridor improved it. **A woman of about fifty-four said one sentence, which was that the arrangement was correct and the worst one anybody had been offered in four months, in that order, and would not say where she had got it from, and nobody asked.**

---

## And the last thing that happened in that corridor is that a man of thirty-two was asked to help and said no, and his no was the fourth no of the afternoon and it was the correct one

"**You could sit in on the first of June,**" said the housing officer. "**There is no form for it and nobody would write you down. I have asked because it would be sensible and because you have been in this case for twenty-four weeks and I have read about you, and I am asking properly and you can say no and I will not ask again.**"

"**No,**" he said.

"**Will you say why.**"

"**Yes. Because the day I am in that building on the first of June, then in about four years there is a man in a room who can say the practice at Sillick Lane was in Cauldwell Buildings on the first of June with a man of thirty-two in it, and he will be right, and I will have put him there.**" He did not soften it. "**And because seven households are being asked to sign a contract with a landlord and one of the things that has kept this case honest for twenty-four weeks is that nobody in it has ever had a landlord. Put me in a building with a landlord and I am a man with a landlord, and in four years that is a handle and not a witness.**"

"**That is the fifth time you have refused something and every one of them was right,**" said Mara Iles, **and wrote nothing down, because the Registrar of a museum is not anybody's housing officer and there is no box for it.**
