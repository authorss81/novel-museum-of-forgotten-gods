# Chapter 0082 — A Nil Return, And A Board

The nil return went to a counter in Morningside by hand at about ten past ten on the morning of Thursday the eleventh of March 2025, in a padded envelope with a receipt for it, and the receipt is in a file on the second floor of a building on Civic Hill, and the envelope is in a building about four miles from that one, and both of those facts were arranged by a Board Secretary who was told to hand it in and not to leave it.

**It is four lines long. Two of them are about the absence of a thing and it is the first document this case has produced that is a document about nothing, and it is the only one anybody in this business will ever sign, and it took four minutes to decide and nine days to be able to ask for.**

---

The decision took four minutes and it was taken at about ten to ten on the eleventh in the registrar's office on the first floor with the door shut, and there were three people in it and one of them was the Director and one of them was the registrar and the third was the Board Secretary with a padded envelope and a receipt pad and no opinion.

"**Read it back,**" said Anja Ward.

"**One. In the words of the officer's letter of the nineteenth of February, the Museum returns nil. It holds no document of the practice referred to in that minute, and it has never held one, and it will not come to hold one.**

"**Two. The Museum holds two documents concerning the matter, both its own, and encloses both: its minute of the twenty-seventh of January 2025, and the acknowledgment of receipt of the sixth of February 2025, ref QA/CR/1426. It holds no other.**

"**Three. No person is named in this return and no household is described in it.**

"**Four. This return is made by two officers, and the cost of it is stated before it is made, and the cost is this: that the Museum by signing it confirms to a conciliation service that it is a party and holds nothing, and a party which holds nothing and says so is a party that can be asked to produce nothing for ever after. That is the correct outcome and it is the worst sentence I have ever put my name to, in the sense that it is the only one of mine with nothing in it.**"

"**You have left my sentence out of line four,**" said Anja Ward.

"**I have, deliberately, and I am going to say why and then I am going to put it back in if you want it in.** It is a second sentence about what a museum is the last thing in, and this document is going to be read by an officer in a room with nine people in it in seven days, and in about four years it will be in a file, and line four is the only part of it anybody will ever read out. **If your sentence is in line four then the return is a policy. If it is not, it is a return.**"

"Put it back in," said Anja Ward, after about four seconds. "**Put it back in and keep the other sentence, and I want it on the record that I was asked and said yes and that I did not need four seconds.**"

---

**And then the thing that made the morning, and it was about a schedule of collections going to a Board at two o'clock that afternoon with a dead man's name in it, and it was a question the Board Secretary asked in a corridor at ten past ten with the envelope in her hand.**

"The schedule goes to the Board this afternoon. **Shall I send a copy to the conciliation service as well, so that the officer has it before Thursday.**"

Nobody in the corridor answered for about nine seconds and the answer came from the person who does not decide things.

"**No,**" said Mara Iles. "**Standing Instruction fourteen, clause six. No document, image, record or copy of Museum material goes into any file that has not asked for it. The officer asked for a statement of what documents we hold and we are giving her two, by hand, in an envelope, today. She did not ask for the schedule. If the schedule is on her desk on Friday morning then in four years there is a piece of paper in a service in Morningside that this Museum put there and did not have to, and there is a version of the next twenty years in which a member of that service reads a dead man's name out of a Museum schedule in a room with nine people in it and it is on a list.**"

"It's her case."

"**It is her case and the schedule is our document and she has asked us for two documents and we are giving her two documents and she is entirely satisfied, and if we send her a third one for free then the first two stop being a return and start being a supply.**"

"**Then what do I put on the receipt.**"

"**Put *nil return, two enclosures, delivered by hand*,"** said the registrar, "**and put nothing else on it, and do not put the schedule in the same bag, and do not leave it on the counter for her to find.**"

---

## The Board sat at two o'clock on the same afternoon and it took ninety minutes and the room is on the second floor and there is a frame on the wall

The frame on the wall of the boardroom is A4 and it is the Director's Standing Conditions of Movement and Access of Schedule 12 Carried Objects, issued at ten o'clock on the morning of the fifteenth of October 2024, four clauses, and it was framed in November by Mrs Farrier on the key board, who has done the key board for fourteen years, and who was asked to do it and did it and went up.

Nobody in the room looked at it. **Condition two of that frame has failed twice — on Wednesday the twenty-third of December over a door mat in a conservation office at about twenty past two in the afternoon, and on Tuesday the twenty-ninth over a classroom mat in a corridor in Hall Lane — and it is in force, and it is not displaced, and it is the most important document in this case, and it was ninety feet from a table at which eleven people sat for ninety minutes on the eleventh of March and it did not come up once.**

The schedule of collections is the only public index this building has. It goes to a Board in March and it is a printed list, and it has been incomplete since about the eleventh of September, and it has about eleven entries on it, and the entries for 1974 are marked incomplete, and it is the second entry that the room spent its time on.

> **SCHEDULE OF COLLECTIONS, proposed. Eleven entries. Incomplete.**
>
> **1.** 1961/LW/44 — Sillick Mill: administrative papers, disposal. Level B, bay 9. Cage. **INCOMPLETE.**
>
> **2.** **A comparison sheet, four mornings of a subscriber file against the state of a block, in an inquiry file under item six. Not accessioned. No accession number. Not a collection. Contains a person's name and a house.**
>
> **3.** 1961/LW/51 — Fitter's lamp, electric, navy enamel. Gallery of Unnamed Hands, plinth 9. **NOT RE-PLINTHED.**
>
> **4.** **An object received 30 December 2024: coir door mat, 700 × 400 mm, no maker, no accession number, on a plastic sheet within a cord line. No name available in the maker's field.**
>
> **5.** 1961/LW/19 — Mill threshold stone, one hollow. Plinth 11. Maker's line empty.
>
> **6.** **A card, four inches by six, cut 24 February 2025, being a statement and not a description, on a front desk in a public lobby, in a plastic sleeve.**
>
> **7–11.** Works, papers and a minute extract, 1934–1936.

"**Entry four is the mat,**" said Ivo Pell, standing up, which he does.

"**Entry four is the mat and entry four is the only entry on that list that is not in this building,**" said Mara Iles. "**It is in a hall in Low Ward. It came in on the thirtieth of December on a visitors'-book line and it is the object that closed a gallery for ten working days and it has a description that is word for word the description of the mat at about number forty in Sillick Lane, and the reason both descriptions are identical is that a card is a class and a class cannot tell two mats apart, and I have been proud of that card for nine days and I have stopped.**"

"**Then why is it on the schedule.**"

"**Because the schedule is the only public index this Museum has and I would not have a thing we have received on a plastic sheet in a public room described in a drawer.**"

## The trustee of about seventy had read the minute, and his question was not the one anybody had prepared for

He is about seventy and he has been a trustee for about nine years and he is a different person from Mrs Vessey, who chairs the board and is sixty-four and is a lawyer and had no view and said so on the record, and he says almost nothing before lunch and this was a Thursday afternoon.

He had the schedule in front of him and he had a pencil out, and he had been reading the minute of the twenty-seventh of January, which is four lines with two names on it, and he had read it before lunch.

"**I want to ask the question I could not ask on the twenty-fourth of February,**" he said, "**and I have written it down and I am going to read it out, and Registrar, if I am wrong I would rather be wrong out loud than right in a recess.**"

"Please."

"**The minute of the twenty-seventh of January says that this Museum will not receive, retain, request or file any record of the practice at Sillick Lane, and that it will record the fact of its own observation only, and that it has no duty in respect of the practice and will answer for being a party in due course. And entry two of this schedule is a document with a man's name on it and a house on it, and it is going into a printed list that any member of the public may ask for.**"

"That is right."

"**Then here is my question, and it is one question, and it has three words in it.**" He put the pencil down. "**Is the schedule a record.**"

Nobody answered for about four seconds.

"**No,**" said Mara Iles. "**A record is a thing kept because somebody has to keep it. A schedule is an index. It says what this building holds and where it is, and it is the only public index we have, and it does not describe the content of anything in it — that is what a catalogue does, and this building has not had a catalogue since about 1890 and I am not going to build one on a Thursday afternoon. Entry two says there is a sheet of paper in an inquiry file and it has a person's name on it. It does not say the person's name. It does not say the house. And I went and looked at the entry before I came in here and I have written it myself, and it took me about nine minutes, and it is the only entry on that list I have written from scratch since January.**"

"And the objection I made on the twenty-fourth," said the trustee.

"**Your objection was that we had told the public twice that we would not put a person's name in a public document and here is one going into a Board paper. That objection was correct and I defended the entry on a different ground, which was that the alternative is a hole, and a hole in a card lets whoever comes next fill it in. Both of those are still true and neither of them answers what he has just asked.**"

"Then what does answer it," said the Director.

"**That the Museum keeps records about itself and does not keep records about them,**" said Mara Iles. "**A schedule of collections indexes what this building owns. What this building owns is a sheet of paper with a registrar's initials and a systems technician's witness on it. The paper is about a place and it happens to contain a name because a name is the evidence, and the name is the dead man's, and he is not one of the people this minute is about. If a member of the public requests that schedule in 2029 they will get a list of things, and they will get it correctly, and one of the things will be a comparison, and they will not get a list of anybody in Sillick Lane. That is the difference and I would like it minuted in those words.**"

"It will not be minuted in those words," said the Chair. "**It will be minuted as *noted*, and I am going to say the reason and the reason is that I have been a chair for eleven years and every time a minute has said *in those words* it has meant that somebody wanted a sentence they could not defend.**"

"Noted," said Anja Ward.

---

**And then the Registrar said the cost, out loud, before the opinion, which is the thing she does and which the Director does, and which between them has been the whole of this building's character since November.**

"**Before we vote, the cost, and I am saying it because the Director says the cost before she decides and it turns out I do the same thing and neither of us learned it from anybody.**"

She put her hand flat on the list.

"**A schedule of collections is the one document in this building that any member of the public may ask for, and anything on it is a thing this Museum has promised to produce to a stranger on request, correctly, in a public building, in about four years' time. On the twenty-fifth of February this building corrected a public card and put the correction under a panel, and the panel stays, and that is a promise with a cover on it, and in 2040 somebody will lift the tape.**"

"**And there is no panel for a schedule entry. There is no panel for an index. A person whose name is in a schedule is in it for ever, in a form that anybody can ask for, correctly, and the only thing I have ever been able to offer anybody in this building as a cover is a piece of A4 and a strip of tired tape, and I do not have a second one of those and I am not going to pretend I do.**"

"And the cost of not putting it in," said the trustee.

"**A document with a hole in it, in a drawer, in an inquiry file, with a dead man's name on it that only four people have ever read.**"

The vote was four to three and the Chair did not vote and the trustee of about seventy voted with the majority and said afterwards that he had voted with the majority and would have liked to be in the minority and had thought about it for nine days.

## And at about ten past four the Director made a proposal she had not made to anybody, and it was not adopted, and it is the last thing that happened in that room

She had been sitting with her hands behind her back for ninety minutes and she had not spoken for the last thirty of them.

"**One thing, and it is mine and it is not on the agenda, and I am going to make it and then I am going to sit down.**"

The Chair looked at the clock.

"**The minute of the twenty-seventh of January is the most useful thing this building has produced this year. It is four lines. It has two names on it. It is the first document any institution in this city has produced about that street that contains nothing about anybody, and it is the reason there is not a list of nine people in a file in a building on a hill, and it is going to be cited, in about four years, by somebody in a hearing, in a room, correctly, because it is the only sentence anybody in this city has managed to say about it that survives contact with a stranger.**"

"And nobody has read it," said Ivo Pell.

"**Nobody outside this building has read it, and I have checked, and I want it recorded that I checked and that it took me about four seconds. Two officers have read it. Two. One of them is in this room. The other one is a registrar in this building who has never been to Sillick Lane in her life. It has been read by four people since the twenty-seventh of January, three of whom work here.**"

"Why are you raising it," said the trustee.

"**Because I have been a Director of this institution for nineteen years and I have never before had produced something I am frightened of, and I do not know what to do with it. That is the whole of my proposal. I have a piece of paper that is correct and useful and unread and I have no idea who it is for, and I have run this building for nineteen years and I have never not known who a document was for.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**That is the proposal. I would like it noted that I made it, and that I am not asking the Board to adopt it, and that I am not asking the Board to direct it anywhere, because a Board cannot direct a document to be read and I have just spent ninety minutes watching a chair refuse me something in nine words and she was right.**"

"Noted," said the Chair, "**as a proposal made and not adopted, at the Director's request.**"

**Nobody arranged that. The Board Secretary put two documents in an envelope because a form said to put two documents in an envelope, and the officer opened it on the Thursday, and it is the first time a sentence written by a Director of this building has been read by a person who had to act on it, and by the middle of March 2025 there is a copy of it in a building in Morningside that is not in this building's count of itself.**
