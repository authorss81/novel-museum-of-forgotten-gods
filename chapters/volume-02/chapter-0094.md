# Chapter 0094 — What It Gave, And What It Did Not

The room was let again on Saturday morning for Sunday, with four pound notes in an envelope and no name on the booking, and the key was on the counter of a shop on a corner in Low Ward at about half past ten, and nobody signed for it, and the shopkeeper has now let this room four times and has asked nothing four times.

**There were five people in it on the Sunday afternoon and there was a kettle on and a piece of A4 on a wall with five lines on it and a date at the end of it, and nobody had come to that room on the Sunday expecting anything, and nobody has said since that they were told to come.**

---

## And for about five hours it was a room above a shop, and here is everything that happened in it before four o'clock

The man of fifty-two came at about half past twelve and read the whole of the boundary out loud to himself, standing, in about eleven seconds, and then he sat down and said that he did that every week now and that he had started doing it in front of people so that somebody would notice him doing it.

Frank Dunning brought the kettle and the plastic wallet and put the wallet on the table and did not open it.

The woman of about fifty from the sheet came at about two and said the thing she says, which is that she has been in it since the eighth of January and has never been asked and has assumed about four times that somebody had decided she was in it, and that she still does not know.

Mavis Ludlow came at about half past two and sat down and said nothing for two hours and then said that she had brought a card in with her and had put it back in her bag.

**Elias Venn got there at about three and had not been asked whether he wanted to be in the room, which is the sixth time in twenty-three weeks.**

At about half past three, the man of fifty-two said the date out loud. At about a quarter to four he said it again.

---

## And at about half past four on Sunday the sixteenth of May 2025 somebody knocked at the door of that room

Nobody in the room had touched the door. The door is on a landing at the top of about fourteen stairs and the landing has one other door on it, which is the door of the flat the shop lets separately, and the flat was empty on the Sunday because the shopkeeper had told the man of fifty-two on the Friday that it would be empty on the Sunday and for nothing else.

**The knock was one, and it was low, and it was on the door, and it was the sound of somebody's hand on a hollow door, and it went through the door and stopped.**

Nobody in the room moved for about four seconds.

"**That'll be Mr Perrin,**" said Frank Dunning.

Nobody opened the door. It was not six o'clock and it was a Sunday and Mr Perrin did not have keys for a landing.

**Nobody opened the door for about nine minutes, and then the man of fifty-two said, in a voice that was not raised, *that'll be the door, then,* and nobody answered him, and the room stayed as it was, and the door was not opened at any point that afternoon or evening, and nobody has ever asked the shopkeeper about it, and the shopkeeper was on the ground floor with a radio on.**

---

## And two people heard it, and one of them has said so once, and neither of them can be checked, and the man with the notebook was not permitted to write it down

"**I heard that,**" said the man of fifty-two, at about nine minutes past four, to nobody in particular.

"**I heard it,**" said Elias Venn.

"**Right,**" said the man of fifty-two.

"**Did anybody else.**"

Nobody said anything, and the woman of about fifty from the sheet said, **"I did not,"** and that was the only person who said anything, and she said it in a flat voice and it was the most useful thing anybody said in that room all day, because it meant there were two of them and not five, and she had made it two by answering.

Then the man of fifty-two said the rest of it, and it took about nine seconds, and it is the only time in twenty-three weeks that anybody in this case has told Elias Venn to do less.

"**Do not write that down.**"

"**I was going to.**"

"**I know you were. That is exactly why.**" He did not get up. "**You write it down, then it is a thing that happened, and in a fortnight you will read it and it will be a thing you were glad about, and in about four years somebody will come to you with a clipboard and ask you to play it back, and you will be fifty and you will not be able to tell the difference between what you heard and what you wanted, and there will be nobody in this city who can help you, because you will be the only person in the room and everybody else will have been in the room for nine minutes on a Sunday.**"

"**You have read that in a document.**"

"**I have been alive for fifty-two years and I have lived on that street for twenty-six of them and I have had about nine knocks at my own door that I could not account for and every one of them turned out to be a person with a parcel, and I am telling you not to write it down because a thing you cannot check is worth nothing and a thing you can check is worth everything, and that is the entire finding of this case and it took you twenty-three weeks and a woman of about fifty with a wall calendar to say it and I am saying it again with the same words because I am not a registrar and I do not have to say it well.**"

---

## And the man of thirty-two wrote down that he cannot write it down, and that is the whole of what he has, and it is a record with nothing in it

He wrote it in the tenth notebook, in the back, on a page with nothing else on it, at about half past four, and it took him about nine seconds, and it says this and nothing else:

> **Sun 16 May 2025, about 16:30. One knock on the door of the room above the shop. I heard it. The man of fifty-two heard it. Two people, out of five. Nobody opened the door. Nobody else has said they heard it. The woman of about fifty said she did not, which is correct of her and is the reason there are two of us and not five.**
> **I am not writing down what it was and I have been told not to and I am obeying a man of fifty-two for the first time in this case and I do not think he knows that is the first time.**

**And that is the entire record. There is no second line. There is no description of a sound, no adjective, no time to the second, and no note about what it might have been, and he has not torn the page out, and it is the only page in that notebook with nothing in it except the fact that something was not written, and it is the only entry in twenty-three weeks that he has written for another person to be angry about.**

---

## And then nobody asked what it was, and the room finished the afternoon, and here is the whole of what this case can say about the afternoon of the sixteenth of May

**That is the entire event. One knock, on a door, at about half past four in the afternoon, on a Sunday, on a landing with one other door on it, from a direction nobody could say, followed by nine minutes in which nobody moved and nobody said a name, and that is all of it, and there is not going to be a second one and nobody is going to be told there has been a second one.**

And this is what it did not do, and every line of it is a fact about the world and not an opinion.

**Nothing came back.** The nine empty flats on that street are still empty and there are still about nine of them and nothing anywhere in this city says what they were. The about four hundred and twelve tenancies in nine blocks are still in a programme that finishes in this ward after the first of October. **About four hundred doors a week in a fixed order that is not a record is still not a record.**

**Nothing was restored.** Nobody was given a tenancy back, a name back, a card back, a field back or a knock answered at a door that had stopped answering in December. The woman of forty-four is still owed money by nobody. The man of about fifty still has a round he is not on. The woman of eighty-one is still on a street she was kept off for thirteen weeks and nobody has asked her a question about any of it.

**Four miles away, in a public room on the first floor of the Museum of Quiet Saints, on a plastic sheet, inside a cord line about a hundred and forty years old, a bell that was cast in 1902 under the name of a body that does not exist has said nothing.** It has said nothing since four minutes past eleven on Sunday the thirteenth of December 2024, which is a hundred and fifty-four days ago, and it is not asking for anything, and nobody in that building is going to ask it about anything. **The fracture in its shoulder is open under the staple and it is not going to close.** The card on the front of its case is under a panel of A4 and tape that has been there since Friday the twenty-seventh of November 2024, which is a hundred and seventy days old, and the panel is the requester's, and the requester has not been asked again and will not be. Annexe D is permanent. Annexe E is in force and is stricter than condition four. **The Director's condition two failed on the twenty-third of December and again on the twenty-ninth and it has not been amended, and it is in a frame on a wall, and it is not going to be lifted by anything that happened in Low Ward this afternoon.**

Nobody in that room above a shop asked about any of that, and nobody in this case has any reason to think it moved.

---

## And what the boundary did, and it is a position and not a voice, and nobody in that room is entitled to say so

The man of fifty-two said it at about ten to five, and he was careful about it, and he said it as a man putting a thing down that he is not sure of.

"**I'll say what I think happened and I will say what I think it was not. I do not know that anything happened. I know that somebody knocked and that I heard it and that you heard it, and I know that nobody said a name, and rule one has two halves and we have had the first half for four months and the second half has been the whole of this case since the sixth of December.**" He looked at the paper on the wall. "**And that is what I think it is. It is not a yes. It is that whatever this is, it has stopped at that door. It has come up the stairs and it has not come in, and there is a date on the end of that paper and it did not come in on the other side of it, and I do not know that it knows about the paper and I am not going to stand in a room and tell anybody that it does.**"

"**Then what do you say happened.**"

"**I say that a practice is a permission,**" said the man of fifty-two, "**and a permission is not asking for anything. And I say that if you build a thing in a building four miles away that expects a knock to be answered, then in about four years you will have built a programme, and a programme has a keeper, and we did all of that in April and it took a woman nine days and a form to find out that the answer was no. And if you build nothing, and you put a date on the end of it and you stop on that date and you do not keep it, then you have not built a programme, and there is nothing in this case for anybody to be in charge of, and that is the most I will say and I am fifty-two and I am not paid and nobody asked me.**"

Nobody said anything about that, and Frank Dunning got up and put the kettle on again at about ten to five, and it was not boiled and he boiled it anyway.

---

## And at about half past five the paper came off the wall and went into the wallet and the fifth line was still true and there is a mark on the wall where it was

It came off at about half past five because the room was let until six and the woman of about fifty from the sheet is the person who has always taken it down, and she has never once been asked to and has never once said she would.

**The last line is: *Nobody signs this. Nobody keeps it. It is on the wall.* Three names went onto a copy in a different wallet on the thirteenth of May, and the copy is in a hall in Sillick Lane, and the original is in a plastic wallet on a table above a shop, and the line is not on either of them, and nobody has moved it, and the fifth line is the only line in twenty-three weeks of this case that has been completely true for twenty-three weeks.**

They left at about half past five. The pound was five pounds and the shopkeeper took it and said the room had been let for one day and shut the door.

**And about six people then went out and did the thing that has not changed since the fourth of January, from three o'clock — which was too late, and everybody knew it was too late, and they did about four doors in half an hour and it was dark and the second rule is the light and not a clock, and Frank Dunning said so, and they stopped.**

---

> **Sun 16 May, 23:30. Twenty-three weeks and a day since the sixth of December. The practice is nineteen weeks and one day old. The bell has said nothing for a hundred and fifty-four days.**
> **One knock. On a door. At about half past four this afternoon, on a landing with one other door on it, and the flat was empty because a shopkeeper said on the Friday that it would be empty and for nothing else.**
> **Two of us heard it out of five people. I have written the only page in this notebook that has nothing on it except the fact that there is nothing on it, and I have written it because a man of fifty-two told me not to write the other one, and I am obeying him, and I want it recorded that he told me by saying the reason and not by saying no. **A thing you cannot check is worth nothing and a thing you can check is worth everything, and that is the entire finding of this case and it took you twenty-three weeks and a woman of about fifty with a wall calendar to say it, and I am saying it again with the same words because I am not a registrar and I do not have to say it well.** And it is the best sentence anybody has said to me in twenty-three weeks and it was said by a man who cannot be checked either, and that is the whole of my difficulty and I do not have a way round it and I am not going to look for one.**
> **Nobody said a name. That is the finding and I am going to leave it there and I am not going to write the rest of it out, and there is a page in this notebook with a sentence on it and a blank under the sentence, and the blank is the point, and the blank is mine, and nobody can check a blank.**
> **And what it gave: nothing that anybody can put in a document. Four miles away a bell has been silent for a hundred and fifty-four days and a crack in its shoulder is open under a staple and a card is under a panel a hundred and seventy days old and condition two has failed twice and has not been amended and Annexe E is stricter than condition four and the standing bar is where it was, and none of that moved, and nobody in that room above a shop has any standing to ask about any of it, and I have not asked.**
> **And what it did not do: it did not give anybody a home, a name, a field, a card, a tenancy or a knock answered. It did not restore anything. It came up a stair and it did not come in, and whether that is a god accepting a boundary or a building settling is not a thing I am able to say and I have noticed that I have started wanting to say it, and I am not going to.**
> **I have been waiting twenty-three weeks to hear something and I heard a knock and I cannot check it, and I have noticed something about myself this afternoon and I am not going to call it anything yet, and I want that written down in the same breath as the thing I have noticed, and not in a separate line, and I have noticed that I keep wanting to write it in a separate line.**
> **The bill goes out. The name comes back.**
> **What a name field is for.**
> **What a legal neighbour is. And what a boundary is, which turned out to be a thing that a thing can be kept out of by, which nobody in this case had thought of and which four of us can now say is possible and only two of us can say we heard it.**
> **And who keeps it. Nobody. Still nobody, and the fifth line is still the truest thing anybody has written in twenty-three weeks, and there are three names in a plastic wallet in a hall that I said this afternoon was not a register and I was right, and there is one page in a notebook with a blank on it, and both of those are the same mistake and one of them is mine.**
