# Chapter 0056 — A Name Field

The Museum of Quiet Saints has five books in which a person is recorded, and a systems index with about nine hundred lines in it, and two hundred and forty-seven entries in it that have a location, and about four hundred addresses on a list that is not a book and has never been a book, and on the twenty-first of December 2024 somebody finally put the four of them next to each other and it took about two hours and the finding was that none of them knows anything about any of the others.

It is not a dramatic finding. It is the dullest finding in the whole of this case and it is the reason about a third of a street in Low Ward is not on anything.

---

**Saturday the twenty-first of December. Ten past ten in the morning. The post room, ground floor, behind the security office.**

**The Museum of Quiet Saints opens at ten and closes at four on a Saturday in the run-up to Christmas, and about ninety people came through the front door before eleven, and thirty of them were children, and there is no school group in the Hall of Former Faiths and has not been since the eighteenth of November, and nobody came to the desk about anything at all.**

There is a manila envelope on the corner of a desk in the post room and it says **DEAD-LETTERS** on it in biro, and there is a number on it in biro, and the number is three hundred and fifty-three.

The number is from the eighth of December. It is the last number anybody wrote on that envelope, and it was written on the last morning of Elias Venn's employment by a man who did not know it was the last morning of anything, and no one has written on the envelope since, because the envelope is a job and the job stopped on the ninth of December and nobody replaced it and the envelope has not been emptied.

It is now on a second envelope. The first one is under the second one, and the second one is not marked.

"The box behind the boiler is the other one," said Margaret Priest. "That's where the returned stuff goes when the envelope's full, and the envelope's been full since about the Wednesday, and the box has been in that corridor since the spring because there was nowhere to put it and it's not ours. It came out of the loading bay in October with a load of shelving that went back in November. Nobody's opened it since."

"How many in the box."

"I haven't opened it. It's a box behind a boiler and it isn't my job and I have been at this counter nineteen years and I have learned exactly one thing about boxes behind boilers, which is that when somebody finally opens one, it is because they have been told to."

"Are they going to tell you to?"

"No," said Margaret Priest. "So it will be there in March."

Ivo Pell came in at about eleven with a mug and looked at the envelope and the box and did not say anything for about nine seconds, and then said:

"That's about four hundred and some items of post that came back to a public museum in about ten weeks, and there is no number on it, and no name on it, and no reason on it, and the only person in the history of this building who has ever been in a position to write on that envelope has not been in the building since the eighth of December." He put the mug down. "I did the dead letters in October. Nobody thanked me and nobody has ever mentioned it and I did two hundred and six of them on the Tuesday and it took me four hours and I have never told anybody that and I have just told you."

"Will you do them now?"

"Now." He picked the mug up. "It is a systems day, the building is open, and I have got the post room to myself until about two, and I have not got anything else to do between now and the fourth of January, and I would rather do four hundred and some envelopes than sit in a systems office with a mug."

---

**The same morning, at about eleven. The systems office.**

The systems index is on a screen in a room that anybody can walk into and read, and it is corrected on the ordinary Tuesday cycle at about ten past seven, and about nine hundred lines of it are the Museum's own and the rest are notes.

Elias Venn was in the room at about eleven because a sub-group notice had come through the post on Wednesday, produced under item six of the terms of reference, which is *any other matter the chair directs*, and which had directed that a person be produced at ten on a Saturday morning in company, and he was in company with the systems technician, and it was after eight o'clock, and it was the only lawful door he had in the city, and a systems day is the one day of the week on which anybody in this building will let a stranger stand in a room with a kettle in it and not ask him a single question about why.

"Before we start," said Ivo Pell. "If we photograph anything on that screen, my condition from the first of December kicks in and it needs your countersignature at the point of making and the reason and the schedule reference, and I have countersigned four exposures since the first of December, all of them mine, all of them countersigned, and I have not enjoyed one of them. So we are not photographing anything."

"Then how do we put two documents side by side."

"We write them out," said Ivo Pell. "On paper. In ink. And a registrar signs it, and it is a document, and it is not a photograph, and it can be read in a hearing in March by a man in a wig who will not be interested in whether the screen was photographed."

He turned the screen round.

---

**The block is called THRESHOLD GROUP — ADDRESSES and it is the only block in about nine hundred lines that is not a record.**

> **THRESHOLD GROUP — ADDRESSES — HALL OF FORMER FAITHS (notification)**
> **Source: district address & occupancy file, daily feed, 04:1x.**
> **Filter: notification zone = Civic Hill / Low Ward north. Rows: 412.**
> **Fields: SEQ · ADDRESS · POSTCODE · OCCUPANCY · LAST PRINTED · PRINT REF**
> **(block not versioned; superseded values not retained)**

**"(block not versioned; superseded values not retained)"** is in the system, in plain type, in a block that anybody with a systems login can read at any time, and nobody has ever read it, because it is the sort of line that is written once in about 2009 and never looked at again.

"That is the whole of it," said Ivo Pell. "**That block has no history. It is not a record, it is a wire. It gets overwritten every morning at about a quarter past four and whatever was in it yesterday is not in this building anywhere, on any disk, in any backup, or in anybody's head, and it is the only block in the index of nine hundred lines that is like that, and the other eight hundred and ninety are things this building owns.**"

"And the addresses."

"The addresses are fine. All four hundred and twelve of them exist and I have walked two hundred yards of the one street and there are fourteen doors on the east side and thirteen of them are on that list, and the one that isn't is a number that has never existed." He put his finger on the screen without touching it. "It's the **occupancy** field. That's the one that matters, and it's the one that is a name, and it's a free-text field because a name is not a fixed thing and the system lets thirty-one characters in and it has thirty-one characters in it for about four hundred and ten of the four hundred and twelve rows and it has got nothing in it for nine of them, and the nine of them are on one street, and the system has not told anybody that the nine of them are on one street, because the system is not a list of addresses, it is a mirror of a file that somebody else runs."

"Which file."

"The district's." He said it flatly. "Address and occupancy, daily, at about a quarter past four in the morning. That's the same feed the electoral roll is built off. That's the same feed the council's council tax base is built off. I'm not guessing at that, it's printed at the top of the file, and I've read it, and everybody in this building has read it, because it comes down on the first and it prints off the top line and nobody looks at the top line."

Elias Venn stood in a systems office on the fourth floor of a building he was barred from and worked out, in about nine seconds, and this time he did not do any arithmetic at all, which was the first thing in four weeks that he noticed about himself.

"Three systems," he said. "A delivery sheet, a council feed, and a museum. And they're all reading the same thing, and the only one of them that anybody ever *knocks on a door* about is the first."

"That's it," said Ivo Pell. "And the reason we didn't know is that we're the only one of the three that gets a **printed** copy, and the only one of the three that has ever done anything as a result of a difference, and we've never compared the print to the feed, because comparing a print to a feed is a job and comparing a print to a print is a job and there's only one print in this building and it's in a box."

---

**They did the comparison in about two hours and it was done with a print run and a screen and a pencil, and the print run is the only reason the finding is a finding.**

> **NOTIFICATION PRINT RUN — HALL OF FORMER FAITHS. 19 NOV 2024. Ref QR/11/2024/0419.**
> **412 cards. 412 rows. 0 blanks. Posted. Banded in brown paper, five bands, eighty-odd to a band.**
> **Second run 02 DEC 2024 (closure and reopening notice). 412 cards. 0 blanks. Hand-delivered to letter boxes, 2 and 3 Dec.**
> **Annual acknowledgement, 12 DEC 2024. 412 cards. 9 blanks. Posted. Returned undeliverable 15 and 16 Dec, all nine together.**

Two prints. Two dates. Four hundred and twelve names each, and both of them correct, and the October and November names are in a box on a shelf behind the front desk and in a store in the basement, and **nobody in this building has ever compared a print run to a feed because a print run is a print run and a feed is a feed and one of them is a thing you put in an envelope.**

And on the twenty-first of December, at about eleven in the morning, with a registrar in the room because there has to be a registrar in the room, Ivo Pell printed the current block to a sheet of paper and put it next to the two prints with the address column aligned, and the address column is identical in all three, in the same order, because it is a feed, and **nine rows had a name in November and have nothing in them in December, and the nine were in one run of the sheet, and the run was one street.**

> **Compare: notification print run 19.11.2024 (QR/11/2024/0419) — 412 rows, 0 blank occupancy.**
> **Notification print run 02.12.2024 — 412 rows, 0 blank occupancy.**
> **Annual acknowledgement 12.12.2024 — 412 rows, 9 blank occupancy. Posted. Nine returned undeliverable, 15–16 Dec.**
> **Feed block as at 21.12.2024, 08:52 — 412 rows, 9 blank occupancy.**
> **The Museum printed nine blanks on the twelfth of December, put them in nine envelopes and posted them, and has not read a print run in its own print room, because a print run is a print run and nobody has ever in the history of this building compared a run to the thing it was printed off.**
> **Nine rows. All nine within SEQ 1391–1409 (Sillick Lane, west side walk order). No row added. No row removed. No address altered.**
> **Not versioned. The change date cannot be stated from this system. The feed carries a source run stamp, not a change history.**
> **I. PELL, systems technician. 28 Dec 2024. In the presence of M. ILES, Senior Conservator and Accession Registrar, and of E. VENN, called as a witness under item 6.**
> **No photograph taken. Screen content transcribed by hand. Screen not photographed and no exposure made. Condition of 1 December 2024 not engaged.**

---

**And then, at about ten past one on the Saturday, Ivo Pell counted the dead letters, and it took him forty minutes, and it is the only counting anybody in this business has done in eight weeks that anybody else has ever heard about.**

There were four hundred and eleven items of post in the envelope and about ninety in the box behind the boiler, and of the four hundred and eleven, **ninety-one were the Museum's own, and nine of the ninety-one were the annual acknowledgement of the twelfth of December, and all nine had come back marked undeliverable in the second week**, and thirty-one of the four hundred and eleven had come back in the third week of December, and of the thirty-one, nine were the nine.

He wrote the number on a new envelope in biro, in his own hand, with the date, and wrote his name on it, and put it on the corner of the desk.

"That is the first time anybody has written a name on that envelope since the eighth of December," he said, "and it is because you are in the room, and it is because you were in the room on the second of December too and you have been in about nine rooms in six weeks because somebody competent was in the room first, and the whole of that arrangement is a load-bearing wall, and I have never once been thanked for any part of it and I am not going to be."

He put the pen down.

"Ninety-one. It's ninety-one, not eighty-eight. I counted twice. Three of the ones I put in the museum's pile are not ours, they're a charity's, and the charity's is in the Sillick Lane run, and I've left them in the general pile because I am not a registrar and it isn't my job to decide whose post a thing is." He looked at the envelope. "That's the honest version. Ninety-one. I counted it twice and I wrote it down and my name's on it and that's the whole of what I did today."

---

**The last two hours of the Saturday were the five books, and the finding about the five books is the reason nothing happens on Monday.**

> **A PERSON RECORDED IN THIS BUILDING, 28 DEC 2024. Where, and whether a question about that person can be answered from the document.**
>
> **1. The visitors' book** (front desk, ribbon, four columns, unaltered since 2021). **Who:** every member of the public who enters. **In it:** a name, a time in, a time out, a reference. **Can it answer *where does this person live*?** **No.** It holds no address and has never held one, because a public museum's book is not an address system, and adding one would be unlawful and is not lawful.
>
> **2. The device day book** (front desk, one line per device, printed head as to records of issue and return). **Who:** every device issued to a person. **In it:** a name, a time out, a time back, and once, a line with a name and no time out. **Can it answer *where does this person live*?** **No.** It records the issue of a thing, not a person.
>
> **3. The key board** (security room, fourteen years, a card number and a time). **Who:** every person issued a card. **In it:** a card number and a time and, since the twenty-ninth of November, one line with a number and a time and no name. **Can it answer *where does this person live*?** **No**, and it is the only one of the five that has never pretended to be a personnel record, and Mrs Farrier, who has done it for fourteen years, has said so in a corridor and been right.
>
> **4. The personnel file** (`PF` + three digits, a binder per year from 1974, columns of surname, grade, start, department, leaver's date and reason code). **Who:** every person this building has employed. **In it:** about nine hundred names. **Can it answer *where does this person live*?** **No**, and the file that could have — the card index of posting sheets, two hundred and eleven lines, every one of them *c/o Museum letting, Sillick Lane, Low Ward* — is a card index in a drawer on an archive corridor, and **it is the only document in this building that ever connected a name to a street, and it does not include one person in this case who is not an employee.**
>
> **5. The systems index** (about nine hundred lines; two hundred and forty-seven entries with a location). **Who:** nothing. It records objects, and locations, and dates of change, and notes. **Can it answer *who is at this address*?** **No.** It cannot answer that question in any form, and it has never been asked to, and **it is not capable of it, and no system in this building is capable of it, and that is not a fault in the design, that is what the thing is.**

**Every document in this building that records a person records them as a person, and not one of them records where they live, and the one document that did was a card index for the payroll, and the payroll was administered by a municipal bureau in another building, and the bureau has a record of a person and of money paid and does not hold a record of employers.**

Mara Iles read that last page at about three o'clock on the Saturday afternoon in a conservation office with the door shut, and she did not say anything for about eleven seconds, and then she said the sentence the whole of it is for, and she said it to a systems technician and to a man who was in the room on notice and not from any of it.

"**This building has two hundred and forty-seven objects with a location in it and it has about nine hundred names in a personnel binder and four hundred and twelve addresses on a feed, and it has never once, in a hundred and forty years, had a record of a person and a place together, and that is not because we are careless. It is because a museum is a place where things are, and we built it that way on purpose, and it is correct, and the entire business of the last two months has been me trying to get a person and a place into the same sentence using instruments that were not built for it.**"

"So you can't help."

"I can put a name on a card," said Mara Iles. "That is the one thing this institution can do that nothing else in this city can do, and I have said that sentence in a corridor twice this month and both times I have wanted it to be a comfort and it is not a comfort, it is the problem, **because a card is a name in a public room and a name in a public room is a hook and a hook is the only thing that has ever caught anything in this business, and the reason it catches is that it is small and it is printed and it goes in an envelope, and about four hundred and twelve households on that list have had one, and nine of them have had one come back.**"

---

And the finding of the twenty-first of December, which is in the registrar's own hand at the foot of it in pencil, and which is four words, and which is the reason the last ten days of the year turned over:

> **First entry, 24.11.2024: the fitter's lamp, 1961/LW/51, cupboard 14.** **It is not ours. It is on our schedule because it is on our sheet with a location on it and because a lamp in a cupboard cannot be a mystery, and a lamp in a cupboard has been quietly the first entry in an incomplete schedule for four weeks and nobody has read this document since the ninth.**
> **21.12.2024. The first entry in the schedule of collections is an object in a cupboard and the second entry would have to be a person at a house and there is no box for that. M.I.**

He wrote the whole of it out longhand on the Saturday night, and the notebook is one page and the last four lines of it are these, and they are the last four lines of that day and there is no correcting anything in them:


> **Sat 21 Dec, 21:15. Five books. About nine hundred lines. Two hundred and forty-seven locations. Four hundred and twelve addresses. Two print runs with four hundred and twelve names on each of them, correct, in brown paper bands, in a box, and a third run on the twelfth of December with nine blanks in it that went into nine envelopes and came back.**
> **The names left between the second of December and the twenty-eighth and the system cannot say when, because the block is not a record, it is a wire, and a wire has no history, and I have spent six weeks learning that a record with a name against it is the only instrument there is.**
> **I have spent six weeks learning that. And this building has spent a hundred and forty years making sure that no document in it ever had a name and a place in it together, and it did that for a good reason, and the reason was correct, and this is the first time in my working life that a piece of paper has been right and still let it happen.**
> **And nobody's job. That is the finding. Not a person. Not a villain. Not even a man with a handheld. There is no job in this city in which anybody's duty is to compare Thursday with Friday.**
> **I have a receipt with a woman's handwriting on the back of it, a cardboard folder with a company's letter in it that I will not put in a Museum file, a pound, a notebook, and no instrument. And the instrument in this city is a print run in brown paper bands in a box behind a counter, and I have seen it, and I am barred from the building, and it goes to the Board in March.**
