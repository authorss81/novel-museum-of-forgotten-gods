# Chapter 0073 — The Landlord's Returns

A bill has a name on it, and on the morning of Thursday the eleventh of February 2025 a woman of about fifty who had been a records officer of the district addressing service for six years said a sentence on a telephone in an office on Marsh Lane that undid nine weeks of this case in a moment, and she said it because a man of twenty-five had asked her the right question for the first time in six years, which was not a question about a field.

"Read me the top line of the file," he said.

"I have read it to you."

"**Not the file. I want the person who is responsible for the connection of services.**"

There was a pause on the line from Marsh Lane of about two seconds, which was, by the standards of that telephone, a considerable event.

"**Ring the water,**" said Mrs Toft.

---

**The water authority is not four miles away and it is not in this case and it is not interesting. It has a telephone number in a book and a department called something like Metered Supply Accounts, and a woman in it read an account holder off a screen for a converted block of flats on the east side of a street in Low Ward in a few seconds on a Thursday morning of February, and read it twice because the first time she had said a letter wrong.**

And what she read was this.

> **SUPPLY ACCOUNT 88 SILLICK LANE — ACCOUNT HOLDER: MORTON HAY & BAKER (LETTING & MANAGEMENT) LTD, 11 PEMBERTON ROW, LOW WARD.**
> **ACCOUNT HOLDER'S ROLE: managing agent. Freeholder of record: NEWTON BAY (ESTATES) LIMITED.**

Neither of them is on a list anybody in this case has ever been given. **Both of them are on a bill. That is the whole of the nine seconds and it is the answer to nine weeks of standing in corridors, and a records officer four miles away worked it out from a definition on page one of a two-page instruction, because a name field is for the bill, and a bill is a document with a name on it that somebody in an office has to look at every month.**

Elias Venn was not in that room and heard it at about half past ten on the Thursday morning in a corridor from a man of twenty-five who came down a staircase to find him, which is the eleventh time in nine weeks that this has happened and the fourth time in four days.

"**A name field is for the bill**," said Ivo Pell, holding a printout, "**and I have been in a building for four years looking at a name field, and I have never once in my life rung a water authority. Nine seconds. Four pounds tenpence if you want to speak to a human being in a month.**"

---

The Director wrote the letter on the Thursday morning and said the cost out loud before she signed it, in a room, and it was minuted in that order. It took about a minute and she was right about it in a way that made the room uncomfortable.

"The cost is this and I am going to state it before I sign it and I would like it minuted that I said it first. **I am going to write to a letting agent's office asking them a question, and the question is going to be about four hundred and twelve households, and I am a public institution with a public address and I have a threshold group of about four hundred and twelve households on my own letterhead, and I am going to put the number four hundred and twelve in a letter to a commercial firm and ask them what they are doing with it.**

"And the second cost, which is the one that is actually mine. **If I put four hundred and twelve in a letter, that letter is a document about four hundred and twelve households, and it will be filed by them, and it will be copied to their client, and their client is a company that owns the freehold of a street I have been printing cards about since 1954, and in about four years somebody will be able to say that the Museum of Quiet Saints has an interest in that stock.** I have spent nine weeks refusing to make a list of people and I am about to make a document about a number of addresses, and I cannot see how to do the second thing without doing the first, and I am signing it anyway, and I would like it recorded that I said that out loud and that nobody stopped me."

Nobody stopped her.

> **To: Morton Hay & Baker (Letting & Management) Ltd, 11 Pemberton Row, Low Ward.**
> **From: the Director, Morrowgate Museum of Quiet Saints. 11 February 2025.**
> **This Museum maintains a local notification list of about four hundred and twelve addresses in its immediate vicinity, maintained since about 1954, for the purpose of telling local residents about open days. Between the fifth and the twelfth of December 2024 nine of those addresses ceased to be deliverable to, and this Museum has established that the cause lies in a field maintained for billing and correspondence routing and not in any decision of this institution.**
> **I am writing to ask you a question, not to request a document. I am not asking for a schedule of current or former tenants, or a rent roll, or any list of names, and I would not put such a thing in this building if it were sent to me.**
> **I am asking whether your firm is the managing agent for any of the properties in the vicinity of the Museum's notification zone, and if so, whether the firm is conducting or has conducted any programme in relation to those tenancies, and if so, on what basis, on what cycle, and with what notice.**
> **The purpose is stated so that you may refuse it. If you decline, a letter saying so is the most useful thing this Museum will receive this month.**
> **A. WARD, Director.**

**It went out by hand at about ten o'clock on the Thursday, because the post from Civic Hill to Pemberton Row is a day and a half and a Director of fifty-three had decided almost at once that a day and a half was a day and a half she could not afford. The room went on with its morning, and at about twenty past eleven the telephone on the front desk rang and a woman asked for the Director by name and gave her name and said four sentences.**

"Sonia Boulton. I'm the letting agent. Your letter came by hand about a quarter of an hour ago and I've read it twice. **I'll come myself at half past three and I'll not send anything, and I'd like it understood that I am not sending anything.**"

"**Why not send anything.**"

"Because a letting agent who posts a schedule of tenancies to a stranger is a letting agent who has posted a rent roll, and a rent roll is the only document in this country that nobody is ever supposed to have and everybody wants. **I have been refusing them since the autumn. Your letter is the first one anybody has put to my office in four months that was not a request for one.**"

---

**The office is at 11 Pemberton Row, which is about nine hundred yards from the corner of Sillick Lane and four miles from a museum on a hill, and it has two rooms and a reception and a rack of keys on the wall, and the woman who came to the museum at about half past three on the Thursday afternoon of the eleventh of February 2025 was about thirty-nine and had a hardback book under her arm and had been in that building for about eleven years.**

She is a letting agent. She holds a qualification whose letters are on her card. She manages about four hundred and twelve tenancies in the ward on behalf of one freeholder, she takes the calls, she does the viewings, she serves the notices, and she has said no to six things in this case that have been asked of her by people who did not know she existed, and the six things were asked by a council's estate team in about October, by a ward councillor in November, by an insurance broker in December, by a journalist in January, by a letting agent's competitor in January, and by a man in a coat in December who came to the office on a Friday afternoon and asked a records clerk for a list of current tenants and was refused by the records clerk almost at once.

**She is not a villain and she has not done anything wrong and she is one of the most competent people who has ever been in a room in this case, and she came on a Thursday afternoon because a Director of fifty-three asked her a question in writing with a purpose stated so that she could refuse it.**

She was let in by a woman at a counter who has been at a counter for nineteen years, who took a pound off her and put her in the visitors' book in the ordinary column, and who has said since that it was the first time in nineteen years anybody had come to this building with a book.

---

**Here is what she refused, in a committee room on the fourth floor, almost at once, and then she said the reason, and the reason is the reason the whole of this case is a case and not a campaign.**

"You have not asked me for the schedule," she said. "Somebody always asks me for the schedule. It has been asked for six times since the autumn. **I said no six times and I would like it noted that the sixth of those was to a man who came into my office and asked a records clerk in front of me.**"

"Noted," said the Director.

"I'm going to say the rest of it in a few seconds and then I'll read you something. **A schedule of current tenancies is not a record. It is the live rent roll. If it goes into a museum it will be in a museum in four years when there is a question about a person at an address, and the person who goes to it will be somebody doing a correct thing inside a department, and it will have my client's name on the letterhead above it. That is not a moral position. I have a professional indemnity and I would like to keep it, and I have also been doing this job for eleven years and I have watched three of my colleagues do that and one of them is not doing this any more.**"

"Understood."

"**And I am not the only one keeping a book.**" She put the hardback on the table. "Every visit I have made since about 2019 is in that, in my hand, with a date, and it is not the schedule and it is not the rent roll and it is not on a system. It is a book. **And I am not giving it to you either, and if you asked me for it I would say the same sentence, and I would like it written down that I have said the same sentence about two different things in one afternoon and that I meant it about both.**"

"Then what are you going to do with the question," said Mara Iles.

"**Answer it,**" said Sonia Boulton.

---

**And she answered it by reading the programme out loud, in a committee room, in about nineteen minutes, with no names in it, and nobody in the room was able to write it down, and that is the most important thing anybody has done in this case in nine weeks and it was done by a commercial professional who was under no obligation to do it.**

"I am going to read the programme, not the schedule," she said. "The programme has nine blocks in it. It has dates in it. It has no tenancy in it. **Nothing I am going to say this afternoon is a person's information and none of it is privileged and none of it is a trade secret, and all of it is my client's commercial position, which I am disclosing to you because a Director asked me a question with a purpose on it and because I have been carrying this since the autumn and there is nobody in this city to carry it to.**"

"And I would like it minuted," said Anja Ward, standing up, "**that you have said that, and that you have said that it is your client's position and that you are giving it, and that no member of this institution will describe it to anybody as a disclosure or a leak. It is an answer to a question. That is all it is and that is the only way this building will be able to use it.**"

"Minuted," said the Board Secretary.

> **PROGRAMME OF WORKS, STOCK TRANSFER — NEWTON BAY (ESTATES) LIMITED. Managed by Morton Hay & Baker (Letting & Management) Ltd.**
> *Internal review paper dated 24 August 2023. Instruction: acquire freehold of residential stock in the Low Ward district; consolidate management; review tenancies block by block against a defined cycle.*
>
> **Blocks in programme: nine. Total tenancies under management: approximately four hundred and twelve.**
> **Cycle: quarterly. The cycle date is the first day of each calendar quarter — 1 January, 1 April, 1 July, 1 October. A block enters programme on a cycle date. No tenancy in a block is renewed at the next term end following that cycle date. Notices are served on the cycle date, at the earliest date permitted, and always on that day.**
> **Term ends within a block cluster, because tenancies in a block were granted in batches between 2016 and 2021. The clustering is a property of the block, not of the programme.**
>

> **Block five — 1 October 2024. Expired. Term ends fell between the twentieth of November and the seventeenth of December 2024.**

> **Block eight — 1 July 2025.**

> **Every notice in the programme was served by the agent on the cycle date. Every notice was served on paper, at the property, by hand, by a person whose name is on the notice. Every notice complied with the Act. No notice in the programme has been challenged. No tenancy has been ended other than by expiry of a term or by a notice served in accordance with the Act and the programme.**

**And she read it out, all of it, at the speed of somebody reading a list, and when she came to block six she slowed down, because that is the one that matters, and she read the dates out loud, and the room did the thing rooms do, which is to stop breathing.**

"Seventeenth of February to the twenty-fourth of March," she said. "That is this month. **That is the whole of what is left of the programme on this street and it is five weeks long and every household in it has had a piece of paper put through its door by a person with a name on it, and every one of those dates is checkable against the notice, and the notice is in the file, and if any household says they did not get one, they can say it to me and I will go and stand on their step about it.**"

---

The second half of the afternoon was the half that has been the reason for the whole of this case. She was the fourth person in nine weeks to say it and the first one to say it about a document.

"There is one more thing and I would like it said before I say it, because it is the reason I read that out and not this." She closed the hardback. "**A tenancy is a document with a name at the top of it and an address in the middle and a start date and a term and an end date, and it is the only thing in this country that says a person is a neighbour of a place. Not a bank. Not a doctor. Not a register of electors, which says a person can vote at an address. A tenancy says a person is responsible for a place, and it is signed, and it is dated, and it can be produced in a room, and it is the thing your landlord would produce if anybody ever asked him who lived there.**"

"Yes," said Mara Iles.

"And you have been looking for a name in a field for nine weeks," said Sonia Boulton, "**and the field is a bill, and the bill is a name, and I have given you that name this afternoon in nine seconds for nothing, and it is mine, and it is a company's, and it has told you nothing about a single person. And the document you have been trying to get at is this.**" She put her hand flat on the hardback. "**And I am not giving it to you, and I have said no six times, and I would like the sixth thing on the record: I have said no to a council, a councillor, a broker, a journalist, a competitor and a man in a coat, and I am about to say no to a museum, and the reason I said yes to the question and the reason I said no to the schedule are the same reason, and it is that a schedule is a list of people and a question is not.**"

---

Then she signed a piece of paper, which is the first document in this case in nine weeks that anybody has produced on purpose, and it is the first one that has a name on it and nothing else on it, and it is the first one anybody in this room has ever seen that could be read out in a hearing without a single person in it.

> **4. The firm will confirm, to any household in blocks one to six, the date on which that household's notice was served and the date of the term end stated on it, on production of the notice, or of any part of it, or of a description of it.**
> **No tenant is named in this statement. No rent, no arrears, no correspondence, and no date of birth is disclosed. The firm has declined to supply a schedule of tenancies and that refusal is repeated here.**
>
> **S. BOULTON, MRICS, letting agent, for the firm. 11 February 2025.**
> **Prepared by the undersigned. Released to the Morrowgate Museum of Quiet Saints in answer to a stated question. Copy retained by this firm.**

"It is four lines of facts and a refusal," she said, standing up. "**It will not tell you anybody's name and it will not tell you what anybody is owed. It will tell a court what a landlord is doing and on what cycle, and that is the only thing a landlord will ever say, and I would like you to understand that I have given it to a museum on a Thursday afternoon and that in about four years there will be a document in your inquiry file with my name at the foot of it and nothing in it, and that is the first time in eleven years that I have written a sentence for anybody instead of a letter.**"

---

A trustee of about sixty-four, who had said nothing for an hour, asked the only question in the room that was not about the programme, and it was the question everybody should have asked on the twelfth of December and nobody did.

"Mrs Boulton. The Museum's addresses and your tenancies. **Are they the same four hundred and twelve?**"

"I have not seen your list and I am not going to," said Sonia Boulton.

"Then how do you know it is the same number."

"**Because I manage four hundred and twelve tenancies in a ward about a mile across and a public museum on a hill takes about four hundred and twelve addresses in its immediate vicinity, and those are not two numbers that happen to be the same.** Those are two different lists of the same streets. **And the difference between my list and yours, and I am going to say it because you have all been decent about asking, is that mine has a person on every line and yours has a filter, and I know yours is a filter because everybody's is.**"

She picked up the hardback.

"**And that is the last thing. You have been trying to catch a machine. And there is a machine, and it is four inches of it at the end of about eighteen months of somebody's ordinary commercial programme, and I have watched a company do it in nine blocks with about four hundred and twelve notices and not one of them late, and I would like you to hear the sentence I am about to say, and then I would like to go home, because it is not mine and I have not come here to be forgiven by anybody.**

"**In about four months' time, nobody will be able to tell the difference between a house that has been emptied on purpose and a house where somebody did not answer the door on a Tuesday afternoon.** And when that is true, and it is going to be true, then it will not be a machine that has done it. It will be that nobody can ask."

She was out of the door at about ten past four and a woman of thirty-nine with a hardback book and about four hundred and twelve tenancies in nine blocks.
