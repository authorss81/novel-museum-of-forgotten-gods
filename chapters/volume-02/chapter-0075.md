# Chapter 0075 — What Is Being Taken

The Hall of Former Faiths was open from ten until five on Wednesday the seventeenth of February 2025 and at about one o'clock in the afternoon, in a public room, with the doors propped and a rope line about a hundred and forty years old down the middle of it and a sheet of A4 on a stand at the door with the time and a room on it, a Director of this Museum stood at the west end and told about a hundred and ten people what a museum is the last thing in. **The doors were propped at ten and the first person through them was a man of thirty-two with a printed card in his hand with the word WITNESS in a box on it and a room and a time, and a systems technician of twenty-five beside him, because the four lines of the twenty-seventh of January make this Museum a party and a party may put a question to a person who was in the room, and because nobody in that building had thought the arrangement was interesting at the time.**

**There was no charge. There was no ticket. About forty of them had come up off Civic Hill because they had had a card off this building in November or December, and about seventy had walked in off the street, and about a dozen of the seventy had come in from a street called Sillick Lane and had not told anybody they were coming and had not needed to, because about four hundred and twelve addresses on a postal round four miles away have been getting a card off this building since 1954 and this is the second time this building has held a meeting in a gallery and the first one was four weeks ago and about ninety people came to it.**

The mat was on the floor on its plastic sheet inside the rope, on a plastic sheet, in the last bay before the bell. It had been there forty-nine days. The card on the wire stand beside it still said **DOOR MAT** in a heading and four lines of description and a struck-through maker's line and, under the line, in a finisher's own hand, four words: ***no name available***. The panel on the front of the case at plinth thirty-four was eighty-two days old and the tape on it was the colour of weak tea. **The bell gave no note. It has given no note since four minutes past eleven on Sunday the thirteenth of December 2024, which was sixty-six days ago, and it is not asking for anything and it is not going to be asked about anything.**

And on a table in a committee room on the fourth floor at about half past ten that morning, in a file belonging to a firm of surveyors and letting agents at 11 Pemberton Row, on a sheet with a reference on it, there was one line of a tenancy file, and a registrar of twenty-eight had asked for that line, and a woman of thirty-nine had read it out in a room in about four seconds because it is her job to read out lines.

> **TENANCY FILE 90-04. Block five.**
> **SCAIFE, T. — 90 Sillick Lane, Low Ward. Weekly, term twelve months. Commenced 3 June 2019.**
> **Term ended 12 December 2024. Notice served 1 October 2024 by M. Hay & Baker, by hand, at the property. Keys returned 12 December 2024. Deposit returned in full 20 December 2024. No arrears. No correspondence outstanding. No recovery action. Forwarding address given.**

**It is the only person's name that has been read out in a room in this case since the sixth of February, and the one on the sixth was his own and he was in the room for it, and it was read out in a committee room with four people in it, and it is on a sheet in a file in Low Ward, and it is a tenant of a letting agent and it is not on a card in this building and it is not in a Museum file and it is not on the practice sheet on a table in a hall in Sillick Lane, and that is not an accident and it is not a gap and it is the whole of the second half of what happened on the seventeenth of February 2025.**

---

The meeting began at about one o'clock. It was four speakers, and three of them had been in this room before, and one of them had not been in this building in her life.

Anja Ward gave four minutes first, and she gave them first because she had decided in the corridor at twenty to one that she would, and because a Director who speaks last has had the room arranged for her.

"Before anybody else, because I have said something in this room before that I was wrong about inside a fortnight, and I would rather get this one right in the first four minutes than be interesting in the fourth. **This Museum is the last institution in the chain and not the first, and we have behaved for ten weeks as though we were in the middle of it, and we are at the end of it, and everything that has happened to the people in this room has happened to them because of a piece of paper this building printed in December and put in a letterbox.**"

Nobody made a noise.

"That is not humility and it is not an apology. It is a fact about a sequence and the sequence has four miles in it and about nine hundred subscribers and about two hundred and thirty postmen and a records officer who has never been asked for anything in six years, and this building is the furthest downstream of every one of them, and it is downstream of a *woman in a shop in Fisher Lane who has sold nine mats since the fourth of January and does not know that she has*. **I would like it understood that we are not here to be the beginning of anything. If this room wants to blame somebody, it has come to the wrong building and I have been the Director of the wrong building for ten weeks and I have been extremely clear about it in every corridor.**"

"And the second thing," she said, "**and I am going to say this now, at one o'clock, in a public room, because if I do not say it now somebody will say it later and make it sound like a boast. The schedule of the last twenty-four days of this Museum's work is going to a Board in March. It contains a correction to a public card. It contains a comparison sheet. It contains a registrar's name and a witness's initials. And it does not contain a single household in this ward, because this Museum has no instrument that reaches a household, and four hundred forms in this building do not reach a door, and a sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet in a hall in Low Ward does reach a door and this Museum has minuted that it will not hold it.**"

"You are telling us the museum is no use," said somebody at the back.

"**I am telling you that we are no use, and that we have said so in a minute, in a room, with the cost stated first, and that the cost was that we are the only building in this county that can put a name on a card and we are giving it away because holding it would make us dangerous to you.** That was the twenty-seventh of January. It is in a file. You are welcome to read it and it is four lines long and it has got my name on it and the Registrar's, and the Registrar has never been to Sillick Lane in her life and put her name on it anyway, and her reason was that a record with one name on it is a record that dies with that person."

---

A woman of about thirty-nine with a hardback book under her arm was let past the rope. She was not Museum staff, and she had paid a pound, and her name went in the visitors' book in the ordinary column, and the time-out column is blank on her line, and the woman at the counter has not mentioned it and would not.

"I am the letting agent," said Sonia Boulton. "**I am not a resident of this ward and I do not live on that street and I have never in my life been a person in a room like this one, and I am going to say four minutes' worth and then I am going to sit down, and I would like it said afterwards that I came because a Director asked me a question in writing with a purpose on it and because I have been refusing things since the autumn and I am tired of refusing things to people who do not know what they are asking for.**"

"**I manage about four hundred and twelve tenancies in this ward for a company that owns the freehold of nine blocks. And for about eighteen months that company has been running a programme, on a quarterly cycle, of not renewing tenancies block by block. And the whole of it is lawful. Every notice has been served on paper, by hand, at the property, by a person whose name is on the notice, on the day the cycle turns. No notice has been challenged. No notice has been late. Not one. I would like everybody in this room to understand that I am not here to tell you that a landlord has done something wrong, because a landlord has not done anything wrong, and if you go home tonight and say that a landlord did something wrong you will be wrong in a public room and wrong in a hearing and wrong in about eleven months' time when it matters.**"

"**What a landlord has done is take a street apart on a schedule and get a machine to do the last four inches of it. And the last four inches are a man with a handheld going round about two hundred addresses a week, and he asks whether anybody is there to take a parcel, and where nobody is, he sets a code, and the code clears a field overnight, and a letter comes back, and a card this Museum printed comes back marked undeliverable. That is the whole thing. There is nothing behind it. There is no conspiracy and there is no man in a room and there is no letter. There is a cycle date and a notice and a lock-up with a skip on the pavement.**"

"**And I have told your systems man the thing I do not think any of you has worked out yet, which is that a tenancy is the only document in this country that says a person is a neighbour of a place. Not a bank. Not a doctor. A tenancy says a person is responsible for a place, and it is signed and it is dated and it can be produced in a room, and it is what a landlord produces if anybody ever asks him who lives there. And that document is the thing being taken, and it is not being taken by anybody. It is being taken by the fact that a house which has been emptied looks exactly like a house where nobody was in on Tuesday afternoon, and in about four months nobody will be able to tell the difference, and that is the whole of the harm and I have thought about it for about five months.**"

"And what is being taken from us," said somebody, "is our homes."

"**It is not your homes,**" said Sonia Boulton. "**It is the ability to be recognised as a legal neighbour, and I am aware that is a phrase and I have watched it come out of my mouth, and I am a letting agent and I have said it about four hundred tenancies in eleven years and I have never once said it to a room before.** And it means this. A name in a field. An address on a return. A knock that gets an answer. That is all it means and none of those three things is a person, and all three of them have been emptied out of about four hundred houses in this ward on a quarterly cycle, and every household in them got a piece of paper through the door to say so, in advance, lawfully, and every one of those people is a *legal neighbour* at the moment that notice lands and stops being one on the date at the bottom of it."

---

The systems technician of twenty-five asked for ninety seconds and got them, and he did not use them on the programme, and the room did not expect that.

"**Can I say the two things that don't fit,**" said Ivo Pell, "**because I've been in a corridor for four days and it's the only thing I know how to do with a room.**"

"Go on."

"**Four of the nine blanks this Museum printed in December sit inside that programme. Three of them are the east side of Sillick Lane in a term-end window and one of them is not, and the one that is not is number eighty-eight, and the tenancy there is current, and somebody walked up to a door at about four o'clock on a Saturday afternoon in December and got no answer because the man who lives there was out.**"

"Which means what."

"**It means that the machine is not the cause of anything, it is the receipt. It's the last four inches and it is four inches long and the four inches are about a fortnight and the fortnight is a man. And four of the nine are a landlord and one of the nine is a Saturday afternoon and five of the nine have got no date in them in any building in this city, and I have been frightened of a big number for ten weeks and I now want to be frightened of those five.**"

He put the mug down on the rail, which he was not supposed to do.

"And the second thing doesn't fit either and it's nobody's fault. **Nobody has done anything wrong. Not the depot and not the district and not the company and not the man with the key. Every step in this is correct and every step in this is correct inside a department and about four hundred and twelve people in this ward are going to find out about it one at a time, by letter, on a schedule, in the ordinary way.**"

---

**And then four people asked questions, and the last of them was at about ten past two in the afternoon, and the man was in his forties and he had come off the street and he had not told anybody he was coming, and he stood up about two-thirds of the way back on the wrong side of the rope.**

"You keep saying nobody's done anything wrong," he said. "**I've been in the flat nineteen years. My notice went through the door on the first of January. My tenancy's finished on the eighteenth of March. And there's about four people in this room who have used the word *harm* at me in the last ten minutes.**"

The room went quiet in a way a room does.

"So what am I meant to say to that."

**The woman who answered him was not Museum staff and was not a resident of the ward and had been under no obligation to be in that room. She answered him in about nine seconds.**

"You're meant to say: **I was there and now I am not, and the difference between those two things is a piece of paper, and the piece of paper is the only thing that makes you a neighbour of anywhere, and it is being taken off you on the eighteenth of March, and the reason it is being taken off you is that your landlord's programme comes round again in July and somebody in an office read a document and decided the block was next.**"

"That's it? That's the whole of it?"

"**That's the whole of it and I am not going to make it sound better than it is by being warm at you about it,**" said Sonia Boulton. "It is not a conspiracy. It is a cycle date. But look at what you have just said in this room. You said *nineteen years*, and you said *the eighteenth of March*, and you have got both of those because of a document, and in about ten weeks that document will say something else and the first of those will still be true and the second one will have been true and will not be true, and **that is what a legal neighbour is. It is not a person. It is a document with your name at the top of it and a date at the bottom.**"

"Which means I am not a neighbour of anywhere in a month."

"**You are a neighbour of somewhere until the eighteenth of March, and then you are a person with a tenancy file in a drawer in Pemberton Row, and that file is the only reason anybody can say you ever lived there, and it is in my office and not in yours and not in theirs,**" said Sonia Boulton, "**and I have to tell you the last part, because you have asked me a question in a room and I am not going to give you half of it. That file is kept for six years and then it is destroyed, and my office destroys them on a Friday, and there is a bin, and the county does not keep a copy. In six years the only version of the last nineteen years of your life at that address will be a forwarding address you gave me in December and a rent counter in a book. That is not a threat and it is not a campaign. It is the ordinary end of a tenancy in this city and nobody has ever thought about it because nobody has ever had to.**"

---

The Director stood up again at about twenty past two. She had about ninety seconds, and she used them, and the first thing she said was not about the programme.

"**Before anybody else speaks, I am going to say the thing I have decided not to say, and I would like the room to know that I decided, and why, because I have spent ten weeks learning the cost of saying things in rooms.**

"**There is a household in this ward whose tenancy ended in that programme and which is owed nothing. Nothing by a landlord, nothing by a council, nothing by this Museum, and nothing by us.** It is not on the practice sheet in Sillick Lane. It is not on the list of the hundred and forty that a woman in this city keeps in a drawer and has refused to give to anybody three times. It is not on the public schedule of interim payments. It is not in the inquiry, it is not on the Museum's threshold group, and it is not on any list of people owed anything, because it is not owed anything, and it is not going to be, and it moved out in December and it is entirely fine.

"**And I could name it.** I asked this morning for the line of the tenancy file and I have got it on a sheet on a table upstairs and I have read the name out loud in a room today, once, in front of four people, and I am not going to say it again in front of a hundred and ten, and the reason is that I stood in this room on the twentieth of January and told you that the name of a thing is a hook, and a name of a *person* in a public room is a hook with a person on it, and I have not found a reason to say otherwise, and I would have found a good one if there were one. **So there is a name in this city that was read out in a committee room this morning and is not going to be read out in this one, and that is the whole of what I have got to offer it.**"

Then she said the other thing, and it took about forty seconds.

"And the last thing. **If you take everything that has been said in the last eighty minutes and put it the way way up, what you have is not a curse, and it is not a machine that has gone wrong, and it is not a monster, and it is not a haunting. What you have is about four hundred and twelve tenancies in nine blocks on a quarterly cycle, and a school of about three hundred and eighty children and about two hundred adults four miles from this room, and about nine addresses on a road called Broad Street that this city has no form for at all, and one institution that can put a name on a card, and the card goes out in December and comes back in the post.** That is the number of people who can be hurt. It is not four hundred and twelve and it is not nine and nobody in this city is entitled to put those two next to each other and do something with them, and I have been told that by two professionals in the last four weeks and I am not going to argue with either of them from a public gallery.

"**And I am going to leave you with the question I have actually got, and it is the only one I have, and this building has spent ten weeks trying to get a registry in Northglass to answer it and on Saturday morning a woman of forty-five told us in writing that the schedule has one box for a place and it is for a burial.** So the question is whether what is happening on that street is a thing, or whether it is a place. And nobody in this room knows. And nobody in this city has a box for it. **And I am the Director of a museum and I have been trying to get that answered for ten weeks and I have failed, and I am going to say that plainly, because a Director who is still trying in March is going to cost somebody another fortnight.**"

---

**And the last thing that happened on the seventeenth of February was not a door and it was not a bell and it was not a god, and it was at about twenty-five to three in the afternoon in a public gallery on Civic Hill, and it is a man of thirty-two standing at the west end of a rope line about a hundred and forty years old, and he was in the room for the whole of it, and he was not asked anything, and he was not on a sheet, and he had paid a pound, and he was there on a printed card with the word WITNESS in a box, in company, not before eight, and the card is not his and has never been his and the thing on it is a question somebody else wrote.**

**He had not written anything down since half past eleven the previous night and he did not write anything down for three days afterwards, which was the fourth time in ten weeks that he had not, and it was now a method and not a coincidence, and the reason was in a minute on the fourth floor of a building he was not allowed to enter, in four lines, with two names on it, and neither of them was his.**

**And about a hundred and ten people went out through a public door on Civic Hill into a February afternoon, and about forty of them went down the hill, and a man of about fifty went to a bus stop, and a woman of about thirty-nine went to a car, and a woman of about sixty-five read the front of the building's visitors' book on the way out and did not say anything about the fact that a letting agent's name was in it between a man with a mat and a woman of eighty-one.**
