# Chapter 0090 — A Boundary With A Date On The End Of It

The room is on the first floor of a shop on a corner in Low Ward and it is let by the hour and it has a fire in it that works and a window that looks at the backs of houses, and it was got on the Thursday before last for a pound, and the pound was four pound notes and a pound coin put into an envelope by about nine people, and the shopkeeper took the envelope and gave over a key and did not ask a single question and has not been told what the room is for and has not asked.

**Nobody's name is on the booking. That is not a difficulty somebody solved. That is the only thing about the room that anybody thought about, and it was thought about on the Friday, by a man of thirty-two, and the thought was that there is no form for this and there is a key.**

## Saturday the twenty-fourth of April, about ten in the morning, and nobody knew what the day was for

There were about eleven people in it and a table that was a table in about 1978 and four chairs with the padding gone and about nine people standing, and a kettle that somebody had brought, and no Museum in the building, and no Museum document, and no minute, and no form.

Elias Venn had a pound and a notebook and a plastic wallet and nothing else, and he had not been asked whether he wanted to be in the room, and he was in it, and that is the fourth time in nineteen weeks and he had stopped counting them in April.

"**Right,**" said the woman of about fifty from the sheet. "**What are we doing.**"

"**Nobody knows,**" said Frank Dunning. "**I put a notice on the mat on the Wednesday and it said there was a room on Saturday and I did not say what for, because I did not know, and I have put notices on that mat about nine times since the fourth of January and about four of them said what to bring.**"

"Then somebody say what it's for."

Nobody said what it was for for about nine seconds.

## And at about four o'clock on the Saturday afternoon a man who had never been asked anything in three months said yes, and drafted it in about four minutes with a bolt pen, and changed his mind in the last twenty seconds of them

He was about fifty-two. He has a first-floor flat on the east side of that street and has had it for about twenty-six years and he has never been on a sheet, and nobody in this case has ever asked him a question, and he has been in this room since about ten in the morning and had said about nine words by four o'clock.

"**I'll write it,**" he said.

"**You have not been asked,**" said the woman of about fifty.

"**Nobody asked me. That's why I'm going to write it.**" He put his hand flat on the table. "**I've been on this street twenty-six years and in three months nobody has knocked on my door, nobody has written to me, nobody has told me a single thing about any of it, and I have read about nine houses on this street empty in one December, and I have watched about nine people get a letter and I have watched what a letter does. So if there's going to be a thing on a wall then I'd rather it was in a hand somebody in this room can read and not a form.**"

He wrote it in about four minutes. **It is a bolt pen because the woman of about fifty had one in her bag and nobody else had anything, and he wrote it on the back of a piece of paper that had been a list of shopping on it, and he wrote it large, and nobody read it while he was writing it, and he did not ask anybody to read it while he was writing it, and that is on the record because it was noticed by three people.**

> **This is what we do.**
> **1. You knock first. Then you say the name.**
> **2. It runs from three o'clock until you can't read a number off the door.**
> **3. Anybody taking part may stop at any point, and nobody's asked afterwards why.**
> **And it goes on until the first of October. On the first of October we stop, and then we will decide whether to start again. Nobody has to promise us anything and we are not asking anybody to.**
> **Nobody signs this. Nobody keeps it. It is on the wall.**

**The first three of those are the rules that have been on a sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet on a table in a hall since the fourth of January, and the third of them has been there since the eighth of January in a different hand from a person who has never been asked about it and does not want to be, and nobody in that room mentioned that when he wrote them out in about four seconds, and nobody mentioned it afterwards either, and that is correct and that is the practice working exactly as it has been designed to work since January.**

"And where's that from," said somebody.

"**The sheet. It's off the sheet.**"

**And the date is the whole of it, and it took him about nine seconds to work out and about nine seconds to say.**

"**The first of October,**" he said. "**Because the first of October is the last day of that programme. After the first of October there is no more of it in this ward, and I have known that since about February and I have never once said it to anybody because there was nobody to say it to.**"

"**That is not a promise anybody has made us,**" said the woman of about fifty.

"**No. That is the point of it. I am not asking a landlord to promise us anything. I am writing down a day on which we stop.**" He put the pen down. "**A thing with a landlord in it is a document with a date on it. A thing with a cycle in it is a document with a date on it. A thing with a date on the end of it that somebody chose is not a document anybody can enforce and it is the first one anybody in this room has been offered, and it is the only one we are ever going to get, and I am aware of that and I have written it anyway.**"

## And the man with the table then said the thing that is the reason the chapter is called what it is called

"**Right,**" said Frank Dunning, over the kettle. "**Now I'm going to say the thing and then I'm going to shut the door at half past four because that's the second one and not the first, and the first one is the door and I know which is which.**"

"Go on."

"**That is a boundary and it is a good one and it is the best thing anybody has written down in three months, and I have read it and I want it on that wall.**" He did not touch the paper. "**And the day somebody's keeping it, there will be a fourth rule, and nobody wrote the fourth one, and I am going to say what it is and I want it written down that I said it before anybody asked me, because I have been waiting since the twenty-eighth of February for somebody to make me.**"

"Go on."

"**The fourth rule is *somebody is keeping it*.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**And I am not going to be the one, and I have not been asked, and I would have said yes on the fourth of January if anybody had asked me and I am not going to be asked now, because a man of sixty-five who keeps a table in a hall is a man with a table in a hall, and the day I stop, it stops, and that has been the arrangement since the fourth of January and it is the correct arrangement and it is the worst arrangement in the world in that order.**"

## And then the man with the bolt pen read it back to himself and changed his mind, and the reason is about nine words, and it is the reason the chapter is not signed

He read it out. Nobody had asked him to and it took about eleven seconds and he read it as a man reading something he has just written, and he got to the last line and stopped for about four seconds.

"**No,**" he said.

"**Which part.**"

"**The signing.**" He put the pen down on top of the paper. "**I've been in this room since ten o'clock and nobody has asked me a single question, and I have been the only person in it that nobody asked, and I have known that since about the fourth of January, and I did not notice it until you handed me a pen.**"

"You've been in a room. You've not said anything."

"**I've been in a room where about nine people have been talking to each other for six hours and one man has been standing at the end of it not saying anything, and I am fifty-two and I have lived on this street for twenty-six years, and I have not been asked one thing, and I have not asked one thing, and both of those are the same mistake and one of them is mine.**" He turned round on the chair. "**So I am not signing a thing nobody asked me about. I have written it because nobody asked and that is the whole of my objection to it and it is the only objection anybody in this room is going to make that is worth the paper.**"

"**Then it is not signed,**" said the woman of about fifty.

"**Then it is not signed,**" he said, "**and it is not going to be signed by anybody, and I would like that on the wall under it, and I am not the keeper of it, and I do not want to be asked again.**"

## And then somebody asked about a register, and the man with the bolt pen said no to it in about nine seconds, and he was right, and it is not the same reason as any of the other refusals in this case

"**Can we have a register.**"

"**No.**"

"**Can I ask why.**"

"**You can ask and I will answer and it is nine seconds and it is not a moral and I am not being clever.**" He did not turn round. "**There are about nine empty flats on this street. Every one of them had a letter through the door in October or December, and the letter went to the house, and the house stopped being anybody's, and I have watched that happen to about nine doors in about four months and I have never once seen a list that said any of them used to be lived in. Not one. There is nothing anywhere that says this street was thirty-one households in November. And if we write one, then in about four years there is a piece of paper in this city that says what this street was, and it will be true, and it will be the only true thing anybody has got, and it will be a list of the houses and not of the people, and the day the person keeping it stops, the street is a street where people used to live, and that is worse than a street where people live.**"

"That is not the reason I expected."

"**No. It is the reason. A list of people is a list of people and I have heard that said in this case by two professionals and I agree with it. A list of houses is worse, and nobody has said that, and I have been standing on this street for twenty-six years.**"
