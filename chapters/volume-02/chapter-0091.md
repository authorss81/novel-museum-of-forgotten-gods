# Chapter 0091 — The Fourth Rule

The room is on the first floor of a shop on a corner in Low Ward and it was let again on Monday the third of May 2025 by the same shopkeeper, on the same terms, and there were four pound notes and a pound coin in the envelope and there was no name on the booking, and the shopkeeper has now let this room three times and has asked nothing three times and is not going to ask on the fourth, and that arrangement is correct.

**The piece of paper on the wall was still on the wall. It had been there since the Saturday before last and it had a date on the end of it and five lines on it and nobody had signed it, and the fifth line says that nobody signs it, and nobody keeps it, and it is on the wall, and all three of those were still true at about eleven o'clock in the morning on the third of May, and two of them were about to stop being true in about four hours and the third was not going to.**

---

## And about nine people came, and the fourth rule was on the wall under the boundary, and nobody took it

The fourth rule is not written on the wall. **It is written on a piece of A4 in a plastic wallet on a table in a hall in Sillick Lane, in a different hand from the three rules, in a hand belonging to a man of about sixty-five who said on the twenty-fourth of April that the day somebody is keeping it there will be a fourth rule and that the fourth rule is *somebody is keeping it*, and who said he would have said yes on the fourth of January if anybody had asked him and was not going to be asked now.** Nobody copied it onto the wall. Somebody had put the sheet itself in a plastic wallet on the table in this room, and it lay face up all day, and about nine people looked at it and nobody said anything about it for about two hours.

Elias Venn got there at ten with a pound and a notebook and nothing else, and he has not been asked whether he wants to be in the room, and that is the fifth time in twenty-two weeks.

**A registration officer of thirty-one got there at about half past ten with a satchel, and she had asked to come, and she had told her office, and her office had said nothing, which is what her office does.**

---

## And at about half past eleven a woman of thirty-one with a satchel said the sentence she had been carrying for nine days, and it was the first thing anybody in that room had heard since eight o'clock

"**I am going to put my hand on that sheet and I want everybody to know that I have put my hand on it, and then I am going to take it off again, and then we are going to have to have the conversation, and I would like to have the conversation first.**"

"**You are not the keeper,**" said Frank Dunning, from the kettle.

"**I know I am not. I have not said I am. I have said I am going to put my hand on it, which is not the same thing, and I want it written on the wall underneath that I said that first.**"

She did not put her hand on it. She said she was going to, and then she waited, and nobody said anything, and then she took her hand off it, and it was still on the table, and the room was still a room above a shop in Low Ward on a Monday morning in May.

"**Right. The fourth rule is somebody is keeping it, and I have been trying to keep it since the twenty-fourth of April, and I have failed, and I failed nine days ago in about four minutes and I have not told anybody until this morning, and I am telling you now because there is nobody in this room I would rather tell.**"

---

## And the reason it failed is not a refusal and not a favour and it took her about a minute and a half

"**My office holds a record. That is what it is for. I can open a form and it has a box on it and the box wants a thing at the top and a date at the bottom, and a person has to be named against it, and the retention is twenty-five years and then it is destroyed on a schedule and I can tell you the date of destruction on a form. That is the best instrument in this city for holding a thing that somebody has agreed to hold.**"

"**Then you have got it,**" said the woman of about fifty from the sheet.

"**No. And the reason I have not got it is that I looked at the form, and the form is a list.**" She put her hands flat on the satchel and did not open it. "**A list of who is on a street. That is what a retention means. Twenty-five years of who is on a street, in a council system, on a form, with a person's name against every line of it, retrievable by anybody in the council who has a reason and by anybody else who can find a reason, and it does not matter that I would not look, because the form is not mine after the twenty-fifth of January.**"

"**Who else could look,**" said Frank Dunning.

"**About four hundred people.**" She said it without any weight at all. "**And that is not a guess, and I have not counted, and I am not going to count, because I have spent nine days working out whether there is one version of this that is not a list and there is not, and I have checked in the only place I can check, which is my own form, and the form does not care what I intend it for.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**So it is not that you are not allowed,**" said the man of fifty-two. He had been in the room since about a quarter to ten and had said about four words.

"**No,**" she said. "**It is that there is nowhere for it to go that is not a list. That is a worse sentence than yours and it took me nine days and I have had a form the whole time.**"

---

## And then somebody said the thing that turns the whole morning into the shape of the whole of the case, and it was said by a man who had not said anything since ten o'clock

"**Then somebody keeps it on paper,**" said the woman of about fifty from the sheet, "**and not in her office, and it is a piece of paper, and whoever holds it is the keeper.**"

"**No,**" said the man of fifty-two.

"**You have not heard the arrangement.**"

"**I have heard the arrangement four times in three months and it is always the same arrangement and it is always wrong for the same reason.**" He turned round on the chair. "**You cannot put a duty in a piece of paper. A piece of paper holds a name against a date. What it does not hold is the bit in the middle, which is the part where the person is allowed to stop.**"

"**What bit.**"

"**Rule three.**" He said it as though he were handing somebody a stair rail. "**Anybody taking part may stop at any point, and nobody's asked afterwards why. That is on that sheet in a plastic wallet and it has been since the eighth of January and I did not write it. And the day somebody's name is against that sheet at the top, then the day she stops, somebody is going to ask her why, and not out of malice — because there is a name and a date and a name and a date is a thing that wants finishing. That is what they are for. I have watched a registrar say it out loud on the twentieth of February and I have got it right.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**A rota,**" said the man of fifty-two. "**You cannot scale a permission. If you can, it is not a permission. It is a duty with a list on it, and a duty anybody can walk away from without filling in a box is a suggestion, and a suggestion with a list of names on it is the worst kind of list there is, because it looks like somebody is in it.**"

---

## And the room made a rota anyway, in about half an hour, because a room above a shop on a Monday morning in May will make a rota if that is what is on the table

That is the finding and it took about thirty-five minutes and nobody argued about it.

**A piece of A4 was turned over. The man of fifty-two wrote on the back of it in the same bolt pen, and it took about four minutes, and there were two columns and about nine lines and a heading at the top that said SATURDAY, and under the heading it said THREE O'CLOCK, and then it said the nine names' doors, in the order they are in, which is the order the knock goes in, and nobody wrote a surname and two of the nine wrote nothing at all and said so.**

Two columns: who is on the mat, and who has the kettle. There is no third column. **There is no column on it for why anybody stopped, and that is not an oversight and it took about four seconds to decide and about nine minutes to explain, and the woman of fifty-four who had refused this Museum three times explained it and had not been asked to explain it.**

Frank Dunning put it on the table in the plastic wallet on top of the sheet with the three rules, and then he took it out again and put it beside it, and then he put it back on top.

"**That is a rota,**" he said. "**It is not a keeper. And on the day I stop being on that rota, that stops, and the same as the fourth of January, and I have said this twice and I am not going to say it a third time.**"

"**You have said it three times,**" said the woman of about fifty from the sheet.

"**Then it is three times,**" said Frank Dunning, "**and I have still not got it right, and I have had a room above a shop and a kettle and a plastic wallet and about nine people since the fourth of January and that is the best thing that has happened to me in about nine years, and I am not going to be asked to give it a name.**"

---

## And then the woman of thirty-one with the satchel said the thing about the Museum, and it is the reason this chapter ends where it ends

She had it in the satchel and she took it out, and it was four pages, and it was a form, and it was not filled in.

"**I have to say one more thing and then I will stop and I have not been asked to say it.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**There is a Museum four miles from here and it has just been told, in writing, that about nine people on a street have a thing that somebody is keeping. And a museum keeps records. And I am not going to say a word against that building, I have been in it twice and both times I was told the truth by somebody who had to be told it and did not enjoy it.**" She squared the form on the table. "**And a record of that street, in that building, in a file, is a list of people in a building that keeps records, and in four years there is a person with a job who has never met any of us whose whole function is that file. And I am not going to hand a form to a museum and I would ask whoever else is in this room not to, and I am not going to be the one who asks, and I have said it and I have not put it in writing and I am not going to.**"

"**There is a rule,**" said a man of thirty-two who had not been asked anything.

"**There is a rule,**" she said. "**A rule from a woman of twenty-eight who works there and who has been on the other side of about nine of these, and the rule is that this building does not put a name into a public document, and it cost her something to write it down in four lines, and it is the only reason that a man with a table in a hall is safe. I am not going to break it because I have got a form in my bag and because I am frightened.**"

---

## And the room went on until about four, and the fifth line was still on the wall, and the fourth rule was on a piece of A4 under a plastic wallet on a table, and nobody was keeping anything

At about four o'clock the woman of about fifty from the sheet read the boundary out loud, all five lines, including the date and the fifth line, and she read the fifth line at the same volume as the other four, and nobody asked her to.

At about half past four the registration officer put the form back in the satchel and said she was going to write to her office on Tuesday and that the letter would say a form is not available for the fourth rule and that she was not going to say why in the letter, and that the reason would be in a minute in her own office and that the minute would not leave her office, and that this was the first useful thing her office had done in six years and that it had not cost anything.

**And at about five, a man of fifty-two took the bolt pen and put a mark against his own line on the rota, which is not a signature and is not a rule and is not anybody's permission, and the woman of about fifty from the sheet said out loud that it was not a signature, and the man of fifty-two said he knew it was not, and nobody wrote down what it was.**

The shopkeeper took the envelope on the way out, and there was five pounds in it, and he said the room had been let for one day, and shut the door, and has never asked.
