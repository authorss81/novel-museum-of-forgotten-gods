# Chapter 0099 — Checked By More Than One Person, Again

On Sunday the thirtieth of May 2025 a man of seventy-four was asked one question about a year, in a room above a shop in Low Ward, and he answered it, and it is the first question anybody in this case has ever asked him, and the reason it is the first is that the case had never had anything to ask him.

**He is Cliff Sowerby. He is seventy-four. He has been at number fourteen on that street since about 1974 and has been a fitter's mate at the mill and has mended the row's standpipe every autumn for fifty years for a pint he would not take, and he is in a Museum personnel file of his own from 1974, and he is a line in a hardback book four miles away, and he is a name on a letter that has been in a coat pocket in a rented room in Low Ward since Friday the thirtieth of October 2024.**

---

## And nobody chose him, and the reason nobody chose him is that everybody in the case had him filed as a name, and a name is not a person who can be asked

"**You cannot ask me,**" he said, when it was put to him, at about eleven in the morning. "**You can ask anybody in this room. I am the only one of the seven of you who has not been asked anything since Christmas.**"

"**That is not a reason.**"

"**It is the reason and it is a good one.**" He had not taken his coat off. "**I have been in this case since the fourth of December as a name. There was a letter about a line on a public schedule and I did not know there was a schedule. There is a name of mine in a building four miles away on a posting sheet for eleven years with a building's address on it and my name on it and neither of them is me. And about four months ago a woman of yours asked a room full of people what they wanted and I was not asked because I have never been on a mat and never wanted to be.**"

"**So why are you here.**"

"**Because a man of thirty-two has got a page in a notebook with a year in it and he has told me the year is a fact about a house and I have been at number fourteen since 1974 and there is one address on that street where I have never once had to knock.**"

---

## And what he said next took about nine seconds and it is the whole of the mechanism of this case, and it did not require anybody in that room to believe a word of it

"**Which address.**"

"**Twenty-two.**"

"**Twenty-two is the one with the wrong year.**"

"**Twenty-two is the one I have never knocked at.**" He turned round. "**I mend the standpipe every autumn. The stopcock is under the stairs and it has never had a lock on it and I have had the run of those three yards since about 1975, and you do not go round knocking when you are going into three back yards for the water. And I have knocked at about nine doors on that row over fifty years because they were empty and you have to knock when a door is empty. And I have never once knocked at twenty-two, and the reason I have never once knocked at twenty-two is that there has been somebody in it every autumn for as long as I have been on that road.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**So when one of you tells me a family went into twenty-two in 1982, I am telling you that the year before that there was somebody in it in 1981, and the year before that, and I can go back a fair way, and I am not doing it for a piece of paper.**"

"**How far.**"

"**I am not going to say a year, because I am seventy-four and I do not trust a year out of my own head, and there is a woman in this case who is a records officer and would tell you that a year is a fact about the world and a year out of a head is a fact about whose head it came out of.**" He looked round the room. "**But I will tell you that it is not 1982, and I will tell you that I would know, and I have not written it down and I am not going to.**"

---

## And the page in the notebook was read out loud, in a room, in front of six people, and the author read it himself, and there was no copy and no camera and nobody made a record of the reading

It was the page from the twentieth of May. It was a list of about nine addresses and the years, in his own hand, and it had come out of nine drawers in nine kitchens in about fifty minutes while nine people were saying the years out loud and he was writing them down.

"**I am going to read it out and I am going to read the rest of the page as well, including the part where I wrote down that I knew I was not supposed to,**" he said. "**And I am not going to summarise the rest of the page, because a man of about fifty read nine of them out loud in a label room in April for an hour and ten minutes and it was the best hour of my life and I am not going to do a version of it that takes eleven minutes.**"

The woman of about fifty from the sheet put her hand flat on the table when he got to the row for number twenty-two.

"**That is me. That is my mother's years. I did not make them up. I have got a tin with cards in it and I have never once looked at the years on them because I did not think of it as a thing with years on it. I said them out loud because somebody was writing them down and it seemed daft to make a person stop and look at a card when you have got people in a room who were there.**"

"**Who gave you the years.**"

"**My mother. She died in 1998 and she had them off a council letter about the rent, and the council letter was about the account being transferred, and the account being transferred means it was in somebody else's name before, and she never had that letter in front of her again.**"

"**So the eleven years is a council letter about a rent account and not a year anybody was in that flat.**"

"**That is exactly what it is, and I did not know that when I said it, and I would not have known if you had not all been sitting round a table looking pleased with yourselves.**"

---

## And then a man of thirty-two said the sentence that is the other half of the finding of this volume, and it took about nine seconds, and nobody in the room understood what he was saying

"**I wrote it down,**" he said. "**That is the whole of it and it took me nine seconds and I was in a room with nine people in it and I read the third line of the first page of that notebook while they were talking, and the third line is *do not write it down*, and it is in my own hand, and I wrote it down, and it is wrong.**"

"**You wrote down what a woman told you in a room.**"

"**I wrote down nine things that nine people told me in a room, and eight of them are right, and one of them is wrong by about eleven years, and the one that is wrong is wrong because a woman of about fifty said a thing her mother told her and her mother was told it by a letter about a rent account, and not one of those three people did anything wrong and not one of them would have known, and there is no form anywhere in this city for checking it and I could have checked it in about nine seconds by reading it back to her.**"

"**You could have read it back.**"

"**I could have read nine addresses and about thirty numbers back to nine people in about four minutes and nobody asked me to and nobody is going to now, and I did not, and that is the entire finding of twenty-five weeks and it is about me.**"

---

## And a registrar of twenty-eight said what kind of thing this is, out loud, in about eleven seconds, and used a word that had not been used in a room in this case before

"**Say the word,**" said Cliff Sowerby.

"**I am not going to say it.**"

"**Somebody had better say it, because the woman has just told us that a thing came out of her mother's mouth and went into a man's hand and came out wrong, and that is a word, and the word has a shape, and if nobody says it then in four years somebody will find it in a notebook and think it is a mystery and it is not a mystery, it is a Tuesday.**"

"**You are right and I am going to say it and I am going to say it once.**" Mara Iles did not get up. "**Memory can be planted. Not by anything. By a person who is fond of you, in a room, out loud, without any intention at all, on a Tuesday afternoon. That is all it takes and it has been taking place in this case since the sixth of December and it took a man of seventy-four to say the sentence that makes it into a finding, and I want it minuted by somebody, and there is no minute, and it is going in a plastic wallet instead.**"

"**By whom.**"

"**By nobody. That is the whole of it and I have been here five months. **Nobody can plant a memory except somebody who has one, and the person who plants it is almost always the person who loves you, and the way to find out is not a form and it is not a system and it is a second person reading it back.** In April a records officer of about fifty said: if I can be wrong about a day of the week off a wall calendar then anybody can be wrong about a day of the week in a notebook, on purpose or by accident, and the only way anybody finds out is if a second person reads it. **She was talking about a day of the week. She was talking about this and she did not know and I have been carrying her sentence about in my bag for a month and it was about this the whole time.**"

---

## And the man of fifty-two then said the thing he said in April, and he said it in the same words, and this time it was not him that needed saying

"**A list of houses is worse than a list of people,**" he said, "**and on the twentieth of May I said that in a room with a piece of A4 on it and eleven of us agreed with me, and I was right, and it has taken ten days for the proof and the proof is a year.**"

"**Your year, or her year.**"

"**Neither. That is what I want said about it and I want it said in this room and not in a corridor in four years. The year is not mine and it is not hers. **A thing that was never checked is worth nothing, and that is not a moral, it is arithmetic, and I said the other half of it on a Sunday afternoon three weeks ago to a man with a notebook and he wrote it down, and he wrote down the wrong number about a house instead.**"

---

## And the list was amended in the room, by the woman who said the years, and the amendment is visible and the wrong year is struck through and not erased

Nobody argued about whether to amend it. **She asked for a pen and she struck through 1982 and wrote above it *before 1982, to be confirmed*, and then stopped and asked whether she might put *I do not know how far back* on it, and the man of fifty-two said she might, and she did.**

The list is on the back of a piece of A4 in a plastic wallet in a hall in Sillick Lane. It has about nine addresses and about thirty numbers on it and two gaps where a year should be, and one of the gaps is now a gap with a sentence in it, and nothing has been erased, and the wrong year can still be read by anybody who comes along and wants to see what a woman of about fifty believed on the twentieth of May 2025.

"**That is what it is for,**" said the registrar. "**You have a list with a wrong year on it and you have not erased the wrong year. In about four years that is the only thing in this case that anybody can check anything against.**"

---

## And then a man of thirty-two gave a man of seventy-four a letter, and it had been in a coat pocket since the thirtieth of October, and it was not opened in the room

He had it in the inside pocket of a coat on a hanger in a rented room in Low Ward and he had carried it for seven months and he had not read it and had not asked to, and he gave it over at about half past three on a Sunday afternoon without any preamble at all, and nobody made a thing of it and nobody wrote it down.

"**That is yours. It was posted on the thirtieth of October and it went into a box and I took the box.**"

"**Then I am not opening it here.**"

"**No.**"

"**I have lived in this house for fifty-one years and I am not opening a letter from a museum in a room with six people in it and I will open it on a Monday and I will tell nobody what is in it and you can ring me in a fortnight and I will say that I have opened it.**"

"**That is fine.**"

"**You have carried that for seven months,**" said Cliff Sowerby, **and he did not say thank you, and it was not unkind, and he put the letter in his coat pocket and the letter went into the same pocket it had come out of, which was a different pocket and a different coat, and that was the end of it.**
