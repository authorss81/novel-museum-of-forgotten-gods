# Chapter 0098 — A Buyer, And It Is A Document

The letter came in on Wednesday the twenty-sixth of May 2025 at about eleven in the morning in a padded envelope, and it was four pages, and the first page was a firm of solicitors asking the Museum of Quiet Saints to confirm nine facts, and the ninth fact was the only one that mattered.

**RACKHAM SEARLE, solicitors, third floor, a building on a street in this city called Lantern Row.** The street and the society have the same name and there is no connection between them whatever, and anybody in this case who has ever said otherwise was talking about a shop.

> **1.** That the Museum held the Low Ward series and had held it continuously since 1961.
> **2.** That the Museum had no record of the removal of household effects from any address in the district.
> **3.** To **7.** That the Museum held no record of the occupancy of any address, in any period, in any register, roll or series.
> **8.** That the Museum had never made, and did not hold, any inspection of any dwelling in the district.
> **9.** That no claim to any item removed from the said addresses between 1 December 2024 and 31 March 2025 is known to the Museum.

There is a box on the form and the box is for enclosures, and there were two.

---

## And the enclosures were a screening schedule and a set of nine headings, and the client was named on the first page, and the client's name is a name on paper in a file in a room

The client was **Dorothy Marsden, of a first-floor suite on the same street as her solicitors**, and she is sixty-one, and the letter said that she instructed the firm in January 2025, and it said that she had made nine acquisitions between the fifteenth of December 2024 and the ninth of March 2025, and it asked the nine questions because she had bought household effects at nine addresses and a purchaser of household effects who cannot show good title is a purchaser who will be asked about it in about four years by somebody with a clipboard.

**The first enclosure was a printed schedule headed with the name of a collectors' society — the Lantern Society, founded in this city, a private network that trades resonant objects and memory fragments and has a members' list and a screening requirement and a countersignature — and it was a single side of A4 with a table on it and about nine rows.**

The columns were: **ADDRESS. DESCRIPTION OF EFFECTS. DATE ACQUIRED. VENDOR'S TITLE. TRACEABLE PRIOR OCCUPANT.**

The first three columns were filled in. The fourth was filled in nine times with the same eleven words. **The fifth column was filled in once, in the fourth row, and the entry in it was a name**, and beside the name, in the margin, the word *ESTABLISHED*, and underneath the name, in a different hand, a line that read **NOT PURSUED.**

And at the foot of the schedule, in a box about two inches deep: **SCREENED AND SIGNED BY MEMBER ______ · COUNTERSIGNED BY MEMBER ______**, and the first of those two blanks had a name in it — Dorothy Marsden, in a hand that was not the hand of the table — and the second of them had a number in it, and the number was four digits, and there was no name against it anywhere in the enclosure.

---

## And the reason was on the sheet, in the letter, in about nine sentences, and it is legible, and it is not the reason anybody would expect

The letter said, at the foot of the second page, and it said it in the ordinary way in which a firm of solicitors sets out its client's instructions:

> **My client's practice is to acquire only at addresses at which no traceable prior occupant can be established, and not to pursue an address at which one can. She has instructed me to say so plainly rather than to leave it to be inferred. Her reasoning is that an object with a family behind it carries its history with it, and the family will surface, and the history will be disputed, and the object will be unavailable to her for the rest of her life. An object from an address with no traceable prior occupant carries no such attachment. My client regards this as ordinary care for the vendor's privacy and not as anything else.**

"**That's the whole of it in nine sentences,**" said Mara Iles, reading it twice. "**And it is not a lie and it is not a euphemism and it is not a code. She has written down, in a letter to a museum, a rule that says: I only buy where nobody can be found.**"

"**Is that a crime.**"

"**No. It is a preference, and it is a preference about privacy, and she has said so, and the sentence *not to pursue an address at which one can be established* is the sentence of a careful person and I mean that as a description and not as a compliment.**"

---

## And a registrar wrote the reply, and the reply is the only document in this case that is a description of a hole, and it went out on the Friday morning by post

She took about forty minutes and she read the cost out loud to the Director before she wrote a word, which is what she does.

"**The cost is that every one of the nine answers is true and the reason every one of them is true is the same reason, and the reason is on page one of that letter and not on page four of mine, and a person reading my reply in about four years is going to see a museum that knows nothing, and they are going to be right, and the letter that proves the museum knows nothing is going to sit in a file beside it.**"

"**Write it,**" said the Director.

The reply is four paragraphs and it answers all nine in order, and the substance of it is this.

> **The Museum holds the Low Ward series. The series opened in 1961. The Museum holds a record of what it has done, which is to print and send, every year since about 1954, one printed card to each of about four hundred and twelve addresses in the district, and to hold a print of that run, and to hold a correspondence file in which the addresses of earlier years appear.**
> **The Museum holds no record of the occupancy of any address. It holds no record of the removal of household effects from any address. It has never inspected any dwelling in the district. It has not been down the street in question in the period to which your ninth paragraph refers, and it has not been down it at all in the memory of any member of staff now in post.**
> **No claim to any item is known to the Museum, and none could be, and that is not a statement of confidence. It is a statement of the size of our records.**

"**That is the truest thing this building has ever sent to anybody,**" said the Director, **and read it twice and initialled it, and did not ask for it to be softened, and there was no minute.**

---

## And on Saturday the twenty-ninth of May, at about eleven in the morning, there were four people in a room on the first floor of a building on a hill, and the reply had gone out by post on the Friday morning at about eleven, and the first question anybody asked was the one that is the whole of the finding, and it was asked by a systems technician of twenty-five, and it took about four seconds

"**When did she buy them.**"

The dates went on the table from the second enclosure and there were nine of them and the first four were in a column.

"**The fifteenth of December 2024. The fourth of January. The eleventh of January. The second of February,**" said Ivo Pell, "**and then the ninth of March, and there are four more, and I have not read them out because the first four are enough.**"

"**Enough for what.**"

"**Those are the days of a round.**" He had a piece of paper with about nine rows on it and it was not from a system and it was not from a file and it had a fortnight's worth of his own handwriting on it. "**FLS-2 goes out on those days. Four hundred and twelve addresses on a sheet that comes out of a rack, in an order, and a man goes down the order with a handheld and he does not skip one and he does not stop, and he has been doing it since about 2019, and the round does not change on a Tuesday in February for any reason that has ever been written down.**"

"**Then she is buying off the round,**" said the woman of about fifty from the sheet, who had come in and had not been asked to.

"**No. That is the wrong conclusion and I want it out of the room before anybody says it in a corridor.**" He put the paper down. "**What I can say is that nine acquisitions in three months all fall on days when a delivery round went down a street in this ward, and that is a coincidence of about nine days and I have done the sum and the sum is not remarkable.**"

"**Then it is nothing.**"

"**Then it is nothing and that is what I have come to say, and it took me about four seconds, and I would like it minuted if anybody wants it minuted.**"

---

## And what he said for the other ten minutes is the finding of this volume, and it took about a minute, and nobody in the room disagreed with it

"**A cycle date is the first day of each calendar quarter. A block enters programme on a cycle date. Notices are served on the cycle date, at the earliest date permitted, and always on that day, on paper, by hand, at the property, by a person whose name is on the notice. Not one notice has been challenged. Not one notice was late. That is a programme and it is lawful and it has a signature on it and the signature is a letting agent's and the agent has been right about everything she has said in this case for four months.**" He turned round. "**And the machine is a man with a handheld in the last four inches of it, and the four inches are about a fortnight, and nobody in this city has ever been able to look at the four inches because there is no form for what a man sees when he puts a code on a field at a door and the person at the door is out.**"

"**And she is downstream of that.**"

"**She is downstream of that, and she is not downstream of anybody, and there is nobody between her and the door.**" He picked the paper up. "**Nine addresses. About nine households were in those flats in November and are not now. A field went empty and a letter went to a house and the house stopped being anybody's, and then, at some point in the following three months, the contents of a flat that no living person can be traced to were sold by somebody with a set of keys, and that is the whole mechanism and every step of it is correct, and I have been in this case for twenty-four weeks and I have never once been able to find the step that is wrong, and I have stopped looking, and that is the most frightening thing I have ever said in a room.**"

---

## And then a registrar said the thing about the name, and said it about her own file, and it is the reason nothing else happened

"**There is a woman's name on a piece of paper in this building as of about half past eleven this morning, and it went in a file on the fourth floor, and it is a file about a request for a document and not about her, and it will be there for the retention period, and in about four years somebody can ask this Museum whether it holds a record of a member of the Lantern Society, and it will be able to say that it does.**"

"**Is that a problem.**"

"**It is the only instrument in this case that has ever caught anything, and it has caught her, and I am going to be careful here: **it caught her doing something lawful.**" Mara Iles closed the file. "**There is no forum in this city in which a person may say that a landlord has taken the ability to be recognised as the neighbour of an address. That was said by a conciliation officer of nineteen years on the eighteenth of March and it is true today. There is no second forum for a person to say that a collector has decided to buy only where nobody can be found, and there will not be one, and nobody has refused one, and none has ever been designed.**"

"**So it just sits there.**"

"**It sits there with her name on it. And I am going to say the last sentence of this and then I am going to go and do something else. **A name on a document is the only thing in this city that makes anybody answerable, and I have just used one, for the first time in twenty-four weeks, on a woman who has done nothing wrong, because she wrote down her own rule and sent it to a museum, and if that is the only use there is going to be of it in this case then the instrument is real and it cuts both ways and I have not slept well and I am not going to pretend to have.**"
