# Chapter 0095 — Nine Flats, And Nothing That Says What They Were

On Thursday the twentieth of May 2025 a room above a shop in Low Ward had four professionals in it and about nine people who live on one street in it, and it was the first time in twenty-three weeks that anybody had asked anybody a question in that room that anybody in the room was not there to answer.

**The question was: what was there.**

---

## And the sentence that opened the afternoon was said by a records officer of about fifty, and it took her about nine seconds, and she said the cost out loud before she said the sentence

She had come on her own time again and she had refused nothing this time because nobody offered her anything.

"**I am going to say the useful thing first and then I am going to say what it costs, and the order matters and I have thought about it since Friday.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**A list of about nine addresses and the years that people were living in them is not a list of people. It is a list of houses. That is the whole of the difference and it is the only difference and it is worth more than everything else anybody has put on a table in twenty-three weeks, and it is the first thing this case has ever been able to offer a street.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**There is a retired records officer in Northglass who went round the country in about 1991 telling local authorities that a register of people who had asked for their names to be removed from a list was a register of people, and that this was so.**" She put her pen down. "**And there is a woman in a corridor in a building on a hill who has said it in eleven words and it is a registration officer's and not hers, and she is right, and I have come forty miles to say that the answer to it is not a form and never was. It is a list of houses with the years on. That is what fills the gap. Not a person. The address and the dates.**"

---

## And then she said what it costs, and nobody volunteered, and the man with the notebook did not put his hand up and did not offer

"**It costs somebody their post.**"

"**Say that again.**"

"**If I were to give you the years, they would come out of the column the district keeps for itself, and that column is not a record of anybody's tenancy, it is the district's own retained value of a field it has already overwritten, and it is carried on the front sheet of every morning's supplied-file archive, and the archive is a binder of every morning from the first of November to the nineteenth of January and after that date there is no morning and no sheet and nothing.**" She said the number of people in the room. "**It is retained because if a delivery fails and the field is empty, somebody has to know what was in the field when the item went out, and that is all it is for, and no human being has ever looked at it until I looked at it.** **If it is used for anything other than that it is used outside its purpose, and the officer who uses it outside its purpose is the officer who has to explain it, and I would be the officer, because I have been asked once in six years and both times the request came from this street.**"

"**You could say that you were asked,**" said the man of fifty-two.

"**I could say it and it would be true and it would not save me, because I would be a person who has taken a routing field and made it a census, and there is one of those in every district in this country and they are all in trouble, and I am fifty years old and I would like to keep being a records officer.**"

"**So the sentence stands and the years do not.**"

"**The sentence stands. The years do not come from me.**"

"**Then who,**" said the woman of about fifty from the sheet, **"because you have just told us what it is worth and you are the only person in this room who has ever said it and I would like that to have been worth something.**"

"**It has been worth something,**" said the records officer. "**It is worth a great deal that a person in this city with a duty has said out loud that the answer is a list of houses and not a list of people, and it will still be true in nine months when none of us are in this room, and nobody can take that off me. I have not come forty miles to give you a form.**"

---

## And then she offered something anyway, and the registrar of twenty-eight refused it, and it was the hardest anybody in this case has ever had to say

"**There is one thing in my building that would do it,**" she said. "**I am not going to be able to give you that either and I am going to offer it anyway so that it is on the table that it exists. There is a column. It is headed OCCUPANT (PRIOR). It is the district's own retained value of the field, carried on the front sheet of every morning, and there are about nine rows in it for your street, and every one of the nine has a name in it.**"

"**Nine,**" said Frank Dunning.

"**About nine. And those are the nine names of the nine households that were in those flats before the field cleared, and it is the only place anybody has ever written down who used to live at those addresses, and I have not been asked for it, and I have not been told by anybody that I may not have it, and I am telling you that it exists and I am not going to send it.**"

"**Why not,**" said the man of fifty-two, **and he was the one who asked, and he asked it in a way that made the room wait.**

Mara Iles said the cost out loud before she said the answer, in about nine seconds.

"**If it comes into this building it is in a museum file, and a museum file is a list of people in a building that keeps records, and I told that room on the third of May that that is the one thing I will not do, and I said it about a form and I am saying it about nine names and it is the same sentence. Nine names in a museum is nine names a person with a job will have to look at one day for no reason anybody can give them.**"

"**Then it is going into the council,**" said somebody.

"**It is going into the council and the council is a list of about four hundred people who can read it, and a register of removals kept by a local authority is a thing I have read about and it is a horror, and I am not going to be the person who starts one in a ward where people have already lost the ability to be recognised.**"

"**So it is in a building and it is nowhere.**"

"**It is in my own building on Marsh Lane in a binder that stops on the nineteenth of January, and after that there is nothing, and nobody has ever looked at it, and that is the correct place for it and I know that and it is the worst sentence I have said in this case.**"

The records officer nodded once and said, **"That is correct and I would like it recorded that I offered it and that it was refused, and that I am going to keep it, and that I am not offended, and that I would make the same offer in four years to somebody else if I was still in post, because the day somebody builds a form for this is the day it should be asked for and not before."**

---

## And then a letting agent of about thirty-nine said the only thing anybody said that afternoon that could be checked, and she said it in nineteen seconds, and she said it on her own time

She had a hardback book under her arm and she did not open it.

"**I am not giving you the book. I have said that seven times and I am not going to say it an eighth time in a room.**" She put her hand flat on it. "**And here is what I can do, and it has been available since the eleventh of February, and nobody in this case has ever told anybody about it, and I found out in December that my own firm had never mentioned it to a tenant. **For any household in blocks one to six, the firm will confirm in writing the date its notice was served and the date of the term end, on production of the notice, or of any part of the notice, or of a description of the notice.** That is on a statement of a programme signed by me. It is free. It takes four days. And about four of the nine addresses you are asking about are in blocks one to six.**"

"**And the other five.**"

"**The other five are not in a block, or the block has gone, or there was no notice, and if there was no notice then there is nothing for me to confirm and I am not going to invent a date to fill a gap in your street.**" She shut the book. "**And I have thought about this all morning on my own time and I want to say the thing nobody is going to like. **A notice is a document that says a landlord has decided something about a house. It is the one piece of paper any of you have that says you were there, and it went to the house, and the house stopped being anybody's, and the notice went with it.** That is not a system failure. That is a letter in a box.**"

Nobody said anything for about eleven seconds.

"**So the ones with a notice we can have,**" said the man of fifty-two.

"**The ones with a notice you can have a date out of. The ones without a notice, the house went quiet and nobody can put a year on it, and I have four and a half years of visits in that book and I am not opening it.**"

---

## And so the list was made, in about fifty minutes, out of nine drawers, and it is a list of houses and it is the first true thing anybody has put on that street

**Every household on that street has had a card from a public museum through the door every Christmas since about 1954. It is a printed card about an exhibition. It has a year on it, and it has the address on it, and it has never once had a name on it, and about nine households still had theirs in a drawer, and nobody had ever compared two of them, and nobody's job is to compare two of them.**

They went and got them. About nine of them, in about fifty minutes, out of nine drawers in nine kitchens and one in a coat, and there were more than nine because three households had kept every one since the nineteen-eighties and had put them in a biscuit tin.

**The letting agent was still in the room and was told, in about eleven seconds, by a man of thirty-two, and she did not argue, and she said: then I am wrong about one half of that and right about the other half, and the half I am wrong about is the half that costs nothing, and the half I am right about is that a card in a tin is not a notice, and a notice is the only thing that can stop a tenancy, and I still am not opening the book, and it will not stop anything in October either.**

**The list is on the back of a piece of A4. Two columns. On the left, the address. On the right, the years, said out loud by the person who kept the card, and written down by the man of fifty-two, and he wrote them down in his own hand and nobody read them back.**

There is no third column. There is no column for a name. **Nobody put a name on that piece of paper and nobody has ever suggested doing so, and when the man of thirty-two said the word *name* out loud at about ten to five, the woman of about fifty-four said "no" in about four seconds and did not give a reason and did not have to.**

Two people stopped while it was being made. One of them said she had kept one card and thrown the rest away and could not remember which years and did not want to guess at them, and one of them said nothing at all and was not asked, and there is a gap in the list where a year should be, and the gap is in the list, and nobody filled it in.

---

## And a man of fifty-two said the whole of his argument out loud, in the same room, on the same afternoon, and did not soften it, and the room did not argue with him

"**I said no to this on the twenty-fourth of April and I am going to say it again now that it exists, and I am not going to be polite about it.**" He did not touch the paper. "**A list of houses is worse than a list of people. I have been on this street twenty-six years. About nine flats on the east side of this road have been empty since December and before December there were people in all of them, and the only trace of any of them is a field that got empty and a door that did not open, and that is true, and it is still true with your cards in a biscuit tin, because what you have made is not a record of anybody. It is a record that some rooms were warm.**"

"**And it is the first thing anybody has given us,**" said the woman of about fifty from the sheet.

"**Yes. And those two things are both true and I have been trying since January to get rid of one of them and I cannot, and I have been wrong once this afternoon and it is not the thing I expected.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**Write it down as it is,**" he said. "**A list of houses, with the years, made by the people who kept the cards, in their own hands, with two gaps in it, and a records officer in the room saying it should not exist, and a letting agent saying four of the nine can be confirmed on paper and five cannot. That is what we have got. I would like somebody to write down that the man who said no to it is the man who has read it out twice.**"

---

## And then a man of thirty-two wrote the years down in a notebook he has not shown anybody, in a room with about nine people in it, and he knows exactly what he is doing

He did it at about ten past six, on a table, in about four minutes, and it was the tenth notebook, and it cost forty pence, and there is a rule at the top of the first page in his own hand in three lines and the third line is **Do not write it down.**

He wrote the addresses down. He wrote the years down. He did not write any names down and there were no names to write down, and he noticed himself not writing them and did not enjoy the noticing.

**He has said since that the rule at the top of that page is about facts a person gives him in a street, and that a list of years read out of nine drawers in a room above a shop is nine facts a person gave him in a room, and that he wrote them down anyway, and that the only reason he did it is that he has been in this case for twenty-three weeks looking for a record and there was one in the room and it was going to go home in somebody's coat.**
