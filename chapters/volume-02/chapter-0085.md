# Chapter 0085 — The First Of April

At about ten o'clock on the morning of Thursday the first of April 2025 a woman of about thirty-nine with a hardback book under her arm knocked on about forty doors on a street two hundred yards from the front door of the Museum of Quiet Saints, and she gave a piece of paper to somebody at each one of them, and the name of the person who handed it over was on it, and it was served in advance, on the earliest date permitted, on paper, by hand, at the property, lawfully.

**A man of thirty-two was about a mile away. He did not come. He was not telephoned. Nobody in the day is him and he is not in it, and he has been the company of this business for sixteen weeks and four days, and the reason he is not in the street is the whole of it, which is that the most important thing that has happened to this ward in three months happened on a street, at about forty doors, in about three hours, and nobody in this city found out about it except a man with a book and a woman with a satchel, and those two had never spoken to each other and had never heard of each other.**

---

## Ten o'clock, and the woman with the book

She had started at the top of the street and she worked down. She did not knock and run. She knocked and waited, and when the door opened she said her name and the name of her firm and the name of the property, and she said the word *notice* before she said anything else, so that nobody was standing in a doorway being surprised.

> **MORTON HAY & BAKER (LETTING & MANAGEMENT) LTD, 11 Pemberton Row, Low Ward.**
> **NOTICE — the property at [house number] — [reference]**
> **This notice is served by hand at the property on the first day of the current quarter. The date at the foot of it is the date. If you have been served in person the agent will stand on this step and confirm it. If you have not been served in person, ring the number on this notice and say so.**
> **S. BOULTON.**

"**I am not going to stand here and read it to you,**" she said, at the first door, to about nine people who had come out because a woman was knocking on a street at ten in the morning. "**It is one page and it is in your hand and it is dated, and the date at the foot of it is the date, and I will stand on this step on the day and confirm it, and if anybody tells you I have said a date I have not, then ring me.**"

"**What is it for,**" said the first man.

"**It is a notice that your tenancy will not be renewed and that it will end on the date at the bottom. It is served at the earliest date permitted by the Act and my client serves on the earliest date permitted, always, and that is not a favour, it is a practice.**"

"And you're not the landlord."

"**I am not the landlord. I am the agent. The landlord's name is on the notice and it is the only name on it that matters and it is a company in an office, and I manage about four hundred and twelve tenancies for them, and I have done for eleven years, and I am on this street this morning because today is the first day of the quarter and this is block seven.**"

---

**She wrote in the book at every door. That is the thing nobody in this case has ever been able to get off her, and she did it in the road, on a wall, with the book open on her forearm, and she wrote the date and the time and the number of the house, and there are about forty of them in it and there have been about four thousand since 2019, and it is a hardback and it is in her hand and it is the only register in this case.**

Somebody asked her about it at about half past eleven, and she said what she says about the rent roll, in the same words, and the second time she has said it to anybody in this case.

"**No. Not to a museum, not to a council, not to a journalist, and not to a woman with a satchel.**"

"**It is only dates.**"

"**It is dates and about four thousand visits and my handwriting, and that is a record of a person doing her job, and the second it is in somebody else's building it is a list of about four hundred households and a person did it on purpose, and I have refused seven things since the autumn and you would be the seventh and a half, and I am not going to explain it eight times in four months to different people about different documents.**"

She said it kindly. That is the part that is hard to write down.

## The street, and what this building has done about it for seventy-one years

**About forty households. The east side runs to the water and the west side is terraces, and the numbers do not run in order because a street like this grew around something other than a plan. There are eleven letter boxes on it and about nine flats on the east side and about forty doors in all that anybody would call households, and the street is two hundred yards long.**

This Museum has printed a card for every one of those households every year since about 1954. It has a drawer in a store behind a front counter with a card on it that says THRESHOLD GROUP and 1954 on it, and the reason those cards exist is that somebody found them in a box and asked what they were for. The last complete print of the four hundred and twelve is a print run of the second of December in brown paper bands in a box behind that counter.

**Nobody in this building has ever been down that street. There is no minute, no request, no item-six direction, no doorway on it in any file in this building, and there are about forty households on it and they have had a card off this Museum annually for seventy-one years and not one of them has ever heard of it, and on the first of April 2025 thirty-nine of them were handed a different piece of paper by a woman with a book, and the fortieth was not, and nobody in this city can say which is which, and the Museum's own block would not tell them either, because the block is a filter and the filter is a postal round number and a district.**

## About two o'clock, and the woman with the satchel, who was there for her own reasons and had not heard of any of it

**Mairin Devlin had been on that street since about ten past two and she was there for one household and not for the street.** She had a satchel with eleven forms in it and a pad of nine houses on it that is not a list and has never been a list, and she was going to a door at about the ninth house in because a woman had telephoned the office on Friday about a bin and had given that address, and the office had allocated the visit on a Monday because Mondays are what they do.

She knocked at about a quarter past two. The door was opened by a woman of about fifty-eight who had been in the house for nine years and who had been handed a notice at about twenty past ten that morning.

"**Are you from the council?**"

"**I'm the electoral registration service. I'm not from the council, there's a difference and it matters and I'll explain it if you want.**"

"**I had a woman here this morning.**"

"**Did you.**"

"**Yes. With a book. Very nice. Knocked, said it straight out.**" The woman had the notice on the hall table and had not put it in a drawer. "**She said it was the first of April and it wasn't a trick and I should ring the number if anybody said it was.**"

Mairin Devlin stood on a doorstep and did the thing she does for a living, which is to make sure that what a person has told her is a fact about a document and not a feeling, and it took her about nine minutes, and it is the only time in this case that two systems that have never met each other have been in the same conversation.

"**What did the paper say, in her words.**"

"**It said my tenancy's not being renewed and there's a date at the bottom.**"

"**Do you know the date.**"

"**No. I haven't looked at the bottom.**"

"**Look at it now and then you'll know it, and that's better than me telling you,**" said Mairin Devlin, "**because I can be wrong about a date and I have been, and a piece of paper in your own hand on your own table can't.**"

The woman looked at the bottom of it. Then she said: "**And they can't take my name off the roll over that.**"

"**No they can't. Nobody can. Being on the roll is a list of people who have told us where they live and it has got nothing to do with whose name is on whose piece of paper, and you can be on it and lose your tenancy in the same week and those are two different offices and they have never spoken.**"

"**So what happens if I go.**"

"**Then you go, and if you move inside this district you tell us and it takes ten minutes and you can do it on a web form or you can ring and ask for me by name. If you go and you don't tell us then your line comes off the roll, and then somebody who moves in behind you has the same problem you had, and that is the whole of the reason anybody would want your name off anything.**"

**And then the woman in the road with the book said good afternoon to a woman with a satchel, which is how the two of them met, and it was four minutes, and one of the four minutes was a request and three of them were a refusal.**

"You've been round this afternoon."

"I have."

"**A notice.**"

"**Yes. Block seven, the first of April, the earliest date permitted, by hand at the property, and about forty of them and I have done about thirty-eight.**"

Mairin Devlin did not ask for the book. She looked at it for about a second and a half and then asked a different question, which is what a person with a satchel does, and it was the best possible question.

"**Is it a record.**"

"**It is my book.**"

"**Right. Sorry. I do a version of that with a form about eleven times a week and I have never got a good answer and I have just realised I have never asked anybody the question in those words.**"

"**You asked it in the right words, which is more than a museum did,**" said Sonia Boulton, "**and no.**"

"**No,**" said Mairin Devlin. "**I did not think so. I asked because I have got a pad of nine houses on my own pad and there is no list of the ones I cannot get, and I have never been able to say that out loud to anybody and I have just said it out loud to a woman in a road.**"

"**Then we are the same and I have never met you,**" said Sonia Boulton, "**and I am not going to be introduced, because if I am introduced then in about four years there is a document with two professionals' names on it about one street, and a name in a field is the only thing this business has ever caught anything with, and I have spent eleven years watching a person get got.**"

---

**And a woman at a door handed the registration officer a copy of the notice and the registration officer could not take it, and the reason she gave was not the reason anybody in this case has been giving for eleven weeks.**

"**You can have that if you want it.**"

"**No. I can't take it.**"

"**It's got your address on it.**"

"**It's got your address on it and not a line of mine, and if I take it I have to write down where I got it, and then it is a document in a council file about your front door, and nobody in that office asked for it, including me.**" She put the satchel strap back on her shoulder. "**I can put on a form that a landlord's agent came to your door this morning and told you your tenancy is not being renewed. I cannot put a landlord's notice in my bag, because then it is a thing I have, and in about four years there will be a question about you and the question will be *did anybody tell her*, and the answer will be yes, and the document they will want to see is in my bag and not in a file, and then it is a thing about you that I have got and you have not.**"

"**That's a strange way to do it.**"

"**It is the correct way to do it and I have got about eleven forms and about nine years of doing it,**" said Mairin Devlin, "**and I am going to write a true sentence about your door in a box that has wanted two lines for nine years, and it is going to be about documents and not about you, and it is going to be approved, and in nine years nobody is going to know why that street has a better roll than the street next to it.**"

And she did, that afternoon, on the back of her own form, in about a minute and a half, and it said: **occupancy change advised by freeholder's agent, quarterly cycle date, resident reports notice served in person 1 April 2025, no local record held.** Four things, three of them about documents and the fourth about a document not existing, and not one word about a person, and it is the second true sentence she has ever been able to put in that box and she did not want a third.

## And in Ferry Bank, on the same afternoon, a woman of seventy-eight was asked for the eighth time and there was nothing to send back

The letter went on the twenty-fourth of March, one page, four lines, by post, to **12 Ferry Bank**, and it said that a registrar had read four lines aloud on a step in Sillick Lane on the twenty-second of March, that no record of the practice at that address is held by this Museum and none will be, that nothing had been written down and nothing would be, and that if she wished to attend anything connected with it she was welcome to and there was no need to reply.

**She did not reply. There was nothing to reply to. The letter said no reply was required, and a thing with nothing in it is not a refusal, and it is not going to be recorded as one.**

Her seven refusals are on a board on the ground floor of a building on a hill and not in a file, because there are seven of them and a list of people who have refused something is a list of people. The seventh came back by post on Friday the twenty-ninth of January in four words — **No. Ask somebody else** — and it is still the seventh.

**On the first of April 2025 that board said seven, and it was correct, and there have been eight requests and seven refusals, and the difference between those two numbers is a woman of seventy-eight who did not answer a letter that did not require one, and no instrument in this city can put that on paper, and the board is not out of date, and in about four years somebody is going to read that board and conclude that she said no seven times and said nothing the eighth, which is not what happened, and there is no form for the difference.**
