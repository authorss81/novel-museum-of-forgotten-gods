# Chapter 0068 — The Lawful Door

A form is the only door in this city that has ever opened anything.

That is not a figure of speech and it is not a theory, it is eight weeks of this case, and it can be laid out in a room in about four minutes, and it was laid out in a room on the morning of Wednesday the twenty-seventh of January 2025 by a systems technician of twenty-five, and the room was a committee room on the fourth floor, and there were five people in it, and one of them was a Director and one of them was a registrar and three of them were a sub-group, and the item on the agenda was *any other matter the chair directs*, and it was item six, and it has been in the terms of reference since the eighteenth of October doing nothing at all.

**And the five of them went through every instrument this city has, in order, and found that the only one of them that reaches a household is a sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet on a table in the hall at 88 Sillick Lane, and that it has no form under it, and that it is not a record, and that it is the only thing anybody has built in eight weeks that anybody has ever stood a person in front of.**

| The door | Who opens it | What it reaches | What it costs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A Director's standing condition | a Director | one object in one room | condition two has no subject and has failed twice |
| A goods-out docket | a pallet and a count | a loading bay | a door mat does not go on a pallet |
| A movement docket | a person who is not an officer or employee | one object | the owner's name is the thing that has gone |
| A Registry classification | a rota on a wall in Northglass | one object | no classification for activity outside a museum context, no emergency provision, first slot 6 February |
| **Four hundred Museum forms** | **anybody with the nerve** | **this building** | **not one of them reaches a house** |
| **A sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet** | **nobody** | **about fourteen doors and about nine people, in person, at a door** | **it is not a form and it is not evidence and it dies with whoever keeps it** |

"That is the whole of it," said Ivo Pell, at about half past ten, "and I have been the man who receives the four hundred for four years and I can tell you that about six of them reach a kitchen table in an office and none of them reaches a door, and **we are a building whose entire competence is knowing things and we have never once in a hundred and forty years put a piece of paper through a letterbox in Sillick Lane, and there is a woman in this city who has done it about nine times in three weeks with a sheet of A4 and a plastic wallet.**"

"So the recommendation," said the sub-group member of sixty-four, "is that the Museum adopts a practice it has no instrument for, on the strength of a bit of paper in a hall."

"**The recommendation is that the Museum asks her for it.**"

---

**And the item went to a board, because items go to a board, and that is what the board is for, and nobody in that room thought about it, and the front desk of a public museum has a board and nineteen years of a woman at it who has never once been thanked for a procedure.**

Margaret Priest wrote it up on a card at about a quarter to eleven on the Wednesday morning in the words the committee had agreed, which were: *Item 6. For discussion: whether this Museum will receive and retain a record of a local practice in Sillick Lane. No decision taken.*

**And Frank Dunning came in on the Wednesday at about eleven, because Frank Dunning has come in on a Wednesday every week since the middle of December, and there is nothing for him on a Wednesday, and he reads the board because it is a board and he is a man of about sixty-five with time.**

He read it. He read it twice. And then he did not go and ask at the desk, which he would have done, because he has asked at that desk before and got the right answer, and instead he stood in a lobby and worked out, in about nine seconds, that five people had sat round a table on the fourth floor of a building he has never worked in and had discussed whether the thing on his table in his hall was going to be kept.

**And then he asked, and the woman at the counter answered him, and the answer is on the page because she gave it to him straight.**

"They've had a discussion. I don't know what was said. I know what's on the card and I know the word on it is *record* and I know there's a plastic wallet in it somewhere, because that's the only thing anybody's been talking about in this city for three weeks." Margaret Priest did not look at the card. "**I'd say no. And I'd say you'd say no, and you'd be right, and if I were a woman of fifty-four I'd say it before anybody asked me.**"

"You've not asked her."

"**No. And I'm not going to, and that's the fourth time in nineteen years I've not asked somebody something in this building and it's the only one of the four I'm proud of.**"

---

**And the woman of fifty-four was asked, and she was asked properly, by the Director, on the telephone, at about half past one on the Wednesday afternoon, and it took four minutes, and the reason she gave is the reason the whole of this business is a business and not a campaign, and she gave it before she was asked the question.**

"Before I ask you," said Anja Ward, "I am going to say what the Museum is thinking of asking you, so that you can refuse the actual thing and not a version of it. We are thinking of asking you for a copy of the sheet on the table in your hall, and of asking you to let us keep it, and of putting it in a file here with a date on it."

"Right."

"And I am going to say the cost of it before I ask, because that is what I do, and because you are owed the cost before the question. **If you give us that sheet, it becomes a record in a Museum file in this city, and a record in a Museum file is a document with names on it, and there are about nine names on it that I have been told about and at least one of them is a woman of eighty-one who was taken to a clinic on the Monday because of what she did on your street.** And once it is here it is not yours and not mine, and in four years somebody in this building will use it to say something about a person, not because anybody is wicked but because that is what documents are for and I have signed four hundred of them myself."

"Are you asking me to say no."

"**I am asking you to be asked properly, and I would be grateful if you would let a woman of twenty-eight put the actual question to you afterwards, because she is the person who has the standing to hold the thing if you say yes and she is also the person who has spent eight weeks refusing to do it, and I would like her on the telephone.**"

---

**And Marion Kelsh was not asked, and neither was Vera Pilling, and neither was Cliff Sowerby, and neither was anybody else, and that is on the page because five people discussed a list of names on the fourth floor of a building on the morning of Wednesday the twenty-seventh of January 2025 and not one person on the list was spoken to, and the reason there is a list is that somebody in January wrote nine names down in a corridor, and nobody has ever asked how those nine got into a corridor.**

Hattie Bramm said no in about eleven seconds and then gave the reason and the reason had been hers since the sixteenth of December, and she gave it as though she were handing back a tool.

"**No. And you've asked me the right way, which I want said, because the last time anybody in an office asked me for something it was a woman of thirty-four in a corridor in November who wanted my hundred and forty and gave me the reason before she asked, and that is the only reason I am talking to you at all.**

"Here's the thing and then I'll go and do my Friday. **A list of people is not evidence until it is in a file, and the second it is in a file it can be used.** That's not mine. A field officer said a version of it in a room in November and I took it and I've been carrying it. And I have got a hundred and forty names in a drawer in number fourteen that I have not given to anybody and am not going to, and I have said no about them three times, and I said no in a museum in December to about twenty people and I said it again in a corridor and I am saying it now to a Director on a telephone.

"And it is not about you. You would use it well. That's the whole of the reason it doesn't matter whether you'd use it well, and it took me nine years to be able to say that sentence and I have not said it out loud before today.

"**Because the day a Museum has got that sheet, it is a museum that knows who lives on that street.** And you cannot un-know it. And there'd be a day — not this year, some Tuesday — when somebody in that building wanted to know whether a person at number eighty-eight was entitled to something, and there'd be a piece of paper on a table with his name on it and a date and a hand it was written in, and the person who went to that piece of paper would be a person I'd never met doing a correct thing inside a department. And I would have handed it over on a Wednesday in January because you were decent about it."

---

**And the decision was taken at about ten past four on the Wednesday afternoon of the twenty-seventh of January 2025 and the Director said the cost out loud before she took it, in a room, and it was minuted in that order, and the minute is four lines long and it is the shortest instrument this case has produced.**

"**The Museum will not receive, retain, request or file any record of the practice in Sillick Lane.** And I am saying the cost of that first, and the cost is this.

"The cost is that we are the only institution in this city that can put a name on a card, and that a woman of about fifty-four and a man of about sixty-five have built something in a street that works, and that on the day anybody asks them what it is they will have to say *a museum, once, on a Wednesday, decided not to have it*, and that is a worse answer than *a museum had it*. **We are going to give away the only thing we have that they do not have, because holding it would make us dangerous to them, and I would like it recorded that I know exactly how that sounds.**

"And the second cost is that the practice is now, in law, nothing. It has no form, no file, no register and no minute of its own, and it dies on the day the person keeping it stops keeping it, and that is the correct outcome and it is the outcome we are choosing, and **we are choosing it for them, which is a piece of arrogance I would like in the minute, and I would like the sub-group to strike it out if it thinks it is worse than what it replaces.**"

Nobody struck it out.

"And the third thing, which is the actual decision, and it is not nothing." She put a sheet on the table. "**The Museum will keep the fact that it saw it. A person of this Museum was present on a pavement in Sillick Lane on the fourth, the eighth and the eleventh days of January 2025 and observed a person stand on a mat and say a name aloud at a door, and a second named officer of this Museum records that fact from a photograph of a card at knee height and from a report made to her by a third person, and has not been to that street in her life. The Museum does not know what the practice is. The Museum does not intend to find out. The Museum holds no record of its content, its participants, or its addresses, and will not accept one.**

"That is not a form. Nobody can use it. **It is a record with two names on it, and the only thing it can be used for is to be asked whether we saw it, and that is a thing we can be asked about in a hearing, and I would rather be asked about it than not have been asked at all.**"

> **MINUTE, ITEM 6, 30 JANUARY 2025. Director's decision.**
> **1. That the Museum will not receive, retain, request or file any record of the practice at Sillick Lane, at the request of the Museum or otherwise. The requester has declined in advance, in terms, and the Museum accepts that.**
> **2. That the Museum records the fact of its own observation only: one officer present on three dates, a second officer who records it from a photograph and from a report, and the fact of a person speaking a name aloud at a door. No content, no addresses, no participants.**
> **3. That the Museum has no instrument for the practice, has not sought one, will not seek one, and has no duty in respect of it.**
> **4. That this Museum, having so decided, is a party to the matter and not a bystander, and will answer for that in due course.**
> **A. WARD, Director. Cost stated before the decision. The day of the week is minuted because a condition that counts days has to hang off a date somebody can check.**

**And the two names are the Director's and the Registrar's, and the Registrar insisted on being named even though **she has not been to Sillick Lane in her life**, and gave the reason in about nine seconds and it is on the page.**

"I have not been to Sillick Lane in my life," said Mara Iles. "**I have been sent a photograph of a card at knee height and I have cut four hundred cards and I have said no to five institutions about a sheet of A4 in a plastic wallet, and if this Museum is going to put two names on a record then it is going to put mine on it and somebody in March is going to ask me what I observed and I am going to have to say *a photograph of a card and a mat on a floor and a woman of eighty-one in a clinic*, and that is the answer and it is the truth and it is not much.

"And the reason I have put my name to it is not to be a witness. **A record with one name on it is a record that dies with that person. A record with two names on it survives one of us.** And I am the one who is more likely to be here in four years than a Director is, and that is not modesty, it is the staffing of this building."**"

---

**And the seventh refusal came back by post on Friday the twenty-ninth and it came back in two days, and it is four words on a piece of A4, and nobody had to go to Ferry Bank, and it is the whole of Doreen Sarn in twenty-four days.**

> **No. Ask somebody else.**

**And it was not put in a file, and that was a decision, and it was made in about four seconds, and the reason was given out loud in a corridor to a sub-group member of sixty-four who had suggested a file, and the reason is the reason of the whole of this case.**

"I am not filing that," said Mara Iles. "**There are six of them and they are not all refusals and they are not all hers. There is one on a bit of paper in a safe in Irving Quay that a man in his thirties did not sign, and one on the back of a leaving card, and one in a cash book in a front room in Ferry Bank, and one in a red disc on an object in a cage, and one in a minute of mine. Six pieces of paper in five buildings about six people who would not have a thing of theirs put in a file. That is a list of people.**

"**A list of people who have refused something is a list of people.** And if I file the seventh then in four years there is a piece of paper in this building about six people, in one place, none of whom consented to being in a list, and **the whole of the argument of this case is that a list is not a list of people, it is a set of hooks, and the second it is in a file it is a set of hooks that this building owns.**" She put the sheet of A4 down on the ledge. "It goes on the board. It is a message. **The board is the one thing in this building that is not a file and is not kept and is not about us.**"

---

**And the man who had been the reason for all of it stood in a corridor on the fourth floor of a building he is barred from at about half past four on the afternoon of the twenty-seventh of January 2025, on a printed card with the word WITNESS in a box, in company, not before eight in the morning, and he read a minute of four lines on a table and did not say anything for about a minute and a half, and then said the thing that is the last line of the afternoon and it is not a complaint.**

"You have just given away the only instrument in this case."

"**Yes.**"

"Right."

"**Yes, and I would like it minuted that you did not try to talk me out of it and I would like it minuted that you said *right*, which is the first time anybody has said *right* to me in this building in eight weeks without then telling me what I am supposed to do about it.**"

"I have been told what I'm supposed to do about it," said Elias Venn. "Somebody told me on a road in Sillick Lane on Monday morning. He said *ask him again in two days and if it's gone up, say the number out loud on a pavement in daylight, and then you've got a record with a name on it that nobody can be got at over.* **And the minute you've just signed has two names on it, and one of them is yours, and it is the only document in this entire case with a person's name on it that was put there on purpose and did not cost a living person anything.**"

He read the minute again.

"**And the sheet on the table in Sillick Lane is now nothing, and it is going to be nothing on the day Frank Dunning stops keeping it, and he is about sixty-five, and nobody has asked him how he is and nobody is going to, and the whole of that practice is about nine people on a pavement and a plastic wallet and a woman of eighty-one who is not allowed back on that street until the middle of April.**

"**And there is no register. There is going to be no register. I have been in this case for eight weeks learning that the only thing that works is a record with a name against it, and the only register in this city is going to be a piece of A4 on a table in a hall in Low Ward kept by a man who is not frightened and is not reassured and is not distressed because nobody has ever asked him a question about it, and when he stops, it stops.**"

"Yes," said Anja Ward.

"**And I know that is right. I have known it since Monday morning on a road and I wrote it down that night and I did not tear it out, and I am not going to be the man who asks him to keep it, because the second I ask, it is a thing I am keeping and I am in a coat in a rented room in Low Ward with no employer and a pound and nine pages, and Hattie Bramm said it to me in a library in December and said it better than I am ever going to say it.**"
