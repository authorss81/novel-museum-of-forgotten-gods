# Chapter 0070 — The Same Date, in Two Systems, Again

The fourth column on the front sheet of the district's file was headed **OCCUPANT (PRIOR)** and it had been under a bulldog clip on a table in a systems office on the fourth floor of the Museum of Quiet Saints since about a quarter past eleven on the morning of Tuesday the nineteenth of January 2025, and it was read on the morning of Wednesday the third of February 2025 at about ten past nine by a systems technician of twenty-five who had known about it for a fortnight and had told a man of thirty-two about it in a notebook on the nineteenth and had not read it.

It is a small column, about two inches wide, and it is empty on the sheet of the first of November and it is not empty from the sheet of the sixth of December onward, **because a column that carries a field's previous value carries it on every morning after the change, not only on the morning of it.** That is the design. That is a courtesy to the post that has been running four years and nobody has ever read it.

**And the nine rows it is populated in are nine rows of names, and on every morning since the sixth of December, or the eleventh, or the twelfth — three different mornings, and that is the point — those nine names have come down the wire into this building at a quarter past four, and gone into a block that keeps no history, and been gone by breakfast. Nineteen mornings of it. The Museum threw away the same nine names nineteen times without knowing it had ever received them.**

"That is the whole of it," said Ivo Pell, at about ten past nine on the morning of Wednesday the third of February, to a registrar and a systems index and nobody else. "**The district keeps the old value. We don't. Our block is not versioned, and every morning at a quarter past four the prior column arrives and the current column arrives and the current column goes into the block and the prior column goes into a drawer in a building four miles away with the rest of a binder, and nobody has ever read the drawer.**"

"And it is a routing column," said Mara Iles. She had her reading glasses on, which she does when she is reading something she intends to be quoted. "**It is not a history. It is there so that a letter going to a house that has just changed hands does not go to the wrong person. It is a courtesy to the post. It is a *have you tried the old address* field.** And it is in that column, on a document, in a system, in a building, for nine rows, and it is the only place in this city where the nine names that this Museum printed as blanks in December exist in a machine."

"Read me the first one."

---

**They did it in the order the binder is in, which is date order, and the binder stops at the nineteenth of January because the records officer on Marsh Lane keeps it on her own floor from the twentieth onwards and has told them so, and there was a line in her letter about it, on the fourth page, and it is not repeated here.**

The first row in date order is 88 Sillick Lane, effective the fifth of December, and the prior column says:

> **88 SILLICK LN — OCCUPANT (PRIOR): `DUNNING, F.` — OCCUPANT (CURRENT): *(blank)* — EFF 05/12 — DS-NCO-11190 — APPLIED 06/12 04:11.**

"Nothing there," said Ivo Pell. "**That's Frank Dunning. That's the name on the card in the plastic wallet. That's the one we've had since the twenty-fourth of December and it's in a district file as a prior value and that's the only bit of good news I've had in a fortnight.**"

The second is 84 and the third is 90, and both of them were effective on the tenth and applied on the eleventh, and the fourth is 80, and **the fourth one stopped the room, and it stopped it because of the date on it and not the name.**

> **80 SILLICK LN — OCCUPANT (PRIOR): `NAYLOR, W.` — OCCUPANT (CURRENT): *(blank)* — EFF 11/12 — DS-NCO-11197 — APPLIED 12/12 04:11.**

"**That's the eleventh of December,**" said Ivo Pell. "**And a man came into that flat in April.**"

Nobody said anything at all for a long moment.

"Naylor," said Ivo Pell.

"Don't," said Mara Iles.

"I'm not doing anything. I'm reading a name off a piece of paper in a system that I have been authorised to read by a records officer who has never been asked for anything in six years. **That's W. Naylor of 80 Sillick Lane and that is the first time that name has been in this building and it is on a document that a subscriber is entitled to hold.**"

"**Then say out loud what it is, because I have not said it and I have thought about it for about as long as it takes a lift and I need to hear somebody else say it.**"

---

**Here is what it is, and it is four sentences and none of them are a discovery about a conspiracy and all of them are a fact about an afternoon in April.**

A man came into the flat at 80 Sillick Lane in April 2024. **A tenancy does not notify an addressing service.** Nobody has ever thought about why not, in this city, in any decade, by anybody, and it is not a rule anybody wrote — it is an absence, and an absence has no author, and that is the entire reason this case exists.

The name in the occupancy field at that address from April 2024 to the twelfth of December 2024 was therefore the name of the man who had that flat before him, and the twelfth is the morning this Museum printed nine blanks off the same block and put them in nine envelopes. That man died in about 2019. **For about eight months the field at 80 Sillick Lane was correct about a dead man and the address was occupied by a living one, and the post came, and the postman knocked, because a handheld will not hand a postman an item it cannot account for and the field it was checking was full of a dead man's name and therefore looked fine.**

And on the fourth of January 2025 a woman of eighty-one stood on the step of number 80 and said the name that was in the field, out loud, to whoever came to the door, and a man came to the door, and he said: *Who?*

And she worked out the correction herself, standing on a step, in about four seconds:

> *you can't just say a name, you've got to knock first, and then you say the name, and if a body's there you say the name to them, and if there's no body you say it to nobody and you write it down and you go home.*

**And the name she said — a name she had known for about forty years, in a street of about fourteen doors, said out loud in January — has not been written down in any document anywhere in this city, in fifty years, until the morning of the fourth of February 2025, and it is on a line in a binder on the fourth floor of a museum, in a column headed OCCUPANT (PRIOR), in a house about four miles from where she said it.**

---

**And the arithmetic was done at about half past ten on the morning of Wednesday the third of February by a man of twenty-five standing up, with a mug, the way he has done it twice, and it is on the page because he was right, and because he is going to be half right, and he says so himself before anybody else can say it for him.**

"Three documents," he said. "That's what we've got. I've written them out. That's all the case has ever had and it is three documents and two buildings and four miles, and the fifth of December is the one everybody's been standing on since the eleventh of January, and it is the wrong one."

"**Say why it's the wrong one and don't be pleased with yourself about it.**"

"Right." He put the mug down. "**Document one is the delivery office change log. That's the fifth of December, sixteen twenty-two. That's a body pressing a key on a handheld on a Saturday afternoon. That's the only one of the three with a person in it.**

"**Document two is the district's inbound change schedule. Same effective date — fifth of December, for this line. And for three more lines the tenth, and for one the eleventh, and for five of the nine nothing at all. But it's a downstream echo. It's the district writing down what it was given. There is a body in it, in the source prefix, four characters long, and no face.**

"**Document three is the file as issued to this Museum on the morning of the sixth of December at eleven minutes past four. And that is the one the Museum has been living inside since the sixth of December and did not know it had.**

"And here's the finding and it's arithmetic and it took me the time to put a mug down, and I'd like everybody in this room to look at it before I say it.

"**The fifth is in two systems. The sixth is in three.** For this line. The sixth is on the change schedule as the applied hour. The sixth is on the front of the file as the issue time. And the sixth is in the delivery system too — not as a keying, as a **nightly file refresh**, twenty to four in the morning, which is a machine clearing a field because a code went in the afternoon before, and that line is in a woman's handwriting on the back of a counter receipt in a plastic wallet in a rented room in Low Ward and it is not a copy and it is not in any file and it is the only reason anybody in this building knows the fifth is the fifth.

"**So the sixth of December is the only one of the three dates in this case that appears in a document this Museum has ever held — twice, in its own subscription, on paper, in a binder that is sitting on a table on the fourth floor of this building.**

"**And now the half of it that I have not said out loud, and I am saying it out loud, because if I do not say it somebody in March will say it and they will not be as careful. The fifth of December is the first of the nine. It is not the run. It is the first one, at twenty-two minutes past four in the afternoon, on a Saturday, by a body. Eight of the nine happened on the tenth, the eleventh, the twelfth, and on mornings nobody in this city can now name. The fifth of December is a fact about a person, and it is in this city in one piece of physical paper, on the back of a receipt, in a woman's handwriting, given out loud in a room with a printer in it on the sixteenth of December to a stranger in a coat, because a woman of about twenty-seven looked at a man in a coat and decided that a man with no job in this city was not going to be told *nothing to do* for a fourth time.**"

**Elias Venn was not in that room. He was in a rented room in Low Ward with a pound and nine pages and a plastic wallet, and he was not told any of it until about two in the afternoon, in a corridor, by a systems technician, in about four minutes, and he has written that down in his own hand as the eleventh time in nine weeks that a man of twenty-five has come down a corridor and told him something in a building he is not allowed to enter.**

And the thing that he did, and it is the whole of the day from his side of it: he sat on the end of his own bed at about a quarter past three in the afternoon on the third of February 2025 and took the plastic wallet out and looked at the four things in it, and the first of them was a printed card with **DUNNING, F. — 88 SILLICK LANE, LOW WARD** on it that had come back marked undeliverable, and the second was a bank statement belonging to Frank Dunning, and the third was a receipt, and the fourth was the thing.

The fourth was a counter receipt from the delivery office on Nether Kiln Lane, about four inches by two, and it is not a Museum document and it is not evidence and it has a woman's handwriting on the back of it in biro.

> **05/12 16:22 · 88 SILLICK LN · NAME · (populated) → (cleared) · NCO · OP-2201**
> **06/12 03:41 — nightly file refresh, not a keying. Field cleared where NCO set in previous 24h. DS-NCO-11190.**

**That is the whole of the only document in this city that contains the fifth of December, and it is in a plastic wallet on a bed in a rented room above a laundrette, and it was not given to him by an institution and it was not requested and it is not in a file, and no institution in this city has ever seen it, and he is barred from the building it is about, and the woman who wrote it has said in a room with a printer in it that a person who asks for a thing like that ends up holding a weapon with no name on it.**

She did not photograph anything. Ivo Pell's condition of the first of December requires a countersignature at the point of any photographic exposure of collections material by a member of staff, and this is not collections material and it is not photography, and she wrote it out in ink by hand, and she initialled it, and there is no condition in this building that covers what she was about to do.

**And Fenn was asked about it, in the label room, on the Friday morning, at about ten past nine, and she is surname only and she has made about four hundred cards and she was asked by a registrar whether a person's name could go on a Museum document at all, and she said the thing she has said all winter in a different key, and it is the fourth time she has been right before anybody has asked her.**

"You can put anything you can support on a card in this building and there's four hundred of them and none of them has got a person on it," said Fenn. "There's a maker. There's a place. There's a date and there's an accession number and about a third of them have got nothing in the maker's line and I leave the line out. **Not one card in this building has got a person on it and I've been here a long time.** And you'll be right to do it, and I'll tell you the reason and then I'll go back to the cutter, and don't write it down.

"**A maker's a thing that made a thing. A person's a thing that got the thing made, and the second one has to be supported, and if it's not on a card it's a hole, and if it is on a card it's a hook.** You've supported it. There's a district file with a date and an hour on it and a registrar's initials at the bottom, and that is more support than about four hundred of the cards in my drawer have got. So put it on. It's the first one and it's a name and it's a dead man and if it isn't on a card then there's a hole in the description of a place where a man used to live, **and I've had a card with a hole in it in a public room since the thirtieth of December and I know exactly what a hole does, it lets whoever comes next fill it in.**"

**And the thing that nobody in the building was prepared for, and which happened at about half past four on the afternoon of Friday the fifth of February 2025 in a committee room on the fourth floor, is that the Director read the sheet, and then read it again, and then said the only sentence anybody in this building has said in nine weeks that is a sentence about the case and not about a document.**

"**Walter Naylor.**" She said it out loud in a room, which is what a Director does with a name. "He has been dead since about 2019. He has been in this building's records for about eight months as somebody who was responsible for the connection of services at an address, and before that he was in them for as long as this city has had an addressing service, and he has never once been asked whether he was all right, and he was fifty-eight and he paid his bill until he died and after he died for about five years and nobody noticed.

"And this Museum has just written his name on a piece of paper for the first time, and it has done so about nine weeks after a woman of eighty-one said it on a doorstep and was told *Who?*, **and the only person in this city who can say anything about him is her, and she is eighty-one, and she went to a clinic on the thirteenth of January because she stood in the cold on that street for about an hour and a quarter and she is not allowed to do it again until the middle of April.**"

She squared the sheet.

"**I want that read into the record in that order, and I want the reading to say that the reason the Registrar wrote a dead man's name on a Museum document on the fourth of February is that a woman of eighty-one said it first and could not write it down and has not been asked since, and that the reason she has not been asked is that nobody in this case has thought to ask her, and that is on all of us and not on her.**"

**And the last image of the twenty-four days this took is not a door and it is not a bell and it is not a god and it is not a name being taken out of a room by a woman with a torch off, and it is on the evening of Friday the fifth of February 2025 at about half past eleven at a table in a rented room in Low Ward, on the last page of a book with nine pages in it, and it is five lines long and it is in his own hand, and he did not tear it out, and the first of the five lines is the answer he has been carrying since the thirteenth of December and the last of them is a question, and it is a different question from the one he started with, and both of them are the same question, and he has not answered either.**
