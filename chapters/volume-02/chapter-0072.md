# Chapter 0072 — Two Answers

The Hall of Former Faiths was open from ten until five on Wednesday the tenth of February 2025 and about a hundred and twenty people went through the front door of the Museum of Quiet Saints between ten and four, and about fifty of them went into the Hall, and the mat was on the floor on its plastic sheet inside the cord line and it had been there for seventy-two days, and the card on the wire stand beside it still said *no name available* in a finisher's own hand with a line struck through above it, and about forty people walked within four feet of it and about four read it, and the panel on the front of the case at plinth thirty-four was seventy-five days old and the tape on it was the colour of weak tea and it was a requester's panel and the requester had not come for it.

**None of that is the chapter. The chapter is on the fourth floor at about eleven o'clock, in a committee room, on notice, in company, and it is four sheets of paper and a minute, and the four sheets came from Northglass on Saturday morning in a woman's hand.**

The first was four lines and it answered a letter of the twenty-second of January and it said *this schedule has no box for it*, and then it set out, at more length than the question required, that there was no object in the Museum's file, that a description is not a classification, and that the Registry has exactly one category for a place and it is for a burial and it requires a date of permanence and the assessor would not use it on a door mat.

The second was the answer to a form that had been registered and posted on the twelfth of December with eleven stamps on the acknowledgements and a period of twelve months in the box for a period, and it said the request had not begun, because a period runs from a determination and there is no determination and there cannot be one while there is no box; and that the reply had gone out of time at nine o'clock on the Saturday morning and was being issued late; and that the question was being listed, and the next determination day was **Thursday the sixth of March 2025**, and the assessor for the district was the assessor who had written it, and a listed question is decided on a stated day with a stated assessor in the room.

**And there was a third thing, which is four lines in a box at the foot of the second one, and it is the box on that form that has never been used by anybody in nineteen years, and it is headed `SUPPLEMENTARY STATEMENT BY ANOTHER PERSON IN THE MATTER`, and it contains a registrar of twenty-eight reading her own letter into it in a room in Northglass on a Saturday, signed and dated, and it is the third document in this case with a name on it that somebody put there on purpose, and the two names on it are the Registry's and the Museum's and neither of them wrote the other's.**

---

"There is nothing on that page about a door mat," said the sub-group member of sixty-four, at about ten past eleven, in a room with four people in it. "Read it again. It is four lines about a classification and a box that does not exist, and we spent a fortnight asking for it."

"We got the sentence I asked for," said Mara Iles. "**On the twenty-second of January I asked whether the answer would be *this schedule has no box for it* or whether it would be something else, so that this Museum could stop asking.** I wrote it in that order and I gave the reason in the same letter, which was that if it was the first then we would know and we could stop, and I said that would be the most useful thing anybody had done for this building in nine weeks. **It is the first. It is the first and it came on the Saturday morning and it is on paper with a date on it.**"

"And the second," said Ivo Pell. "The form. A/3. That's the one that matters."

"That is a form that has been sitting in a post room in Northglass since the twelfth of December," said Mara Iles. "**And it has not been refused and it has not been granted and it has never started, and the reason it has never started is the sentence on the first sheet, and the two sheets are the same fact twice and only one of them is about us.**"

She squared the four sheets on the table. It was about eleven o'clock and the room had the blinds half down because it was February.

---

**And then the Director said the cost out loud before she decided anything, standing up, with her hands behind her back, which is what she does, and it was minuted in that order, and it took about a minute and a half and nobody interrupted her.**

"The cost of minute one is this. **We are giving up the only external office in this city that has ever said no to us on the record, and we are giving it up voluntarily, and I am signing that with two names on it and a date on it, and in March a member of the public is going to ask me why this Museum spent nine weeks of a registrar's and a systems technician's time asking a registry a question it had already answered in a letter of the thirtieth of December.**"

"And the reason for it," said the sub-group member.

"**Because I wrote to her on the twenty-second of January and asked her whether the answer would be the thing she had already told me, and I asked her in writing, and she has now told me in writing that she had already told me, and she has been right and she has been right since the seventh of November, and there is not one more thing to get out of that office except a day and a woman in it.**"

"And minute two." She did not put her hands down. "**The second thing is that we are accepting a determination date we have no standing to reach. The sixth of March is a Thursday and there will be a woman in a room and our question on the list, and we have nothing to put in front of her, because to put something in front of her we would have to lodge a proposal, and to lodge a proposal we would have to name a thing, and there is no thing, and the assessor has told us so in writing and she is right.**"

"So the sixth of March is a date on a question that cannot be asked."

"**The sixth of March is a date on a question that cannot be asked,**" said Anja Ward, "**and we are going anyway, and I would like that minuted in those words, because in about nine weeks somebody is going to describe this case as a campaign and it is not a campaign, it is two people going to a building to be told no properly by a woman who is right.**"

> **ITEM 6 — MATTER DIRECTED BY THE CHAIR, 10 FEBRUARY 2025.**
> **Received: two documents from the Quiet Accord Registry, issued 6 Feb 2025, ref QA/CR/1426 and the A/3 file. One is the answer to this Museum's letter of 22 Jan 2025. One is the answer to a form lodged 12 Dec 2024.**
> **Determined: that this Museum will not re-propose classification of the item received on 30 Dec 2024, and will not ask the Registry that question again.**
> **Determined: that this Museum will attend the determination day of Thursday 6 March 2025 and will put no document before the assessor.**
> **Cost of both determinations stated before both were taken. A. WARD, Director.**
> **E. VENN, called as a witness under item 6. Present. No copy. Two originals retained by the Registry. Present for the fact of receipt only.**

He read the last line twice. *No copy. Two originals retained by the Registry.*

On the nineteenth of January it had said *no document taken by the witness. No copy.* On the sixth of February it said the other half of the same sentence, which is that the document itself is not here and never was. **Both of those sentences are the Registrar protecting him from becoming a man with papers, and both of them are true, and the difference between them is about four feet of distance in a corridor and he is the only person in this city who can tell the difference and he is not going to be able to explain it to anybody in March.**

---

**And then, at about half past eleven, a systems technician of twenty-five stood up with a mug and asked the question nobody had asked for nine weeks, and it is on the page because he asked it in the wrong order and got the right answer anyway.**

"What's a date of permanence."

He said it to the table, not to a person, the way he says things.

"It's a box on a form," said Mara Iles. "In a place classification. It's the field that says how long the place is going to be a place."

"Right. **So a place needs a date before it's a place.**"

"It needs a date before it can be *classified* as a place. Which is not the same and is the entire argument of this case and neither of us is going to win it at half past eleven on a Wednesday."

"**No, listen.**" He put the mug down. "I have been sitting here for twenty minutes listening to a woman in Northglass describe the only two boxes that office has got. One is a thing. One is a place, and the place one is for a burial, and it wants a date of permanence. And she would not use it on a door mat, and she was right, and she was funny, and I have been thinking about it for a day and a half and I cannot get round one thing."

"Go on."

"**A door mat has no date of permanence because you can buy another one for two pounds. That is not an argument about the mat. That is the answer to her question, and she asked it in the only terms her form has.**" He looked at the mug and then at the window. "**And a house has one. A house has got a date of permanence, because somebody put a term on it and somebody manages the term and somebody wrote a piece of paper about when the term is over. And nobody in this room has ever asked where that piece of paper is, and it is not in a museum and it is not in a registry and it is not in a file, and it is four miles from this building, and it is the only document in this country that says a person is a neighbour of a place.**"

Nobody said anything for a moment.

"And if that's the answer to the object-or-place question," said Mara Iles, "then this room has had it for nine weeks and has not noticed, because a door mat and a burial are not the same category and I am not going to sit here and pretend they are. **What she has given us is a form that wants a date. And a date is a document. And a document is what this Museum does, and I have spent nine weeks refusing to do things and I am not going to un-refuse one because a woman in Northglass was funny about a mat.**"

"That is the wrong response," said Ivo Pell, "and I'm the one who said the thing, so I'll say the wrong response out loud. **The wrong response is to go and find the date. The right response is to notice that we have been trying to get a Registry to tell us what a thing is, for nine weeks, when the thing we actually cannot find out is who a person is, and that is not a classification question and it is not in any form in this building.**"

"**It is the moral floor,**" said the sub-group member of sixty-four.

"No it isn't," said Mara Iles. "**It is a systems observation and I would like it kept as one. The floor has been said, twice, by two people who are not in this room, and I am not going to have a fourth person say it by accident in a committee room with the word *regard* in it.**"

She wrote something down and turned the page round, and it said:

> **What is not known: the freeholder of the properties in this Museum's threshold notification zone; the date of any tenancy; the person to whom a determination notice would be addressed if one existed. The Museum has maintained a list of about four hundred and twelve addresses for the purposes of local notification since about 1954. There is a printed card in a store drawer with 1954 on it and the words THRESHOLD GROUP on it. Nobody in this building has ever asked what a threshold is on a list of addresses, and nobody has ever asked who maintains it.**
> **M. ILES. 10 Feb 2025.**

---

**And it was the systems technician who answered the second half of it, standing up, in about nine seconds, and what he said is the reason the last nine weeks of this case are about to turn over.**

"It's a filter," he said. "**Our block isn't a list. Everybody in this building has been calling it a list for nine weeks and it is a filter, and I built it, and it has got about nine hundred rows in it and the bottom four hundred and twelve are selected by a postal round number and a district, and the selection criterion is geography.**"

"Meaning what, in English."

"Meaning **a household that moves to a different postal round inside the district stays on the list and a household that moves out of the district falls off, and neither of those is a decision about a person, and nobody in this building is told which of the two has happened and neither is anybody else.**" He put the mug down. "**And the maintenance of it is a delivery office. Not a document. A sheet of continuous fanfold paper that a man carries out of a rack on a Monday morning and puts back on a Friday, and the selection is in it, and the four hundred and twelve comes out of geography and not out of anybody. And the only complete copy of the four hundred and twelve names this building has ever had is a print run of the second of December in brown paper bands in a box behind a front counter, and it is the last good copy and nobody has ever put it next to anything.**"

"Then what is the second document," said Anja Ward. "If ours is a filter. What is the other side of the comparison."

"**A tenancy,**" said Ivo Pell. "**A tenancy is a household, with a person in it, and a start date and a term and an end date and an address and a landlord, and somebody whose job it is to keep it. It is the only document in this country that says a person is a neighbour of a place, and there are about four hundred and twelve of the things in this ward, and none of them is ours, and I have never seen one, and neither has the Director, and neither has the registrar, and there is no box in this building for it because it is not an object.**"

---

**And then somebody asked the obvious next question, and the answer to it is one sentence long and it is the reason the rest of this case is shaped the way it is.**

"Ask the postman," said Mara Iles.

"**We can't.**"

"Can't?"

"**He went on Tuesday.**" Ivo Pell had the sheet of A4 in his hand and he did not need to read it. "**The round changed on Tuesday the ninth. FLS-2 came out of the rack on a Monday and went back in on the ninth and there are about nine hundred in that rack and they keep them two years for audit and then they go in a skip. He did not tell us. We did not ask him. He told a man in a coat on a pavement in Low Ward in January that he would rather be a man who got moved than a man who knew, and he was right, and we honoured it, and on the morning of the ninth the only document in this city with a selection of about two hundred and ten addresses on it went back into a rack in a building three quarters of a mile from this one, and it will be a piece of paper in a skip in two years, and nobody's job was to know it was different from the other eight hundred and ninety-nine.**"

He put the sheet down.

"**And I don't even know what day of the week the ninth of February was, and neither does anybody in this building, because we did not ask him, and I have been thinking about that for an hour and it is the first time in nine weeks that the thing we have lost is a thing somebody refused to give us for a good reason.**"

Nobody in that room had anything to say to that, and the Director said so.

"Then the freeholder," she said. "**Who owns the buildings on that street. I have been a Director of this institution for nineteen years and I have signed four hundred letters to a list of about four hundred and twelve addresses and I do not know who owns the ground they stand on. Put that on the search and bring me something with a company name on it, and do it in writing, and if it takes six weeks, it takes six weeks.**"

---

**And the thing that happened in a corridor on the fourth floor at about ten to one on the afternoon of Wednesday the tenth of February, in about four minutes, and which nobody in that room thought was important, is that somebody wrote a sentence on a card and could not find anything to do with it.**

It is the sentence from the first of the two documents, and it is four words long, and a systems technician of twenty-five wrote it on the back of a printout in block capitals because he said he could not think of it in lower case.

> **THIS SCHEDULE HAS NO BOX FOR IT.**

"I've had that sentence in a drawer in my head for nine weeks," he said, "as a thing that came to a door and went away, and on Saturday a woman of forty-five put it on paper and gave it to us and I have written it on a card, and I want somebody to tell me what we do with it, because I have been holding it for a day and a half and I cannot get anywhere with it."

"It's a refusal," said Mara Iles. "**It is a refusal and it is correct and it is the second one in nine weeks and the first one was on the seventh of November and we have won nothing and lost a gallery.**"

"**Then why is it on a card.**"

"Because somebody had to write it down," said the Director, from the end of the corridor, on her way to a meeting. "**Nobody wrote it down in November and it cost us a fortnight. Write it down and leave it and if nobody can say in four days why it is the most useful sentence anybody has said in nine weeks, then it isn't one, and it goes in the bin.**"

**It is on a card in a systems office on the fourth floor of a building in Low Ward with the words THRESHOLD GROUP on a drawer card from 1954 in it, and it is there on the fourteenth of February, and on the fourteenth of February four people in that room read it and one of them said out loud that he had worked out why, and it is on the page at the end of that day and not on this one, and the four days in between are about four hundred and twelve tenancies and a woman of about thirty-nine with a book.**

---

> **She has told us we have not got an object. That is the finding and it is nine weeks old and I have been circling it since the fourth of December without ever saying it in a sentence, and a statutory officer in Northglass has said it to me in a second paragraph and signed it. A description is not a classification. A card is a description. We have been holding a card up at a registry for nine weeks.**

I have been carrying a clock that was not a clock.
