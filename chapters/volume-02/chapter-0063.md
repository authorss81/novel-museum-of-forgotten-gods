# Chapter 0063 — No Name Available

The Hall of Former Faiths was open from ten until five on Wednesday the twentieth of January 2025 and at about one o'clock in the afternoon, in a public room, with the doors propped and a rope line about a hundred and forty years old down the middle of it, a Director of this Museum stood at the east end beside a bell in a sealed case and explained to about ninety people what a museum is for.

**There was no charge for the hour. There was no ticket. There was a sheet of A4 on a stand at the door with the time and a room on it, and about sixty of the ninety had come up off Civic Hill because they had received a printed card in November or in December from a building they had never been inside, and about thirty had walked in off the street because a museum on a hill is a thing you can walk into, and **the group this meeting was for is the third group, which is the households whose letters have come back marked undeliverable, and there are about nine of them and nobody in this building can say which nine.**

That is the finding of the afternoon and it was not said in the afternoon and it is on the page anyway, because the number of people who can be hurt is the only number this thing escalates by, and on Wednesday the twentieth of January 2025 it stopped being about four hundred and twelve addresses and it became **a room with about ninety people in it and about nine addresses that this Museum has a card for and cannot write to, and the nine are not knowable from inside this building, and the wire has no memory, and the print run of the second of December is in a box behind a counter and has all four hundred and twelve names on it, and nobody has ever put the two things next to each other on a table.**

**Anja Ward had decided to hold it on the Wednesday afternoon, in about four minutes, and she had said the cost out loud before she decided, which is her habit, and it was minuted in that order, and the minute is the first document in this case that records a decision and its cost on the same line.**

"The cost is this. **I am going to stand in a public room at one o'clock on a Wednesday and say out loud, in front of about ninety people, that this Museum has had a false name on a public card for about a hundred and twenty years, and that I did not know that until the thirtieth of December, and that neither did my Registrar, and that the false name is the name of an organisation that does not exist.** That goes in a minute. That minute goes to the Board in March. The Board has an auditor in it and four members of a sub-group and two of them have been in this building since 1988 and one of them has been a trustee for twenty-two years and he signed the determination notice in November."

"And then what," said the sub-group member of sixty-four.

"Then this Museum is a building that printed a body's name on a card about a bell and called it a history of an object, for a hundred and twenty years, in a public room, and there is no version of that sentence which is not going to be in the newspapers in February, and I have decided that I would rather it came out of my mouth in a room with ninety witnesses in it than come out of a journalist's mouth in a corridor." She did not put her hands behind her back. "**I have been a Director for nineteen years and I have never in my life held a meeting in a gallery, and I am aware that holding meetings in galleries is what institutions do when they have run out of other rooms, and I am doing it anyway, and I would like that in the minute too.**"

---

**The room itself, at one o'clock on the twentieth of January, is the thing nobody outside this building has ever stood in and it is worth putting down properly, because everything that has happened in this case has happened four miles away from it.**

Thirty-one plinths down the middle. A rope line on stanchions, and the rope is dark and thick and there is more of it than there appears to be. Nine plinths carry objects and twenty-two carry cards explaining why they do not. Four of the nine are threshold things — a stone with a hollow worn in it, a cast iron grid, a boot scraper, a piece of floor with a dip in it where about four hundred thousand pairs of feet have gone in one direction. The other five are a bell, a bucket of coiled rope, a shutter off a shopfront, a bread paddle, and a hand bell in a case at the east end on plinth thirty-four, which is larger than life-size, cast in 1902, with a staple repair in its shoulder and the fracture still open under the staple.

**And on the floor, on a plastic sheet, inside the rope, in the last bay before the bell, a coir door mat, seven hundred by four hundred millimetres, with a card on a wire stand beside it at about knee height.**

**And on the front of the case at plinth thirty-four, at a slight angle, a sheet of A4 printed and designed by a woman of fifty-four at a kitchen table in Low Ward, taped on since the twenty-seventh of November, and the tape has gone the colour of weak tea, and the Museum's own card is in place beneath the panel and has been under it for fifty-four days, and nobody has taken the panel down because it is the requester's panel and the requester has not come for it.**

She was asked about the panel during the meeting by a man in the second row who had read the *Chronicle* and wanted to know whether the tape was the Museum's fault.

"It is my fault," said Anja Ward. "It has been on there since the twenty-seventh of November and I have not taken it down and I am not going to, and I will explain why in about four minutes and it is not because I am hiding anything, it is because that panel belongs to a woman who is not on my staff and a requester's panel is not mine to remove, and if I remove it I am saying that whatever is under it is being dealt with, and it is not, and she has not asked me to and she is not here."

"Is she here?"

"No. She has not been in this building since the twenty-seventh of November and she has not come today and I have not asked her to and I am not going to, and if she is watching a video of this on a telephone somewhere in Low Ward then she has heard me say that, and she will do what she likes about it."

---

**The forty minutes in the middle of it were not hers, and they are the reason the afternoon is not about a Director.**

She gave four minutes to the room and the four minutes are on the page because they are the clearest statement anybody in this city has made of what this institution is, and she said it standing still, and she said it four times in different words because the first version was not true enough.

"**A museum is a place where things are.** That is the whole sentence and I am not going to decorate it. We keep things. We write down what they are. We say who made them, where they came from, and when we don't know we say we don't know, and that is the entire trade and it has not changed since about 1860 and it is not going to change for me.

"**And there is one thing this building can do that nothing else in this county can do, and I am obliged to say it plainly because you have all come here to find out what it is.** We can put a name on a card. Not a person's name — we have a personnel file with about nine hundred names in it and it is on the fourth floor and you cannot get at it and neither can I without a key. **A thing's name. Maker, place, date.** A maker's name on a card in a public room is the only thing this city does that a ledger and a form and a database do not do, and I have said that sentence twice this month in a corridor and both times I wanted it to be a comfort and it is not a comfort, it is the problem, and today is the day it stops being either and becomes a fact in a room.

"So here is the fact. **The name of a thing is not a decoration. It is the hook. It is small and it is printed and it goes in an envelope and about four hundred and twelve households in this city have had one off me in the last three months and nine of them have had one come back marked undeliverable and I did not know that until the twenty-first of December and there is a person in this room who worked that out before anybody in this building did.**"

Then she said the sentence, and she said it slowly, and she said it facing the room and not the bell.

"**There is a card on the front of that case. It has been there about a hundred and twenty years. The bell was cast in 1902. At the bottom of the card, under the description, in the line where the maker's name goes, it says *Municipal Works*.** There is no municipal works. There has never been a body of that name in this city and there is no file in this building under any form of the reference printed above it. Three claims on the foot of that card are false and one of them is a name and the name is of a body that does not exist, and it has been in a public room, in a public building, on Civic Hill, under a public classification, for a hundred and twenty years, and **I found out on the thirtieth of December and I am the Director.**"

**The room did not make a noise. About ninety people and a room with a bell in it and a Director saying that her institution had lied in public for a hundred and twenty years, and the room did not make a noise, and that is because the sentence was flat and short and had a date in it, and a sentence with a date in it is a fact and a room knows the difference before anybody in it does.**

"Somebody is going to ask me who wrote it," she said. "I don't know. It is not in this building's hand and it is not in a handwriting anybody can match and **I am not going to stand here and offer you a person, because the last time this building offered the public a name it could not support, it was the name on a card, and it is a hundred and twenty years old, and not one of the people who cut that card knew either.**"

---

**And then a man of twenty-five stood up about two-thirds of the way back on the wrong side of the rope and said, in the voice of somebody who has rehearsed a thing in a corridor and is now saying it in front of ninety people, that he was the systems technician and he wanted four minutes.**

He got them. He asked for them at the door from a woman of about fifty who has been at that counter for nineteen years and who gave them to him without asking him a single question about why, **and the only thing that happened in that doorway is the thing that has happened in this building four times since the twenty-fourth of December: somebody competent decided, without being asked, to let a man into a room.**

**Hattie Bramm had not been in the building since the twenty-seventh of November. She was not in it on the twentieth of January either. She was in a library back room in Low Ward with a kettle on and she telephoned the front desk at about twelve twenty and asked whether the meeting was going to happen and whether there was a form.**

There was no form. There has never been a form for this. Five institutions were asked in January and all five said no and all five were right, and the reasons are in this case already and none of them is a decision anybody took lightly.

"Right," said Ivo Pell, from the wrong side of the rope. "**I'm the systems guy. I get a file every morning at about a quarter past four. It has about four hundred and twelve rows in it and one of the columns is a name, and that name is not ours. It comes from a records office four miles away and it is a field in a system, and on the mornings between the sixth of December and the twelfth nine of those four hundred and twelve names were not there any more, and this Museum printed nine blanks out on the twelfth and posted them and all nine came back marked undeliverable, and that is how we found out, which is a horrible way to find out.**

"**And the thing I want to say is the thing I have been trying to say to my own system for four years and can't, so I am going to say it to you.** Every one of you has, somewhere, a name in a box in a system. Your bank. Your doctor. The council. The school. And that name is a **billing contact**. It is who we send the bill to. It is not your family. It is not a list of who's at your address. It's the person responsible for the connection of services, and it's got thirty-one characters in it and it's got an update button, and it empties itself overnight when somebody presses a key on a clipboard on a street corner, and **it does not know that a person lives there.**

"**That's it. That's the whole of what I know and I've known it since the fourth of December and I'm twenty-five and I'm not a mystical and there's nothing supernatural about any of it, and the horror — and it is a horror, and I'm not going to call it anything else — is that about four hundred and twelve households in this city have an address that their own systems think nobody is responsible for, and every one of those four hundred and twelve is a person, and not one of them is on a list that anybody in this building could print.**"

**And a woman near the back said, quite loudly, "Well, why are you telling us and not the council?"**

"Because the council didn't put it there," said Ivo Pell. "A man did, on a walk survey, in about four o'clock in the afternoon, because he knocked on a door and nobody answered, and the code he pressed is printed at the foot of his own log with the consequences on the same line, and he has read it, and his boss has read it, and **the reason nobody has ever asked any of us any of this is that every single person in the chain is doing a correct thing inside a department.**"

---

**And the thing nobody said, which is the whole of Movement 2 and which the room worked out by itself at about two o'clock and did not put into words, is the number.**

Not four hundred and twelve. That was the number on a wire.

**Four hundred and twelve households is not a number of people and it has never been able to be converted into one**, and about nine of them were not in the room, and the nine of them are not refusing to come and do not know there is a meeting and cannot be told there is a meeting, because a museum's letter to a household goes down an occupancy field, and if the field is empty the letter is returned, and if the letter is returned the Museum stops, because that is what a system is for. And the Museum printed nine of those nine itself, on the twelfth of December, and posted them, and all nine came back marked undeliverable, and it found out on the twenty-first of December from a woman at a front desk who has never been thanked for it.**

"The Hall," said Anja Ward, at about two o'clock, to nobody in particular, which is how she said the important things. "**You are all in the Hall of Former Faiths. This is a public room on a public floor of a public building and you can all come in and about a hundred and forty of you come in on an ordinary day and I have been a Director for nineteen years and it is the reason this building has a roof.**

"And in about four minutes I am going to tell you what this Museum is going to do about the mat on the floor, and then somebody is going to ask me a question about it, and I am going to answer it, and I want you to know before I start that **I have not got an answer to the question I am going to be asked and I have decided to hold a meeting without one**, on the grounds that a Director who waits until she has the answer waits for ever, and this building has been waiting since the thirtieth of December, and the object at plinth thirty-four has lost a gallery and a fortnight because of a piece of coir and nobody in this building knows whether that was a thing it did or a thing that happened, and **I am not going to ask it, and I want that in the minute as well.**"

---

**What she said about the mat took ninety seconds and it is the correct answer and it is on the page because it is the first thing anybody has said in this case that is a policy rather than a position.**

"**That mat is not going to get a name.** There is no name for it. It was made in a shop and it cost about two pounds and there is a maker's mark on the back of the rubber of every mat of that kind in this city and the maker's mark is a word of about six letters in a language nobody here reads, and the label room will not put a name it cannot support, and I am not going to make them, and Fenn has told me to my face she thinks what she has done is the most dangerous thing anybody can put on a public card in this building and she said it before she did it.

"And the card will keep the four words. **No name available.** It has been on a wire stand in a public room for twenty-two days and about a hundred and forty people a day have walked within four feet of it and about four have read it, and that is not a failure of the card. **That is what a card is. It is a small printed thing at knee height and nobody stops.** And I have learned more in nine weeks about what this building is for from a card at knee height than I have from a hundred and forty years of anything else on this floor, and I am not going to say thank you to a piece of coir on a plastic sheet, and I am not going to pretend to anybody in this room that this is a lesson. It is a nuisance. It cost us a gallery and about ninety people on the Friday the eighth of January with a pound each, and I said that cost out loud in a room in December before I signed the paper and I am saying it again now because about ninety of you are in it and you are entitled to hear it from me and not from a notice on a board."

**And then a man stood up, third row from the front, and he was about sixty and he had a fold in his coat and he had not asked a question all afternoon, and he said:**

"**Then what is the rope for.**"

And Anja Ward said: "I do not know."

And a second man said: "It's a hundred and forty years old."

And Anja Ward said: "**I know how old it is. I have had it looked at, and measured, and weighed, and counted, and I have had a rope and a knot in it looked at by a woman who has been a gate clerk for thirty-one years, and I still cannot tell you what it is for, and I have not asked anybody in this building who would know, and I am asking this room, and if anybody in this room knows, I would be very glad.**"

Nobody knew.

**The meeting ended at about ten to three and about forty people stayed, and they stood in the room with a bell and nine threshold things and a mat on a plastic sheet, and two of them asked a man of twenty-five about the file and he answered them for about nine minutes, and nobody touched the mat, and nobody touched the rope, and the tape on the front of the case at plinth thirty-four is the colour of weak tea and has been for fifty-four days.**

---

Elias Venn was in the room for the whole of it. He paid his pound and signed the visitors' book in the ordinary column and the time-out column, which nobody fills in, is blank on his line, and which is the only instrument in this city that has ever opened anything for him.

He had stood at the west end of the rope at about ten to one with a pound in his hand and a notebook in his coat he did not take out, and he had counted the plinths twice, which he does, and which he was embarrassed by, and which he wrote down afterwards and did not tear out.

**A man of thirty-two, in a room with about ninety people in it, counting plinths. That is where the counting has got to. He stopped counting objects in December and started counting rooms, and on Wednesday the twentieth of January 2025 he counted thirty-one plinths and nine things and four rope stanchions in a row and he could not tell anybody why, and the only person in that room who would have understood the question was a woman in a library back room in Low Ward with a kettle on who was not there.**

He wrote it out at about half past eleven that night on the sixth page of a book with nine pages in it, and the last four lines are these.

> **Wed 20 Jan, 23:30. A Director stood in a public room and said out loud that this building has printed the name of a body that does not exist on a public card for about a hundred and twenty years, and that the bell was cast in 1902, and that she found out on the thirtieth of December and she is the Director. Nine times somebody said the word *municipal* in my hearing today. I have written it down because I did not expect to be able to hear it in a room and be the only person in the room who is not astonished by it, and I have not been astonished by anything since the sixth of December and I do not know what that is.**
> **About ninety in the room and about nine addresses this Museum has a card for and cannot write to. Not four hundred and twelve, and not ninety subtracted from it either, because those are the two kinds of number and this case has been told since the twenty-sixth of November that a difference is an adjudication.**
> **The nine are not knowable from inside the building. The wire has no history, the block is not versioned, and the print run of the second of December that has all four hundred and twelve names on it is in a box behind a counter and has never been put next to a feed. Nobody in that room could have told me which nine of those addresses were in it, and nobody in that room could have said who lives at any of them, and the number of people behind the nine is not nine and is not four hundred and twelve and is not anything that anybody in this city can put in a document.**"
> **Ivo Pell stood up on the wrong side of the rope and said the thing. Twenty-five years old, in a gallery, to ninety people, and he said *every one of them is a person, and not one of them is on a list that anybody in this building could print.* He did not look at me once. He was not looking at me. He was talking to about ninety people and I was one of them, and I have been in nine rooms in seven weeks because a competent person was in the room first, and today I was furniture, and it was the best afternoon I have had in two months.**
> **And at twenty to two somebody asked what the rope is for, and the Director said she did not know in front of ninety people, and I have been in this building for three years and nobody has ever asked that question out loud, and I asked it in a corridor on the thirtieth of December and got a document back, and I have not asked it since, and I am the only person in this city who has known for a fortnight that nobody knows.**
