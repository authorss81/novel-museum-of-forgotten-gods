# Chapter 0093 — The Practice In A Public Room

The meeting room at the Sillick Lane branch library is on the first floor, up about fourteen stairs, and it holds about thirty people standing or eleven sitting, and it has a table with four legs that do not wobble, a kettle, a noticeboard with the library's opening times on it, and a window that looks at the roof of the low building across the yard.

**A man of thirty-two was about four miles away. He did not come. Nobody in that room is him and he is not in it, and he has been the company of this business for twenty-two weeks, and the reason he is not in the room is the whole of it, which is that the two people who stopped this afternoon are not going to be named, and he does not know who they were, and nobody is going to tell him, and that is not an oversight and it took the room about nine seconds to not ask.**

**It was booked in writing on Wednesday the fifth of May 2025 and used on Saturday the fifteenth, from three o'clock to six, and the booking is a piece of paper with the date of the booking on it at the top and the date of the use at the bottom, and the two dates are ten days apart, and the woman who took the booking wrote both of them out herself because the form only has one box for the day and the box is the day it is used.**

She was about thirty and she had a ring of keys and a cardigan with a pen in the pocket and she has not given her name and nobody has asked her for it, and she read the form back to the woman of about fifty from the sheet before she took the money, because that is what she does.

"**Three to six. On the fifteenth. And the room goes back at six because Mr Perrin has the keys and Mr Perrin finishes at half past five.**"

"**What happens if we are still in it at six.**"

"**Nothing happens, you are just in a room that is not yours any more and he will stand in the doorway and wait, and he has never once had to do that and I would like it to stay that way.**"

---

## And the first thing that happened in it at three o'clock was that a piece of paper went on a wall with library tape on it, and the tape has an owner

They brought the copy. It is the copy the man of fifty-two made on the Monday, in the bolt pen, on a fresh piece of A4, and it has five lines and a date on the end of it, and the last line says that nobody signs it, nobody keeps it, and it is on the wall.

"**That is not our wall,**" said the woman of about thirty with the ring of keys.

"**It's a noticeboard,**" said Frank Dunning. "**It's got a frame on it. You can have the frame.**"

"**You can have the frame and not the board, and the tape is ours, and the pins are ours, and if there is a pin left in that wall at half past five Mr Perrin will take it out and put a note on the counter with a number on it, and that number is a job for somebody in a building on a hill and I have seen it happen about four times.**" She put the roll of tape on the table. "**Take as much as you want. Take all of it. It is better if you take all of it.**"

They took all of it.

**The copy went up on the noticeboard at about ten past three with four drawing pins and about two feet of library tape, and it went up crooked, and nobody straightened it, and it came off at about twenty to six and went back into a plastic wallet, and the noticeboard went back to the library's own notices, and there is a small mark on the cork where the pin was and nobody has said the word out loud.**

---

## And then a man of fifty-two said the sentence that the room had been got for, and he said it before anybody asked him, because nobody ever asks him

He had come in at about ten past three and stood with his back to the stairs.

"**Right. Somebody has to say why we are in here and not out there, so I'll say it.**"

"**You don't have to.**"

"**I have been waiting four months for somebody to make me so I am going to say it whether I have been asked or not.**" He looked at the tape on the noticeboard. "**On the sixteenth of April a registrar who works in a building on a hill was asked three questions in a room in Morningside, and the third one was: who is the person who says where it has to happen. And she would not answer it, and she gave a reason, and the reason is a sentence I have already written down in a notebook and have not stopped thinking about: you cannot put a permission in a place and keep it there, because a place holds things and a permission is a thing you are allowed to stop doing, and the person who is allowed to stop is the person who holds it, and they are on a step and not in a building. And I have been thinking about that for four weeks and here is what I have.**"

"**Go on.**"

"**A room like this is the closest this can get to a place without turning into a programme. That is all it is and that is why we are in it.**" He did not gesture. "**We are not in a museum, because a museum has a director and a schedule of collections and a rope that is a hundred and forty years old, and the day this is in a building with a collection then it is a programme and a programme has a keeper and a keeper is a person with a job.**"

"**And not Frank's hall.**"

"**And not Frank's hall.**" He said it gently and he did not look at him. "**I have said for four months that a hall a man owns is a man's hall, and Frank has agreed with me out loud about six times and I have never once seen him like it. This is a room with a landlord in it and a date at the end of it, and the date is six o'clock this evening, and at six o'clock we go, and that is the only thing about this room that is any good and it is a thing that is no good by half past six.**"

"**That's the first honest sentence anybody's said about a room since January,**" said Frank Dunning, **and put the kettle on.**

---

## And at three o'clock they went out, and the practice is the thing that has not changed since the fourth of January and it is four lines long

**1. You knock first. Then you say the name. 2. It runs from three o'clock until you can't read a number off a door. 3. Anybody taking part may stop at any point, and nobody's asked afterwards why.**

There were about nine households and about fourteen doors, and the reason there are more doors than households is that a household is not a door, and three of the nine are two doors.

They went out in the order on the rota, which is the order the knock goes in, which is the order of the doors on the street and has been since the fourth of January and is in nobody's system anywhere. **Nobody wrote a list. The rota had two columns and about nine lines and about a fortnight of pencil ticks in the first column, in pencil, in a hand that is not any of the nine hands because it is Frank Dunning's, and a pencil tick in a plastic wallet in a hall is not a record and everybody who has ever held that wallet knows it is not a record.**

Mavis Ludlow came. She was on the step at twenty past three with her cardigan on and she knocked at four doors and she said her own name out loud at three of them, which she had not done the week before, and somebody heard her say it and did not comment.

The woman of forty-four came, and she said the thing about the money again, on a doorstep, to about four people, in about the same words as in April, and she has said now that she is not going to stop saying it and that anybody who wants her to stop can wait.

**Vera Pilling came, and she is eighty-one, and she went up the east side on her own, and she knocked at one door and said a man's name at it, and somebody answered, and she did not say anything else, and nobody in that street asked her a single question about any of it and about four of them noticed.**

---

## And at about half past four two people stopped, and nobody asked either of them why, and it took the room about nine seconds to not ask

The first was a man who had gone out with the third group and came back in at about half past four and stood by the kettle, and Frank Dunning looked at him and then looked at the other side of the room.

"**Do you want a cup of tea,**" said Frank Dunning.

"**No. I'll go home.**"

"**Right.**"

**The second was the woman of about fifty from the sheet, who had been in the practice since the eighth of January and had never stopped in four months, and who said one sentence, and the sentence was not a reason.**

"**I'll see you next week.**"

And that was all, and nobody asked, and nobody wrote it down, and Elias Venn, who was not in that room because he was on the second landing of a stair in a building on a hill, does not know who the two people were, and would not say if he did.

---

## And at about twenty to six the boundary came off the wall and the room was given back and the two dates on the form are the whole of the difference between it and a hall

They did not stay until six. They stopped at about twenty to six, because the numbers on the doors on that stretch stop being readable at about ten to six in the last week of May in Low Ward, and the second rule is the light and not a clock, and the man of fifty-two said the time out loud and nobody argued with him about it.

They came back up about fourteen stairs into a room that was theirs for another twenty minutes and they took the boundary off the noticeboard. **It came off in one piece with the tape still on it, and the tape came off the cork and left a clean rectangle, and the woman of about thirty with the ring of keys said "that'll come off" and it did come off, in about nine seconds, with a bit of elbow grease, and she said that was the fourth time in about two years and she kept the bit in a drawer for it.**

The wallet went into the wallet. The kettle went off at its own time and nobody turned it off.

At six o'clock Mr Perrin came, and he is about seventy and he has had the keys since before the woman with the ring of keys started, and he stood in the doorway and looked at the noticeboard and at the corner and said nothing, and she said "it's all right, it's come off," and he said "right," and went away, and that is the whole of what happened at six o'clock.

---

## And then somebody asked the question that has been open since the fourth of December, out loud, in a room with a landlord in it, and got the answer it has been getting for four months

It was not asked by anybody from the building and it was not asked by a professional. It was asked by the woman of about thirty with the ring of keys, at about five to six, with the tape in her hand, and she asked it because she had let a room to nine people for three hours and she wanted to know what she had let it to.

"**Is this a thing or is it a place. Because I have to wind the alarm off at six and I would like to know whether I am winding it off for a thing or for a place.**"

Nobody answered for about nine seconds.

"**I'll tell you what I have got,**" said the man of fifty-two, **and he did not pretend to be answering it.** "**You have got a room, and it is a place, and it is not ours, and that is the only reason we are standing in it. What we are doing is not a thing and it is not a place, and nobody in this city has ever worked out how to write down what we are doing, and a registrar in a room in Morningside has already told us in about forty seconds that they are on a step and not in a building and that there is no instrument, and she was not guessing and she was not being careful, and she gave the reason, and the reason is on the bottom of one of the pages of my own notebook.**"

"**And you don't know.**"

"**No.**" He put his hands in his pockets. "**And I have had a month to think about it and I have got no nearer than on the fourteenth of January, and I am fifty-two and I am not going to pretend I have.**"

The woman of about thirty with the ring of keys put the tape in the drawer with the bit of cork in it, and said **"right"**, and that was the last question anybody asked in that room all afternoon, and it got nothing, and the man of fifty-two said in about nine seconds that he had had a month to think about it and had got no nearer than on the fourteenth of January, and he is fifty-two and he did not pretend.
