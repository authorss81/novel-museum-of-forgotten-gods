# Chapter 0055 — The Carrying

**It failed on Monday the twenty-third of December, in a conservation office, and nobody in the building had been able to say why for twenty-one hours.**

There are four people in this business who are neither an officer nor an employee of the Museum of Quiet Saints, and there is no column in any return this building files in which any of them can be written, and on the twenty-fourth of December 2024 that fact was the only thing that made a door mat leave a public museum four miles from Sillick Lane.

It took three days. It should have taken about four minutes, and everybody in the building who worked on it has said some version of that sentence since, and the person who has said it most, and worst, is a man of thirty-two who was barred from the building on the first of December and who cannot be in it except on notice, in company, not before eight in the morning, and who was in it on the twenty-fourth for two hours and forty minutes because a printed card with the word WITNESS in a box sent him to the fourth floor.

---

**The first two days were the Museum looking for a form.**

Mara Iles had been looking since Monday. She had gone through the whole of it, and she described the going-through of it in a corridor at about half past four on the Thursday with the door shut and a folder under her arm, and it is worth putting down because it is the argument of the entire case, in the mouth of the person who has the most forms in the city.

"**There is no instrument in this building for a member of the public taking a thing of their own out of a public room, and there never has been, and the reason there has never been one is that in a hundred and forty years nobody has ever wanted to do it, because a person does not walk into a museum with a door mat.** That is the finding and I got there on the Monday by about four in the afternoon and I then spent two days looking for a form anyway, which is what I do, and which is what made me late."

"What did you find."

"Four things that nearly work." She counted them on her fingers and did not enjoy doing it. "A gift form: that is a transfer of ownership to this Museum, and he is not giving it to us, he wants it out of a corridor, and if I put it on a gift form I have taken a door mat off a man in Low Ward and there is no disposal route for a door mat."

"A bequest form. No."

"A bequest form is a longer version of the same answer." A loan form: no. "A loan form would have been the good one, and it is the good one, and a loan is a thing going the other way, and a loan form has a **borrowing institution** on it and a **lender** on it and about four boxes for a collection manager and a schedule reference, and there is no borrowing institution, there is a man in a cardigan, and the collection manager's box would have been mine and the schedule reference would have been blank and the thing would have lived in this building for ever."

"That is the fourth thing."

"The fourth thing is the goods-out docket, and it is the one we actually used, and it is the only one of the four that is designed to let a thing leave a building without anybody owning it, and here is what it says at the foot."

She turned the folder round and put a printed sheet on the top of it, and the foot of it said this:

> **MUSEUM OF QUIET SAINTS — GOODS OUT — LOADING BAY.**
> *Item · Quantity · Method of carriage · Destination · Date and time out · Collected by (name and grade) · Signature of collector · Countersignature, Director.*
> **This docket records the movement of material from the loading bay of this Museum. It is not evidence of title. Material not owned by this Museum may be moved out only on the written authority of the Director, which must be attached.**
> *(A material count is made against this docket. The docket remains open on the desk of the post holding it until the item is on its pallet and the pallet is sealed.)*

"So," said Mara Iles. "There it is. **Everything in this building comes out of a loading bay on the back of a pallet, and there is a box for the count, and the box is on the docket, and the docket is a counting instrument.** And on Thursday morning I stood in a corridor and read the word *pallet* on a form nine times and I said the sentence that I have not said in front of a Director in nineteen years, which is *a door mat does not go on a pallet*."

"So the gate is the Director's written authority."

"The gate is the Director's written authority and it has to be attached and it cannot be, and here is the sentence, and I want it on the page in her words because she said it to me in a corridor on the twenty-fourth of November and I have not heard it since and I heard it again on Monday in a different tone and I have been thinking about it for two days."

**Which is the sentence Anja Ward said in a corridor on the twenty-fourth of November and has not unsaid.**

"I cannot sign for it. I cannot indemnify it. And I cannot undertake anything about it." Anja Ward said it at about ten to four on the Monday, standing in a doorway, with no folder this time, and she said it in a level voice and then she said the rest of it, which is new, and which is the reason nothing happened on Thursday.

"And on the twenty-fourth of November I said that to a registrar in a corridor and it was the answer to a question about a crate. I have not taken it back and I am not going to. But I have had two days with it and I have worked out why I said it, and it is not because I am frightened of a door mat. **It is because I have nothing to be responsible for.** I cannot be responsible for a thing that has no number, in a building whose every control is a number, because a control is a promise that the thing is where somebody thinks it is, and a door mat in a corridor of a public museum in the week before Christmas is not that, and if I sign a piece of paper saying that I have it, then somebody in four years is going to come and ask me where it is, and I am going to have to say *I signed a piece of paper about it in December 2024 and it did not have a number on it*, and that is a sentence about me and not about a mat, and I have spent nineteen years not having a sentence about me in this building."

She did not raise her voice and she did not move and she did not put her hands behind her back.

"So: no."

**So: no, for two days, and then on the Thursday morning at about eleven, something happened that was not in any of the four documents, and it happened because a man of twenty-five came back from a Saturday systems day with a printout and said a sentence in a loading bay.**

---

**Ivo Pell had been in the building since seven and had done about a hundred and forty jobs, and at about eleven he came up to the first floor with a printout about the door controls, and Mara Iles told him the problem in about nine words, and he said: "There's a book."**

"There are eleven books in this building."

"There's a book at the front desk with a ribbon in it and four columns and it has been the same book since I came here, and about a hundred and forty people a day put their name in it, and it is the only document in this building that has ever recorded a person going into a public room, and it is not a form, and that is why nobody has thought of it, because it is not a form and I have never had to think about it."

"You mean the visitors' book."

"I mean the visitors' book," said Ivo Pell. "**Eleven rows. It has got a column for the time in and it has got a column for the time out, and the time-out column is the one nobody fills in, and the reason nobody fills it in is that a public museum does not care when a person leaves, which is true, and the reason it matters today is that this man came in at ten past ten and he is going out at about four and we have a mat on a pallet-shaped hole in a form and the only thing anybody in this city has ever used to move a person and their belongings from A to B is a signature and a time.**"

Mara Iles stood in a corridor for about four seconds and did not say anything, and Elias Venn, who was standing at the end of the corridor in a coat because he was due at a committee room at one and had come early, worked out in those four seconds that something had just happened to a person he had known for nine weeks and he would not find out what it was until March.

Then she said: "Fenn will not do the card."

"I know. That was Wednesday's idea and it is right and it is Wednesday's idea and Wednesday is a label room, and a label room makes cards about things that are in the collection."

"Then what is it."

"It is a line in a book," said Ivo Pell, "and about eleven pounds, and a man with a plastic bag, and about nine minutes' walk to the tram, and it is the cheapest thing anybody in this business has ever done."

---

**They asked the wrong person first, and that is on the page because it is why the right person said yes.**

Elias Venn found Perry Loach in a service corridor at about five past five on the Thursday evening with a mop head under his arm and a fold in overalls, and he asked him, and the man said no.

"Perry, I need somebody to walk a door mat out of a service corridor, down two flights, across a yard and out through a roller door at about six, and to put it in a plastic bag in a man's hands and sign a line in a book, and it will take you about nine minutes and it is the twenty-fourth of December and the grid does not have you down for the twenty-fourth because the contract runs to the thirty-first and I know that."

Perry Loach did not put the mop down.

"No."

"Can I say why."

"You can say why and I'll tell you no again." He shifted it. "**You asked me. That's the whole of it and you didn't even notice you'd done it.** I've got a grid with my hours on it and no name on it, and it closes on Friday, and if a thing of mine is on a piece of paper in a corridor in this building on Friday because a man without a job asked me, then on Friday somebody is going to look at that piece of paper and find my name on it attached to a door mat, and I have spent nine years making sure that the only place my name appears is nowhere. **You don't want a thing of mine to end up on a piece of paper because you asked. You want the office to ask.**"

"I can't ask the office. I'm barred from the building."

"Then you're stood up," said Perry Loach. "**You want somebody to do something, you get a letterhead to do it with.**"

Elias Venn went up two flights and put his head round a door on the fourth floor and said, "The Director will see you for four minutes about a door mat."

The Director said yes in about two words and then read the rest of it standing up, and then she said one thing, which is on the page because it is the reason it worked and because it is the fourth time in two months that somebody in this building has moved about four inches to the left of their own department.

"Tell him the office asked."

---

Perry Loach walked a door mat out of the Museum of Quiet Saints at about twenty past five on Thursday the twenty-fourth of December 2024, and the sequence of it is a record and it is short and it is the whole of what happened.

> **24 DEC 2024 — REMOVAL FROM BUILDING, NON-COLLECTIONS MATERIAL. Entry in the visitors' book, ordinary column, and the counter's own line beneath it. No docket raised: no pallet, no material of this Museum, no authority required, the removal being by the person who presented the item and to the address from which he presented it.**
>
> **Time in 10:12. Time out 17:24. About seven hours, of which about four minutes was at the loading bay and about nine minutes was a walk across a yard and out of a roller door and down to the gate.**
>
> **Carried by: P. LOACH, services contract, 6h 40m that week including this. No grade exists. GRADE FIELD LEFT EMPTY AT THE COUNTER'S INSISTENCE.**
>
> **Received by: FRANK DUNNING, 88 Sillick Lane, in his own name, in his own hand. Signature witnessed at the gate by M. PRIEST, front desk, nineteen years, who wrote *not a Museum object* in the reason column and initialled it.**

And the part of it that is the whole point of the chapter is a row of a table in the visitors' book, and the row is the last line of that day's page, and it reads:

> **17:24 — OUT. DUNNING, F. — own property, not ours. A. WARD, Dir., asked.**

**"A. WARD, Dir., asked."**

Four words, in a column, on the last line of a page, on the twenty-fourth of December, in a book with a ribbon in it, and it is the only document in the entire business in which a Director of this Museum has ever recorded that she was asked by somebody to do a thing, rather than that she did it.

Elias Venn did not see it. He was at the fourth-floor landing at half past four with a card in his hand that said WITNESS and a room on it, and he was not in the building at twenty past five, and he was barred from the building, and the correct behaviour of a man with a WITNESS card is to leave at the minute the witness business is finished, and he did, and he took the tram down the hill at about ten to six.

**He found out on the twenty-eighth, from a systems technician, in a corridor, in about four seconds, and neither of them made anything of it, and it is the only time in eleven weeks that a thing has come to him and been better than he expected.**

---

**On the way out, Margaret Priest gave the man his own card back.**

The card that had come back marked undeliverable. The annual acknowledgement, DUNNING, F., 88 SILLICK LANE, issued the twelfth of December. She took it out of a bundle of about four hundred in a box on a shelf behind her and gave it to him across a counter at about ten past five on the twenty-fourth of December, and she gave it without asking anybody, and it is the only thing she has done in nineteen years that was not asked for.

"That's got your name on it."

"It has." He looked at it for a while. "It's come back once."

"It'll come back again," said Margaret Priest. "There's about nine of them a week now in that run and they're all in a box behind the boiler and nobody's opened the box on Friday."

"Why are you giving it to me?"

"Because you came up that counter on Monday and asked me for it and I told you it was in a box," said Margaret Priest, "and because I have been putting things in boxes on this counter for nineteen years and about four of them in nineteen years have ever come back out again, and I have never once been asked to put one back, and I would like there to be one."

He put it in the same plastic wallet as the bank statements. That is where it is. That is where it was on the third of January and that is where it was on the eleventh, and it is the only piece of paper in the world that has Frank Dunning's name and Frank Dunning's address on it and has not come back through a machine with a dash in a box on it, and it exists because this Museum prints four hundred and twelve of these a year and keeps them, which is not policy and is not good practice and is the luck of the whole thing.

**And the last thing that happened on the twenty-fourth of December is that the description of a door mat went into a drawer in a registrar's office on the first floor, in a folder with nothing written on the front of it, and it is the only description of any object in this case that is about a thing's job, and it has no accession number on it, and it has no name on it, and it is the only instrument this business has produced in three days.**

And that is where Volume 02 turned over, on the twenty-fourth of December, four miles from a man in a cardigan with a plastic wallet, and the turn was not a monster and the turn was not a door opening and the turn was a sheet of paper with a measured length on it and a sentence in a registrar's mouth:

*"A description is the only thing you get when there is no number. And a name is the only thing you get when there is no number. And I have spent five years writing boxes and I have spent three days unable to find one, and the reason I could not find one is that the thing is not a Museum object, and the reason I could not make it a Museum object is that nobody in this country will accept a gift of a door mat from a man who cannot prove that the mat is his."*
