# Chapter 0058 — The Card

The Hall of Former Faiths closed at three o'clock on Thursday the thirty-first of December 2024 and reopened at ten o'clock on Wednesday the thirteenth of January 2025, and the notice that went on the board outside the door at about ten past two on the Wednesday said why, in four lines, and the fourth line was the one the Board saw, and the Board saw it in January and did not move it, because a Board minute of 18 November had already established that the sub-group does not move a Director's date.

> **HALL OF FORMER FAITHS — CLOSED TO THE PUBLIC.**
> **Reopens 10:00, Wednesday 13 January 2025.**
> *Closed under condition 3 of the Director's standing conditions of 15 October 2024, following the posting on 30 December 2024 of a proposed disposition in respect of an item received into this Museum on that date. Ten working days, counted from the posting. The tenth working day is the thirteenth. The object at plinth 34 is not moved, not handled, not opened and not approached. Annexe D (permanent) is unaffected. Annexe E is unaffected and remains in force.*
> *The Museum apologises to the Hall's threshold group of about four hundred addresses.*

**And a door mat from a classroom in Hall Lane closed the most-visited room in a public museum for ten working days in the middle of the holiday, and that is the whole of the escalation of Volume 02 up to this point, and there is no monster in it and there never is going to be.**

The mat went onto the floor of the Hall of Former Faiths at about twenty past eleven on the morning of Wednesday the thirtieth of December 2024, on a plastic sheet, in front of a rope, in a public room, in a building four miles from Sillick Lane, and it lay there until the thirteenth of January, and about four hundred and twelve households had been told on the second of December that the room was open, and about ninety of them will come on the Friday morning of the eighth of January with a pound each and find the door shut and a card in a plastic sleeve.

**And it was a Wednesday morning, and everything that has ever happened to Elias Venn in this business has happened on a Tuesday, and he noticed that on the Wednesday, in a corridor, at about six in the evening, and did not write it down.**

**This is what the turn was, and it was a card, and the card was not the mat's card. It was one about a bell, and it is about a hundred and twenty years old, and it is the Museum's own.**

---

**The thing that had to happen on the morning of the thirtieth was that a Director had to put her name to a document, and it is the second document she has signed since the first of December, and she said out loud what it costs, and she said it before she signed it, and it is on the page because she insisted that it be minuted in that order.**

"Before I sign this, I am going to say the cost out loud, in a room, and the minute will record that I said it before I signed and not after, and if the minute does not record that then I have not said it.

"**Condition three says that between the posting of a proposed disposition and the execution of that disposition there is no public access to the object. I have written condition three. It is in my own standing conditions and it is the same sentence as clause three of Standing Instruction 14, and I wrote the clause and I wrote the condition, and both of them were excellent in October and I have been waiting all term for something to test them and I did not expect it to be a mat and I am not going to pretend I have not been waiting.**" Anja Ward, Director, fifty-three, standing against the east wall of a committee room on the fourth floor with her hands behind her back.

"Saying it now. **The notice goes up this afternoon and the room shuts tomorrow morning, the thirty-first of December, and the tenth working day counted from this afternoon is the thirteenth of January, because the first of January is a public holiday and a condition counts working days and not calendar days and I have had somebody count them for me twice. The Hall of Former Faiths is the reason this building has a roof. About four hundred and twelve addresses were told on the second of December that the room was open. There are going to be about ninety people at that door on the Friday the eighth with a pound each, and I have said the cost out loud first, in a room, in December, on purpose, because I am not going to be a woman who says it in January. And the honest answer to why is that a door mat has more classification in front of it than a bell has, and that is because the bell was here first and because the mat is in a school with three hundred and eighty children in it, and I would take a mat over a bell every day of my working life and I have.**

"And the second cost, and this is the one I am actually signing for, is this. **There is no Registry route that will get me an answer in ten working days and I have not looked, because I already know.** The rota is a paper on a wall in Northglass. There are four assessors. The first slot is the sixth of February. And an emergency inspection is not provided for, and a woman I have never spoken to has written me that down in one line on a form with her name and a reference on it, and she is right, and I have had her letter eleven days and I have not told the sub-group until this morning because I wanted the minute to have a cost in it."

She signed it at about twenty past eleven, and the minute records that she said the cost first, and she had asked for that in September after the memorial and had never asked for anything since. She had also recorded, on the same minute, the day of the week, because the notice was going up that afternoon and the room was shut the next morning, and because a condition that counts working days has to hang off a date somebody can check, and it is on the page because she is a Director who has been caught out by a weekday exactly once in nineteen years and does not intend to be caught twice.

**And the answer the Museum got, in the form it gets all its answers, from a woman of about forty-five that nobody in the building has ever spoken to, in a Registry office in Northglass, on a printed form, and it is here because it is the shortest and the most correct document of the year:**

> **QUIET ACCORD REGISTRY — SCHEDULE 12 PROPOSAL, acknowledgment of receipt.**
> **Ref. QA/CR/1426. Received 30 Dec 2024.**
> **Classification proposed: carried object, place-based, activity observed outside a museum context.** *(Received into a public gallery in this city on 30 December 2024. Not accessioned. Not a gift, a bequest or a loan.)*
> **Acknowledge: this schedule has no classification for activity observed outside a museum context. Your proposal cannot be listed against a classification. It is not a Schedule 12 proposal and it is not a Schedule 9 proposal. It is referred to your own schedule of collections and to the inquiry.**
> **No inspection slot is available before 6 February 2025. There is no emergency provision. I am not sorry. If you are about to display it, do not display it on the strength of a letter from this office.**
> **S. TULLY, assessor, quiet accord registry.** *(Name on the rota of 23 December 2024. First appearance on any rota in this file. Not previously spoken to by any member of staff of this Museum.)*

**"S. Tully, assessor."** Five months of rota bulletins in a systems technician's folder and a name on it, and nobody in the building had ever said it out loud, and the first time it was said in a room it was in a sentence that refused the Museum and told it not to display the object.

**The Registry has no box for it.** It is the thing a registrar wrote on a pad in a conservation office on the twelfth of October and said out loud in a public gallery in front of eighty people on the nineteenth of November, and it is now on a printed form from Northglass with a reference and a date on it, and it is the finding of the whole of Volume 02's first movement: *the schedule has no threshold in it for the number or age of the people in a room*, and its cousin, *the schedule has no classification for an object whose activity happens in a place and not in a room.*

And nobody could classify the mat. And so the classification route was shut, and the classification route being shut, the Director's own route was the only one left, and her own route is the conditions, and condition three is the only instrument in the building that will move a thing out of a public room, and moving the mat out of a public room is what the classification question required.

**So a door mat closed a gallery.**

---

**The thing that happened at about half past one in the afternoon is that a woman of about fifty-five in a label room asked somebody a question and the question was whether a public card in this building is allowed to have nothing in the field where the name goes.**

It is Fenn. Surname only. She has been finishing cards in this building for a very long time and she has made about four hundred of them and she cut the card for plinth 34 in October and she will not put a name on a card she cannot support, and she said no twice to a suspended night guard in October and both of her noes were correct and one of them is the reason the whole of the last three months exists.

"Eleven mats' worth of card, eleven blanks," she said. "That's the job. You have a thing with no maker and you write *no maker identified* or you leave the line out, and I leave the line out, and then there's a space on the card the size of a name, and about a hundred and forty years ago somebody decided that a space on a card the size of a name is a hole in a museum and you don't have holes in a museum."

**"Who decided that?"**

"Everybody. In order, over a hundred and forty years, and none of them wrote it down." She put the card cutter down, which is the thing you do when you are about to say the important part. "Now. I've been asked to cut a card for a door mat and I've got no maker and no date and no place of manufacture and no collection line, and the form wants a heading, and a heading is a name. So either I put a name on it or I leave a hole. And I'm not putting a name on it. And I can't leave a hole, because everybody before me made sure you can't leave a hole. And the way they made sure of it is the thing I want somebody to look at before I do it."

"Where."

"Eleven feet that way," said Fenn. "It's the one that's been under a panel for a month. Get somebody to lift the tape."

---

**Nobody lifted the tape. The card came off instead, and the panel stayed where it is, and it has been where it is since the twenty-seventh of November and it is a sheet of A4 printed and designed by a woman of fifty-four at a kitchen table in Low Ward, taped to the front of a case at a slight angle over a plinth card, and the last line on it says *the pay office kept its own book. That book is on the table*, and it is still taped on, and the tape has gone yellow, and nobody has taken it down because it is the requester's panel and the requester has not come for it, and a requester's panel is not a Museum's to remove.**

The card came off the case at about twenty past one in the afternoon, in a room with a Director in it and a registrar in it and a Board Secretary and a sub-group member of sixty-four, and it came off with a card tool, and it was four inches by six with a number in the top right, and the number is `Acq. 4471 / B`, and it is not a Museum reference in any form, and the top half of the card is the description that eighty people read on the twenty-seventh of November:

> **HAND BELL.** *Unpitched at rest, no inscription, no figure, no devotional content. Larger than life-size, used for sounding, and worn hollow inside the lip where it was held. Cast 1902. Staple repair, fracture remains visible. Quiet Accord classification Annexe D permanent, to be displayed, under no circumstances to be removed from public view by a museum acting alone.*

**And nobody in that room read the top half. They had all read the top half in November. It is the bottom half that is the finding, and the bottom half is four lines that have been on the foot of a card in a public gallery for about a hundred and twenty years, below the description, where nobody's eye goes, and behind a panel since the twenty-seventh of November, and it says:**

> *Recovered on the closure of a textile works, Low Ward.*
> ***Acq. 4471 / B.***
> *Municipal Works, unremarkable industrial object, retained for completeness of the Low Ward series.*

Three claims on the foot of a public card, and not one of them is true, and the registrar said so in a public gallery on the twenty-seventh of November in about two minutes and has not once said it in a room since, and it is on the page because the whole of the case is on the page and the foot of the card is the case.

"There is no textile works in Low Ward that closed in 1902," said Mara Iles. "There is no textile works in Low Ward that was *casting* in 1902. The Low Ward series was opened in 1961, so the card files a thing cast in 1902 into a series that did not exist for fifty-nine years after it was made. And four-four-seven-one over B is not a Museum reference: it has no year, it has one letter where the form takes two, and there is no file for it in this building under it or under any form of it."

"And the fourth line," said the sub-group member of sixty-four, reading it. "*Municipal Works, unremarkable industrial object, retained for completeness of the Low Ward series.*"

"That is a name," said Mara Iles. "**It is in the position where a name goes, it is the name of a body that does not exist, and it has been on a public card in this building for about a hundred and twenty years, and it is the only name on that card, and there is a panel over the top of it that a woman of fifty-four printed herself and put up for the workers on the twenty-seventh of November, and the panel is still on the case and nobody has taken it down and nobody is going to.**"

"Who wrote it?"

"Nobody knows. It was not written by me, and I have said in public, twice, that it is not a Museum number and that the rest of it is three false claims, and I have been waiting eight weeks for somebody to ask me about the word *Municipal Works* specifically, and nobody has, because it is the fourth line and it is under a panel, and **that is the entire mechanism. It is not that the lie is old. It is that the lie is at the bottom.**"

---

**And then the label-room finisher was asked the question the whole of the afternoon is for, and she answered it in a corridor, and she had cut four hundred cards and had never been asked.**

"So the field's either got a name in it or it hasn't," said Fenn. "And it can't be a name, because there's no name. And it can't be blank, because a blank's a hole. And the reason it can't be blank is not me. **The reason it can't be blank is that the last time this building had a blank in a public room, somebody filled it in later, and I wasn't here and I don't know when, and I know because the foot of that card has got a body on it that doesn't exist, and that is what happens.** You don't leave a space. That's the rule. And the rule isn't written down and it isn't mine and it isn't yours and it's about a hundred and forty years old, and the whole of my job is that rule, and I have made about four hundred cards off it."

"What if it stays blank."

"Then it stays blank and it's the first one in a hundred and forty years and every person who comes in here for the next hundred and forty years will look at it and see a space where the name should be and they will not know that anybody decided anything." She picked the card cutter up again. "**And you know what a space is, don't you. A space is the most honest thing a card has ever had on it and it is the single most dangerous thing you can put on a public card in a museum, and both of those are true, and I am going to do it, and I am not going to be thanked, and I would like it minuted that I said it was dangerous first.**"

---

**The card was cut at about four o'clock on the afternoon of the thirtieth of December 2024 and it is four inches by six, and it has five ruled lines, and it has one heading and four lines of description, and the heading is a class and not a name.**

> **DOOR MAT.** *Coir on rubber backing, 700 x 400 mm. Wear, one band, approx. 380 mm wide, 120 mm from the edge nearest the door. Backing perished at two corners, one corner re-laid; one edge repaired, different fibre, two staples. No inscription, no name, no number, no maker's mark, no devotional content. Function: a fixed-size surface for a person to stand on at a doorway while a person decides whether to open a door. Presented by a member of the public. Not a gift. Not a bequest. Not a loan. Not accessioned. Location: on view, Hall of Former Faiths, on the floor, inside the rope.*
> ***Maker's line: [ no name available ]***
> **No accession number. No collection line. No series. Not in the schedule of collections: there is no box for this and the Registrar will not invent one.**

And the maker's line has a line drawn through it, and under the line it says, in the finisher's own hand, four words:

> ***no name available***

**And that is the first public card in this building in about a hundred and forty years that carries a name in the position where a name goes, and the name it carries is the sentence *no name available*, which is not a name, and which is a hook, and which will be in a public room for six weeks over the holiday with about four hundred and twelve households who have been told the room is shut, and about ninety of whom will come and stand in front of it anyway because they have a pound and a card telling them the room is shut and nothing else.**

And the card does not name Frank Dunning, and it does not name the child, and it does not name the mill, and it does not name a body called Municipal Works.

**And that is what the turn was, and it was a card, and a card is the only instrument that has ever caught one of these.**

The number of people who can be hurt went up on the thirtieth of December and it went up by a gallery and a school and a round and about four hundred and twelve households, and it did not go up by a single object, because nothing happened, and nobody was harmed by a mat.

It went up like this.

A row of flats in Sillick Lane, about fourteen doors on the east side, of which four have a field with nothing in it. Two hundred and ten addresses on a delivery round. A road in Broad Street and about nine addresses that came off it in three days. About four hundred and twelve households on a list that is a wire and not a record, nine of which had a name in them in November and nothing in them on the twenty-first of December. A school of about three hundred and eighty children and about two hundred adults, with a mat in a corridor for one weekend because a push bar could not be pushed with a hip. A registrar's room, a systems office, a delivery office, a records office on Marsh Lane, a bonded-goods clerk six weeks ago, and a front desk with a box behind a boiler. **A bell, on a plinth, in a sealed case, in a room that is shut, in a building four miles from all of it, which has given one note on the twenty-seventh of November and one on the thirteenth of December and none since, and which has lost a gallery and a fortnight of a rope line's public room because of a thing made of coir, and is not asking for anything, and will not be asked about.**

No monster. There is a mat, a bell, a stone with a hole in it, a bucket of coils, a list that nobody versioned, a code with four words at the bottom of a log, and about two hundred and thirty people who press about eleven thousand keys a day and cannot be told from each other.

And at about half past five on the thirtieth of December 2024, in a corridor on the fourth floor of the Museum of Quiet Saints, a man of twenty-five told a man of thirty-two what had happened in a room, and neither of them made anything of it, and this is the conversation, in full, and it took about four minutes.

"*Door mat.*"

"There's no name. She wrote *no name available* on it with a line through it and she said she'd rather do that than put a name on it and she said it was the most dangerous thing you can put on a card and she said that before she did it." Ivo Pell shifted the folder. "And they took the plinth thirty-four card off the case and read the bottom of it out in front of the Director, and there's a panel still on the case, and the tape's gone yellow, and nobody's taken it off because it's the requester's."

"Shut till the thirteenth of January," said Ivo Pell. "Ten working days. And I want you to know that I have been in this building four years and I have never once heard anybody say anything like that, and I have thought about it every hour since, and I have not worked out whether it was a joke."

Elias Venn stood in a corridor on the fourth floor of a building he was barred from, on the last working day of the year, with a pound in his pocket and a pocket notebook he bought on the corner on Monday for thirty-five pence with nine pages in it and eleven lines written on the first of them and a registered letter in the inside pocket of his coat that was not his to open and had been there since the thirtieth of October, and he did not go down to the Hall of Former Faiths and look at the mat, because he was not allowed in, and because the room was shut, and because there was nothing to look at that was not already on a card.

Then he said the thing he had thought of at about ten past five and had not said at ten past one when he had had the chance in a room with a Director in it, and he said it to a man of twenty-five in a corridor, and it is on the page because it is the last sentence of the first movement of Volume 02 and it is the question the whole of this book has been asking since the first night of October:

"**Ivo. What is a name field for.**"

And a man of twenty-five stood in a corridor with a folder under his arm and thought about it for about nine seconds, and gave an answer that was not the answer, and then gave the answer.

"For finding him," said Ivo Pell. "That's what it does. It doesn't know that. It's a field, and you put a key in it, and the system's job is to go *get* — you put a key in, and the thing comes back, and the thing that comes back is a person, and the system does not know a person from a postcode, it's a lookup, and it will go and get you a postcode just as fast." He put the folder down against the wall. "That's it. That's what a name field is for. **It's not for the name.**"

The corridor was quiet for about four seconds.

"So the only difference," said Elias Venn, "between a man who is a person and a man who is a postcode is a name in a field, and there is no field on this earth that knows which one it's looking at."

"There's a field on this earth that knows," said Ivo Pell. "It's about four inches square and it's got about four hundred lines in it and it's got two hundred and forty-seven locations and it's got a hundred and forty years of a rope line in the building around it, and it does know. **And it has a maker's line with a body on it that doesn't exist, and it has had since about 1902, and nobody read the bottom of it for a hundred and twenty years.**"

Neither of them said anything else. Ivo Pell went to lock a systems room. Elias Venn went down the stairs and out of a door he was not barred from, which is the front door, because the side door needs a card and the front door is the door the public uses, and it cost him nothing, and it is the only door in this city that has ever opened anything for him without anybody deciding to.

He wrote it out longhand at about ten past eight that night, at a table in a rented room in Low Ward, and the last six lines of the entry for the thirtieth of December 2024 are these:

> **The card is a card about a door mat and the name's slot has *no name available* in it with a line through, and the finisher says that is the first one in a hundred and forty years and that she said it was dangerous before she did it.**
> **The plinth thirty-four card has got three false claims and a body that does not exist on the bottom of it, under a panel a woman printed herself, and the tape's gone yellow, and nobody has ever read the bottom of a card in a public room, and that is the whole of the mechanism of this case and it is not a monster and it is a foot of a card.**

A museum is the only place in this city that could and has chosen not to, and it took four hours and a hundred and twenty years of somebody else's rule.**
> **And I asked Ivo what a name field is for, and he said *for finding him*, and then he said *it's not for the name*, and then he said a museum knows, and has had a false name on the bottom of a card since 1902.**

There is a field, and a date, and a body pressing a key at about four in the afternoon on an ordinary weekday, and about four hundred and twelve households, and one hundred and twenty years of a rope line, and a girl of nine who stands on a warm mat by a radiator and is not going to be a thing in a museum.**
