# Chapter 0061 — Clause Seven

The Hall of Former Faiths opened at ten o'clock on Wednesday the thirteenth of January 2025 and about ninety people came into it before eleven, and a coir door mat was on the floor inside the rope, on a plastic sheet, and it had been there since before the room was shut on the thirty-first of December, and nobody in that building could say whose it was, and nobody went near it, and nobody had gone near it for fourteen days, and there was a card on a wire stand beside it at about knee height with four inches by six inches of card in it.

The card said **DOOR MAT** in a heading, and then four lines of description ending in the sentence *Function: a fixed-size surface for a person to stand on at a doorway while a person decides whether to open a door*, and then a line struck through, and then, under the line, in a finisher's own hand, four words.

> ***no name available***

**Nobody read the card. That is the finding of the morning and it is not a small one: about ninety people walked the length of that room in about fifty minutes, and about seventy of them got within four feet of a four-inch-by-six-inch card carrying the first sentence this building has ever printed in the position where a name goes, and not one of them stopped.**

Elias Venn noticed. He was standing at the west end of the rope with his hands behind his back at about eleven past ten, having come in through the front door ten minutes before the doors opened because he had nowhere to be and because he is a man who arrives before the doors, and because he is not spending the pound in his pocket on a mat and could not have told anybody why.

**He is not a night guard.** He has not been a night guard since the eighth of December 2024. He is thirty-two and he has no post, no card, no key, no radio, no torch, no employer, a pound, a new pocket notebook bought on a corner for thirty-five pence with nine pages in it, and a municipal remittance that arrives on the first of the month at cost centre M.Q.S. 4 for a post that no longer exists. He is on a payroll. He is in a building that pays him and he is not allowed past the second landing, and both of those are true at the same time and neither of them is temporary.

He read the card four times. He read the description. He read the four words under the line, and he read the maker's line above it, which is a line through a space where a name goes, and he stood there and worked out, almost at once, that he had heard that description read out loud in this room on the twenty-second of December to a man of about sixty-five, and that it was the same description, word for word, and that he was the only person alive who could have checked.

**He did not check it.** That is the fourteenth time. He has a list. He is not going to write it down again.

---

**The room itself is the oldest thing he has ever been inside and nobody in this building has ever said so in a public room.**

Thirty-one plinths down the middle of it. A rope line. Nine of the plinths carry things and the other twenty-two carry cards explaining why they do not carry things, and the nine are carried patterns and four of the nine are threshold things, and the ninth of the nine, at the east end, is a bell in a sealed case with a staple repair in its shoulder, and there is a panel taped over the card of the plinth in front of it, at a slight angle, printed by a woman of fifty-four at a kitchen table in Low Ward, and the tape has gone the colour of weak tea.

**Forty-seven days.** It went up on the twenty-seventh of November and it was still up, and the requester had not come for it, and a requester's panel is not a Museum's to take down.

And the bell gave no note. It gave no note at about eleven, and it gave no note at about half past one, and there were ninety people in the room on the first day it was open, and it has now said nothing since four minutes past eleven on Sunday the thirteenth of December 2024, which was forty people, and **nobody in this building knows whether losing a gallery and a fortnight to a coir mat was a thing it did or a thing that happened, and it is not asking for anything, and it is not going to be asked about anything.**

---

**At about a quarter past ten, on the fourth floor, in a systems office with the blinds down, a man of twenty-five had a form in front of him that he had taken out of a drawer at about ten past nine and had been looking at for an hour.**

He had known about clause seven since the second week of January, which was the second week he had been in post, and the reason he had not used it was that he had never had anything to use it on. He had said so out loud, twice, to two different people, in a loading bay and in a corridor, in the last fortnight, and both times he had said it the way a man says a thing he has been carrying and has finally put down.

"Ivo. What is a name field for."

"For finding him. That's what it does. It's not for the name."

**He had it in his head in a loop and it was because he was frightened, and men who are frightened make jokes, and Ivo Pell was twenty-five and frightened because he had a form and a day and about seven hours of it left.**

> **DISTRICT ADDRESSING SERVICE — SUPPLY AGREEMENT, SUBSCRIBER QUERY.**
> **Clause 7. One line. One subscriber. Twenty working days from the effective date of the line queried.**
> **Subscriber: MORROWGATE MUSEUM OF QUIET SAINTS. Subscriber no. and name of the contact to be given by the subscriber.**
> **Line: ADDRESS. FIELD. VALUE SOUGHT, WITH DATE.**
> **This form is not a public document. Retained by the addressing service. Not a copy to the subscriber unless the subscriber has asked for one in the box below.**

There were eleven boxes and the eleventh one said *copy to subscriber: yes / no*, and there was no box anywhere on the form that said *why*.

**And that is the whole of the morning and the whole of the next four days, and it is on the page because a man of twenty-five was in a room with it: there is no box on the form that says why.**

---

**The telephone rang at about half past eleven and it was a woman of about fifty in an office four miles away who had never been asked for anything in six years.**

He took it on the fourth ring because he was not expecting it and because he has a system that is: if the building rings, answer it, and if it is somebody you do not know, ask them which floor they are on before anything else.

"Morrowgate Museum of Quiet Saints, systems."

"Marsh Lane. District addressing service. My name's Toft. I am ringing you and I am not going to pretend I have a reason, because the honest reason is that I have got a form in front of me with a box on it and I have to fill the box in at four o'clock this afternoon, and the box says *no query received*, and I have never had to fill that box in."

"Ivo Pell. Systems."

"That is the second time somebody in your building has given me that name. Right. **Then I am going to tell you what the box is and then I am going to ask you a question I have no authority to ask, and if you do not answer it I am going to fill the box in at four o'clock and that will be the end of it.** The box exists because your twenty working days on the last of the changes run out at the close of today, and in six years nobody has ever queried a line, and today is the day it has run out for the ninth time. **And then, out of order, because it is the only part of this I care about: why does a museum want to know what a field said on a Wednesday in December.**"

---

**And he answered her the way you answer a competent person, which is by giving her the true thing and then the boring thing and then the true thing again.**

"Because we can't date it ourselves. That's the whole of it. Nine lines in our copy of your file went blank in the second week of December and we can't tell you when, or in what order, or whether it was nine on one day or one a day for nine days, because the block we read from isn't versioned. It gets overwritten every morning at about a quarter past four and whatever was in it yesterday isn't anywhere. And we have a print run of the second of December with every name on it and a print run of the twenty-first of December with nine blanks in it, and both of them are correct, and there's nothing in between."

"You want the intermediate values."

"I want to know what your file said on the fourth of December for one address, and I want to know it on the third as well, and I want to know the date your own record puts on the change."

"That's not a bad question," said Mrs Toft. "**That's the first sensible question anybody's asked me about a field in six years and I want you to know that it is sensible and not interesting. Everybody who rings me wants the reason. You want the value.**"

"I don't want the reason."

"Nobody wants the reason. **The reason is a code and a legend and a contractor's operator number and I could not give you the reason if you asked me for it, and the reason is not the point, and if you turn up in March with a solicitor asking me why a field went blank I will tell him the same thing, which is that the field is maintained from returns and walk surveys and is not a function of this authority.**"

"Then I won't bring a solicitor in March."

"Don't do that either," she said. "Do it exactly the way you're doing it, on the form, one line, and put your dates on it, and it takes four minutes and nobody has to be careful."

---

**And then she asked the three questions, and they were the three that matter and she asked them in about eleven seconds and she asked them in order, and he answered all three, and it is on the page because the whole of this business is that somebody competent asks three questions in the right order.**

"One. Are you the contact for this subscriber."

"Yes. Systems technician, four years, second name on the file."

"Two. Is anybody else in your institution going to read the answer."

"Three people. A registrar, a systems index, and a sub-group of an inquiry that sits in March."

"Three." She said it flat. "Then you are not the only person who is going to see it and you have told me that, which almost nobody tells me, and I am going to write down that you told me. **And the question under that one, which is the one that actually matters, is whether the answer contains a person's name.**"

"Yes."

"Right." He heard her put a pen down, and then pick it up again, and then put it down. "**Then I am going to tell you what I do, and it is the only thing I do, and I have never had to do it before.** I print the value, for the line, for the dates, on a sheet, and I put the date and the time on it, and I put my name on it, and it goes in the file with the query, and it is not a public document and it is not a copy to you unless you tick the box. And it is a person's name on a piece of paper in a building four miles from yours, and that is the correct and only outcome, and there is no version of this where I put it in an envelope and post it to you, because an envelope is a copy and a copy is a thing that goes in a bag, and I have spent six years watching people put things in bags."

"Copy to subscriber," said Ivo Pell.

"Say it properly, I want to hear whether you mean it."

"**Yes. Tick the box. Copy to subscriber, and put my address on it, and if it needs a signature I will drive it over and stand in your corridor.**"

"Good."

---

**He put the phone down at about twenty to twelve and then he did not write the four sentences for about an hour and a half, and the reason he did not write them is that he has been a person who writes things down for six weeks and every single thing he has written down has turned out to be a fact about a person that a person did not know was a fact about a person, and he was aware, standing at a desk on the fourth floor of a building he is allowed into on a card with the word WITNESS in a box, that this was the first document he had ever been in a position to help make, and that it was about somebody's mother.**

He wrote them at about ten past one, on a sheet of Museum notepaper, and then he typed them into the box on the form, and the form is four paragraphs and the four paragraphs are these, and they are on the page because they are the first sentences anybody in this business has ever written about a person for a stranger, and there are four of them and it took him an hour and a half and about nine false starts.

> **1.** I am the systems technician for the Morrowgate Museum of Quiet Saints, which is a subscriber under the district supply agreement, and I am writing under clause 7 about one line, and the line is 88 Sillick Lane, and my name is on the subscriber contact field and has been for four years.
>
> **2.** What I am asking for is the value of the occupancy field on that line on 3 December 2024 and on 4 December 2024, and the effective date your own record gives for the change to that field. I am not asking why it changed. I am not asking you to tell me anything about the circumstances of the household at that address, or of the person named in the field, or of the connection of services there.
>
> **3.** I am asking because nine lines in this Museum's copy of your file went blank between a print run of 2 December 2024, which has a value in every row, and the state of our block on 21 December 2024, which has nothing in nine of them, and because our block is not versioned and retains no superseded values, and therefore this Museum cannot state a date, and a date is the only thing anybody in this building has asked me for since the first of December.
>
> **4.** I understand that the answer will contain a person's name, and I am asking for it on that basis and not in spite of it, and I am the person in this institution who is accountable for a file I cannot read the history of, and I have a registrar in this building who will decide what happens to the answer before it is used, and I am telling you her name in advance so that you know it is a woman's decision and not a technician's.

He read it four times. He changed one word in the second paragraph. He did not change anything else. He sent it at about ten past two on the afternoon of Wednesday the thirteenth of January 2025, which was the twentieth working day, and which was the morning the Hall of Former Faiths had been open for two hours and twenty minutes, and he was not in the Hall and could not have been in the Hall because he was on a systems day and the Hall is on the first floor and the systems office is on the fourth and there is no reason on earth for a systems technician to be standing in a gallery at eleven o'clock on a Wednesday.

---

**The answer came at ten past four the same afternoon and it took Mrs Toft four minutes to produce it, and she has said since that she cannot account for the four minutes, and that the file is ten sheets from the front of a binder that is the first one on the left.**

> **DISTRICT ADDRESSING SERVICE.**
> **Value of field OCCUPANCY, subscriber MORROWGATE MUSEUM OF QUIET SAINTS (threshold group notification, Hall of Former Faiths), line 88 SILLICK LN.**
>
> **3 December 2024 — `DUNNING, F.`**
> **4 December 2024 — `DUNNING, F.`**
>
> **Effective date of change carried on the district schedule for this line: 5 December 2024. Applied to the subscriber file: 6 December 2024, 04:11. Source prefix `DS-NCO-`.**
>
> **The value on 4 December 2024 is a billing-contact field. It is the name given to this address as the person or organisation responsible for the connection of services at it. It is not a register of residents, a register of electors, or a list of persons.**
>
> **E. TOFT, records officer. 13 January 2025, 16:10.**

**"It's a name we already had," said Ivo Pell, to a screen, at about a quarter past four, to nobody. "That's the name. That came back marked undeliverable on the fifteenth of December and it's in a box behind a boiler in this building with four hundred and eleven other things in it. I could have had it in a plastic wallet since the twenty-fourth of December and I didn't, and neither did he, and neither did anybody."**

That is true and it is the correct finding and it is also, on its own, a very small result, and Ivo Pell looked at it for about eleven seconds and then said the thing that turned the morning over, out loud, to an empty room, because he is twenty-five and he was alone.

"**She didn't have to look for it. Four minutes, and it was ten sheets from the front of a binder on the left. Four years I've had a file coming down every morning and a woman four miles away has had the whole of it filed by date in a drawer, and neither of us has ever once asked the other a question.**"

---

**And at about ten to five he told the Director, in a corridor, because the Director asks him about systems things on Wednesdays and it saves him a telephone call on Thursday, and because the minute of the thirteenth of December is going to have one line in it about the clause and he would rather write that line than have it written for him.**

He did not have a card and he did not have standing and he was not the subscriber. That is the whole of the chapter and it is the first time in this case that a lawful door has opened on the correct side of a building and he has been standing on the wrong side of it, and he has been on notice with a card with WITNESS in a box, in company, not before eight, and it is a real door and it works, and it is not his.

"I can't ask it," he said, and it came out of him before he had decided to say it, and he had not meant to say it to her.

"You can't ask it. Correct."

"I wrote it," he said. "This afternoon. Four paragraphs, about a person, to a stranger, and I have known about that clause for four years and I have never had a reason to use it, and the reason I had a reason to use it today is that a man with a notebook stood in a systems office on Monday and said *what is a name field for*, and he is not the subscriber either, and **he cannot be, and I never told him he could not be, and I should have.**"

"You should not have told him anything. What happens in this building about systems happens in this building." She looked at the sheet. "Say it the other way round, then. What is the minute going to say."

"That a subscriber queried a line under clause 7 on the thirteenth of January, in writing, in four paragraphs, and that the answer was produced in four minutes and it was a name this institution already held, and that the registrar decided what happened to it, and that no document was taken by a witness who was in the room." He squared the sheet on the ledge, writing uppermost, which is what Mara Iles did to him on the twenty-second of November. "**And that the person who asked the question was not the person who wrote it, and I am both of those, and one of us is in a disciplinary file four miles away, and the one that is in the file did not write a word of it.**"

"That is a sentence about you."

"That is a sentence about a procedure," said Elias Venn. "It happens to have a person in it. That is the whole of what I have learned in seven weeks and I have learned it from a woman who refused me in a library."

Anja Ward picked the sheet up. She read all four paragraphs, and she read paragraph four twice, and then she said the thing that is on the page because she is a Director who has been caught out exactly once in nineteen years.

"**Say that last sentence in the minute. That you were not the subscriber and could not be, and that a person who was not you did it, and that the person who did it is twenty-five and has four years in post. Do not let anybody in March turn that into the Museum asking a question. The Museum did not ask a question. A man asked a question and he was not allowed to, and he found the man who was, and that is the whole of the institution's contribution and it is four sentences and a form.**"

---

And at about twenty past five Elias Venn came out of the Hall of Former Faiths, having been in it for six hours and ten minutes, and the last thing he had done in it was read a card four times and not check anything, and the first thing he did when he got out into the cold was to stand on the pavement and work out whether he was going to be able to sleep, which he could not, and why, which was that he had spent a day in a public room with a hundred and forty years of a rope line in it and about ninety people in it and a card that said *no name available*, and not one person in the room had asked what the card meant, and he had wanted to explain it to every single one of them, and he could not explain it to anybody, because to explain it you have to start with a delivery office on Nether Kiln Lane, and by the time you have said that you are talking about a name field in a system, and nobody who has come to look at a bell in a glass case wants to hear about a name field in a system.

**That is the machine. Not the code. Not the four miles. A card in a public room, and a man who cannot say one word about it in the ninety seconds anybody will give him.**

He wrote it out at about half past eleven that night, at a table in a rented room in Low Ward, on the third page of a book with nine pages in it, in the middle of it and not at the end, and then he did not tear it out, because tearing it out is a thing he would do on his own in a room with a door shut.

> **Wed 13 Jan, 23:30. The Hall is open. About ninety in it before eleven and about a hundred and forty through the door by four. The mat is on the floor on a plastic sheet inside the rope and a card says DOOR MAT and under a struck-through maker's line it says no name available in a finisher's hand.**
> **I read the description four times and then I stood there and worked out that I had heard that same description read out loud in that room on the twenty-second of December to Frank Dunning, word for word, and I am the only person alive who could have checked whether the mat on the floor is the mat he carried out of the building in a plastic bag on the twenty-fourth, and I did not check it. Fourteenth.**
> **Nobody in that room read the card. Seventy people walked within four feet of it. Not one stopped. I have been trying to work out all afternoon why that is, and it is not because they do not care, it is because there is no word on it that asks a question. A bell in a case asks you a question. A card with a space in it asks you nothing at all, and a thing that asks you nothing can be walked past by a person for fifty years and it can be true for a hundred and twenty.**
> **Clause seven closed at ten past two this afternoon. I am not the subscriber and cannot be. A man of twenty-five wrote four sentences about a person to a stranger and one of the four says that what is coming back is a person's name, and it took a records officer four minutes to find it, and she said in the tone of somebody stating a fact about a drawer that nobody had ever asked her for anything, and that was true, and the answer is a name we have had in a plastic wallet since the twenty-fourth of December.**
> **And on the sheet is a line I have not looked at yet. She has printed it because she is competent and I am not going to be able to sleep tonight and I have got nine pages and a pencil and a boiler, and it says the effective date is the fifth of December and the district applied it at eleven minutes past four on the sixth, and the district applied it on a Sunday, and the district does not key anything on a Sunday morning.**
> **A name field is for the bill.**
> **And I still do not know what it is for, and I have not got a stop word, and I have not been in a room in six weeks that I could walk into, and the comparison is on Monday and I have got four days with nothing in them, and I am going to spend two of them outside a pound shop on Fisher Lane doing arithmetic I have no business doing.**
