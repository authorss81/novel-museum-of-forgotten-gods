# Chapter 0057 — The School

St Anselm's is on Hall Lane and it is a two-form-entry school of about three hundred and eighty children and about two hundred adults, and it was built in about 1871 for the children of the mill, and the school has kept its roll on a corridor wall in a folder since about 1948, and the folder is the best name-to-place record within four miles of the building, and on the twenty-ninth of December 2024 nobody there knew that it was the best name-to-place record within four miles of anything.

The first thing anybody in that building did about a shrine in a classroom was the correct thing, on the correct day, with the correct document, and it took about nine minutes.

---

**It was found on Monday the twenty-second of December at about four in the afternoon by a caretaker, and it was found because it was in the way.**

The room is a Year 5 classroom on the ground floor with two external doors, one to the corridor and one onto the yard, and the yard door is an exit, and the exit is on a fire risk assessment that a woman of fifty-four updates every August, and the assessment is a document about eleven pages long and it has a line on it that says *no obstruction at any exit or entrance, including temporary items*, and the word *including* is on the line and the *including* is the whole of it.

On the wall by the yard door there was a table. On the table there was a cloth, and on the cloth a candle in a jar with a wick in it that had never been lit since anybody could remember, and a card standing against a jar of coins, and a photograph in a plastic frame of a woman who was not alive. And against the leg of the table, standing on end in a fold, was a door mat.

Coir face, rubber backing, seven hundred by four hundred, rolled.

"It's blocking the push bar," said the caretaker. "You can't get the door open with your hip and there's a bin against the other side of it."

He moved the table eleven inches. He did not open the mat. He rolled it out onto the floor of the corridor with his foot, because it was in the way and because he is a caretaker, and he put it against the wall of the corridor opposite a radiator, and **that is where it was on Monday morning, and about four inches of its edge was on the floor of a corridor that every child in that school walks down eleven times a day.**

**And that is how a household object reaches three hundred and eighty children. Not by a god. By a push bar, and a word on a form, and a caretaker with a foot, and an object put against the wall opposite a radiator because a corridor is warm and that is where a thing goes.**

**And it happened on the same afternoon that a man of about sixty-five carried one up the hill to a museum in a plastic bag, four miles and four miles from the other, and nobody knew, and nobody will ever find out from this book, and it is on the page because it is the whole of the second movement of this case and it is going to be in a file in March.**

---

**Marion Kelsh found out on the Tuesday morning, the twenty-third, at about eight, and dealt with it in about nine minutes, and she is fifty-four, and she is the school business manager, and she has done that job for nineteen years, and there is no mysticism in her at all.**

She did four things and they are all on a piece of paper.

She read her own fire risk assessment, at the desk, in about forty seconds, and found the line.

She went down to the corridor with a clipboard.

She took a photograph of the mat on the floor, in the corridor, on her own telephone, with a scale rule in the frame, because she has done that for a school insurance file since 2011, and she has never in her life needed anybody's permission for it, and she is not Museum staff and is not subject to a condition dated the first of December and does not know that a condition dated the first of December exists anywhere in this city.

And she filled in a form. Not the Museum's. Not a Registry form. Not a form about gods, or objects, or classification, or carried patterns, or a determination, or a rope line.

> **ST ANSELM'S (HALL LANE). FIRE RISK ASSESSMENT — ANNUAL REVIEW, RECORD OF ACTION.**
> **Item found: obstruction at exit, external door, ground floor, corridor side. Object: personal item brought into a classroom by a pupil. Removed from the entrance and stored.**
> **Date found 22.12.24. Corrected 23.12.24, 08:40. Action by: M. Kelsh, school business manager.** *(Form untouched. The form is a school form and has no amendment box in it, and nobody had to find one.)*
> **Storage: school office, locked filing cabinet, shelf B, item logged on the premises inventory at line 214.**

**"Stored,"** she said, when a supply teacher asked her at about nine o'clock on the Tuesday why it was in her filing cabinet, **"because the assessment says it can't be in a corridor, and because it belongs to a child, and because a thing that belongs to a child is not a thing this school has any business throwing out. So it's on the inventory. Line 214. It's got a line number. It's got a number, which is the school number, not an accession number, and it is a number and it is on a piece of paper and the piece of paper is in a folder in a drawer in a building that has never heard of a museum."**

---

**The Museum found out about the mat on Thursday the twenty-fourth, at about half past ten in the afternoon, because a woman telephoned the front desk and Margaret Priest took the message and put it on a board, which is what the board is for, and the Museum shut at six that evening for four days, and the message sat on the board over the weekend, which is the correct behaviour of a board and is the reason nothing happened until the twenty-ninth.**

She rang at about half past ten. She was not on the notification list. She was a person who had a mat in her corridor.

"I'm not going to pretend I know what your museum does," Marion Kelsh said, later, in a corridor, to two people. "I'm going to tell you what I know. On Friday a caretaker moved a table out of the way of a fire door and rolled a mat into our corridor, and on Saturday about forty children went round it, and on Sunday the head said we were having a discussion about it in assembly and I said no, and I said no because it is a mat and a woman in a photograph and a jar of coins, and the day we put that in an assembly is the day thirty-eight children in one room have got a shrine in it, and there is one person in this school responsible for that and it is me and it is not a decoration."

She said the rest of it in a corridor at eleven o'clock on the Tuesday with the door of a classroom shut behind her, and Elias Venn was in that corridor because he had come to the school at about ten o'clock on the Tuesday morning, the twenty-ninth, alone, uninvited, unannounced, and with no reason that anybody could audit, and he was let in by a man at a gate who asked him which child he had come for and he said *none* and the man said *there's nobody to see* and he said *I know, I'll wait by the office* and the man let him stand by the office, and he stood by the office for about an hour, and that is the whole of his standing in this case and it is better than any door he has ever had.

"I've read the determination," Marion Kelsh said. "It came in the post in November, a card about an object in a gallery, and it went in a box in an office and it sat in a box in an office for a month because we had a term. And I read it over the Christmas and it says the object is displayed and may not be touched and it says a rope, and I want to know one thing and I'm asking it properly and if somebody wants to tell me no, tell me no. **Is that the thing in our corridor.**"

"No," said Mara Iles.

"You don't know."

"**I know exactly what it is," said Mara Iles, "and no, it is not that object, and the fact that I know exactly what it is and you do not is the whole of the last four days of my life and I would like to tell you about it, but I am going to do it in the order I have to do it in, and the order is a document."**

And the document was the resolution of the sub-group of the eighteenth of November 2024, and Mara Iles read it out in a corridor at St Anselm's at about ten past eleven on Tuesday the twenty-ninth of December, in full, to a woman of fifty-four and a man of thirty-two and nobody else, and it is on the page because it is four lines and because it is the first time in six weeks that anybody had read it to somebody who had not already read it.

> **RESOLUTION OF THE SUB-GROUP, 18 November 2024.**
> **That the Hall of Former Faiths remains open, and the school term continues, and no party not holding a collections pass is to be admitted to that room while the object is under Annexe E; and that no school group is to be admitted to that room at all; and that the hall certificate stands.**
> **Noted: the term booking of St Anselm's (Hall Lane) for 11.00 on 7 November 2024, ref. SK/BK/0912, was struck through on 18 November and initialled, and no person has since asked by whom.**

"It is four lines and it is the best piece of drafting any of us has done this year," said Mara Iles, "and it is a resolution about **that room**, and it is a resolution about **that room** and it has been read eleven times in six weeks by six people in this building and every one of them read it as a rule about schools. It is not a rule about schools. It is a rule about a room."

"So it doesn't cover us."

"It does not cover you, and it has never covered you, and **it has never been read to a school.**" She put the sheet away. "And I have been in this business nine years and I have signed about four hundred conditions and I have never once in my life read one out to somebody outside the building, and I have just read one out in a corridor in Hall Lane, and the reason I have done it is that you have got a mat in a corridor and I have got a form that I cannot use, and the two of us are the only people in this business who have ever had to write down the same sentence."

"Then what happens to the mat."

"It comes to Civic Hill," said Mara Iles, "and it gets described, and it gets proposed, and then the trouble starts, and I am going to tell you the trouble now rather than in three weeks because you have been extremely straight with me and because you have got about three hundred and eighty children in a building and I am not going to let that sit behind a form for ten days."

---

**The trouble is condition two of the Director's standing conditions of the fifteenth of October 2024, and it had already failed once, on **Monday the twenty-third of December**, in this building, over a different mat, and nobody in the corridor at a school in Hall Lane knew that, and nobody in this building knew it was about to fail again, and a condition does not get better by being asked twice.**

> **2.** No Schedule 12 carried object to be removed from the building except on a docket countersigned **by a person who is not an officer or employee of this Museum.** My signature is not sufficient.

A mat from a classroom is not a Schedule 12 carried object and never has been. But the moment anybody proposes that it be classified as one — and somebody must, because it is in a school, and a school is a building with children in it, and no institution in this city can have a question open about a thing on a corridor — the conditions begin to apply, and the second of them says that the thing cannot be removed from the building except on a docket countersigned by a person who is not an officer or employee of this Museum.

**And the only person who can countersign it is a person who is not an officer or employee, and the only such person who is connected to that object is a girl of nine in Year 5 whose name is written on the underside of the corner of it in ballpoint, and the Museum cannot establish that the name belongs to a person at the address written beside it, and the school will not release a pupil's property to a person the school cannot identify.**

The condition was written on the fifteenth of October by a Director of fifty-three to stop a Museum employee being the only signature on a document about a thing in a public room. It is the best condition anybody in this building has ever written. It is in force. It has not been displaced. And on **Monday the twenty-third of December** it produced, in a conservation office in this building, at about twenty past two in the afternoon, over a different mat, the following sentence, and then on the twenty-ninth it produced the same sentence again over this one, in a corridor in Hall Lane, and the sentence is:

"**We cannot give it back. The second condition says a person who is not an officer or employee of this Museum countersigns it, and the only person like that who has anything to do with it is a child, and I will not put a nine-year-old's name on a docket in this city to move a door mat, and the child's name is the only name I have, and it is on the mat in pencil, and I cannot prove it, and so we hold it, and holding it is the second condition working exactly as it was written to.**"

Nobody argued. There is nobody to argue with, because the condition does not have a subject. It has a box.

Anja Ward said one thing into the silence, and it was about herself, and it is the fourth time in two months she has said a thing into a silence that is the correct answer:

"**Then I have written a condition that can hold a door mat in a building for ever and I could not have told you that in October and I have been a director for nineteen years and I did not know, and I am not going to pretend I designed it and I am not going to pretend I did not.**"

---

**And then Elias Venn asked Marion Kelsh a question, and waited for the answer, and it took eleven seconds, and it is the only thing he did in that corridor that day.**

He had been standing by the office since ten o'clock. He had not been in the room. He had not been asked anything. At about twenty to three he asked her, in a corridor, with four children going past, and he waited, and she answered.

"You said you'd logged it. Line 214."

"On the premises inventory. It's a book. It's a hardback with a printed page and a number and it's got a column for the room and a column for the item and a column for who it belongs to, and it's been here since about 1994 and I write the lines myself on the last Friday of the month." She looked at him properly for the first time. "You're the one who came and stood by my office for an hour and didn't speak to anybody."

"Yes."

"Why."

"Because a man came to a school on a Monday with no reason and I thought you'd want to know that he'd come, and I did not know what else to do with him."

Marion Kelsh almost laughed and did not.

"Right. Well, I'll tell you what that is and then you can tell whoever you like. **Your museum has a card with a name on it, and my school has a book with a name on it, and the two of us are the only two places in this county that could put a name on that mat. And the school isn't going to, because it's a pupil's mat and the pupil is nine and I'm not putting a nine-year-old's name on a thing in a museum in January.**"

"Would the museum put one on?"

"No," said Marion Kelsh. "**And that's the bit I've been turning over since Friday. Because a museum's whole job is putting names on things. That's it. That's a museum. You put a name on a thing and you charge a pound and you put it behind glass and you let people come and look at it. And if your museum's as good as its form says it is then the one thing it will not do, all winter, with nobody watching, is put a name on a card that it can't stand behind. So no. And I'd have bet money on it in August and I wouldn't have believed the argument till Friday.**"

She looked down the corridor at a door mat lying against a wall opposite a radiator with four inches of it on the floor, and a wear hollow in it about a foot long, and thirty-eight children in a building that has three hundred and eighty of them in it.

"**And I'll tell you the other thing, because you'll write it down and I'd rather somebody did. My book has got a name and an address and a classroom in it for every child in this school, and I could write you that name in about four seconds, and I could put it on your card, and I am not going to, and it is not because of data protection and it is not because of a rule. It is because that child has got a mat by the door because it is the warm place by the radiator, and she stands on it every morning, and about thirty-eight children in that room do, and if I put her name on a card in a museum then she becomes a thing that is in a museum, and she is nine, and I would like her to be a person who stands on a mat.**"

And Elias Venn stood in a corridor at St Anselm's at about three o'clock on the afternoon of Tuesday the twenty-ninth of December 2024 and worked out, in about nine seconds, the entire shape of the next four days, and he was right about all of it, and it is on the page that he was right about all of it and that it took him nine seconds in a corridor while a caretaker's bin went past.

**There are two institutions within four miles of that mat that could put a name on it. A school, which will not, because she is nine. And a museum, which has a hundred and forty years of putting names on cards and has, in that whole time, exactly one recorded instance of a card in a public room going out with nothing in the field where the name goes, and that instance is the object at plinth eleven, and the label room will not do it twice.**

He wrote it out at about seven that night at a table in a rented room, and the last line of the entry for the twenty-ninth of December is the sentence that the thirtieth is built on, and it is the shortest one he has written since the twenty-seventh of November.


> **Tue 29 Dec, 05:40, school, in the dark, at a table. A school has a book with a name and a place in it and will not use it. A museum has a card with a name on it and has used one it could not support.**
> **Condition 2 failed on the twenty-third of December, in this building, over the mat at 88, and it has failed again this week over a mat in a school, and the reason both times is that the only person who is not an officer or employee of this Museum and who has anything at all to do with a mat of this kind is a child of nine.**
> **And it is the twenty-ninth and the Director is signing something tomorrow and I have not been told what it is and I did not ask, and the sub-group can call me and has not, and I have not been in a room since the twenty-second of December and the last time I was in one I was a witness.**
> **Three hundred and eighty children. Four hundred and twelve addresses. About fourteen doors. One pound.**
> **And I have still not been in a room, and there is going to be a card about this on the thirtieth, and I am barred from the building, and nobody has told me the day, and I did not ask, and that is the position I am in and I have had nine weeks to get used to it and I have not.**
