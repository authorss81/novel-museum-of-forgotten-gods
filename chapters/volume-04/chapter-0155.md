# Chapter 0155 — A Wage Schedule, And A Pencil

**On Thursday the sixteenth, Friday the seventeenth and Saturday the eighteenth of December 2025 a name came up out of the wax on the back of a counter on the fourth level of a building on Civic Hill, three layers down, in a hand nobody can attribute, and a registrar of twenty-eight spent two days going looking for the person it belonged to and found a payroll line and a piece of pencil and a shelf of ring binders with nothing in them.**

**Nobody has gone missing in the sense the word is usually used. A man has not been on a floor, and no one in this case can say when he stopped, because the two documents that would say it are a wage schedule and a piece of pencil and neither of them has a date on it in a form anybody can check. He is called Ivor Trask and he is fifty-eight and nobody in these three days has established where he is and nobody in these three days has established that he is not somewhere.**

**A wage schedule is a record of what a person was paid. It is not a record of what a person was. That is the whole of the difference and the whole of this chapter and it is said once, out loud, by a woman of twenty-eight, in a corridor, on the Saturday afternoon, and nobody improved on it.**

## And on the Thursday morning a workroom counter was wiped down and the name of a man came up out of the wax, and a curator of fifty-two read it and said she had seen it on a wage schedule

The fourth-level workroom is a room with a bench, a sink, a kettle, a rack of about nine trays, and a counter about two metres long along one wall, and the counter is where the condition forms are stacked and where the trays get put down and where about nine people a day put a cup of tea.

**The back of that counter has been kept, in pencil, as a night rota for longer than anybody in this building can date, and it has been kept the way a thing is kept when there is no printed version: by a person, on a surface, in a medium that comes off.**

The woman who wiped it down was a member of the conservation staff of about thirty-four on her own time, and she was doing it because somebody had told her to, and what she had been told to do was wipe the counters on the fourth level on a Thursday, and she was doing it with a cloth and a bottle and no more thought about it than that.

She got about four feet along the back of it and stopped.

"**There's writing under this.**"

"**There is always writing under it,**" said Menna Prothero, from the tray rack, without looking up. "**That counter has had four layers on it since I came and the bottom one is probably somebody's initials from 1988 and I have never wiped it off and I never will.**"

"**It's a name.**"

Then the cloth stopped moving, and a curator of fifty-two came and stood next to a member of the conservation staff of thirty-four and looked at about four inches of the back of a counter, and Elias Venn came and looked at it too, and there were three of them at a counter.

It was in pencil, on the third layer down, and it had survived because the three layers above it were not the same pencil and the wax of one does not lift the other. The letters were small and slightly leaning and they said **I. TRASK**, and there was a figure against three of the nights in about eleven different places, and the figure was a number of hours and the number of hours was always the same.

"**Say the name.**"

"**Ivor Trask,**" said Menna Prothero. "**And I have seen that name on a wage schedule and I have seen it about nine times, and it is a night establishment name, and the wage schedules for the nights are in a ring binder in the finance office on the second level and anybody may read them because they are a payroll document and a payroll document in this city is a thing the public is entitled to see.**"

"**You have known the name for how long.**"

"**Since about 2019,**" she said. "**And I have not said anything about it, and I am going to be asked why, and the reason is that I write the labels and I do the condition checks and I have never in nineteen years been the person who asks a question about a name on a rota, and that is a fact about my job and not about my character, and I have thought about it since you said the name out loud and I would rather it was asked in a room than on a counter.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**Say what a wage schedule is.**"

"**It is what an employer has to give anybody who asks for it,**" said Menna Prothero. "**It is a record of what a person was paid. It is not a record of what a person was. Those are two different things and everybody in this case knows that and everybody in this case has been treating the first one as though it were the second one for about four years, and the second one is in a different building.**"

## And on the Friday morning a registrar of twenty-eight went and looked for a person, and a records clerk of forty-four took her to a steel shelf, and there are fifty-two ring binders on it, one a year from 1974, and personnel files are never retired and his was never opened

The level-two archive corridor has a steel shelf and the shelf has annual ring binders on it, one binder a year from 1974 and every year present, and there are fifty-two of them, which is a year for every year since the shelf was put up and not a selection, and a personnel file reference is `PF` and three digits and a personnel file is never retired, and a `PF` number is allocated when a file is opened, and a file is opened when a person is appointed.

"**How do you open one.**"

"**You get a form off the wall in the personnel office, and the form wants a name, a start date, a post, an establishment, and a supervisor's signature, and the supervisor signs it and the number goes on it and the number is written on the spine in about four seconds, and then it goes on this shelf, and then it is never retired.**" Rhiannon Iddon put her hand flat on the 2019 binder. "**That is it. That is the whole procedure. It takes about nine minutes and about four people and the only thing anybody in this building is ever asked to sign is the supervisor's line.**"

"**Which is yours.**"

"**Which is mine for the fourth level and it was mine in 2019 and it is mine now, and I have signed about four hundred appointment forms in nineteen years,**" she said, "**and I could not tell you one of them now, and I have not looked at one of them since about 2014, and that is not a complaint about myself, it is what a file is.**"

She opened the 2019 binder on a table in the corridor, standing up, because nobody sits down in that corridor.

A page holds twenty-eight ruled lines and an appointment form is seven of them, so there are four forms to a page, and the 2019 binder has about two hundred and ten forms in it in nineteen months, which is about fifty-three pages, and the spine of each one has a `PF` number on it in her round hand, and the numbers run in order, and there are no gaps.

"**Say the numbers.**"

"**They run in order and there are no gaps,**" said Rhiannon Iddon. "**Which means two things and the first one is good. It means nobody has taken a binder off this shelf, because a gap in a run of `PF` numbers is exactly what a missing binder looks like and there is not one.**"

"**And the second.**"

"**And the second is that there is no page in this binder with a name on it that is not on a form, and there is no form in this binder for anybody who was not appointed, and there is no `PF` number on this shelf for a man called Ivor Trask, and I have gone through 2018, 2019 and 2020 and the four after that with a finger on the spines and I have not found one.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**Say what that is not.**"

"**It is not a disposal. Nothing has been destroyed. Nothing has been shredded, nothing has been taken off this shelf, nothing has been lost, and I can prove every one of those four things in about nine seconds and I am not going to because they are not the point.**" She shut the binder. "**A file is never retired. A file is opened when somebody is appointed. There is no process in this city by which a file exists for a person who was never appointed, because the appointment is what opens it, and if there is no appointment then there is nothing to retire, and nothing to retire means there is nothing to look for, and that is not a hole that somebody made. That is a shape that nothing was ever put into.**"

"**So he was never appointed.**"

"**So there is no form that says he was ever appointed, and I am not going to say he was never appointed, because a form is a record of an appointment and not a record of a person and a person is not a form, and I have been in this building nineteen years and I am not going to learn that lesson from a payroll line.**"

## And on the Friday afternoon at about half past two a woman of fifty-one who runs the casual night establishment said three things, and none of them was a name, and she has never met him and she is not a villain and nobody improved on her

Hilary Voss has run the casual establishment for the nights in this building for nine years. "**Say what an establishment is.**"

"**It is casual,**" said Hilary Voss. "**It is thirty-eight people on a casual contract and casual means you are called when you are needed and you say yes or no and you are paid for what you do. It is not a department. It is not a roster. It is a list of people who can be phoned, and the phone calls are how it works, and there is no form for a phone call.**"

"**And the name.**"

"**The name is on the schedule because the hours were on the schedule, and the hours were on the schedule because somebody on the floor wrote them on a sheet, and the sheet came to me, and I built the schedule, and I signed it, and I did not meet him and I do not know whether he worked here.**"

"**The fourth is that in about 2018 I had a telephone call from a man who said he had somebody who could do nights, and he gave me a name and a date of birth and I wrote it on a sheet and I put the name on the establishment list, and I did not take a reference, and the reason I did not take a reference is that casual night cover on the fourth level has not taken a reference since before I did this job and there is no box on the form for one and there has never been one.**"

"**So what do you have.**"

"**I have a payroll line in a ring binder on the second level for a run in 2019, and about nine lines before it with the same name on them, and a date of birth of the eleventh of June 1967 on every one of them, and a payroll number, and an establishment code, and the hours, and the hours are always the same, and I have looked at about nine of those lines this afternoon and they are all the same man and all the same hours and not one of them is a different person, and that is the whole of what I have.**"

"**And the pencil.**"

"**And a piece of pencil on the back of a counter with a name on it three layers down,**" said Hilary Voss, "**and I have not seen it until today and I am not going to tell you what I feel about that because I do not have to and you did not ask.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**Say the other half,**" said Mara Iles.

"**The other half is that I am not the person who is wrong here and I am not going to let anybody make me into the person who is wrong here, and I will tell you exactly what I did and it was this: I put a name on a rota that a man gave me on a telephone and I paid him for hours on a floor and I checked the hours, and I never once went and looked at a ring binder on the second level to see whether the name on my schedule was a person this building had appointed, and I did not do that because in nine years it has never occurred to me to do that, and I would like it written down that it had never occurred to me, because somebody is going to come and ask me whether I knew, and the answer is that I did not know and I did not think about it and that is the whole of what happened and there is no version of it where I did anything else.**"

"**And I am not resigning over it and I am not going to be asked to, and if anybody in this case wants to treat me as the person who let a man disappear out of a building then they are going to have to do it in a room with about nine people in it and I will say this again.**"
