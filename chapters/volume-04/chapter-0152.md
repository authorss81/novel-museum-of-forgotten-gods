# Chapter 0152 — What A Floor Is For

**On Wednesday the eighth and Thursday the ninth of December 2025 a registrar of twenty-eight was asked again about a question she had asked in a car park in September, and was told that the asking of it is on somebody else's desk, and did not get an answer. On the Thursday a corridor on the fourth level of a building on Civic Hill was walked from the west stair to about two metres short of its east end by three people, and a curator of fifty-two who has been on that floor for nineteen years asked out loud what a floor is for, and answered it in about two seconds, and the answer was *for the labels*, and it is the right answer, and nobody in the corridor improved on it.**

**There is no supernatural event in this volume and this chapter is where the reader is told that by being given a corridor instead. Nothing opens. Nothing sounds. A man of thirty-two stands at the end of about ninety metres of a passage with his hands in his coat and is the only person in this case who can hold a thing, and there is nothing in that building he is permitted to hold, and the chapter does not ask him to.**

## And on the Wednesday morning in a records office on the ground floor a curator of fifty-two asked a registrar of twenty-eight a question in about four seconds, and the registrar told her the asking of it was on somebody else's desk, and the curator did not get an answer either, and she said she had not come for one

The records office is a room with two desks in it, a plan chest, a shelf, and a window that looks at the back of the service lane, and it is where the quarterly binder is brought when a quarter has to be reconciled against something, and a quarter had to be reconciled against something because a quarterly binder is a document about a period and the only other document about that period on the fourth level is a book in a drawer and nobody had opened the drawer yet.

There were four people in it. Elias Venn was in it as a member of the public who had been walked in by a registrar at about ten past nine and who was sitting on a plan chest with his coat on, which is what a person without a post is in a building with a post.

**Menna Prothero is fifty-two. She has been Curator of the Quiet Wing, west range, fourth level, for nineteen years, and she came into this building in 2006 to be a conservation assistant and has been on that floor since about 2007, and she writes the labels, and when she says she writes the labels she means she writes them by hand in the label room and cuts them herself and has never once had them printed.**

"**Ask me the thing,**" she said.

"**I have not asked you anything.**"

"**You have been in this room for four minutes and you have not asked me anything and you have not looked at my book, and you were in a car park in September and you asked the Director a question and she did not answer you, and I was not there and I have not asked you about it, and I am not asking you about it. I am asking you the other one.**"

"**Say the other one.**"

"**Is a label a record of the person the object belongs to.**"

Nobody said anything. Rhiannon Iddon, who was forty-four and had kept the Museum's accession records for nineteen years and who had brought a quarterly binder to a table and had not opened it, put her hands flat on the binder.

"**Say the whole of it,**" said Mara Iles, "**because you have said it in about four words and I want to know which four words you mean.**"

"**Is a label a record of the person the object belongs to,**" said Menna Prothero. "**Not of the object. Of the person. Because that is the question I have been living inside for nineteen years and I have never once been asked it and I have asked myself a version of it about nine times a year, and about four times a year I get to a place where I think I know the answer, and then I go and do a label, and the label tells me I do not.**"

"**And you have asked it.**"

"**Once. In a car park on Marsh Lane, at about ten past six in the evening in September, and the answer was no, and then the answer was nothing, and then it was a change of subject, and I did not press it and I am not going to tell you that I pressed it, and the amount of pressing that happened was none.**"

"**Say the date.**"

"**The eighteenth of September. I have it in a diary and I have not read the diary since.**"

Mara Iles stood with her back to the plan chest for about nine seconds.

"**Say what you want,**" said Elias Venn.

"**I want it written down that somebody asked it,**" said Menna Prothero, "**and I have wanted that since the eighteenth of September and it has taken me the whole of October and November and a quarterly binder to get here, and I am not asking you to answer it and I would be surprised and a bit worried if you did.**"

"**Then here is the answer to a different question,**" said Mara Iles, "**which is where the asking is. The asking of that question is not mine and it has not been mine since September, and it is on somebody else's desk, and I have written it in my own book and not in a minute, and I am not going to hand the book over and I am not going to ring the person, and if you want it moved then you have to move it and I am not going to help you move it.**"

"**Who.**"

"**I am not going to tell you who and I am not going to lie to you about it either, and the two of those are the same sentence, and I have said that sentence in this case about four other things.**" She turned round. "**And you have not got an answer out of me either, and I want that written down in your diary as well, because in about four years one of us is going to be asked which of us knew what and we are both going to reach for the same half of this morning.**"

Nobody improved on that.

"**I do not need an answer,**" said Menna Prothero. "**I have needed nineteen years and I have got about four days a month of it and it is enough for the work.**"

She put two fingers on the table, not on the binder, and about a metre from it.

"**What I do need is somebody to know that the question exists. That is the whole of what I came down here for. I have been on that floor since 2007 and I am the only person who writes the labels on it and nobody above me knows that the labels are not decoration. I have said that to a Director once, in a car park, and got a change of subject, and I did not raise it again because I was tired that week and because I am not a fool, and it turns out those are the same thing.**"

## And on the Thursday morning a registrar walked a man with no post up a stair to a floor he is not employed on, and neither of them said why, and the ninth week of a school year, which is a thing nobody in that corridor had any reason to think about, was not mentioned either

## And on the Thursday at about twenty past eleven a curator of fifty-two asked out loud what a floor is for, in a corridor, and answered it in about two seconds, and a man of thirty-two asked her the same question four minutes later and got the same answer, and nobody in ninety metres of passage improved on it

The corridor on the fourth level of the west range runs about ninety metres from the west stair to the east end of it, and it is a corridor: a strip of floor, a run of cupboards on the north side with the door numbers on brass strips, a run of nothing on the south side because the south side is the plant and the plant is a wall, a strip light in the ceiling that is on a sensor and came on in three places and not in the rest of it, and a smell of paper and cold rubber and the particular dust of a floor where the windows are not.

There is a door at the east end.

They came to about two metres short of it and stopped, and nobody said why, and it was not because of anything anybody knew, and then they went back to the cupboards, because the cupboards are where the work is.

"**Right,**" said Menna Prothero, at about two metres short of the east end, to nobody, in the voice of a woman who has been alone on a floor for a long time and has thought a sentence through more than once. "**What is a floor for.**"

Mara Iles did not answer it. Elias Venn did not answer it.

"**For the labels,**" said Menna Prothero.

She said it in about two seconds and she did not stop afterwards, and then, about four minutes later, Elias Venn asked her the same question in the same corridor, and she gave the same answer in about two seconds, and nobody improved on it then either.

Nobody in this case has ever written down what a floor is for. There is no page one for a floor. A collection has a schedule and a schedule has a purpose and the purpose is printed in the top corner. A floor has a use, and a use is a noun, and the noun in this building is *storage* on the paperwork of about four years ago and *quiet* on the sign at the stair, and neither of those is an answer and both of them are true.

"**Say the rest of it,**" said Mara Iles, "**because I have written down two words and I would like the paragraph.**"

"**The rest of it is that the labels are the load-bearing thing on this floor, and by load-bearing I do not mean important. I mean that if the label is on the object then the object is what the object says it is, and a person, a date, and a thing they put in a place, and nobody can ask another question about it. And if the label is off the object then the object is an object, and an object on this floor with no label on it is an object that anybody who can get through a door can walk out with, and there is no second list, because a second list would be a list of the same objects and a list of the same objects is a list of people.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**Say the last of it,**" said Mara Iles.

"**The last of it is that a label is the only thing in this city that stands between a person and a question, and I have never once been able to explain that to anybody in a room without it sounding like a threat, and it is not a threat, it is a form.**" She put her hand flat on a cupboard door. "**Somebody built a form. That is all a label is. Somebody in about 1971 sat down and made a thing with boxes in it, and the boxes are a maker and a place and a date and a description and an accession number, and none of the boxes is a person, and a person is safe behind a thing that has got no box for her.**"

"**And when somebody asks what is behind it,**" said Elias Venn, "**what is the answer.**"

"**There is no box for that,**" said Menna Prothero. "**There is no box on this form for what is behind a label and there is no box in this city for it and there has not been one since about 1971, and I am not going to put the question in this chapter and I am not going to say it in a corridor, and I will say it in a room if somebody asks me in a room and asks me properly and is prepared to hear the answer.**"

"**Is somebody going to ask you in a room.**"

"**I have been waiting nineteen years and no, not yet, and I have not asked, because a woman of fifty-two who has been on a floor nineteen years and has not asked is a woman who has decided not to know, and that is a thing I have earned and I am not going to give it up to be interesting.**"

She turned round and went back to the cupboards and unlocked the one with the brass strip that says **TG**.

## And the corridor is a corridor, and a floor has no page one, and nobody in ninety metres of it improved on *for the labels*, and a man of thirty-two could not check the order of the four things in it and said so in four seconds

Three things happened on that floor between about eleven o'clock and about half past one on Thursday the ninth of December 2025 and only one of them is the subject of this section and the other two are here because the first one is a question and a question on a floor is never by itself.

Rhiannon Iddon came up at about half past eleven with the quarterly binder and could not get the cupboard open because the key is on a board in the records office and there are seven keys on that board and none of them is labelled, and Menna Prothero opened it in about four seconds with a key off her own belt, and said that there have been seven keys on that board since 1998 and that she has a key and that the board is not wrong, the board is just not hers, and then she opened the drawer.

The other two things are in Chapter 153 and the chapter says so and does not put them here, because a floor is not the place to find out what is in a drawer and the drawer is not what the corridor is for.

And here is the last thing that happened on that floor on the ninth of December 2025 and it is the only thing in the chapter that is about the man of thirty-two.

He wrote it down. He has a notebook that costs thirty-five pence and there are four lines at the top of the first page, and one of them says that a fact a person gives him in a street is theirs until it is in a file, and he wrote Menna Prothero's answer down in his own book at about ten past one on the Thursday afternoon, in a corridor, in about twenty seconds.

Then he wrote four things under it, and he wrote them in an order, and then he stopped and read them back to himself twice and could not tell whether the order he had written them in was the order she had said them in, and **he has no form for the order of a list, and there is no second person in this city who can check one, and the one second person he has reads back what he says and finds nothing wrong in it and cannot find anything wrong in it.**

He said so, out loud, in a corridor, in about four seconds, to a curator of fifty-two and a registrar of twenty-eight.

"**I have written down what you said and I have written down the order I wrote it in and I cannot tell you whether that is the order you said it in, and I am not able to check it and there is nothing in this building that would let anybody check it.**"

Menna Prothero looked at him for about four seconds and did not ask him a single question about it, and the reason she did not ask, and she gave it in about two sentences and nobody improved on it, is that she is a woman of fifty-two who has been on a floor nineteen years and who knows precisely what a person sounds like when they are telling the truth about the shape of a hole, and she has heard about nine of them.

"**All right,**" she said. "**Say the four things and I will say them again in whatever order you want and you can write whichever order I say them in and then it will be right, and that is not a favour, it is that I have been saying the same four things for nineteen years and I have never once had anybody write them down in a book.**"

So he read the four things out, and she said them back in the order he read them, and he wrote that order down, and **the order she said them in is the order they are in his book, and he cannot check that, and he did not need to, because the person who said them to him is standing in the corridor and has agreed to say them again on Wednesday, which is a thing no instrument in this city can do and which is not a record and is not going into a file and is not a card.**

*For the labels. A label is the only thing that stands between a person and a question. A label has no box for a person. And nobody has asked me in nineteen years.*

And the corridor is a corridor, and there is a door at the east end of it, and three people came to about two metres short of it at about eleven o'clock in the morning on the ninth of December 2025 and stopped, and nobody said why, and nobody described it, and nobody opened it, and nobody said what was behind it, and it was not the subject of the morning, and the subject of the morning was a woman of fifty-two saying four sentences in a corridor and a man of thirty-two writing them down in the wrong order or the right one and not being able to tell, and a registrar of twenty-eight standing at a plan chest with a card in her pocket that opens a door that logs card events and watches about ninety metres of passage happen and does not write it down.
