# Chapter 0153 — Two Leaves, Close Together

**On Friday the tenth and Saturday the eleventh of December 2025 a drawer on the fourth level of a building on Civic Hill was opened with a key off a curator's own belt, and there were forty-eight books in it, one for every year from 1978 to this year, and forty-seven of them had been written in for a night or two and abandoned, and every one of the forty-eight had been written in by the same hand, and that hand had not changed once in thirty-one years, and two leaves had been taken out of one of them with a blade.**

**It records, in date order, who was on the floor on a night and what came in or went out. That is a real order and it is exactly the order Elias Venn cannot check, and the chapter says on the page that he cannot check it and does not say why, because the reason is not in this book and the reader has it and I am not going to say it in a chapter about a drawer.**

**Nothing in the ten chapters of this movement is a knot in a line, and the word is not in this volume, and no chapter is going to raise it.**

## And on the Friday morning a records clerk of forty-four brought a quarterly binder up ninety metres of corridor and a curator of fifty-two opened a drawer with a key off her own belt, and there were forty-eight books in it

The quarterly binder is a ring binder with a printed spine and a label on the front that says the year and the quarter, and it is a binder about a period, and it exists so that what a quarter of a Museum did can be looked at in about four minutes without opening a file.

A quarter had to be reconciled. The reason it had to be reconciled is that a records clerk of forty-four has done it every quarter for nineteen years and this one had come back from a holiday with a note on it, and the note said, in the records clerk's own shorthand, *check the TG run against the drawer*, and nobody in this case had ever asked what the TG run was.

"**The TG run is the floor ledger,**" said Menna Prothero. "**It has been the floor ledger since before I came, and it is not on the schedule of collections, and it is not accessioned, and it is not in the schedule of the archive, and it is not a personnel file, and it does not have a work order against it, and it is a book kept by a person in a drawer, and I have never once been asked about it and I have never once been asked to stop keeping it.**"

"**What is the reference.**"

"**The run is called `TG/` and the year goes after the slash, and the year is the year. That is the only reference in this building with a year in it where the year means the year and not the year a series was opened, and I have watched four people assume otherwise and every one of the four was reading a card and not a book.**"

"**Say the run.**"

"**`TG/78/` at the back and `TG/26/` at the front, and there are forty-eight of them and I know it is forty-eight because I have counted them about four times, and there is one for every year from 1978 to this year and I did not write that sentence meaning anything by it. I am a person who notices shelves.**"

She said that last part to Elias Venn, and she said it to him and not to the room, and it is the first of about four things she says to him in this volume rather than to a room, and none of the four is a question.

"**And the front one.**"

"**The front one is this year's and it has one page in it and nothing on the page. And the one behind it has one page in it and nothing on the page. And it goes back like that for about nine of them.**" She put her hand on the spines. "**`TG/09/` is the last one anybody has ever written in and it is the same size as every other one of them and it is written in to the hundred and fourth leaf, and there is nothing on any of the leaves after that, and there never was, and a book that is three-quarters empty is a book somebody put down in April and not a book that came to an end. And `TG/10/` has four lines on it and nothing else, and nobody has written in any of the sixteen years since.**"

"**Say the year.**"

"**Two thousand and nine.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, and Rhiannon Iddon said the thing that a records clerk of forty-four with nineteen years of a Museum's accession record is professionally obliged to say, which was the whole of it in eleven words.

"**Then there is no floor ledger for this floor for sixteen years,**" she said, "**and there is one drawer, and it is not locked, and I have been asked to reconcile a quarter against it.**"

## And every one of the forty-eight is ruled, and quarter-bound, and in the same hand, and the hand is the same in the first and the last, and nobody in this case has ever seen anything like it

Menna Prothero took `TG/78/` out and then took `TG/09/` out and put the two of them side by side on the top of a cupboard in the corridor, because there is nowhere else on a floor like this to put two books that are about half a metre long. She put the older one on the left. The 1978 one is about thirty millimetres thick and the 2009 one is a little over a third of that, and they are the same length and the same boards and the same cloth, and a person who has been buying the same book for thirty-one years has bought it from the same man.

Both of them are ruled in blue at about three millimetres, in a line that has gone soft at about nine lines in from the left and has not gone soft anywhere else, and the ruling was done by somebody who ruled a great many books at about nine lines and did not enjoy it. The binding is quarter: cloth on the spine and the corners and paper boards, and the boards have a shine on them at about four inches from the foot, which is a hand. The paper is off-white and there is a tide line in it about a third of the way down, which is a cupboard on a floor above a plant room and a wet winter in about 1994 and it is in forty of the forty-eight books.

"**Read me a page,**" said Mara Iles.

Menna Prothero turned `TG/87/` to the third of February and did not read it and she said why and the reason was four words long.

"**Because it is one hand, and you want to hear one hand, and I have got one hand in two books and the books are twenty-one years apart.**"

"**I do want to hear one hand.**"

"**Then you can have about a week of it and I will read it, and it is the same in every book, and I will tell you where the habit changes so you can hear the change, and there are four habits that change and about nine places in thirty-one years where the pen is different, and one of the nine is eleven days in the spring of 2009, and I am not going to tell you which one, and I am telling you that there is one because you are going to want one and it is better that you know there is.**"

She read. It is not a diary and it is not a list and it is not a note.

> **3 ii 87. N. — 22:10 – 06:00. IN: one case, paper, from store 2, unaccessioned, no work order. Hall checked 23:00 and 02:00.**
> **4 ii 87. N. — 22:10 – 06:00. IN: one folder, paper, from the label room, unaccessioned. Hall checked 23:00 only.**
> **5 ii 87. N. — 22:10 – 06:00. Nothing in. Nothing out. Hall checked 23:00 and 02:00.**
> **6 ii 87. N. — 22:10 – 06:00. OUT: one folder, paper, to the label room, unaccessioned. Hall checked 23:00 and 02:00.**

"**That is it,**" said Menna Prothero. "**That is what the book is. A date, a person, the hours, and what came in or went out. It is in date order, going forward, every night, one line to a leaf, and there is a line for every night somebody was on this floor, and there is no line for a night nobody was, because in thirty-one years there was never a night nobody was. One book is one year. There are forty-eight of them and about eleven thousand lines in all and I have not counted the eleven thousand, I have counted the forty-eight, and the last year of the eleven thousand is a quarter of it long because she stopped in April, and that is not because anything was taken out of a book, and I want that in whatever you are going to write, because those are two different sentences and only one of them has a blade in it.**"

"**And the hand.**"

"**The hand is one hand.**" She turned the 2009 book a little towards the light and did not touch the page, and then she turned the 1978 one the same way and put them side by side again, and the two hands were the same hand. "**I have read about four hundred thousand cards in nineteen years and I can tell you that a hand does not stay like that. Everybody's hand changes. Yours changes between a Monday and a Thursday. This one has not changed once in thirty-one years and there are forty-eight books on that shelf to prove it and I have looked at all forty-eight about four times. A hand that does not change over thirty-one years is not a hand that is being careful. It is a hand that is being fast and it is a hand that has done the same four things about nine times a fortnight for thirty-one years and has never had to think about any of them, and that is not a virtue, that is a Tuesday.**"

"**And the hours.**"

"**The hours are the same in that week.**" She turned four pages with the flat of her hand on the edge. "**Twenty-two ten to six. On the thirty-first of October, and on the ninth of November, and on the fourteenth of December, and on the nineteenth of January, and that is four pages out of about three hundred and fifty and I have not gone looking for a pattern because looking for a pattern in a person's hours is what got somebody fired in about 1998 in a different building and I have not forgotten it.**"

Nobody said anything.

"**Say it,**" said Elias Venn.

"**I am not going to say it, and I will say why, and the why is the professional part of me and not the rest of me: because the number of hours a person stood on a floor in a fortnight in 1987 is not a question anybody has a standing to ask, and the person who wrote that book is not in this building and has not been for a long time, and a book in a drawer is a book about a person, and a book about a person is a record of that person, and a record of that person belongs to that person.**" She squared the book. "**I have kept that book in that drawer since 2009 and I have never once been asked to produce it and I have never once been asked to destroy it, and I could have destroyed it in about four seconds at any point in sixteen years and I have not, and if anybody in this room thinks I should have, they should go and find a form that says so, because I have not got one and I have looked, and I have looked properly, and looking is what I am for.**"

## And two leaves are gone, and they are eleven days apart, and they were not torn, and the cut is old enough that the brown on the stub faces is the brown on the back of the last written page

Nobody was looking for them. That is the thing about it and the chapter is going to be exact about it: nobody on that floor on the Friday morning of the tenth of December 2025 was looking for two missing leaves, and they were found because a records clerk of forty-four was reconciling a quarter against a drawer and put her thumb in the middle of a book to hold it open, and the book did not lie flat where a book lies flat.

"**There,**" said Rhiannon Iddon. "**That is not a bad hinge. That is a hinge that has been cut and re-cut.**"

"**Say the page.**"

"**Ninety-one.**" She had it in about four seconds. "**And there is another one at one hundred and two.**"

So: two leaves, at ninety-one and one hundred and two, and there is one line to a leaf in that hand, and the difference in the numbering is eleven, and the two entries are eleven days apart, and both of them are in the 2009 book.

"**Say the dates.**"

Menna Prothero read them off the facing pages without any difficulty at all, because the book is in date order and it is one hand and it is dated every night.

> **1 iv 09. N. — 22:10 – 06:00. — [leaf gone]**
> **12 iv 09. N. — 22:10 – 06:00. — [leaf gone]**

"**Eleven days,**" said Mara Iles, "**and a week of a hand and an entry every night for thirty-one years, so a gap is a hole in a year, and there are two holes and they are eleven days apart in the same month, and both of them are in the last year anybody wrote in that book.**"

"**Not torn,**" said Menna Prothero.

"**Say how you know.**"

"**Because a tear goes, and this has not gone.**" She put a fingertip an inch above the stub and did not touch it. "**A torn edge feathers, and a torn stub on a bound book hangs, and these two are flat and clean and the fibre is standing up square on both of them, and that is a blade and the blade was sharp, and a dull blade on paper of this age leaves a grey crush along about two millimetres. There is no crush. Somebody used a good blade and somebody who uses a good blade on paper knows to change it.**"

"**How old.**"

"**I am not going to put a year on it and I want that in whatever you are going to write, because a conservator who puts a year on a cut in a book in a drawer is a conservator who has made a finding and I am not making one.**" She looked at the back of the last written page and then at the two stub faces, and she did it the way a person looks at two things they already knows are the same colour. "**What I will say is that the brown on the two stub faces is the same brown as the brown on the back of the last written page, and the brown on the back of the last written page came from the same cupboard over about forty years, and the brown on those two stubs is not lighter than it, which means it has had the same forty years, and which means the cut was made while the book was still in that drawer being written to.**"

"**That is 2009.**"

"**That is a shelf, and a drawer, and a wet winter, and a colour, and I have just told you I am not going to put a year on it.**" She closed the book and squared it. "**What I will say is that the cut is old, and that it is old by the only test I know, which is that nothing about it is fresh.**"

And then the room was quiet for a different reason, and it was Mara Iles, who said:

"**Then somebody has taken two things out of a Museum's record and has not told the Museum.**"

"**Yes.**"

"**Say what that is.**"

"**It is nothing. It is the most ordinary thing in this building.**" Menna Prothero took her hand off the book. "**I have taken four things out of this collection in nineteen years. Two were foxed and went into a bin and I wrote nothing down. One was a thing I could not stop handling and I put it in a box in the label room and I have moved it four times. One was a photograph of three people and I did not take it out, I have never seen it, and it is not my photograph, and it is in a box on that floor and has been since about 1998, and the name of the photographer is in pencil on the back and the names of the three people are not in this building at all.**"

"**You are not in trouble.**"

"**I know I am not in trouble, and that is the whole of the finding and I want the room to have it properly: I have never been in trouble for that, and nobody ever would be, and there is no form in this city that anybody could have filled in to tell me to do it, and there is no form that anybody could have filled in to tell me not to.**" She looked at the drawer and at the forty-eight books in it. "**A thing may be taken out of a book and nobody is required to be told, and the reason nobody is required to be told is that the book is not a record of anything. It is a book in a drawer. It has no number that a computer can hold. It is not in the schedule of collections, it is not in the archive schedule, it is not a personnel file, it has no work order, and a person cannot be produced from it by anybody who is not already in this corridor.**"

## And on the Saturday morning at about eleven o'clock four people established that nobody could have told the Museum, and that is not a mystery either, and a man of thirty-two said out loud that he cannot check the order of the book and did not say why

The Saturday was quieter. The building is not shut at the weekend and there are about four people in it on a Saturday morning and none of them is on the fourth level, and the fourth level is not on the meter, which is a thing about the meter and not about the floor.

Here is the whole of what four people established in about fifty minutes, and it took fifty minutes because three of them wanted to check.

**Two. **Four. "**No.**"

"**It is the only record of who stood on this floor that has ever existed,**" she said, "**and it stops in 2009, and two pages of it are gone, and the person who wrote it has not been asked about anything by anybody, and neither am I going to be, and I would like the room to understand that I have made that decision and not anybody in this corridor and not whoever sent you up ninety metres of stair with a key.**"

And the corridor was a corridor, and the drawer was shut, and it does not lock.
