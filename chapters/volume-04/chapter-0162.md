# Chapter 0162 — A Hundred And Four, And A Hundred And Two

**On Monday the tenth and Tuesday the eleventh of January 2026 two numbers were put on a table in a room on the second level of a building on a hill, side by side, and both of them are right, and the whole of a fortnight in April 2009 is the distance between them.**

**The first is one hundred and four. It is a number a woman of sixty-seven has carried since the fourteenth of April 2009, given in a room on the twenty-eighth of December, written on a sheet of A4 with a date on it and no reference number, and not minuted. The second is one hundred and two, and it is what a records clerk of forty-four established with her thumb in about nine minutes by counting the written leaves in a quarter-bound book that is the same size as the other forty-seven of them and a quarter of the way full. The difference is two. The two are the first of April 2009 and the twelfth of April 2009, and they are eleven days apart, and the number one hundred and four is a count of a book plus two leaves that are not in the book.**

**Nobody in that room improved on either number, and a registrar of twenty-eight wrote the finding out in one sentence and then said the sentence was not the finding, and the finding is that the only number in this case that nobody can alter is the same number as the one nobody can check, and those are not two facts. They are one fact with a page on it.**

## And on the Monday morning at about ten o'clock four people put two numbers on a table, and the fourth was a records clerk of forty-four who counted with her thumb, and the arithmetic was not the protagonist's

The room is on the second level. It has the table, and four chairs, and the window that looks at the back of the service lane, and it is about a floor and a half below the fourth level.

There were four people in it. A registrar of twenty-eight. A curator of fifty-two, who came down again and has now stopped saying that she was not asked. A records clerk of forty-four who keeps the quarterly binder and who signs about four hundred appointment forms a year and could not tell you one of them now. And a man of thirty-two with no post.

There were two objects on the table. One was a sheet of A4 with a number on it. > **One hundred and four.** Counted by Mrs B. Nancarrow, keeper of the floor ledger, 1978 to 2009. Given in a room on the second level on 28 December 2025. Not minuted. No reference number.

"**Say the second,**" said Mara Iles, "**and I am asking a records clerk of forty-four and not a man of thirty-two, and he will tell you why in about four seconds if you ask him, and I would rather he told you than me.**"

"**I will tell him,**" said Rhiannon Iddon, "**and here it is. She turned the book round on the table and put her thumb in the gutter and ran it down.

"**And here is the thing I want said before anybody says anything else. Two leaves have been taken out of this book. "**Say the difference.**"

"**Two,**" said Rhiannon Iddon. "**And the two are the first of April 2009 and the twelfth of April 2009, and one line to a leaf off the first of January, and I did that on the back of an envelope before I said the number out loud, because I am not going to say a number in a room that I have not checked.**"

"**And I could not have done that,**" said Elias Venn, to the table, in about four seconds. "**Not the arithmetic. The arithmetic is anybody's. I can read every line in that book and I cannot tell you whether the hundred and four she carried is the hundred and four in that book, because that is a question about whether two lists are in the same order and there is no form for that, and I have been out of order since the twenty-seventh of October and I am not going to get back in.**"

"**You are not needed for that,**" said Rhiannon Iddon, "**and I want to be careful how I say it, and I am going to say it the way I said the thing about the binders in December. I did not need you. I put my thumb in a book. That is a bench and not a memory and it took nine minutes and it does not matter in the slightest order what anybody thinks of me.**"

## And the room put the two numbers side by side and they agreed, and about four seconds later somebody in it noticed that agreeing was the whole of what either of them could do

They lay on the table for about two minutes and nobody touched them.

"**Say what we have,**" said Mara Iles. "**Not what we think. What we have.**"

"**We have a number that a person carried and a number that a book has,**" said Rhiannon Iddon, "**and they are both correct and they differ by two, and the two are the two nights that are not in the book.**"

"**And say what that means.**"

"**It means the hundred and four is not a count of that book,**" said Rhiannon Iddon. "**It is a count of that book and two leaves that were in it and are not in it and are not anywhere. A page-count of a book you have cut leaves out of is a count of a book plus a gap. The gap is inside the number. You cannot take it out and you cannot check it, because the only way to check it is to produce two leaves, and the only way to be wrong about it is to cut two more, and nobody in this case is going to do either.**"

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds.

"**Say the other half,**" said Mara Iles, to the man of thirty-two, "**because I have been looking at this table for about two minutes and there is something on it and you are the only person at this table who reads.**"

"**Say that last part again,**" said Menna Prothero, from the wall.

"**I did not notice.**"

"**Say the rest of it. ## And a registrar of twenty-eight said that there are three fortnights in the one fortnight, and that a page-count has no units on it, and that nobody in the room can say which of the three it is counting

## And on the Tuesday morning at about half past ten a registrar of twenty-eight wrote the finding out on a sheet of A4 with a date on it and no reference number, and read it out, and a records clerk told her there is no form for it
