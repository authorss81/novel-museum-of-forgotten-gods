# Chapter 0039 — Item Five

The schedule of contents review was not a Board meeting. It was a sub-group of a Board meeting, and it was three trustees and the Director and the Board Secretary and the registrar, and it had been sitting on a Wednesday afternoon since the first of September, and it had four items, and it had a fifth item that had been added on the ninth of October by a man of forty-four who is not on the rota any more.

The paper went up on the board in the Board Secretary's office in the last week of September and it has been the same paper since:

> **MUSEUM OF QUIET SAINTS — SCHEDULE OF COLLECTIONS (REVIEW) — ITEM 5.**
> *Review of the entries for 1961 / LW / 41 to 52 (Sillick Mill papers, received 7 Nov 1974).*
> **Note added 9 Oct 2024: the physical material is UNAVAILABLE SINCE 11 SEPT 2024. A proposed disposition is before the standing instruction. Review deferred at minuted request.**

And nobody could remove the note, because the note was the agenda, and you do not strike an item off an agenda in a paper that has gone to three trustees.

The sub-group sat from eleven until about six on Wednesday the eighteenth of November, and the item order was one, two, three, five, four, and nobody rearranged it, and item five took two hours and ten minutes and item four took one hour and forty minutes, and item five had been on the paper since a papers request landed on the agenda in the middle of October while item four was still being drafted, and that is the whole of the explanation and nobody gave one.

Elias Venn was on the ground floor. He was at the front desk from seven until a quarter to one and then he went and did the dead letters at a table in the loading-bay office because the bay office is the only room in this building with two chairs and a door that shuts, and there were three hundred and thirteen of them and it was a Wednesday.

He knew there was a sub-group because the board in the corridor said so, and because the lift had been in use four times by the time he got in, and because Anja Ward's door was shut at nine o'clock, which it never is.

He did not know what was on it and he did not go and find out, and the reason he did not go and find out is that on Monday morning a woman of fifty-four had said *I'm not the Director, do not ask me things* to a woman of seventy-eight, and Elias Venn had spent five weeks learning that a man who cannot be asked anything is the most useful person in a building, and that if he starts going into rooms on the strength of a schedule on a board then he stops being that.

---

The first thing that happened at about half past one was the telephone in the loading-bay office, and it was Nunn, and he said one sentence.

"Twenty past four, and I'll be at the desk."

"Yes."

"That's it. I don't need you to do anything. I'm telling you because you've been the only one who's ever asked me about it and I said I'd tell you and I'd rather say it than have you work it out." A pause. "They've sent it up again, by the way. The paperwork. From the company. It's got a new number on it and it went through the post this morning and I logged it at half past eleven."

"How many working days does the new one have?"

"It hasn't got any. It hasn't got a service date on it, so the clock has not started, and as far as clause three is concerned it isn't a proposal, it's a letter." A pause. "And a letter is not a disposition, and a disposition is the only thing in this building that moves anything out of a room, and at five o'clock this afternoon the last one of those runs out, and after that there is nothing on any desk here that anybody can execute. The box in bay 9 is a cage with a box in it until somebody serves a date on a piece of paper, and they can serve it in December, and I will be here in December, and I am not going to be here in a hurry."

"When is the tenth working day."

"Five o'clock this afternoon, and it dies in the dark tonight at about four minutes past midnight, because that is how the last one went and I am not going to be creative." Nunn's voice did not change at all. "And I will tell you the only thing I have found funny since Monday, because there has been nothing else. There is a sub-group sitting on the second floor from eleven o'clock this morning with an item on its paper that says *the physical material is unavailable since the eleventh of September*, and a minute note under it that says the material is unavailable because a proposed disposition is before the standing instruction. And at five o'clock this afternoon there is no proposed disposition before the standing instruction and there never will be one again unless somebody puts a date on something. And nobody on the second floor knows that, and nobody on the second floor can know it, because it is not an item, and there is no box on that paper for *the reason the last box stopped being a live possibility*, and that is the best sentence anybody has produced about this building in five weeks and it is one of mine, and I am a transfer clerk with a form, and I have written it down."

"Nunn—"

"I'm not going to argue about it. I have been in this building six years and I have written *did not travel* once and I am not writing it a second time for a company that cannot count." The line went. "Five o'clock. I'll be at the desk. And don't come up, there is a room full of trustees on the second floor and I am not on the agenda."

---

At about half past six, when the sub-group had been going for seven and a half hours, the lift came down and the Board Secretary came out with a folder and went to the front desk and put a bundle of about forty pages on it and said, "That's typed and initialled and it's the minute, and the pack for the Board on the twenty-fourth goes up on Friday, and there's a copy for the Director, and one of you lot can have it, and it's not urgent," and went back up.

Elias Venn was at the counter. He was on the front desk until one and then he was in the bay office and then he was back on the counter at four because the counter covers the late shift, and the counter is public, and there were nine people in the lobby at half past five on a Wednesday in November and two of them were waiting for somebody.

The bundle was face down. The top sheet had a printed line on it that he could read from where he was standing, upside down, which is a thing he can do.

> **MUSEUM OF QUIET SAINTS — BOARD SUB-GROUP (COLLECTIONS AND INTERPRETATION) — MINUTE OF THE MEETING OF WEDNESDAY 18 NOVEMBER 2024.**

He did not pick it up. It is a bundle on a front desk and there are nine people in the lobby and one of them is a woman with a pushchair.

He picked it up at about seven, when the lobby was empty, and he read it standing up, and he has read about four hundred pages of Museum minutes in five weeks and he knows the shape of this one before he is a third of the way down it, because item 5 is the ninth page and item 5 is two hours long and everything is in item 5.

> **5. SCHEDULE OF COLLECTIONS — ENTRIES 1961 / LW / 41 TO 52.**
>
> **5.1** The sub-group noted the minute note of 9 October 2024 that the physical material was unavailable since 11 September 2024 and that a proposed disposition was before the Standing Instruction.
>
> **5.2** The sub-group considered a statement of 19 October 2024 held in the Board Secretary's file under Standing Instruction 14 clause 6, being a statement of the Senior Conservator and Accession Registrar concerning a plinth card at plinth 34 of the Hall of Former Faiths, an erasure upon it described as partial, and the contents of drawer 6B of the Gallery of Unnamed Hands.
>
> **5.3** The Chair asked whether a person named in that statement appeared in the Museum's records.
>
> **5.4** **The Senior Conservator and Accession Registrar read the name out.** *Case, Wendel — grade 2 porter, 1994 to 2012 — as it appears at entry 1961 / LW / 52 (b), Sillick Mill papers, and as it appears on a card in the Registrar's historic staff file which is in part illegible and in part amended.*
>
> **5.5** The Registrar stated that the entry at 1961 / LW / 52 (b) had been made by herself on 29 October 2024 in consequence of the Manifest Transfer Schedule required by the Standing Instruction, that the schedule was drawn up from a 1974 carbon receipt and not from the box's label, and that the entry was made because a receiving party who signs for a gate book cannot afterwards say the gate book was nobody's.
>
> **5.6** The Registrar further stated that she had written the statement of 19 October 2024 in the conservation office on the Saturday before it was lodged, in the ordinary way, in answer to a request that had been received at a front desk, and that she would do it again, and that she said so unasked.
>
> **5.7** The sub-group noted that no record of a leaver exists for the person named, that the staff file is in part illegible and in part amended, and that the Museum's own payroll deduction schedule for the year 1994 carries a bureau remittance for a person of that name at that grade.
>
> **5.8** **RESOLVED:** that the Museum write to the Municipal Payroll Bureau, Remittances and Deductions, in the form set out at appendix, for such confirmation as that office is able to give; and that the Director report the position to the Board at its next meeting.

Elias Venn stood at a front desk in a public museum on Wednesday the eighteenth of November at about ten past seven in the evening and read item 5 a second time, and then he read 5.4 a third time, and he put the bundle down on the counter with the face up, which he should not have done, and which nobody saw.

Then he did the thing he had stopped being able to help doing in the second week of October, which was to put the number of people in a room next to the number of people who could be hurt by it, and the room was this building, and the number was about nine hundred on a Saturday and about two thousand a day, and the thing that was going to come out of item 5 was a surname, and a surname is not a number.

He wrote it down on the back of a dead letter envelope at the counter, standing up, because the counter is public and the bay office is two doors down and he had about four seconds' worth of feeling in him and no time to walk.

> **Wed 18 Nov, 19:10. Minute, Board sub-group, item 5.**
> **A name is in a Museum record. It is in a minute, dated, minuted, and a minute is a document, and a document survives me, and I have spent five weeks being afraid of a name in a corridor and the name is now in a filing system with an accession to it and it will be in a Board pack on the twenty-fourth of November.**
> **It is Wendel Case. Grade 2 porter. 1994 to 2012. It is on the museum's own staff file, in part illegible and in part amended. It is on the bureau's deductions for 1994. It is on a card in a gallery and the card was cut and the cut is partial. It is on a list of eleven names that Mara Iles wrote on the twenty-ninth of October and signed, and it is in a minute in a Board Secretary's file, and none of those five has ever been in the same room as any of the other four, and in a fortnight a room is going to read it out.**
> **And there is a woman of fifty-four coming to that room who is not on any of the five, and who told me in a loading bay on the twenty-ninth of October that she has a hundred and forty names of her own, and that nobody in this building has ever asked her for one.**

He put the pen in his pocket. He picked the bundle up and squared it to the edge of the counter, because it was skewed and it was on a public counter, and then he stopped, because a document that is squared to the edge of a counter by a person who has just read it is a document that a person has touched, and the day book says who touched things and when, and he had entered a drawer in a day book on Sunday and he was not going to do it twice in a week over something he had no business holding.

He left it skewed.

---

He got Mara Iles at half past seven on the front desk extension, and she answered it in a corridor.

"Don't," she said.

"I've read the minute."

"Then you have read a document that three trustees sat through for seven and a half hours this afternoon and that will go to the Board on the twenty-second, and there is nothing you can do about it and nothing you should do about it, and if you telephone me about it tonight I shall say four sentences and they will all be procedure, and you will not like one of them."

"I wasn't going to ring you about it."

"You rang me about it."

"I rang you because the third one is about the twenty-ninth of October and I have been in that loading bay and I know what you said when you signed it."

There was a pause of about two seconds, which from Mara Iles is a paragraph.

"The Registrar's answer is this," she said. "A name came into a Board paper. I wrote it. I wrote it on the twenty-ninth of October at about five o'clock in the afternoon at a table in a loading bay with a folder and a pen, and I did it because a transfer schedule that does not name a gate book is a schedule that lets a company say the gate book was nobody's, and I would do that again on a Saturday in the conservation office, on my own, in a hurry, with nobody asking me to, and I said that out loud in a room with three trustees in it this afternoon and I said it because it is true and because a minute is a document and a document is the only thing in this building that has ever told anybody anything."

"Mara—"

"Second. A name in a minute means a request goes to the Municipal Payroll Bureau, Remittances and Deductions, in the form at appendix, and the form has two questions on it, and the first is *did you pay this person* and the second is *was this person employed by anybody*, and the bureau can answer the first out of a remittance record and cannot answer the second out of anything at all, because a bureau's remittance record for a person is a record of the person and of money paid and is not a record of the employer." Her voice did not go up. "So Mr Lound is going to have to complete the same form twice, and answer the second half of it with nothing, and sign it, and the nothing is going to be in a Museum file with our request number on it, and in about eleven years somebody is going to read that form and think we could not be bothered to ask the second question. And he has already answered one of them once, on the twenty-ninth of October, in about forty seconds, because I asked him a question, and he is a chief clerk in a building of nine hundred people and he has never heard of us."

"Third."

"Third, and this is the one that will keep me up, and I am telling you on a telephone at half past seven on a Wednesday so that it is a fact and not an opinion: that name has been in four places in this city since 1994 and nobody has ever put two of them in a room, and today a registrar put two of them in a room in front of three trustees because a *retired records clerk's post* went to systems in March, and because a trustee has a right of access to Board papers, and because a member of the public lodged a set of photographs at a front desk, and because I wrote a four-paragraph statement in the conservation office on a Saturday in October and lodged it on the Monday, and because clause six put it in a file where a trustee could find it." A door closed somewhere behind her. "There is no villain in that sentence. Not one. Every single step of it is a correct procedure being correctly applied by a competent person. That is what has happened. Do you understand? That is what this building is."

---

He did not tell anybody else and there was nobody to tell, and he went and did the last of the dead letters and the count was three hundred and thirteen and the count did not matter and he counted them anyway.

At half past eight he rang his mother and it took her about forty seconds to answer and the laundrette was open because it is open until ten every night.

"Nothing's happened," he said.

"Then that's a telephone call," said his mother, "which is a thing people don't do."

"Two people I know are in a room and a person's name is in a piece of paper and it's the right name and it's about fifty years late."

"Are you all right?"

"No," said Elias Venn. "I'm the only person in this city who knows it's happened, and the only person in this city who knows is not allowed in the room, and in about eight days I'm going to stand up in front of six families and read it out to them, and I have known about it for one hour and there is nothing whatever that I can do about it, and I have been in this state since about half past seven this evening and I have not even got angry yet."

"You won't get angry. You'll get busy." She put a machine on. "You always get busy. Your father got angry and it's the worst thing in this room, so I don't know where you got it from, and you didn't get it from me, and I've had a good think about that over the years."

---

Item 4 is the last four pages of the bundle and it is the reason the bundle is forty pages and not twenty, and Elias Venn did not know that any of it was in there, and read it standing at a public counter at ten past seven in the evening on Wednesday the eighteenth of November, and it is the only thing he read that night that he has never been able to put down.

It is headed: **HALL OF FORMER FAITHS — SCHEDULE 12 OBJECT AT PLINTH 34: DIRECTOR'S REPORT AND CERTIFICATION STATUS.**

And what was decided in it, in a room on the second floor between about three o'clock and about twenty to five in the afternoon, by a director of fifty-three and three trustees and a board secretary and a registrar, was that the Hall of Former Faiths remains open, and the hall of former faiths remains open, and the school term continues, and no party not holding a collections pass is to be admitted to that room while the object is under Annexe E, and no school group is to be admitted to that room at all, and the hall certificate stands.

And the reason it stands is condition four of the Director's own conditions of the fifteenth of October, which bars the room while any Schedule 12 carried object in it is caged, moved, or otherwise out of its last certified condition — and the object at plinth 34 is sealed, certified, re-anchored and has not been handled since the seventh of November, and is therefore not out of its last certified condition, and condition four is satisfied, and has been satisfied every day since the seventh, and the exhibit stays open.

And nobody had to point that out, because a director who wrote four conditions at six in the morning in October and lost a bell to one of them in November does not have to be told which of her own conditions has been satisfied by an object doing nothing.

And Annexe E is stricter than condition four, and the sub-group did not say so, and it did not need to: a stricter instrument had existed in this building since the seventh of November, in a document read into the record by a statutory officer with a duty, in about four minutes, on a disturbance report lodged at a front desk by a member of the public. It was the strictest thing about that object and it was in a Museum file and the Museum had written it about itself.

---

What is in that file, and in the minute, and on the fourth page of it, is a certificate. A reproduction, in colour, at A3, on a projector, because the original is in a case on a plinth and cannot be moved, and the sub-group was looking at a photograph of a document.

> **QUIET ACCORD — SCHEDULE 12. CARRIED PATTERNS.**
> *Objects and records carrying a set form held in common use.*
> **ANNEXE D (PERMANENT). A PATTERN REQUIRING CONTINUED OBSERVANCE. TO BE DISPLAYED.**

Mrs Angela Vessey, who had chaired a sub-group of a Board for six years and had been a trustee since 2019 and had a right of access to Board papers, read the top line out loud, in a room, because she was sixty-four and she reads headings.

"Carried patterns," she said. "And then it says — a pattern requiring continued observance."

Nobody in the room used the other word. Not one person, in an hour and forty minutes, and there is no way on this earth of establishing afterwards whether that was because nobody in that room had it in their mouths or because everybody in that room did.

"Director," said Mrs Vessey. "Is that a religious classification?"

"No," said Anja Ward. "It is the oldest classification in this city and it predates this building by a hundred years and it is applied to about four per cent of what we hold, and it means an object that carries a repeated human act. That is the whole of it. It is not a statement about what anybody should believe, and it is not a statement about what the object is for. It is a statement about what the object has been doing."

"For how long?"

"For a hundred and twenty years, in this case. It is a hand bell, no maker's mark, no devotional content, cast in 1902, and the Annexe D assessor has said in three separate documents that there is nothing devotional about it, and I have never had cause to disagree with her about anything."

"And this Museum," said Mrs Vessey, "displays a set of these. In public rooms. On open plinths."

"Yes. Nine of them across three galleries, and about two hundred in store, and the store ones are not displayed and are not a public matter, and the nine are on plinths with a rope line, and the rope line is the whole of our provision, and it has been the whole of our provision since the building opened."

"And *continued observance.*" Mrs Vessey read the line again. "Director, who is observing it?"

Anja Ward, who has been the Director of this building for nineteen years and who has never once been asked that question, took about four seconds, and answered it in about forty words, and she has not forgotten it in nineteen years.

"Whoever is in the room," she said. "That is the mechanism. It has been the mechanism for two hundred years in this city and it is the reason the buildings are free, and it is not a metaphor — the people who come in on a wet Saturday and stand in front of a case and look at something for ninety seconds are doing it, and they do not know they are doing it, and if they stopped, it would stop." She looked at the projection. "And that is why I would not let anybody use the word in a minute, because the word would be accurate, and a minute is a public document, and the moment it is in a public document it becomes a thing this Museum has said about itself in the past tense."

"Registrar," said Mrs Vessey. "Is the object at plinth 34 one of the displayed ones?"

"Yes," said Mara Iles.

"Thank you," said Mrs Vessey. "**4.4** The sub-group noted the Director's report and resolved that the hall certificate of 7 November 2024 stands, and that the Director's condition four is not engaged, and that no further certification is required before the end of the current term."

And then the minute goes back two lines and records a resolution the sub-group did not debate, which the Board Secretary typed because Anja Ward asked her to and did not minute herself, and it is the last line of the last page of the item:

> **4.5** The Director asked that it be recorded that the Annexe E stay of 7 November 2024 is a stricter instrument than the Director's condition four, and that it has been in force since that date, and that the Museum's own file is the only place in which that is written down.

Elias Venn stood at a front desk in a public museum at ten past seven on Wednesday the eighteenth of November 2024, in an empty lobby, with the heating on, and read a document put together entirely out of correct procedures applied competently by people with duties, and in which the name of a porter who was never on a staff list is now on the record of a public museum, and in which a hundred and forty years of quiet municipal housekeeping has turned out to have a printed name for what the Hall of Former Faiths has been doing with a door open and a rope line and a pound a head.

He did not write the word down. There was a dead letter envelope in his coat and there was a pen in his pocket and he has written down every single thing that has happened to him in five weeks, and it is not on the page, and he has not been able to account for that, and that is the only thing in five weeks he does not do.
