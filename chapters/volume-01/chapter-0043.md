# Chapter 0043 — Nine Sentences

The Hall is on the first floor at the end of the west range, and the Board room is on the second, and the lift takes forty seconds, and Elias Venn went up it at about a quarter to ten in the morning on Tuesday the twenty-fourth of November with a letter on the table in front of it, because he was told at half past nine that the Chair had asked for him.

"I want to be very clear about the terms of it," said Mrs Angela Vessey, from the far side of a table the length of a small boat, at about a quarter to ten, in a room with a window that looks at a yard. "You are here for item two. Item two only. There are five items and four of them are the schedule of contents and the Gallery condition report and you are not in any of them and you will not hear them. You will sit there—" she put a finger on a chair against the wall by the door "—with the door behind you, and you will not speak unless you are asked, and if you are asked you will answer the question that is asked and not a larger one."

"Yes, Mrs Vessey."

"And you will not have a copy of this minute, because a copy of this minute is a document about a meeting you were not invited to, and I have been a trustee of this institution for five years and I have never in five years seen a suspended member of staff given the minutes of a Board, and I am not starting at ten o'clock on a Tuesday with a bell in the building." She put her pen down. "Is that fair."

"That is fair and I would rather have the terms than not have them."

"The Chair is agreeing to a request from the Director," said the Director, "and I would like it on the record that I did not make that request. I made a request for an item on the agenda. The Chair has decided that the author of a statement which is in the pack should be heard on the item that quotes it. That is the Chair's discretion and it is not mine, and if it goes wrong it will be the Chair's and not the Director's."

"That is exactly right," said Mrs Angela Vessey, "and I have made the Chair's decision in a way that is defensible, and I would like to know that both of you have noticed I have made it in a way that is defensible."

---

There were five people at the table besides the Chair: two other trustees she had not met, a trustee of about seventy who said almost nothing before lunch, and the Board Secretary with a bundle. The Director sat at the end nearest the window. Mara Iles sat two chairs along from the Director, with a folder and her own pen, and she was not signing as an officer and had said so when she was introduced.

And Elias Venn sat on a chair against the wall by the door with his hands on his knees, in a building he was not on the staff of, in a room he had been in eleven times in three years, at a table he was not a member of.

The Board Secretary read the request. It was two pages and it had come in through the front desk on Friday the twentieth of November in an ordinary envelope with nothing on it, and it had been opened and logged because everything is opened and logged, and the log line was in a clerk's hand and said *2 pp. from H. Bramm, Low Ward, re Hall of Former Faiths*, and the clerk had added, in the same hand, *no firm, no ref*.

"Six paragraphs," said the Board Secretary, and then read it out, which is what happens in this institution and which is the only reason anybody can be got at.

> *I am asking for one hour of a room on Friday the twenty-seventh of November, from ten to eleven in the morning.*
>
> *I am asking for the Hall of Former Faiths because that is the room where a number came out of a bell on Saturday the seventh of November in front of fifty-one people, and I was in it, and I have been in eleven rooms in this city about this and this is the only one that could hold the number.*
>
> *I am asking for the count called at the gate on the morning of the thirtieth of October 1974, being the last working day, to be called aloud in that room by me, in front of the people, with a book in front of me, because I was the gate clerk and I wrote it down and I have said nothing about it for thirty-one years.*
>
> *I am not asking for a service and I am not asking for a prayer and I am not asking for anybody to say anything about God. I am asking for a number to be said out loud in a room where the people it is about can hear it, and for it to go into a record that will still be there when the four of us who can get to Low Ward are not.*
>
> *I am asking that whatever the Museum's own paper says, if it is wrong, that the wrongness be said out loud in the same room, by the Museum, and not in a leaflet.*
>
> *I am asking for the list. Not the hundred and forty, which is mine and which I will not give away and have said why. The list of who is in the room. And I am asking that the Museum does not decide it.*

Mara Iles read the sixth paragraph twice, because it was the one the room needed, and then she looked at her own minute of the fourteenth of October and read two lines out of it, in a level voice, in the order it was written.

"Five. Two. That on Saturday the seventh of November 2024 a Schedule 12 carried object at plinth 34 in the Hall of Former Faiths was reported as having reached a person who had not consented to it, in a public room, in the presence of fifty-one persons, thirty-eight of whom were children, and an Annexe E stay was attached at ten past one in the afternoon by a Registry assessor in front of eleven adults. Six. That the Registrar's answer is that a material-change inspection was held on Thursday the nineteenth of November and the determination was that there has been no material change in the condition or classification of the object, and that the stay stands, and that the determination is on the public register from Friday the twentieth with a registration number, and that the route to it is the requester, whose form is QA/CR/1187, and the requester is the man sitting by the door."

Nobody looked at the man by the door.

"That is the request," said Mara Iles. "Two pages from a woman of fifty-four who has been in this building twice, once as a member of the public and once by my invitation, and who has not been asked for anything by any member of staff in six weeks, including by me, and I have written that down at the end and I am not going to read it out."

"Why not," said the trustee of about seventy.

"Because it is a note to myself and it is not a matter for the Board," said Mara Iles, "and if I read it out then it becomes a thing this Museum has said about itself in the past tense, and I have watched four people do that in six weeks and I am not doing it."

The Chair let about four seconds go by, which was generous of her.

"Director."

---

And Anja Ward stood up, which she did not have to do, and read nine sentences off a sheet of her own paper, and she did not look at the sheet.

"I am permitting it. It is on Friday the twenty-seventh of November at ten to eleven in the morning in the Hall of Former Faiths, and it is a closed room, and I am the person who closes it."

"The Annexe E stay is stricter than my condition four, it has been in force since the seventh of November, and it is stricter, not weaker, and I have said so in a minute on Wednesday and I am saying it again in this one. Condition four is satisfied and must stay satisfied. The object is not opened, is not touched, is not handled, is not cleaned and is not moved, and the cover is not put on it, and the case stays where it is on plinth thirty-four with the seal on it."

"There will be no child in that room. The Registry has no threshold in the schedule for the number or age of persons in a room, this Board has been told that on a public register this week, and I am not spending it twice."

"There will be eighty people and not one more, and the eighty are the families, and the list is the families' and not mine, and there is a name against every seat and a form at the door with a name and a relationship and a right to leave."

"No member of Museum staff will state a figure in that room. Any figure spoken in that room will be spoken by a member of the public, and will be a figure with a name against it."

"There will be a workers' panel on the case, and the wording of it is the families' and this Museum may not amend it and may not add to it, and our own plinth card stays in place underneath it. That is the compromise. It is a bad compromise and I am offering it because it is the only one on the table."

"The press line is mine, it is one sentence, it contains no religious word, and nobody in that room will speak to a journalist, and I will be the only person in this building who talks to the press on Friday."

"The Museum will pay for the room, and will indemnify reasonable attendance costs, and will not settle, and will not say that it will not settle, and our insurer's position is in a document and I am not going to offer you a document as though it were comfort."

"And here is what it costs them, in one sentence, because you should not have to work it out: the request came in on Friday the twentieth and this Board sits on the twenty-fourth and the event is on the twenty-seventh, which is four days from the request and three days from this room, and both of those are too short; the room is closed, so a woman who wants to come and is not on a list of eighty does not come; the list of eighty is a list of names and it is not the list of everybody, and there is no list of everybody, and that is not a fault of this Museum's making and it is a fault of this Museum's making; and the Museum's own acquisition record for 1974 goes into that room, in a file, on the table, with a date on it, and it says that this Museum bought that mill office on the seventh of November 1974 and did not pay a disposal fee."

She sat down.

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, and then Mrs Angela Vessey said:

"Director, that is nine sentences and I want to say three things about them and I am going to say them in order and I am not going to be fair about the second one."

"Go ahead."

"First: you have permitted an event in a public gallery, under a statutory stay, in a room this Board certified for school groups on the seventh of November, and you have done it by attaching conditions that are defensible, and every one of the nine is something I would have written. I have been a trustee of this institution for five years and I have read the Annexe D heading in this room and I have watched you not put a word in a minute, and I have not been able to work out for five years whether that was discipline or luck, and this morning I have decided it was neither and it was a woman who knew what a minute is for." She turned a page. "Second: condition four is not a condition. Condition four is a surrender, and you have dressed it as a condition because a surrender in a minute is a resignation and you have been a director for nineteen years. You have handed the list of who is in a room in this building to a woman of fifty-four, and you cannot take it back, and I want it minuted that I said to you *that is a surrender* and that you said yes."

"Yes," said Anja Ward.

"Third." Mrs Vessey looked at the folder in front of her. "I want to know who is going to be in the room on Friday, and I am not asking the Director, because the Director has just told me she does not decide, and I am asking you, and I am asking you in front of the Director so that whatever you say gets written down with both of you in it."

"I do not know," said Mara Iles. "There are about eleven names I have and a list of a hundred and forty that she will not give away, and I have not asked her for it and I am not going to ask her for it, because she has told me in a document why she will not, and the reason is *a list of a hundred and forty names stops being a list of people and starts being evidence*, and she is right, and I have not got an argument."

"Whose document?"

"Her own. On her own paper. In her own hand. Dated the twenty-ninth of October." Mara Iles squared her folder. "I have got a copy of a schedule of contents with one line in it, dated Tuesday, and the first line that has been in that document since the eleventh of September is about something that is not ours, and it has a name in it and it is the Director's, and I am not going to pretend to this Board that the Museum's record-keeping improved this month because of me. It improved because a Director put her name on a piece of paper in her own office on Friday morning at eleven o'clock so that a registrar could bring a page into a file."

Mrs Vessey wrote for about four seconds, and then she said: "The second trustee asked a question in the recess and I am going to ask it in full, because it was a good question and it deserves the room."

---

"There is a release in my pack," said the trustee of about seventy, "drafted by the insurers, and it is standard, and I am going to read you the operative sentence and then I am going to say why I am not happy about it. *The attendees undertake that they have no claim against the Museum of Quiet Saints in respect of any matter arising from the attendance.*" He put it down. "It is boilerplate. It is in every pack. And it is the right thing to have in the room, because there will be eighty people in a public gallery in this building on Friday morning at a request made by one of them, and the Museum's liability position is documented and is not comfortable, and the document in front of me says so in nine lines."

"No," said Anja Ward.

Mrs Vessey did not interrupt, and the room did not either.

"No," said the Director, "and I will give you the reason and it is not a legal one. That sentence is a release from people who were short-paid for thirty-one years. It costs this Museum nothing. It is worth a stamp. It is on the letterhead of this institution and it would be signed by about eighty people, most of them in their seventies, most of them who have never signed anything in this room in their lives, in a room where a bell is about to be counted, and it would go into an inquiry file in about seven years." She put her hands flat on the paper. "And then the next thing anybody does with this business is point at it. That is the whole of my reason. In four years somebody will stand up in a room and say *the Museum got them to sign something*, and it will be true, and there will be a document, and I will have spent three conditions buying the appearance of a process and sold the only thing I actually had, which is that nobody in that room has signed anything for anybody, ever, in fifty years."

The trustee of about seventy picked the sentence up and read it again and put it in an envelope and the envelope went into the Board Secretary's folder with a minute note on it that says *not executed*, and that is all that ever happened to it, and it is in the file.

"Item two, resolved," said Mrs Angela Vessey. "Director, in your own words, and I want the conditions minuted as you said them and not as a summary, because a summary is how a condition becomes a thing that was always a condition."

"The minute will carry my words," said Anja Ward.

"Mr Venn." Mrs Vessey had not turned round. "I have read your statement of the fourteenth of October four times. I have read it because it was in a pack in a file that I had a right to, and it is four hundred words and three and a half pages of them are about you. I am going to ask you one question and I am going to ask it as a trustee and not as a woman who reads headings, and then I am going to let you go, and you may write to me afterwards and I will read it." She turned her chair about ninety degrees. "Why on earth should this Museum do this? The object is stayed. The acquisition is this Museum's own, in 1974, for nothing, and the wage on the mill's last morning is not the Museum's debt and never was. The families can be given a claim, and a claim is money, and money is what a Museum can do. Why a room, and a bell, and eighty people, and a woman saying a number out loud in public?"

Elias Venn took about two seconds, and he answered the question that was asked and not a larger one, which was what he had been told to do at a quarter to ten in the morning by the Chair, who has the hands of a person who has wound things for fifty years.

"Because the Hall of Former Faiths has got a bronze bell on a plinth in it under a card with a number on it, and the number is not a Museum accession number, and the card has been cut, and the erasure is described in a Registry photograph as partial, and the photograph has been in a public register since Friday morning." He kept his hands on his knees. "And because this Museum has nine Schedule 12 objects on open plinths in three public galleries and about two hundred in store, and the only provision it has ever made for any of them is a rope line, and the rope line is not a safety measure. It is a mechanism. Whoever is in the room is doing the observing, and it has been the mechanism for two hundred years in this city and that is the reason the buildings are free, and the Director has never been asked that question until Wednesday and she answered it correctly."

"Go on."

"So either the object on plinth thirty-four is not a carried pattern, or a rope line is not a provision. And I am not the person who decides which, and neither is this Board, and neither is the Registry, because the Registry has said in a determination this week that the schedule has no threshold in it for the number or age of the people in a room, and that is not an oversight, that is the answer. So on Saturday the seventh of November fifty-one people were in that room and thirty-eight of them were children, and one of them finished a sentence a man in his early sixties started, and nobody in the room stopped it, including the only person who could have, including me, and including the woman who wrote the request and who had a word that would have stopped it and would not use it because using it would have stopped the count." He stopped. "That is the whole of why. And I am not asking this Museum to admit a debt. I am asking it to open a door it has kept shut and to let somebody say a number in a room, and then to be wrong in public if it turns out it is wrong."

Mrs Angela Vessey looked at him for about four seconds, and then she turned back to the table.

"That is the first sentence anybody has said in this room this morning that was not an answer to my question," she said, "and I am not going to say anything else about it in front of the Director because I have got four items left and the Director has a Gallery to certify at two o'clock. Minute it as an answer and not as a statement, because he was not asked for a statement."

"An answer and not a statement," said the Board Secretary.

"And Director—" Mrs Vessey had a page in front of her. "One more, and this is a request and not a question. The object on plinth thirty-four is a Schedule 12 carried object under a permanent Annexe D classification and a stay, and I have read the heading, and I am not going to use the word, and I am going to say the thing underneath it. *A pattern requiring continued observance. To be displayed.*" She squared it. "If the count is called on Friday and the object answers, then this Museum will have, in a public room, in front of eighty people, on the record, produced the thing that four hundred copies of a schedule in four hundred buildings exist to prevent. Is that a reason to refuse."

"That is the reason to do it," said Anja Ward.

"Then I am not going to ask you the second half of that question," said Mrs Angela Vessey, "because I do not want to know the answer on a Wednesday, and I have spent five years learning that the questions I do not want answered are the ones that make me a trustee instead of a reader."

---

The Board sat from ten until eleven minutes to one and reconvened at twenty-five past two and finished at about half past four, and the minute is forty-one pages, and item two is nineteen of them, and conditions one to nine are minuted in the Director's own words, and the Chair has recorded that two of them are not conditions of this Board at all, and the minute says so.

> **2.8. RESOLVED** that the attendance of a workers' memorial in the Hall of Former Faiths be permitted on Friday 27 November 2024, 10:00–11:00, the Hall being closed to the public for that hour and to all parties thereafter until 09:50 on Saturday 13 December 2024, subject to the following conditions of the Director, which are minuted in her own words and not summarised at her request:
> **2.8.1** the Annexe E stay of 7 November 2024 is stricter than condition four of the Director's standing conditions of 15 October 2024 and remains in force; the object is not opened, handled, used, cleaned, moved or approached; the cover is not placed upon it.
> **2.8.2** no person under the age of eighteen shall be admitted to the Hall of Former Faiths on 27 November 2024. The resolution of the sub-group of 18 November 2024 (no school group; no party not holding a collections pass) is not displaced by this resolution and binds.
> **2.8.3** attendance is limited to eighty persons, the list of whom is determined by the requester and is not amendable by this Museum, and a name is recorded against every seat.
> **2.8.4** an attendance form is completed at the door, with name, relationship to the persons named in the wage records, and the right to leave at any time without giving a reason.
> **2.8.5** no figure is to be stated by any member of Museum staff in that room. Any figure spoken in that room is spoken by a member of the public and is recorded with the name of the person who spoke it.
> **2.8.6** a workers' panel is placed on the case, its wording determined by the requester; the Museum may not amend and may not add to it; the Museum's own plinth card remains in place beneath it.
> **2.8.7** the Museum issues one written line to the press and no member speaks to a journalist on 27 November 2024. The line contains no religious word.
> **2.8.8** the Museum pays the cost of the room and indemnifies reasonable attendance costs; it does not settle and does not state that it will not settle. The insurer's position is at appendix 3.
> **2.8.9** the Museum's own acquisition record for 1961/LW/41–52 is placed in the room on the table, with its date, available for any person present to read.
> **Chair's minute:** *2.8.3 and 2.8.6 are not conditions of this Board. They are concessions. I have been told by the Director that she intends them as conditions and I have accepted the minute as she asked. I record the difference.*
> **A. VESSEY, Chair.**

---

Elias Venn was in a corridor on the second floor at ten to five on the twenty-fourth of November with a sheet of Museum notepaper in his hand on which he had written down nine sentences in the order they had been said, in a flat hand, without a full stop, because full stops invite improvements.

The Director came out of the Board room with the Board Secretary and a folder and said goodnight to the Board Secretary, and then stopped, and stood about four feet away from him and did not come closer, which he noticed and wrote down afterwards.

"That was well done," said Anja Ward. "That answer of yours. It was four sentences and it answered the question."

"I had a week to write it."

"You had about four seconds and you did not use four of them, which is not the same and is better." She put the folder under her arm. "And I am going to say the one thing I said in that room that was not procedure, and then I am going to go home, and I would like it understood that I am saying it in a corridor and not in a minute, because a minute is a public document and this is a director talking to a man."

"Go ahead."

"Nineteen years." She looked at the window, which looks at a yard. "Nineteen years of four o'clock meetings and a collections schedule and a rope line and a form for everything, and I have been perfectly correct every single day, and in nineteen years I have never once in this building opened a door for anybody, because opening a door is not a function of a director, it is a function of a building, and buildings open their own doors." She turned round. "And on Friday morning at ten o'clock one of them is going to be opened by me, in public, with a minute about it, and I am going to be extremely unpopular with about nine people in this city, and four of them are on the Board and one of them is me."

"You'd rather be hated than wrong."

"I would rather be hated than wrong," said Anja Ward, "and I have been right about very little this month, and if I am wrong about Friday then it is in item two of a forty-one-page minute and you can all have it, and I would like it recorded that I did not dress it up in there and that nine sentences took me about twenty minutes and four of them were about what it costs them."

"They are on a page of my own."

"Good," she said. "Then somebody's got them in his own handwriting, and that is the only thing in this business that has ever worked."

She went down to the first floor and across to the Gallery of Unnamed Hands at about ten to five, and a woman of fifty-five with a body camera and a printed list was packing up in it, and Elias Venn went down to the front desk and did the dead letters until about twenty to nine, and there were three hundred and forty of them and he did not write the number down, and the number had stopped meaning anything about four hundred of the time.

At nine o'clock he wrote eleven lines into the notebook, and they were the nine conditions, and then he wrote one line under them that was not in the minute and was not in the corridor and was only ever on that page.

> **Tue 24 Nov, 21:05. Nine conditions, verbatim, and the ninth one is the only one that is a price, and it is the price, and the price is a receipt dated the seventh of November 1974 with no disposal fee on it, on a table, in a public room, in front of the people.**
> **And the people who are paying it are not the Museum.**
> **I have been in this building for thirty-nine days and I have learned one thing and I have learned it from a form, and the thing is that this institution will do almost anything correctly and it will do it at somebody else's expense, and the somebody is always a person who cannot write, and the reason the Museum gets away with it for two hundred years is that the person who cannot write is not in the room when it is decided.**
> **On Friday I will be in the room. That is the whole of what I have managed to arrange in six weeks, and it is not nothing, and it is not nearly enough, and I am going to be forty-five minutes of a closed room in a public gallery and then I am going to be a man with a notebook and no post.**
> **And nobody is going to be in that room at four foot six off the floor, which is where I put the poster, and I put it there, and I have never been able to work out why I did that and I have had a month to work on it.**
