He put the pen on the page, and the sentence came.

And he stopped, with the nib about an inch off the paper, because he knows the sentence. He wrote it on Monday the sixteenth of November at about twenty past eleven in the morning, standing up in a loading bay with a clipboard on his knee and a bay-check sheet on top of a cage, in about forty seconds, and then he copied all five lines into this, which is what he does, and it is in his own hand, and the nib pressure is his and the lean is his and he could not tell you a single time in his life when he has written a better sentence.

And it was on the page in front of him.

Not in his hand. In a rounder and lighter hand than his, with less pressure in it and a slant that went the other way, in ballpoint, on the same fourth page from the end, underneath his own, and it was the same sentence, word for word, including the bits he had put in that were not about a cage, and it was dated *Mon 16 Nov, 11:20*.

He did not put the pen down. He sat and looked at it for a long time, and the room was very quiet, and the boiler ticked, and the letter was on the shelf above his head with his father's name on it in his mother's hand and the paper gone soft along a fold that had learned to hold itself.

There was nothing wrong with either sentence. That is what he could not get past. There was nothing missing from either one and nothing extra, and neither half referred to the other, and both were complete, and a page of a notebook is a page of a notebook and there is no possible reading of a clean page with one sentence on it in his own hand that would account for the same sentence in another hand on the same page written on the same day.

He was not frightened.

That is the part he has not been able to put down since, and it is the part he put on the page, because he puts everything on the page, and the page is the fourth page from the end, and the sentence beneath his hand reads *Mon 16 Nov, 11:20*, and he does not know which of the two he wrote first, and he is not going to be told which, and there is no second entry and there is nothing missing and there is no name gone and no headache and no blood, and he got up at half past nine and put the kettle on and stood in a kitchen for about nine minutes and did not find it frightening at all.

He should have found it frightening.

He has had five weeks of a thing that frightened him — a bell in a case, a public gallery, a woman of fifty-four typing a gap in a run, a man of seventy-eight on a pavement, a woman of seventy-eight in a front room with a fire on, a name on a door in a bucket, a red disc in a post room — and every single one of them frightened him, and the things he was frightened of were all *processes*, and this is not a process. This is two sentences and he cannot tell which of them he is.

He wrote nothing else on the page. He shut the notebook and put it in the inside pocket of his coat, where it had been every night since the tenth of October, and it is still the one article in this building that no standing instruction has yet managed to find a clause to put it under.

Then he went to bed, and the crack in the bell's shoulder is still open under the staple and it is not going to heal, and the object is on plinth thirty-four, and the Hall is open, and the lights are on, and nobody may touch it, and a determination with a number on it is going up on a public register in Northglass in about eleven hours, and there is a form in his coat that says **TWENTY-EIGHT DAYS FROM THE DATE OF DETERMINATION — 17 DEC 2024** and a box on it with a period on it, and in seven days he is going to have to stand up in front of six families and explain what a bell is, and the day after that a woman of seventy-eight is going to open an envelope in her own front room at four o'clock in the afternoon with a gas fire on, and he still does not know, and he has known since the tenth of October, and the only difference between the tenth of October and the nineteenth of November is that in November he has said out loud, on a tape, in a room, that he did not know.
