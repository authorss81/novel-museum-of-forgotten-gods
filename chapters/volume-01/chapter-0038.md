# Chapter 0038 — The Fourth Monday

Anja Ward's office is on the second floor and has a window that looks at the yard and not at the city, and she had a table with nothing on it except a folder and a pen and a mug, and it was 09:20 on Tuesday the seventeenth of November and Elias Venn had been in the building since seven and had a docket that died at five o'clock the following afternoon and a notebook in his coat with seven lines on the back of a bay-check sheet.

"Twenty minutes," she said. "And then I've got a meeting, and it's not about you, and I have not moved it, and I want that first because people always think I move things."

She put a card on the table. It was a card the size of a catalogue entry and it was in her own hand and it was dated the twentieth of October, and it had been in a folder on her table, and she turned it round so that it was facing him and then she turned it back so that she could read it while she read it out, which she did, in a level voice, and did not editorialise.

> **E. Venn, 32. Suspended from night duty 07:00, Sun 11 Oct 2024, on work order 4412-S, a door 7 corridor fault he did not close. No further incident attributed to the same cause in five weeks and none likely. He has been the mechanism twice: once for a Registry stay on a Schedule 12 object by his own request of 19 Oct, and once, possibly, for a disposition by his own conduct. He reports honestly and late. He is the man in the room now and not the man with the fact, and the man in the room is not safer. A person who causes the events he investigates should not be the investigator. I do not have a mechanism to say that in a document and it is the only reservation I hold.**
> **A. WARD, 20 Oct 2024.**

She read it to the end and then put the card face down on the table.

"That's it," she said. "It's been in a folder on that table for four weeks. It is now going in a drawer in a file, dated today, and it is going in with today's date and not with October's, because if it goes in with October's date it looks like something I decided before I had all of it, and I had not had all of it on the twentieth of October and I have got all of it now, and the reservation has not gone away and it has got four weeks more evidence under it."

"Why are you reading it to me?"

"Because in about four years there will be a person in a chair who wants to know when a director first thought that, and I would like there to be a witness to the fact that I thought it in October and did not say so to the man it was about until November." She put the card in the file and closed the file. "And because it is the honest reason and the other reason is that you are about to be extremely difficult about a decision I am about to make and I would rather you had read the card first."

---

"There are two decisions," she said, "and they are both mine, and neither of them is yours, and I am going to take about four minutes on each and you are not going to like the second one."

"Okay."

"The first one is dated. It is a decision under Standing Instruction 14, clause five, on a matter I have had on my desk since the twenty-third of October. I am dating it today, and not with the date I should have dated it, which is the fifteenth of October, and I want that on the record as a refusal and not as a date. I am not going to back-date a decision I did not take, because in about four years somebody is going to read this file and work out that a director sat on it for a month, and they might as well work that out from me."

She turned a sheet round.

> **DECISION — STANDING INSTRUCTION 14, CLAUSE FIVE.**
> **Matter:** Four photographic exposures of Gallery flat file drawer 6B, open, in place, including a card, made 6 March 2024 by I. PELL, systems technician, in the course of a scheduled condition survey of the Gallery's six flat files for the insurance schedule of collections; entered in the systems index on 6 March under *schedule of collections, condition, Gallery flat files*, sheets initialled S. PELL; four of the 246 exposures delivered to Low Ward police station on 23 Oct 2024 by the same person, in his own working capacity, without request, and recorded as delivered in that capacity.
> **Finding:** The survey was scheduled, was in the index, and would have been made in the same month by the same person whether or not the drawer had been subsequently photographed and delivered. The exposures were of a record in the course of a survey of records. Clause five is not complied with. It is not complied with because the act it prohibits is not the act done.
> **This finding is made on the basis of the act and not on the basis of a distinction of professional status, and if a member of staff photographs collections material in the course of a duty, the duty is the reason and the status of the photographer is not a defence to it in any other case.**
> **No disciplinary action.**
> **Consequent condition, with effect from 1 Dec 2024:** Every photographic exposure of collections material, labels, or records made by a member of staff in the course of any survey, condition check, or schedule compilation shall be countersigned at the point of making by a supervisor, and the countersignature shall carry the reason and the schedule reference. The systems technician's post is unchanged. The condition is not a demotion. It is the removal of a man's ability to be alone in a room with a camera, and I am aware of that, and I am doing it, and I would like it recorded that I am aware of it.**
> **A. WARD, Director. 17 Nov 2024.**

She put it down.

"That is defensible," she said. "It is also horrible, and it is horrible in a way I have thought about for four days, and I would like you to understand that I have thought about it, and then I would like you to not say anything about it, because the next part is worse."

---

He had it at eleven. Ivo Pell came down off the systems floor with a printout under his arm and read it standing in the corridor outside the Director's office, and read it twice, and then said:

"It's clause five."

"It's clause five."

"Right." He put the printout under his arm again. "So. March is fine. March is completely fine, and I have been sick about March for four weeks, and you've just told me March was a duty and the duty was the reason, and I want to say something about that and I want to say it now while she's in there and can hear it through the door." He raised his voice slightly, toward the door. "Thank you."

Anja Ward's voice came through the door. "Mr Pell, I did not decide it in your favour and you are not to thank me, because I have taken the only thing off you that made you safe and I have given it back to you as a condition and you will not like December."

"I like it now."

"You will like it less in three weeks when you have to find a supervisor to countersign a condition survey of a flat file at half past ten on a Monday." He turned to Elias Venn in the corridor. "Right. And the other thing, and I'm saying this to you and not to her, and she knows." He was not smiling. "I gave four photographs to a police station. I did it because four exposures of an erased card in a drawer were the only thing in the world that could say what had been done to that card, and at the time I did not know that saying it would cost me, and now I know exactly what it costs, and I would still do it, and that is a thing I have had to sit with for four weeks and I have not been able to tell anybody, and I have told a wall." He knocked on the door. "Are you going to say the sensible thing?"

"Mr Pell has not asked me a question."

"Then I will say it for you," said Ivo Pell. "The sensible thing is that the thing I did was the right thing and I have been sick about it and now I have been given permission and I do not want the permission. That is the sensible thing. Everybody in this building who has ever done anything has said that, and I have watched all of you say it since the start of October, and I have got a very strong memory for it because it is the sentence my sister says about her chest." He went up. "Tell her the door thing was me and not the wall."

---

The second decision was in a corridor at about four o'clock in the afternoon, and it took about nine minutes, and Elias Venn was standing in it for eight of them and did most of the talking in a corridor for the fourth time in five weeks, which Mara Iles has told him is not a fact about him and is a fact about the building.

She had a form. It was one sheet of Museum notepaper, printed on the front, and the front said:

> **MUSEUM OF QUIET SAINTS — DISCLOSURE OF DOCUMENTARY MATERIAL HELD ELSEWHERE**
> **1. The document:** [ ]
> **2. Date sent:** [ ]
> **3. Held by:** [ ]
> **4. Reference on the envelope, if any:** [ ]
> **5. Why it has not previously been disclosed to this Museum:** [ ]
> **6. Action requested of the Director:** [ ] Request the document into a Museum file, under the date of this request. [ ] Other, specify.
> **7. Date of request:** [ ]
> **8. Signature:** [ ] Director.

"There is no Museum number on it," Elias said, "and there is no clause."

"There is no Museum number on it because I did not put one on, and there is a print run of four hundred of these in the cupboard behind me and they go out with a pack of compliment slips and a pen, and the whole point of them is that they are not Museum documents, they are a *request* to somebody, and a request does not need a number and a request does not need a clause." She handed him the pen. "Six. Write: *I am asking for the document to be brought into a Museum file under the date of this request, so that it is no longer an unsolicited document in a file the Museum did not request, which is what clause six makes it, and which is why it is worth nothing.*"

He wrote it.

"And five. *Because I was not asked.* Which is not an excuse. It is the reason, and the reason is that nobody in this building ever asked me for anything, and a thing you are never asked for is a thing you cannot send in."

"The second one is the important one." She took the sheet back. "Elias, the first package is in the Board Secretary's file and it is worth nothing. It is worth nothing because clause six is a *disposal* bar: a document lodged unsolicited in a file the Museum has not requested is a document nobody may take out of that file and use, and cannot be moved, and cannot be re-disclosed, and the only route out of it is for the Museum to *ask for the document*, and a Museum that asks for a document it already has is making a fresh receipt for it, and a fresh receipt puts it in a file the Museum did request, and after that it is a Museum record and it is worth whatever it is worth."

"And you have never asked."

"I have never been told about the second one." She said it without any edge at all, which was worse. "That is the thing I have been going to say to the Director since the twenty-eighth of October, and I have not said it, and clause six has been sitting on both of them for a month, and it is *my* clause, and I wrote it, and I have been carrying a rule I wrote to keep a set of photographs out of a file and it has kept a set of photographs out of a file and a package out of a solicitor's safe out of a Director's knowledge, and both of those are the same mistake, and I made it in September, and it has taken me five weeks to be able to write it on a form in a corridor."

"You found out on Friday," Elias said.

"I found out on Friday at about half past four, in a doorway, in about ninety seconds, and you told me because you have run out of other people." She folded the sheet once. "And I want to say clearly that the thing I have always asked for has not been given, and I am not going to ask you for it in a corridor on a Tuesday, and if you decide to give it to me you will do it in my office with the door shut and you will do it on a day and not at half past four in a doorway, and I will put it in a drawer and I will not read it in front of you."

They went up together to the second floor and Mara Iles put the form on the Director's table and said, in a corridor, in a doorway, in about forty seconds:

"The second package. Vail & Thwaite. Posted the evening of Tuesday the thirteenth of October, registered, a reference and a date on the outside, and it has been in a firm in another city ever since, and I have known about it for sixty seconds, and it is in the sixth box on this form and the seventh wants your signature and a date. The first one is in the Board Secretary's file and cannot move and is worth nothing, and I am not asking you to do anything about that this afternoon."

Anja Ward read the form. She read it twice, and she read the sixth box and then the fifth box, and then she put it down and looked at the two of them in the doorway.

"Thursday," she said.

"Thursday is the material-change inspection," said Mara Iles.

"Thursday the inspection is at eleven and this is a form with one signature on it and it takes four seconds." She picked up her pen, and did not sign it, and put the pen down. "No. I am not signing it this afternoon, and I am going to tell you both why, and it is because I have been the Director of this building for nineteen years and in nineteen years I have never once been handed a piece of paper by a member of staff in a corridor and signed it on the spot, and the reason I have not is that a Director who signs things on the spot is a Director who is managing the paperwork instead of running an institution, and I have watched two of them do it and one of them is me and I did it for nine years and it took a bell."

"So when," said Mara Iles.

"Not this week. I have an inspection on Thursday, a determination behind it that I do not control, a Board sub-group on Wednesday that has been sitting since the first of September, and six households who have been promised a room on the twenty-sixth by a Director who no longer controls the first sentence of anything." She squared the form. "Friday. I will sign it on Friday or I will write to you on Friday and say why not, and either way you will have it in writing, and that is not me being difficult, that is the first time in five weeks that anybody in this building has got a date out of me, and I have given you one."

She picked the form up and put it in the folder and did not close the folder.

"Which brings me," said Anja Ward, "to the fourth Monday."

---

"Twenty-five minutes past nine on Wednesday the twenty-fifth," she said. "Here, and before you ask, the twenty-fifth is a Wednesday, and I know it is a Wednesday, and I have written *Wednesday* four times on a piece of paper this morning and crossed it out three times. It was the fourth Monday. It is now the seventh. It is not a disciplinary hearing and it is not a capability and it is not a hearing under the standing instruction, because there is no incident to refer and clause seven has nothing to bite on, and I have been calling it a review since the eleventh of October and it has never been anything, and the notice on the noticeboard still says *fourth Monday* and I am not going to take it down, because a notice is a document and a document is corrected, and a person is not. And I have put it on the Wednesday, the day before the hearing, on purpose, because on Thursday at six o'clock six households are going to be in a room with a field officer and I would like a file with something in it from the Wednesday."

"What is it."

"It is half an hour in which you say whatever you want to say about the last five weeks and I ask you about it, and it is minuted, and you get a copy, and the copy goes in a file, and after that there is no further review in your suspension because there is nothing further to review." She looked at the window and the yard. "And I have to tell you the part I do not like, and I would rather tell you here than in a room with a minute-taker."

"Go on."

"There is one thing I have to put in front of you and it is not on the card." She turned back. "In five weeks you have done the following: you got a Registry stay put on a Schedule 12 object by asking for an inspection in a corridor; you put a person's name in a room with a tape recorder in it who had never been asked to be in a room; you sent a set of photographs to a police station, which was Ivo's doing and which I have just permitted and which was correct; you stood in a public gallery on a Saturday with fifty-one people in it and asked a question of a bell; and you have stood in eight corridors in five weeks and been the man who is not on the payroll."

"I have."

"Every one of those is correct. I have checked them all and they are all correct and three of them are the best work anybody has done in this building in a decade, and if I were asked to write a sentence about you for a panel I would write that sentence and I would sign it." She stopped. "And every one of them happened because you were in a room. Not because you had a right to be in it. Because you were in it. In each case you got into a room you were not permitted in by signing a visitors' book as a member of the public, or by having a card that still opens a door it should not, or by being the only man in the building at a quarter past one in the morning. And in October the police asked me, on the telephone, whether a suspended night guard had been in a corridor at one in the morning, and I said yes, and I did not tell them anything else, and I am the Director of a public museum."

"Then say the rest of it."

"The rest of it is that I have run this building for nineteen years without a single instance of anybody being stopped, and I have done it with forms, and the forms are good, and in October I wrote four of them in a hurry at six in the morning, and one of them is Annexe E, and it has taken a bell out of my gallery for an unknown period, and it is *working*, and the reason it is working is that you lodged a form at a front desk in October for a reason that was six words long and entirely true." She put her hands flat on the folder. "I am not going to say to you that you are dangerous. I have had the advice and I have not accepted it. I am going to say that you are a procedure that has not got a name, and that I do not know what to do with a procedure that has not got a name, and that on the twenty-fifth of November at half past nine I am going to have half an hour in which you may tell me what to do with it, and that you will not enjoy it, and that I have run out of instruments, and so have you, and neither of us has said that to the other one yet."

---

He was in the building until a quarter to eleven.

The docket was in the building and it was signed and it was in the system and it was going to die at five o'clock the following afternoon, and there was no version of any document he had access to that changed that, and Nunn was going to be there, and it was going to be furniture for the second time in eleven weeks.

He did the dead letters. There were three hundred and nine of them.

At about half past nine, in the ground-floor corridor by the fire assembly point, on a plywood board with four routed lines on it, there was a notice that had been up since the twenty-first of October, and underneath it somebody had put a second notice in a plastic sleeve on the same board, and the second notice had been up since half past eight that morning, and nobody had told him, and nobody was going to.

> **ASSISTANCE AT EVACUATION — LEVEL 2 & ABOVE — REMINDER.**
> *A person on their own is a party. A person who is not sure whether they are a party is a party.*
> **From 1 December 2024, countersignature required at the point of making for any photographic exposure of collections material, labels or records. Schedule reference to be stated. — Ref: SI14/5, 17.11.24.**

He stood and looked at it for a while.

Then he went up the hill, and at about ten he got the tram, and on the tram he took out the notebook and turned to the page with the seven lines on it, and he read item 7.

> **7. The notebook itself. A hundred and fifteen pages. Nobody in this building has read it. Mara Iles has never seen it. She has asked for it and I have not said no, and that is worse, and I have been not-saying-no to a woman I have four times promised there is no more private channel with.**

And his mother, in a laundrette in Low Ward, on the Monday night, had said: *take it in and shut the door and give it to her and watch her face while she reads the first page, and then never do it again for the rest of your life.*

And on Wednesday the eighteenth of November, at about half past ten in the morning, he was going to stand in a room with a projector and a Board sub-group and a name he wrote on a Saturday in a conservation office, and on the twenty-sixth he was going to stand in a room with six families and a field officer and a fee of a hundred and forty pounds, and on Thursday morning he was going to stand in a room as a member of the public and be right, and none of those four rooms had anything in them that he had not got.

That is the position on the seventeenth of November at twenty to eleven, at the end of the day, and it is the position everything after it is about: **he has run out of forms, and the only two moves left in this business are on Thursday and on the twenty-sixth, and neither of them is his, and he has a hundred and fifteen pages in a coat pocket that nobody in the world has read, and a woman of twenty-eight who has asked for it once.**

He got off the tram at the top of the hill and walked down, in the cold, past the shuttered shop, and he went into the one that was not shut, because it opens until eleven and it is the only room in this city where nobody has ever asked him a single question, and he sat down with his back to the wall where he can see both doors.

He did not do the letters. He took the notebook out and put it on the table and turned to the fourth page from the end and read the sentence that is on it, once, in his own hand, with his own lean on it, and it was still his, and he had read it that way every night for five weeks without once asking himself why he kept going back to it. Then he wrote the date at the top of a clean page, and one line under it:

> **Tue 17 Nov, 20:55. Wednesday is a sub-group and Thursday is a determination and neither of them is mine. Everything I have said so far has been said in a room with somebody else's form on the table.**
> **Item seven is the only move left that is mine and I have to decide it before Wednesday morning, because on Wednesday a name comes out of a minute in front of a projector and after that a hundred and fifteen pages in a coat pocket is a different object from what it is tonight, and I know that, and I have known it since the sixth of October, and I have been not-deciding it for five weeks.**

He put the notebook back in the inside pocket of his coat and went home, and he did not tell anybody, which is what he has been doing since the eleventh of November, and it is why there is a bell in this business that exactly one person has heard, and he is sitting in a café on a Tuesday night in November with a notebook nobody has read.
