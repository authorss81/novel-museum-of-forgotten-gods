# Chapter 0049 — A Hundred and Fifteen Pages, and a Box for a Period

Tuesday the eighth of December 2024 was the last day of his appointment and it was, by his own count and without any difficulty, the most tedious day of his working life, and he wrote that down on the Monday night, and it is the correct note for a man whose last act at work is a piece of paper.

He got up at seven in a rented room above a laundrette in Low Ward and did the dead letters at the kitchen table, and there were three hundred and fifty-three of them and he did not write the number down. He walked to the bottom of Sillick Lane at about half past nine and got the tram up the hill and walked down past the shuttered shop and stood in the entrance of a public museum for eleven minutes, because it was open and because it cost a pound and because he had decided, on the Sunday, in a plastic sleeve, in an empty lobby, that he would pay the pound.

It is a pound a head. He stood in the lobby and paid the pound and went up the stairs and walked the length of the ground floor, and about two hundred people went past him in about forty minutes and not one of them looked at him, and he looked at about nine plinths and four cases and a poster of a weighbridge at four foot six off the floor, and he looked at the poster for a long time.

He is not a night guard. He is not a suspended man with a post. He is a man of thirty-two who used to be a night officer in a public museum and who is, as of Tuesday the eighth of December 2024, a suspended man with a notebook, a cost and no authority, and there is no mechanism anywhere in this country that can put those three words on a form in that order, and he has looked.

He wrote that on the Tuesday night, and then he wrote the day's whole entry, and it is four lines long, and they are the four shortest lines in the notebook:

> **Tue 8 Dec.** Terminated 08:00, no fault, no appeal, barred from 1 Dec. Paid the pound and walked the ground floor and looked at the poster for about four minutes and it is a picture of a weighbridge with one line under it and the line is a lie, and I put that poster up, or I had it put up, and a girl of eleven asked a registrar why there wasn't a label and the registrar said because it's a photograph of a place, not of an object, and the girl said that's not the same thing, and she was right, and she was about eleven.
> **And I stood there this morning for four minutes and could not work out why I did it, and I have had two months to work it out, and I think it is because I wanted a thing to be in a room that I put in a room, and I have not got one of those and I am not going to get one, and a poster at four foot six is the smallest version of it a man can commit and get away with.**

He read the thirteen days' notice again on the Tuesday night and it says what it says, and clause 11 gives a Director a power and not a process, and he went and looked up clause 11 on the Tuesday night in a book of the Museum's constitution that he had borrowed from the Sillick Lane branch library on the Thursday, and it is two lines long, and the two lines say that a Director may determine the terms of the employment of any officer, and there is nothing else in it, and there is no appeal against the exercise of it because there is no finding to appeal against, and **a clause that is two lines long and gives a person a total power over another person with no reason required and no appeal is the shortest and most complete instrument in this business and it is four hundred years old and it is on the second page of a book in a public library and I had never read it.**

**On Friday the eleventh of December 2024, in a conservation office on the first floor of a building he was barred from, with the door shut, he handed Mara Iles a hundred and fourteen pages.**

The date was the eleventh. He gave it to her on the twenty-third of November in her own office, and she wrote it down so that he could not move it, and he did not move it, and it is on a card in a drawer in her office with her initials and a date, and it is item eight of nothing and item seven of the list in a hundred and fifteen pages, and it is the only promise he has made in six weeks that he kept exactly.

He brought it in a plastic wallet from the copy shop on the corner, which cost him thirty-five pence, and he had copied nothing out and recopied nothing and he had not made a fair copy, because she had said she wanted the pages and not a version of them.

He put it on the table and sat down and said: "Four readings, a corridor, four seconds of a name I cannot produce, and about a hundred and ten lines of times. It is in date order except for three pages and I have not renumbered them because renumbering it is a thing I would do on my own in a room with a door shut and it is exactly the thing I am not doing."

Mara Iles did not open it straight away. She put her pen down, and squared it, and said:

"Before I read it. Three things, and then I am not going to say anything for about twenty minutes."

"Go on."

"First: I am going to read it here and you are going to sit there, and I am not going to ask you a single question while I do it, and if I want to know something I am going to write it on a pad and ask you afterwards, and if there are eleven things on the pad then you are going to answer eleven questions and I am going to type them up and you are going to initial the type-up, and it will be a Museum record and it will be about a Museum object, and it will be in my file, and it will not be about you."

"That is better than I asked for."

"It is not better than you asked for, it is the only version of it that is any use, and you asked for the wrong version for six weeks and I have been waiting for you to ask for it." She opened the wallet.

"Second. I am going to read the last page first, and I know what is on it, because you told me what was on it in this office on Monday the twenty-third of November and I wrote a receipt on the back of it and put it in a drawer and did not read it, and I have thought about that receipt every day for eighteen days, and a receipt is a thing you put in a drawer when you have decided to trust somebody, and I have not done that with a document since I was about nine."

"That is the one I would take back if I could."

"You would not take it back," said Mara Iles, "and that is not a compliment to you, it is a fact about a man who keeps a hundred and fifteen pages in a coat pocket for six weeks and then gives a hundred and fourteen of them away in a room on a date he made a registrar write down on the twenty-third of November because he knew he would move it, and there is not a single page in there I cannot use and not one page in there that is mine, and I am going to read it and I am not going to do a thing with it tonight, and I want that in a minute if you would like to type it."

"Type it."

"I will type it," she said, "and it will say *received 11 Dec 2024, 114 pp., in the author's hand, unaltered, read in the author's presence, no copy taken by the recipient, no questions asked during reading.* And that is a minute, and minutes are hers, and I have never in five years written a minute that has anything to do with a person."

"Third."

"Third is the hard one, so I'm going to say it before I start, because if I say it afterwards it will sound like a thing I decided at the time." She squared the wallet with both hands and left them on it. "Elias, I am not going to be able to do this for you in about eleven months, and I want you to hear the sentence in this room, in December, from me, while we are both sitting down."

"Say it."

"I do not know what you are for," said Mara Iles. "That is not a figure of speech and I am not being kind. I have spent six weeks beside the only man in this city who can do a thing nobody else can do, and I have watched that thing be used in a loading bay at one in the morning, in a public gallery in front of fifty-one people of whom thirty-eight were children, and in a public room in front of eighty people, and on four other occasions it did not work or was not asked, and **in no case has it been the answer to a question, and in every single case it has been a thing that happened in a room while a man was standing in it, and I have spent six weeks waiting for a form that says what the room is supposed to do.**"

She opened the wallet.

"And there isn't one. I looked. There is a box for a period on a Registry form and it expires on the seventeenth of December, and there is a clause seven in a standing instruction about destroying a bureau record, and there is a director's card in a drawer with a sentence on it that I have read about four hundred times and cannot act on, and there is not one piece of paper in this building that says *a person who can hear an object may be asked to do so by anybody, under any circumstances, with any protection at all.* And that is the thing I have to tell you in December, because you are going to spend the next eleven months looking for somebody to be in a room with you, and there is not going to be one, and the not-going-to-be-one is a policy, and the policy is being written by a woman of thirty-four in Northglass who has never seen a piece of the evidence and has been right about everything, and I am not going to let you find that out in March."

Elias Venn sat in a chair in a conservation office on the first floor of a building he was barred from and said:

"That's all right."

"That is not all right."

"No," he said. "It's the second time anybody's told me. The first one was a woman of seventy-eight in a flat in Ferry Bank on the sixteenth of October and she said it in about forty words and then she said if you're the only one then by Christmas there's a hundred people in this city who need you to do it. And I've had two months to think about that and she was right and I have been doing it anyway, and I put it down on Friday on a card in a public gallery, and the reason I put it down is not nobility, it's that if I have got it then I get asked, and if I haven't got it then somebody has to ask a question in a room and write down what the room said, and the second one is the only one of the two that has ever worked."

Mara Iles looked at him for about four seconds and then she said, "That is the best sentence anybody has said to me in five years, and I am putting it in the minute, and you will initial the type-up," and she read a hundred and fourteen pages.

She read them for twenty-two minutes. She did not ask a question for twenty-two minutes. She did not make a fuss. She turned four pages upside down, once, and then turned them back, and she read a page of the first reading twice, and she read the four seconds of a name nine times, and at one point she said, "This is a load-bearing sentence" — about a line in a loading bay about a form — and wrote it on a pad and nothing else, and at no point in the twenty-two minutes did she look up.

And then she put the hundred and fourteen pages back in the plastic wallet, in order, and squared them, and said:

"That is a record and it is now a Museum record, and it is about a Museum object, and it is in my file, and it is not about you, and you do not have it back and I am not going to lend it to you."

"That's not what I—"

"I know it isn't what you wanted," said Mara Iles. "What you wanted was for me to know you. And I do know you, and I have known you since the sixth of October, and I have got a hundred and fourteen pages and a receipt on the back of the twenty-third of November and a man in the fourth row of a public gallery saying a stranger's name, and that is more than anybody in this city has, and I would like you to understand that a hundred and fourteen pages is not a price. **It is a record.** And you are going to be all right, and I am going to be all right, and the arrangement is that from now on there is no more of this in a coat pocket in any building, and there has not been any of this in a coat pocket since the eleventh of December, and that is the end of the only private channel there was, and it took nine weeks to close it, and it was closed from this side."

That is the whole of it, and it is on the page, and it is the last private thing that is given to anybody in this whole business.

**The form went in on Saturday the twelfth of December 2024 at about twenty past eleven in the morning, five days before it expired, and it is a piece of A4 with a box on it for a period, and it is the last thing he does in this building in any capacity at all.**

He wrote it out by hand, at a table in his rented room, on Monday the seventh, having thought about it for eighteen days, and he wrote it in eleven minutes and he did not show it to anybody, and the reason he did not show it to anybody is not secrecy and is on the page:

> **Wed 9 Dec. I have not told one person that I have this and Roth told me in a public gallery in front of six people on the nineteenth of November that I would not, and said that if I did not use it she would regard that as the correct outcome, and she was wrong about one thing and right about the rest: I am not going to use it alone.**
> **The reason is not the question. The question has been in my head since the nineteenth of November and it has got simpler every day and it is one sentence and it is not clever. The reason is the box for a period, and the box for a period is the whole instrument, and I have spent two months learning that the box is the document.**

It is a Registry form. It is the same form he lodged at a front desk on Monday the nineteenth of October with six words on it — **THE OBJECT IS OUT OF CONDITION** — and it is a printed sheet and it has a box at 1 for the document or the matter, and a box at 2 for a period, and a reference, and a signature, and it is the only form in this entire business that anybody in it has ever been given that is *in his favour*.

> **QUIET ACCORD — REQUESTER'S FURTHER QUESTION. Form A/3.**
> **1. The question put to the office, to be listed and decided on a stated day with a stated assessor in the room:**
> **Whether, before it certifies, re-certifies, or permits the continued display in a public room of any Schedule 12 carried object, the Registry will ask the question the schedule has no box for: how many people are in the room, and how old are they.**
> **2. Period sought: TWELVE MONTHS.**
> **Reason for the period, in the space provided: *because every other period in this business has been about somebody needing something by a Friday, and this one is about every Friday after this one. The Registry's inspections are announced on a rota. There are about forty rotas in this city and I have read every one of them and they are published eleven weeks in advance. I would like to be in the room for a year of them. — E. VENN, 12 Dec 2024.***
> **3. Name of requester.** [ ] **4. Route relied on:** Annexe E, dispute route, requester. Ref. QA/CR/1187. **5. Signature.** [ ]
> **6. To be completed by the office.** [ ]

**He put a period of twelve months in a box and the reason in the space provided, and it is the only number in a box in the whole of this business that is about the future rather than about something that has already happened and been counted.**

He read it four times. Then he took it to the post office in Lantern Row, which is a small one, and had it weighed and stamped and sent registered with acknowledgment of delivery to the Registry's public counter address, which is the address he has written in a notebook since the sixteenth of October, off a carbon in a drawer, in a typewriter face, dated the seventh of November 1974.

The clerk at the counter asked him the name and the address and he gave them both without a pause, and the woman at the counter wrote them on a form and did not ask him why a man was sending a registered letter to the Quiet Accord Registry with a printed form number in the reference box, and he put eleven stamps on it, and it cost four pounds and sixty, and it is the only money he has spent in eleven weeks on anything.

Then he stood in the middle of Lantern Row for about four minutes in the cold.

Then he went and telephoned Mara Iles, and it was the ninth thing he had not told anybody about and the first one he told on purpose, and the whole telephone call took ninety-one seconds:

"That was the assessor. On the nineteenth of November. In the Hall. She gave me a form with a box on it for a period, twenty-eight days from the date of determination, and the last day is the seventeenth of December, and she wrote the date on the back of her own copy in pencil and would not send it, and she said that if I did not use it she would regard it as the correct outcome. I did not tell you I had it. I have had it for twenty-three days and I have told two people in the world and one of them is the one I have just telephoned."

"Where have you sent it."

"Registered. Today. To the public counter address, with a requester's further question in the box and a period in the box for a period, and the period is twelve months, and I have put a reason in the space provided, and the reason is that every other period in this business has been about somebody needing something by a Friday."

"And the question?"

"The Registry's, not mine. There's one thing the schedule has no box for and I have read it about nine times and the box does not exist in any of the four hundred copies, and the Registry's own assessor told eleven people in a public gallery on the nineteenth of November that the schedule has no threshold in it for the number or age of the people in a room." He said the sentence out loud on a pavement in Lantern Row. "*How many people are in the room, and how old are they.*"

"That is a good question."

"It took me twenty-three days to write it and I had it in about the first four, and I have spent the other nineteen days frightened of putting it in a box and sending it, and I want you to know that, and I am telling you and not asking you anything."

"I am not going to say anything," said Mara Iles. "And I have four things and I am going to say one of them and then stop. **Roth will not send you anything back.** She will list it, and it will come to a day with a name on it, and you will get a letter, and the letter will be a form with a date on it and a reference, and it will be from a woman who has never seen a piece of the evidence, and she will be right, and it will take about eleven weeks. And in eleven weeks you will still be thirty-two in a profession that does not exist, in a city where nobody in it knows you, and there will be a question in a room with an assessor in it that you put there, and you will be permitted to be there, and that is the whole of what a form is."

"That's all right."

"That is not all right," said Mara Iles, "and I have said my one thing and I am going to ring you back at eight o'clock on Monday because I am not doing this on a Saturday, and it is Saturday, and I have had a hard six weeks."

**The door was not opened.**

The sub-group resolved on the fourth of December, at the end of its fourth sitting, that it did not need the room and asked not to be given it, and the resolution is item 1.2 of the minutes and it is one line and it is the only line in forty-one pages of that inquiry that three trustees agreed without anybody speaking:

> **1.2** That the sub-group has no need of the room at the east end of the Quiet Wing corridor and does not ask to be given it; that item 1 is reserved to the Director; and that the Director will open it, herself, as the first act of this inquiry, at 09:50 on Sunday 13 December 2024, at the same hour as the reopening of the Hall of Former Faiths, and that **no member of the sub-group, no member of Museum staff and no member of the public will be admitted to the fourth level for that purpose except the Director, the Registrar, the conservator, and one person who is not Museum staff and is not a member of the public.**

The last clause is a minute made by the Board Secretary on the Director's instruction and the Director did not minute herself, and it is the fourth thing in eleven days that she asked somebody else to write down for her, and the reason is on the face of it, and the face of it is *a person who is not Museum staff and is not a member of the public*, and there is exactly one person in Morrowgate who fits that description and the Director has never met him.

**He has not been told. He is not on the distribution. He was barred from the building on the first of December and his appointment ended on the eighth, and a man who is not Museum staff and is not a member of the public is not anybody's responsibility on a Sunday morning at ten to ten in the morning, and the notice on the board outside the Hall of Former Faiths that said *no school group* has been taken down and has been replaced with one that says nothing at all, because there is nothing left to say about it on that board, and that board has been the most frightening document in this building for two months.**

He is going to be told on Monday, by post, and it is in the post, and Mara Iles put it in the post on Saturday the twelfth at about four o'clock in the afternoon on a sheet of Museum notepaper that is not a Museum document and has no number on it, and the sheet says:

> **Sunday 13 December, 09:50. Hall of Former Faiths reopens, and then, at the east end of the Quiet Wing corridor on level four, the Director opens the door.**
> **There is a clause for you. You are not Museum staff and you are not a member of the public. Come at half past nine. Bring the notebook. **Do not bring it into the room.** — M.I., 12 Dec 2024.**

And it is the sixth document in two months that has been put into a museum by a person who was not asked, and it is the first one that anybody has ever sent to him on purpose, and he will read it on Monday, and Monday is not this.
